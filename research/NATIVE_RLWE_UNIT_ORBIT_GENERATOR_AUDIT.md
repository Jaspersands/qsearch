## 1. Correct The Completion Ideal First

In an integer negacyclic lift of the normalized public matrix `[I,M]`,
write a relation as `v1=(g,-f)`, with

```
k=(g-Mf)/q in R,
B=[qI,-M;0,I],  v1=B(k,-f).
```

For ANY second kernel vector, write

```
v2=(G,-F)=(MF+qz,-F).
fG-gF=q(fz-kF).
```

Therefore the possible constant determinants are governed by the ideal
`(f,k)`, not just `(f,g)`. Let delta_K be the minimal positive integer in
`(f,k)`. Exact transformed HNF of `[C_f,-C_k]` computes it and a Bezout
witness `fz-kF=delta_K`. Then `G=MF+qz` is automatically kernel-compatible,
and the resulting rotation basis has determinant `(q delta_K)^d` and
index `delta_K^d`. The Schur, reciprocal-energy and Babai certificates in
[the balanced-relation note](NATIVE_RLWE_BALANCED_NTRU_TARGET.md) apply with
this multiplier.

This is the MINIMAL positive constant determinant multiplier for that
fixed relation within K. It is not a minimum among arbitrary different
relations, all integer bases, or nonconstant determinant polynomials.
The earlier ambient-ideal construction remains a valid sufficient method,
but can overcharge the index: `(f,g)=(f,qk)` is contained in `(f,k)`.
Thus delta_K divides the ambient integer order and the scalar norm-gcd.

For the native relation `(q,0)`, k=1,f=0: delta_K=1 although the ambient
ideal `(q,0)` has integer order q. A full native kernel basis exists,
but its geometric profile is bad. Primitivity and useful geometry are
different obligations. This corrects an unnecessary restriction in the
previous sufficient completion recipe, not that recipe's correctness.

The kernel ideal is lift-invariant: `M -> M+qH` gives `k -> k-Hf` and
leaves `(f,k)` unchanged. The GEOMETRY of a chosen native second line
need not be lift-invariant: changing the lift is cross-block mixing.

Checking `(f,k)=R` and extending such a relation are ordinary exact
integer linear algebra, even when R has a nontrivial class group. No
principal-ideal, class-group or unit-group oracle is required for that
verification/completion step. The hard part remains finding a useful line.

## 2. A Common Unit Cannot Change The Line Or Its Volume

For a nonzero relation, define its joint Gram matrix

```
A=C_g^T C_g+C_f^T C_f,  a_geo=det(A)^(1/d).
```

For a common field unit u, multiplication sends

```
v1 -> u v1,  A -> C_u^T A C_u,
det(A) -> det(A) Norm(u)^2 = det(A).
```

The projective direction `[g:f]` is unchanged, as is the coefficient metric's
geometric mean a_geo. Units can redistribute embedding sizes, not turn an
arbitrarily high-volume line into a q-scale one. If the orbit is already
well scaled, this redistribution can be extremely useful; do not claim
units never help.

For a rotation basis with this first R-block and constant ring determinant
Q=q delta, split its 2d GS lengths into two blocks. Their squared products
are det(A) and Q^(2d)/det(A). AM-GM gives, for EVERY common unit and every
completion preserving that first block's volume,

```
Gamma >= d[a_geo+Q^2/a_geo] >= 2d Q.
```

The same first-prefix argument covers integer unimodular changes confined
to each whole R-block. Mixing blocks can change the first-prefix volume
and is outside this restriction.

The relaxed reciprocal-energy proxy has a similar lower bound. For
`c=1+d^2/4`, Jensen on the logarithmic eigenvalues gives

```
c trace(A')+Q^2 trace(A'^-1) >= d[c a_geo+Q^2/a_geo].
```

This relaxation allows every positive embedding rescaling of determinant
one, a larger set than actual units. If even it fails, a unit optimizer
cannot rescue the proxy. Passing the relaxation does NOT certify a unit
with the required precision, integrality or algorithmic cost.

## 3. Stronger Fixed-Native-Line Obstruction, Including S-Units

The two native R-lines are generated by `(q,0)` and `(-m,1)`, where M is
the fixed centered negacyclic/adjoint lift. ANY nonzero field scalar u
that sends the first vector to an integer point IN K must have u in R:
its first block must belong to qR. For the second vector, integrality of
its second block already forces u in R. This includes proposed S-unit
divisions; fractional scalars do not evade these membership conditions.

For nonzero u in R, `|Norm(u)|>=1`. Consequently

```
||(qu,0)||^2 >= q^2,
||(-mu,u)||^2 >= det(I+M^T M)^(1/d).
```

The second inequality follows from the transformed joint Gram determinant
and trace/d. It is an ALL-SCALARS statement on two FIXED lines, not a
bounded unit search or an asymptotic probability claim.

If both lower bounds are >=t^2/E^2, ANY basis whose first column belongs
to either line has `E^2 Gamma>=t^2`. This rejects the all-coset Babai
certificate for arbitrary column order, different scalars per column,
and mixtures within each native line. It does NOT reject cross-block
sums, a different lifted line, arbitrary lattice methods, a particular
chosen-coset witness, source-specific statistical decoding or quantum
algorithms. A bad global certificate does not prove that every individual
nearest-plane output is bad.

On the saved native d64 kernel, both lanes pass the EXACT obstruction:

```
q^2 E^2 >= t^2,
det(I+M^T M) (E^2)^d >= t^(2d).
```

No floating eigenvalues or computed unit basis is needed. Ordinary
classical LLL already escapes these lines: its saved first row has BOTH
nonzero f and nonzero k. A quantum unit wrapper around the unmodified
native generators would be solving the wrong search space even with
free, perfect unit access.

## 4. What The Literature Supplies, And What It Does Not

[Eisentrager, Hallgren, Kitaev and Song](https://www.cse.psu.edu/~sjh26/units-stoc.pdf)
give a quantum unit-group algorithm polynomial in degree and log
discriminant. That is a real number-theoretic primitive, not a proof of
the joint-metric, rank-two relation finder required here.

[Biasse and Song's expanded S-unit treatment](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02280v1)
details S-unit computation and related ideal tasks. Their polynomial
class-group/PIP-related applications require the stated assumptions,
including GRH where used. Compact algebraic representations do not by
themselves give short, explicitly expandable coefficient vectors or
metric-aware kernel maps. Keep every representation/precision cost.

[Cramer, Ducas, Peikert and Regev](https://eprint.iacr.org/2015/313.pdf)
prove efficient log-unit decoding for suitable typical short-generator
distributions in prime-power cyclotomics. Their general principal-ideal
SVP guarantee has factor `2^(tilde O(sqrt(d)))`. Neither statement directly
supplies native uniform rank-two relations with polynomial balance.
That upper bound is too weak to certify the polynomial headroom here;
this does NOT prove the actual algorithm fails on our ensemble. Do not
claim every unit-selection problem requires a generic CVP oracle: their
special decoding result is precisely a counterexample to that overstatement.

The missing reduction would need to show that a useful original-metric
native relation exists in the presented line and satisfies the decoder's
cost/distribution promises. Computing units or shortening a canonical
principal-ideal generator is not such a reduction.

## 5. Keep One Genuine Conditional Opportunity

For a general relation, a field scalar u preserves integrality AND kernel
membership exactly when `u(f,k)` is contained in R. If `(f,k)=R`, this
forces u in R, so no fractional scalar lowers its Gram determinant.
If `(f,k)=alpha R` is a proper principal ideal, division by alpha does
give a new integral kernel relation and lowers the volume. Quantum PIP
could matter in extracting this common content. Units then act on the
primitive quotient and may repair its embedding shape.

This is NOT a discovered native generator. Both native lines have
coordinate ideal R, so there is no hidden common content to extract.
An input-generated principal-content vector still needs a SHORT primitive
quotient in the original metric. Planting such a quotient changes the
input promise; drawing arbitrary coefficients in a known ideal does not
create the required cancellation between qk and Mf. Nonprincipal content
needs a separate construction, not a silently invoked generator.

Retain this branch only if a proposal gives an actual native-law source of
such vectors, a costed content-extraction/shape procedure, and a stronger
matched classical comparison. A generic promise that "PIP returns a
short vector" is inadequate.

## 6. Revised Search Space And Falsifiers

The next generator must search NEW projective directions `[g:f]` obeying
`g=Mf (mod q)`, with controlled original height, reciprocal energy and
kernel-coordinate content. Known unit-period structure operates within
a direction. Rank-two modular rational reconstruction is not automatically
an abelian hidden subgroup problem. A quantum construction must explain
how its state/evaluation access reaches new directions and charge its
normalization and preprocessing; conditioning for free on short relations
would assume the operation being sought.

Ranked next theory work:

1. Derive an actual inter-line quantum operation, or a decisive classical
   reconstruction attack on the SAME native labels and source records.
2. Establish typical joint-energy/content laws for a costed relation
   source, rather than assuming LLL outputs or ideal generators are Gaussian.
3. Investigate common-content extraction only with a credible native-law
   construction and evidence that the primitive quotient is useful.
4. Unit-shape optimization AFTER a useful volume-scale direction exists;
   not a standalone unit-enumeration engine on the native basis.

Falsifiers: exact orbit-volume rejection; classical LLL/primal recovery;
proper-source distribution mismatch; field-dependent hidden CVP cost;
nonintegral S-unit output; or a valid primitive quotient whose final
profile still fails. Failed sufficient gates do not establish hardness.
