# Structured EDCP: Coherent Gaussian Packets And The Continuous Surrogate

Date: 2026-09-28. LOCAL DERIVATION / REVIEW PENDING.

Question: can coherent combinations of individually poor canonical Gaussian
states repair the native conditional state efficiently? This follows
`STRUCTURED_EDCP_CONDITIONAL_METRIC_AUDIT.md`, not a restored circuit search.
The answer depends on the access model. Small individual overlaps do NOT
bound the fidelity of an arbitrary normalized superposition. They DO charge
its standard linear-combination preparation and ancilla-only postselection.

A separate continuous calculation gives an exponential product-packet rank
requirement. That is NOT a quantum circuit lower bound: the same continuous
Gaussian is generated by explicit Gaussian transformations and lies inside
a well-established classical simulation regime for Gaussian measurements.
The discrete modular conditioning, rather than continuous entanglement,
remains the unresolved operation. No novelty or verified-proof claim.

## 1. An Access-Sensitive Packet Bound

Fix labels and a frequency u on the good event of the conditional metric
audit. Write psi for the native normalized integer-coset state. Let
phi_1,...,phi_M be normalized candidate packets in that SAME coefficient
Hilbert space, with certified overlaps

    |<psi|phi_j>|<=E_j.

Centers, positive embedding metrics and complex combination coefficients
may depend on the labels and u. Grant controlled preparation of every packet
for this audit; actual preparation costs must still be supplied by a proposal.
For arbitrary complex c_j define

    y=sum_j c_j*phi_j, alpha=sum_j |c_j|, n_y=||y||,
    F=|<psi|y>|^2/n_y^2,
    Lambda=alpha/n_y.

Assume alpha,n_y>0; a zero vector is not a successfully prepared state.
The triangle inequality gives the exact certificate

    sqrt(F)<=sum_j |c_j|*E_j/n_y,
    Lambda>=sqrt(F)/max_j E_j.                                         (1)

This is independent of M and of the dictionary's Gram condition number.
Near-linear dependence can increase the normalized output fidelity only
by also increasing the cancellation/normalization cost in (1).

The standard prepare-select-unprepare construction has successful output
y/alpha, hence

    p_success=n_y^2/alpha^2=Lambda^(-2),
    p_success*F <= (sum_j |c_j|*E_j/alpha)^2.                            (2)

For completeness, prepare index probabilities pi_j=|c_j|/alpha and the
joint state sum_j sqrt(pi_j)|j>|phi_j>. Project the index onto the unit
vector with coefficients sqrt(pi_j)*exp(-i*arg(c_j)). The resulting data
vector is exactly y/alpha. This directly charges the normalization; no
abstract rescaling or good Gram condition is assumed.

Linear combinations through controlled unitaries are standard; see
[Childs and Wiebe, Hamiltonian Simulation Using Linear Combinations of
Unitary Operations](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1202.5822), including Appendix A.
Equations (1)-(2) here are the direct source-overlap specialization, not a
claim that all algorithms using those unitaries have the same cost.

### More General Index-Only Instruments

Starting with sum_j sqrt(pi_j)|j>|phi_j>, allow an arbitrary
trace-nonincreasing instrument on the INDEX and ancillary registers, but
no further operation on the coefficient data. Let p be its success and
F the successful data state's squared fidelity with psi. Put
z_j=sqrt(pi_j)*<psi|phi_j>. The successful index effect E satisfies 0<=E<=I,
so

    p*F=z^dagger*E*z<=||z||^2<=sum_j pi_j*E_j^2.                       (3)

This includes multiple accepted outcomes, mixed output and arbitrary index
basis changes. Discarding failed outcomes cannot turn (3) into deterministic
high-fidelity preparation. Data-register feedforward or a genuinely new
data-mixing unitary is outside this scope and must be analyzed separately.

## 2. Broad Versus Narrow Packets

Use the conditional audit's sigma,s0,epsilon,kappa and its joint label event.
For B_j satisfying lambda_max(B_j)<=1/(2*s0^2), its all-center bound is

    E_broad=C_epsilon*eta^(d/4),
    C_epsilon=(1+epsilon)/(1-epsilon), eta=4/(kappa+2).

Thus ANY all-broad dictionary obeys

    p_success<=min(1,E_broad^2/F),
    ordinary amplitude-amplification repetition proxy
                  >=sqrt(F)/E_broad.                                  (4)

For target F>=0.99, epsilon=0.01 and kappa=d, the numerical references are:

| d | log10 upper base success | log10 lower repetition proxy |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 64 | -38.9377490511 | 19.4688745255 |
| 256 | -231.601906310 | 115.800953155 |
| 1024 | -1233.43099599 | 616.715497997 |

The sigma,s0,L and joint exceptions are the comparison choices in the
conditional audit; this table does not establish those premises anew.
It covers arbitrarily many packets, even with free index preparation.
This is a repetition proxy for amplifying THIS successful block, not a
lower bound on all circuits involving packet-preparation unitaries.

Narrow packets remain a real loophole. The existing uniform bound

    E_narrow=min(1,C_epsilon*sqrt(2*r/(1+r^2))),
    r=sigma/(sqrt(2)*s0),

only supplies a polynomial obstruction in the stated asymptotic regime.
It does not rule out polynomial amplification. If a fraction lambda of
the LCU coefficient l1 mass uses narrow packets, (2) gives

    p_success*F <= [(1-lambda)*E_broad+lambda*E_narrow]^2.              (5)

For a general index instrument use the corresponding probability-weighted
square bound from (3), not (5)'s stronger LCU-specific expression. A viable
packet proposal must now exhibit useful narrow packets, their actual
preparation, a LOWER overlap/success bound, and charged erasure/amplification.
Merely adding more broad packets or optimizing complex coefficients fails.

### Necessary Cancellation Countercontrol

Let psi,b be orthonormal and

    phi_+=e*psi+sqrt(1-e^2)*b,
    phi_-=e*psi-sqrt(1-e^2)*b.

Both individual overlaps are e, yet their normalized equal superposition
is exactly psi. Its standard preparation succeeds with probability e^2.
This saturates (2). It explicitly falsifies the tempting claim that small
individual overlaps alone exclude a high-fidelity vector in their span.
These are algebraic controls, not quantum-algorithm candidates.

## 3. A Stronger Rank Statement In The Continuous Model ONLY

The ambient audit's continuous native Gaussian factors over d/2 base
conjugate pairs. At one such pair, the complex embedding coordinates have
covariance K=W*W^dagger, where

    W_(r,0)=1,
    W_(r,j)=a_j(zeta)+beta^j*omega^(r*j),
    beta^L=q-zeta, omega=exp(2*pi*i/L).

Partition one complex extension coordinate from the other L-1. Local
whitening on the two sides reduces the position-Gaussian correlation to
one circular-complex pair plus independent modes. Its residual parameter is

    a_cond=[K_(0,0)*(K^(-1))_(0,0)]^(-1/2).

Regression onto ALL other coordinates cannot be worse than onto coordinate
1 alone, so a_cond<=sqrt(1-|corr(z_0,z_1)|^2). The ambient good-label
condition therefore implies a_cond<=2/kappa for each good base pair.

To obtain the quantum Schmidt spectrum, first take ONE real pair with
unit position variances and correlation c. Set

    a=sqrt(1-c^2), xi=(1-a)/(1+a).

The normalized square root of its Gaussian probability density has a
Mehler expansion with squared Schmidt coefficients

    lambda_n=(1-xi)*xi^n, n>=0.                                        (6)

For example, use normalized Hermite functions at scale
gamma=1/sqrt(2*a), with expansion parameter sign(c)*sqrt(xi).
Their Mehler exponent equals
-(x^2-2*c*x*y+y^2)/(4*(1-c^2)), the required square-root density exponent.
This also fixes the probability-versus-amplitude factor of two.

A circular-complex pair comprises two real pairs, so its largest squared
Schmidt coefficient is [2*a/(1+a)]^2, at most eta^2 on a good base pair.
Groupwise independence now gives, across the corresponding global cut,

    largest squared Schmidt coefficient <=eta^(d/2).                  (7)

The general Gaussian modewise decomposition is established in
[Botero and Reznik, Modewise Entanglement of Gaussian States](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0209026),
Theorem 1. Equations (6)-(7) specify the parameter matching for this native
position Gaussian, rather than treating a classical covariance as a quantum
entanglement certificate without constructing its wavefunction.

Any coherent sum of M product vectors across this cut has Schmidt rank at
most M, irrespective of cancellations or Gram conditioning. Schmidt
truncation therefore gives

    F_continuous<=min(1,M*eta^(d/2)).                                   (8)

This includes arbitrary product functions, not only product Gaussians.
For F>=0.99 and kappa=d, necessary log10 M values at d=64,256,1024 are
38.9551214094,231.619278668,1233.44836835. These are CONTINUOUS product-rank
references, not integer-fiber circuit or packet-count lower bounds.

## 4. Why This Is Not Evidence Of Quantum Computational Hardness

Two independent scope failures prevent that inference.

First, integer modular conditioning is not a tensor-local operation on
embedding coordinates. Even in the simple finite coefficient representation,
conditioning a positive product g(x)g(y) on x+y=0 mod Q produces

    sum_x g(x)*g(-x)|x>|-x>,

with Schmidt rank Q whenever g is everywhere positive. Uniform g gives Q
equal coefficients. Thus a SINGLE conditioned product packet can already
have large Schmidt rank. The real embedding lattice is not an independent
Cartesian grid either. Do not transfer (8) to conditioned lattice packets.
The valid discrete statement is (1)-(5), using certified coset overlaps.

Second, the continuous state has an explicit efficient Gaussian description
and transport. At a base pair write

    W=V*S, V_(r,j)=beta^j*omega^(r*j),
    S=I+e_0*(0,a_1(zeta),...,a_(L-1)(zeta)),
    S^(-1)=I-e_0*(0,a_1(zeta),...,a_(L-1)(zeta)).

The nonidentity part squares to zero. With a_rest=(a_1(zeta),...,a_(L-1)(zeta)),

    cond(W)<=(q+1)^((L-1)/L)*(1+||a_rest||)^2.                          (9)

Indeed V has singular values sqrt(L)*|beta|^j, with |beta|>1, and both
||S|| and ||S^(-1)|| are at most 1+||a_rest||. Balanced coefficient lifts
give ||a_rest||<=sqrt(L-1)*d*(q+1)/2. For fixed L and q polynomial in d,
these matrices and their inverses have polynomial magnitude/conditioning.

Realify W as T. The quadrature transformation

    (position,momentum) -> (T*position,T^(-T)*momentum)

is symplectic. Its coordinate unitary sends an independent Gaussian to the
native correlated continuous Gaussian, including the determinant Jacobian.
An SVD decomposes this transport into orthogonal mixing and one-mode
squeezing. Polynomially bounded singular values require logarithmic
squeezing parameters; this is not an exponentially long product expansion.
Usual precision, energy and Gaussian-gate costs must still be stated.

Moreover, known Gaussian inputs, quadratic Gaussian transformations and
quadrature measurements, with the allowed feedforward, admit efficient
classical simulation under the conditions of
[Bartlett, Sanders, Braunstein and Nemoto](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0109047).
No speedup evidence follows from sampling this continuous surrogate.
Unknown input-state/oracle access cannot be replaced by a known covariance
description for free. Nor does this simulation theorem cover discrete comb
inputs, modular conditioning, or arbitrary non-Gaussian measurements.

The continuous transport sends an integer lattice to T*Z^N, not to a fresh
independent coefficient grid or an automatically erased modular fiber.
Rounding, discarding carry registers, or assuming a coherent inverse of a
many-to-one map would remove precisely the unresolved resource. Smoothing
of scalar probabilities does not justify that quantum-state substitution.
