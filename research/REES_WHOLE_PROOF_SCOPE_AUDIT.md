## 1. Decision

No counterexample to the short composition in REES_HSP_REDUCTION.md was
found in this audit. Its central hypotheses are substantive but compatible:
ordinary coherent function access; a prime-field Lie algebra; fixed class
below the characteristic; fresh copies; and complete-instrument precision.
The literature supplies the required subgroup correspondence and fixed-degree
diagonal solver. The new composition, not those ingredients, needs scrutiny.

The important scope change is a CONDITIONAL COROLLARY:

> If the proposed Rees receiver is correct, ordinary HSP for uniquely encoded
> finite nilpotent groups of any fixed nilpotency class is solvable in bounded-
> error quantum polynomial time. Neither the prime factors nor the exponent
> need be bounded. Group-operation and presentation-acquisition costs are paid.

The oracle contract here is the standard uniquely encoded black-box group
model with supplied generators and identity, coherent group operations
(including inverses), and a fixed-width XOR function oracle
U_f|g,y>=|g,y XOR f(g)>. Thus U_f inverse is available, not inferred from
copy access. Time is polynomial in the supplied encoding/generator/label
lengths and log(1/epsilon), for each fixed c. All oracle uses are charged.

This is wider than the explicit-table input of the compiler. It follows by
composing existing input/reduction methods with the proposed core, not by
running the present Python module on an opaque group. The old class-four
input notes already establish the shape of this transport. The argument
below extends the proof, not their class-four implementation, to fixed c.

Do not spend the next theory pass implementing a new prime-power SDE solver,
extending a field routine to a torsion ring by substitution, or rebuilding
the old potential compiler. Those are unnecessary for this corollary.

## 2. Audit Of The New Core

**The original ambient need not be gradable.**
[REES_NONGRADABLE_INPUT.md](REES_NONGRADABLE_INPUT.md) supplies integer
certificates for the known Dixmier--Lister algebra: at every p>3 all
derivations have cube zero, precluding a positive grading. This persists
for arbitrarily many direct-sum copies. The public Rees compiler still
has correct evaluation maps and dilations; selected hidden-preimage checks
retain nonnormal, nonabelian and cross-factor diagonal subgroups. This is
an algebraic scope control, not a full quantum instrument run or a proof
that all finite automorphism actions on the original group are impossible.

**Subgroups really give linear subspaces.** Fact 2.14(3) of
[d'Elbee et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.17595v2#S2.SS3) states the
subgroup/Lie-subalgebra correspondence for exponent-p groups of class c<p.
Fact 2.13 identifies scalar multiplication with group powering. This is
over F_p, not an arbitrary extension field. For example, the additive
subgroup F_p of F_(p^2) is not F_(p^2)-linear. Restrict scalars first if
using extension-field coordinates; choose interpolation points in F_p.

**Evaluation is a genuine homomorphism.** In A=sum t^r gamma_r(L), a
bracket above degree c is zero in L, not merely dropped. Evaluation therefore
preserves the bracket, and every finite BCH Lie monomial. The coefficient
map from c distinct nonzero evaluations is invertible over F_p. Applying it
modulo h proves the tuple-fiber claim without computing h or its intersections.
The associated graded cannot replace A: its degree-one quotient loses h's
original derived coordinates. No dimension or class restriction on h is used.

**Central conditioning is physical, including forbidden outcomes.** With
R_g|x>=|x*g^-1>, the operators Pi_lambda=|Z|^-1 sum_z chi_lambda(-z)R_z
are mutually orthogonal projectors summing to I. They can be measured by
an auxiliary uniform Z register, controlled central translations and its
abelian Fourier transform. The target remains the FULL group register.
For K=H intersect Z, the outcome has mass |K|/|Z| on K-perp and zero elsewhere.
Only positive-mass outcomes are normalized. The denominator |G||K| in the
conditional density is necessary; K is never an algorithmic input.

**The action order and sign do not assume normal H.** For control values
q,r, tracing the targets gives Tr(rho_lambda R_(sigma(r)^-1 sigma(q)))
after the dilation. This is chi_lambda(z) for g=h*z and zero off HZ.
Right-coset equality is symmetric, so g in HZ gives exactly the desired
quotient density kernel. A different consistent character sign changes
all moments by a common sign, not the cancellation equation. Replacing
right translations by left translations without changing the source is
not justified when H is nonnormal.

**The diagonal solver has the correct promise.** The input is an arbitrary
list of observed vectors, not a uniform distribution assumption. Sections
3.1--3.3 of [Ivanyos--Santha](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.09016) give a
fixed-degree polynomial-time method with sufficient arity
B_d(r)=[d^(d(d-1)/2)(r+1)^d]^ceil(log2(d+1)). Their gcd reduction covers
d not dividing p-1. Check the returned moment and nonzero support exactly.
There is no search through p^M assignments in the algorithmic claim.

**Conditioning does not accumulate a hidden postselection cost.** Every
positive-mass record tuple and every valid solver witness gives the same
quotient density. Zero-scale targets are consumed. Fresh independent
source preparations, rather than just correct one-copy marginals, justify
tensor contraction. Classical dependence of the scales on ALL observed
labels is allowed because the identity holds pointwise for each tuple.

**Recursion preserves the premise.** Images of the homogeneous lifted
subalgebra remain invariant under every nonzero dilation in each public
graded quotient. At degree one the hidden image is all h, not its image
in L/L'. Thus the last abelian procedure already returns the full answer.
This removes normalizer recursion from this exponent-p core ONLY.

**Polynomial time is a fixed-class statement.** Public bracket evaluation,
BCH, controlled translations, uniform preparation and abelian QFT have
polynomial bit/gate cost at fixed c. There are polynomially many nodes in
the fresh-copy tree then, even though its constants are enormous. Approximate
the whole instruments, including their classical records and abort outputs;
use a hybrid bound. Closeness of separately normalized rare branches is
neither required nor a sufficient replacement.

## 3. No Free Coordinate Oracle

First take a known p>c and a uniquely encoded exponent-p group of class
at most c, generated by s given elements. Identity, multiplication and
coherent ordinary f are the input operations. The following construction
uses no information about H. It generalizes the written class-four route
in CLASS_FOUR_BLACK_BOX_INPUT.md.

For fixed c there are universal two-input rational group words ADD_c and
BRACKET_c giving Lazard addition and bracket. A constructive proof already
appears in BOUNDED_CLASS_METABELIAN_HSP.md: correct the lowest wrong weight
in the free two-generator Lie algebra using integral Hall commutators;
repeat through c. Denominators have prime factors at most c and are units
modulo p. Their lengths W_c and coefficient bit lengths depend only on c.
Ordinary repeated squaring evaluates them in O(W_c log p) group operations.
This is not a bound uniform in c. UNIVERSAL_LAZARD_WORD_COMPILER.md now
implements exact filtration correction and replayable word certificates
without a fixed table cutoff; no native quantum circuit is synthesized.
Published effective inverse BCH is prior art; this
argument does not import an unread implementation complexity theorem.

Enumerate right-nested LIE brackets of the s generators of lengths <=c.
Their number t is at most sum_(j=1..c) s^j. Jacobi and nilpotence show their
F_p-span is a Lie subalgebra containing the generators, hence all L. The
original generators alone need not span additively. Define

    F: F_p^t -> encoded G,    F(a)=ADD_i(b_i^a_i).

This is a surjective homomorphism to the additive group of L. It is not
the ordinary product of the b_i powers. Its unique output encodings hide
ker F. Known abelian HSP learns that kernel; a linear complement gives
an additive basis g_1,...,g_n and its public forward encoder E. This uses
zero original f calls. The kernel/quotient construction is the method of
[Cheung--Mosca](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0101004), not new here.

For each z=BRACKET_c(g_i,g_j), apply abelian HSP to

    F_z(a,u)=ADD(E(a),z^u).

On a correct basis the kernel is span((-coord(z),1)). Recover the classical
coordinates, verify by forward encoding, and obtain the public bracket
table. Require the right kernel dimensions and check the resulting Lie
identities, but do not confuse those checks with proof of completeness.
Charge at most 1+binom(t,2) kernel calls with fixed polynomial clocks on
ALL histories, including histories after an erroneous earlier kernel.
Canonical RREF kernel outputs permit independent-repeat amplification.

Once setup succeeds, transport an oracle query by computing E(x), calling
f, and reversing E's computation while retaining x and the hiding label.
No coherent E^-1 is requested. Reversible evaluation of a public circuit
is not the same as solving its inverse problem. Return final generators
by E. Amplify in this fixed chart, not by voting on arbitrary generator
lists from independently reacquired bases.

This proves a conditional black-box exponent-p corollary with polynomial
setup in s, encoding length, log p and log(1/error), at fixed c. It does
not provide a classical basis algorithm or an implementation of quantum
kernel acquisition. The separate copy-only note supplies the source
interface without changing this paid, H-independent setup.

### Alternative: Keep The Unknown Relations In A Free Preimage

The acquired-chart route is sufficient, not mandatory for a LOCAL
exponent-p call. FREE_LAZARD_PULLBACK.md gives a public free class-c Lie
algebra F on the supplied generators and a surjection pi:F->log G.
Evaluate pi with public inverse-BCH words, without target coordinates or
relation-kernel acquisition. The ordinary composite hides pi^-1(h).
One fresh original copy and controlled right translation produce its exact
mixed coset density, with no kernel-index postselection or source inverse.
Run the explicit-Lie Rees core on F and project a recovered FULL preimage
basis to G. Surjectivity is the supplied-generator premise, not inferred
by testing sample relations. The opaque backend now has a general fixed-c
compiler, retaining shorter class-four formulas on their valid range.

The free grading need not preserve pi^-1(h). The Dixmier--Lister kernel
relation [a,d]+[b,[a,c]] mixes degrees two and three and supplies a concrete
falsifier of omitting Rees. Also, free dimension d<=sum_(j=1..c) s^j may
EXCEED target encoding width b. Section 5's b-based numerical envelope is
for the acquired-chart route only. This alternative uses actual free
dimensions/ranks in every core, register and precision budget, plus paid
public evaluation. It simplifies a dependency, not necessarily the runtime.

## 4. General Exponents And Primes

Use the established fixed-class nilpotent-group reduction of
[Ivanyos--Sanselme--Santha, Section 3](https://www.irif.fr/~santha/Papers/iss12.pdf).
It provides the refined-polycyclic computational interface, Sylow splitting,
normalizer/quotient reduction to trivial or order-p hidden subgroups, and
exponent-p restriction for p exceeding the class. These are imported
algorithms, not new Rees claims. Their group computations and quantum
presentation acquisition are part of the total time.

For a local p>c call with |H_local|<=p, every hidden element lies in the
order-p subgroup Omega_1. Restrict the ordinary oracle to that public
subgroup, acquire its exponent-p Lie chart as above, and run the Rees core.
Do NOT restrict the original arbitrary H to Omega_1 once and stop. In
C_(p^2), taking H=G would then return only the subgroup of order p.

For p<=c, p is bounded because c is fixed. The EXACT bounded-prime theorem
of [Imran--Ivanyos](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.08376) requires source unitary
and inverse, but its Proposition 3 supplies a forward-only bounded-error
HSMC route. Central erasure consumes copies; final ordinary Fourier
sampling does not need the source inverse. REES_COPY_ONLY_OUTER_REDUCTION.md
spells out the transport and original-copy budget. Public group-operation
inverses are still paid. Approximation of mixed outputs alone still would
not justify inverse calls if one chose the different exact algorithm.

The small-prime branch is not a field-valued Lazard argument. For example,
the involutions of a dihedral 2-group need not form a subgroup. Nor are
all large-prime factors free inputs: quantum order finding, factoring and
presentation acquisition are charged in the imported preprocessing. No
integer-factorization difficulty is counted as a new quantum speedup.

Public quotient sections may be nonhomomorphic. The outer controller's
invariant is that the divided-out known subgroup K is contained in H and
normal in the CURRENT normalizer. It makes f on representatives well-defined
on the quotient. Flatten successive public representative computations
into one forward circuit, one original f query, and one reverse circuit.
There is no exponential nesting of query wrappers. This outer normalizer
recursion remains necessary in the cited general-exponent reduction;
the Rees cover only removes it inside the exponent-p core.

## 5. Whole-Run Accounting

Let b>=1 bound the unique encoding width and let c>=1 be fixed. Then
log_2|G|<=b. On correct histories the sum of all Sylow composition lengths
is <=b. A normalizer recovery makes at most r(r+1) local solver calls in
a p-group of composition length r: <=r successful enlargements and at
most one final scan, each of length <=r. Consequently

    B=b(b+1)

caps all large-prime local calls across Sylows. Impose it operationally;
abort instead of allowing incorrect histories to remove the bound.

Split a total epsilon into four equal allocations: group preprocessing,
all large-prime chart acquisition, all large-prime core runs, and all
small-prime runs. Set each local large-prime allowance eta=epsilon/(4B).
At most c bounded-prime calls share their allocation. A first-error union
bound applies even though the local inputs depend on previous answers.
Every chart kernel call is further capped/amplified within its allowance.

On the acquired-target-chart route, all correct local Lie dimensions and
lower-central ranks are <=b. A
conservative bound on ORIGINAL HIDING QUERIES in the LARGE-PRIME CORE CALLS is

    Q_large <= B*c*(b+t)*product_(d=2..c) B_d(b),
    t=ceil(log_2(12B/epsilon)).

Each lifted source uses at most c original queries, with clean public
representatives. This bound deliberately replaces local ranks by b.
Its b-exponent at fixed c,epsilon is at most

    3 + sum_(d=2..c) d*ceil(log_2(d+1)).

For c=9 this is147, not145. The145 belonged to a single explicit-Lie core.
This is NOT the exponent of the whole gate runtime: add presentation,
coordinate acquisition, the SDE computation, arithmetic, approximation,
and the bounded-prime algorithms. Nor is Q_large the total query count
if the input has small-prime Sylow factors. The module records these
exclusions explicitly instead of inventing their numerical constants.

## 6. Attempted Refutations And Remaining Bottleneck

- Dropping F_p-linearity breaks interpolation modulo h. The input is
  explicitly restricted to F_p; restriction of scalars is charged.
- A nonnormal H does not break the right-action proof; replacing the
  action convention without changing the state can break it.
- Nonuniform labels do not break the pointwise conditional identity.
  Omitting |H intersect Z| or normalizing forbidden labels does.
- Source correlations do break the tensor contraction. The existing
  countercontrol remains active; fresh preparations are mandatory.
- Supplied group generators do not automatically form an additive basis.
  Bracket closure and paid quantum kernel learning repair that gap.
- Passing kernel-relation checks does not prove kernel completeness.
  The success guarantee comes from the amplified known quantum algorithm.
- Small primes and large exponents do not fit the core directly. Separate
  published branches, not modular arithmetic substitutions, handle them.
- Growing class defeats the polynomial claim. DCP, GI, code equivalence,
  and ECDSA implications have not been obtained.

The audit removed an input-scope uncertainty; it did not find a useful
application or independently validate the new core. A literature search
through 2026-10-22 found the standard class-two, bounded-prime fixed-class,
and special semidirect-product results. The scope in
[Morales, v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.05321) is restricted scalar-action
semidirect products and structured quasi-Hamiltonian groups. These inspected
statements do not subsume the conditional corollary. Search absence is
not a novelty proof, and the whole literature has not been exhaustively read.

2026-10-22 latest-paper check: [Imran, arXiv2610.28368v2, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.28368v2)
states a bounded-error polynomial algorithm for hidden PARABOLIC subgroups
of GL_n over effectively presented finite chain rings. Its input has a
flag-stabilizer promise and an oracle on the full general linear group;
its digit-lifting methods use structured socle probes. The statement does
not subsume arbitrary hidden subgroups of the nilpotent input here. This
comparison inspects the theorem and interface, not an independent proof
review of the preprint (v1 October20, v2 October21).

Two local interface checks prevent an unjustified transfer. First, for
odd p every parabolic of GL_n(F_p) contains all nonzero scalar matrices,
so it cannot equal a p-subgroup. An embedding of the ambient p-group
alone supplies neither the parabolic promise nor the extended hiding
oracle; a new reduction would have to supply both. Second, the Rees Lie
cover's evaluation at nonzero s is not evaluation of the coefficient
RING F_p[t]/(t^(c+1)): a ring homomorphism sending t to s would require
s^(c+1)=0. Rees evaluation is instead a Lie homomorphism because the
corresponding high-weight brackets vanish in L. Shared polynomial notation
does not justify exchanging the access models. These checks do NOT rule
out a different reduction or a useful transfer of the new paper's methods.
