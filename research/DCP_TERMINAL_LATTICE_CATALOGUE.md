# DCP Moment Routes: Count Final Lattices, Not Their Histories

Status: LOCAL DERIVATION / REVIEW PENDING. No independent theorem review,
formal proof, novelty claim, quantum algorithm or computational lower bound.

## Research Decision

The existing constant cube-section gap is stronger than the stale near-log
frontier text suggests. This pass goes further: terminal lattices have short
generating certificates, regardless of how many earlier rank transitions
occurred. Counting these certificates removes the history/position overhead.

For fixed register offset, the resulting nonnegative nongeneric-tuple bound
vanishes for every `k=o(sqrt(n/log n))`, rather than only
`k=o((n/log n)^(1/3))`. It also controls these SAME contributions under
joint uniform-legal, per-label uniform-legal and planted target laws. It does not eliminate selected
geometric events, signed observables, implicit witness algorithms or arbitrary
collective quantum measurements.

Do not invest in another moment-order sweep in this regime unless its proposed
signal is explicitly outside this dominated class. Do not promote the result
to a blanket prohibition on structural quantum advantage.

## 1. Exact Source And Statistic

Let `N=2^n`, `m=n+c>=1`. The labels `a_1,...,a_m` and independent target
`S` are uniform in `Z_N`. For a fixed instance define

    C(a,S) = #{x in {0,1}^m : a.x=S mod N}.

For an ordered distinct k-tuple of assignments, let A have these assignments
as its k rows. Append the target column `-1`. Its integer column lattice is
unchanged if the sign of that column is reversed:

    L = span_Z(1, A's Boolean columns).

Let r be its integer rank, d_i its nonzero Smith factors, and

    I_L = product_i |d_i| = [Sat(L):L].

Uniform independent labels and target give the EXACT probability

    P(all k assignments solve) = N^-r product_i gcd(N,d_i).       (1)

Call the tuple GENERIC when `rank_F2([1,A])=k`. Equivalently `r=k` and
all d_i are odd. Its probability is exactly N^-k. Every other tuple is BAD.
Its probability is at least N^-k, including small n and large Smith factors.
Consequently factorial-moment excess is nonnegative. Define D_k(a,S) as the
number of BAD ordered distinct tuples that actually solve this instance.
This is a nonnegative integer statistic, not an efficient evaluator.

## 2. Sparse Terminal Generator Lemma

Every lattice generated by 1 and Boolean columns, of rank r, has a generating
subset containing 1 and at most

    H_r = floor(sqrt(r^r)),
    g_r = r-1+floor(log_2 H_r)                            (2)

DATA columns. The remaining all-ones column is not charged as data.

Proof: select r-1 actual independent data columns, with 1, spanning the
terminal rational space. A nonzero r-by-r Boolean minor has absolute
determinant at most H_r by Hadamard. The saturation index of this initial
lattice divides every such full-rank minor, so is at most H_r. If it is not
yet the desired integer lattice, some remaining actual column is outside it.
Adjoin that column. The rank stays r, and a proper same-span enlargement
divides the saturation index by an integer at least two. After at most
floor(log_2 H_r) such repairs the exact original lattice is generated.

The independent columns may be selected FIRST even if the original tuple
presented them later. This is an existence/counting certificate, not a claim
that online transfer histories are shorter. Preserving only the rational
span would be wrong: the probe retains a rank-four determinant-two lattice
which needs an additional column to become Z^4.

Pad the certificate with zero columns to length g_r. There are at most
`(2^k)^g_r` possible ordered padded certificates. Thus this bounds the NUMBER
of terminal lattices of rank r. No selection of coordinate positions and no
factor polynomial in m is needed. This catalogue may be exponential in k;
it is not a polynomial enumeration algorithm.

## 3. Constant Cube Gap And Source Bound

Reuse the existing cube-section lemma, with its hypotheses retained:
the real span contains 1 and its coordinate restrictions are distinct because
the assignment rows are distinct. For rank r<k,

    b_L = |L intersect {0,1}^k| <= (3/4)*2^r.

Briefly, an injective coordinate projection identifies the span with R^r.
A remaining noncoordinate linear functional has coefficients summing to one
and at least two nonzero coefficients. On each corresponding two-bit square,
at most three of four values can be Boolean. For a full-rank BAD lattice,
its mod-two image is proper, so `b_L<=2^(k-1)` instead.

The saturation index I_L is also at most H_r, using actual independent Boolean
columns. Equation (1) is therefore at most H_r/N^r. For a fixed terminal
lattice, at most b_L^m column sequences can generate it. Counting sequences
whose columns merely lie in it OVERcounts safely. We only apply the gap to
lattices satisfying the distinct-coordinate condition, not every certificate.

Put rho_r=3/4 for r<k, and rho_k=1/2. Summing over possible ranks gives

    B_k(n,c) = sum_{r=2}^{min(k,m+1)}
                 H_r * 2^(k*g_r+r*c) * rho_r^m,

    0 <= E[(C)_k]-(2^m)_k/N^k <= E[D_k] <= B_k.          (3)

Rank one cannot have two distinct coordinate restrictions when it contains 1;
rank above m+1 is impossible. Duplicate certificates only increase the bound.
Odd-index full-rank lattices are generic and must not be charged as bad.
No independence assumption about k-tuples or random-map replacement is used.

Because `g_r=O(r log r)`, the positive exponent is
`O(k^2 log k+k|c|)`, versus a negative exponent Omega(n) at fixed offset.
Thus (3) vanishes whenever `k^2 log k+k|c|=o(n)`. For fixed c this includes
`k=o(sqrt(n/log n))`, including k=n^(2/5). This is a sufficient regime, not
an exact boundary or evidence of a signal above it.

The report's finite bounds use integers only. For rank-deficient terms,
`(3/4)^5<1/4` implies `rho^m<=2^(-2 floor(m/5))`. The full-rank decay is
exactly 2^-m. Ceil-log2 Hadamard and rank-sum overheads are conservative.
Finite numerical rows are not the asymptotic proof.

## 4. Rare Targets: Four Different Source Laws

UNIFORM independent target: Markov applied to D_k gives

    Pr[D_k>=tau] <= B_k/tau.                             (4)

JOINT uniform source conditioned on legality: the exact first two moments are
`E C=lambda=2^c` and `E C^2=lambda+lambda^2-lambda/N`. Cauchy--Schwarz gives
`Pr[C>0]>=lambda/(1+lambda)`. Therefore

    Pr[D_k>=tau | C>0] <= (1+2^-c)*B_k/tau.              (5)

Conditioning changes the LABEL marginal. This is NOT automatically the law
that first draws uniform labels, then draws a uniform legal target for EACH
label. That per-label law has the following SEPARATE argument.

PER-LABEL uniform-legal target: write `L(a)=#{s:C(a,s)>0}` and
`w(a)=N/L(a)`. The zero assignment ensures L>=1. Per-label Cauchy--Schwarz
and the deterministic identity `sum_s C=M` give

    w(a) <= (N^-1 sum_s C(a,s)^2)/lambda^2,
    E_a w(a) <= 1+(1-1/N)/lambda <= K=1+2^-c.

For ANY legal event, let A(a) be its probability under a uniform residue
target. Then `0<=A<=L/N=1/w`. Its per-label legal probability is
`p=E_a[w A]`. Another Cauchy--Schwarz application gives

    p^2 <= E[w] E[w A^2] <= K E[A].

Taking the event D_k>=tau and using (4) gives

    Pr_per-label-legal[D_k>=tau]^2 <= K*B_k/tau.          (6)

This square-root loss still preserves exponential decay in the stated regime.
It explicitly keeps the original label marginal. This change-of-measure
lemma is reusable for other LEGAL events with a bound on their uniform source
mass; it does not assume occupancy independence or a quenched Poisson theorem.

PLANTED target: draw uniform labels, draw a uniform Boolean assignment, and
set S=a.x. Relative to the independent uniform-target law its density is
`C/lambda`. Extend any BAD k-tuple by another solution. If the new solution
equals an existing one, there are k choices. Otherwise the extended tuple
remains BAD: its mod-two image projects onto the old deficient image. Hence
the per-instance inequality

    C*D_k <= k*D_k+D_(k+1)

is exact, with ordered tuples. The change of measure yields

    Pr_planted[D_k>=tau] <=
        (k*B_k+B_(k+1))/(lambda*tau).                    (7)

Use k+1 inside the sufficient regime, not only k. For fixed c and tau=n^-A,
all four bounds vanish exponentially once the stated overhead is o(n).

These inequalities cover label/target-adaptive selection only when its signal
is pointwise dominated by the charged statistic, or when it certifies an
event contained in `D_k>=tau`. They do not authorize changing the source,
granting free rejection sampling, or claiming that ALL rare fibers are easy
or absent. Generic tuples and ordinary Poisson occupancy remain present.

## 5. Attempts To Falsify The Derivation

1. Integer versus rational span: retain an index-two starting basis repaired
   to index one. Charge the repair and verify exact HNF equality. Reordering
   must not discard this correction.
2. Column-certificate versus actual-tuple counting: enumerate all reachable
   Boolean-column lattices at orders two through five. Extract certificates
   from actual contained Boolean patterns and verify exact preservation,
   Hadamard bounds and the separate rank-deficient/full-rank cube ratios.
3. Random-map assumption: enumerate COMPLETE modular label distributions at
   n<=3, including all-zero, even-only, colliding and illegal-target instances.
   Compare exact factorial moments with their independent-map baseline and
   the rational bound. Small enumeration checks the formulas, not all n.
4. Target-law bias: compute BAD counts, `C*D_k`, inverse legal fractions and
   per-label legal events on every n=2,m=3 source instance. Check the
   next-order and occupancy inequalities per instance before averaging;
   verify that uniform and per-label legal event probabilities differ.
5. Numerical optimism: test the certified binary upper bound against exact
   rational expressions at small n. Separately recompute its integer
   arithmetic from the generated artifact.

## 6. What Remains Worth Trying

High priority remains a costed NONUNIFORM witness generator on the actual DCP
source, or a physical collective channel whose reversible implementation
implies one. Witness finding is not refuted by ordinary occupancy looking
random. A concrete source-aware preconditioner, collision transport operation,
or nonnegative statistic outside (3) needs an explicit input/output contract
and classical competitor before another scaling run.

A signed observable is only an escape if its decoder implication, variance,
estimator cost and absence of domination by D_k are supplied. Merely renaming
the moment statistic or increasing k is not a mechanism. An arbitrary
quantum circuit need not be represented by this moment expansion; no all-circuit
no-go follows. The maximum-occupancy law, e.g. k near n/log n, is outside the
sufficient regime and has NOT been proved here.

Primary-source context, not novelty evidence: the DCP/lattice and average
subset-sum route is [Regev](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0304005); the optimal
measurement and restricted-measurement subset-sum connection is
[Bacon, Childs and van Dam](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0501044). This
derivation is a research-route audit, not a replacement algorithm for either.
