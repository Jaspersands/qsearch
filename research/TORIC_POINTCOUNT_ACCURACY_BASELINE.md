# Classical baseline at the toric point-count accuracy scale

2026-11-06. Independently reviewed written classical composition. The
ambient estimator retains the source's odd-characteristic restriction;
the exact Jacobi branch is valid in every characteristic. Four prescribed
algebra controls pass. No new quantum algorithm,
novelty claim, or refutation of the full quantum theorem.

## 1. Scientific question and exact inputs

Maeda--Yano, arXiv:2608.23929, Problem 1.1 and Theorem 1.2, give a quantum
algorithm for the torus point count of an EXPLICIT Laurent polynomial

    f(x)=sum_{j=1}^s a_j x^{u_j},  a_j in F_q^*, u_j in Z^n,
    N_f=#{x in (F_q^*)^n : f(x)=0}.

The augmented support U has columns (1,u_j), rank rho over Q, and entries
bounded by C. The promised additive error is

    epsilon q^(n+s/2-rho).                                  (1)

For fixed bounds rho<=r0 and C<=C0, the published quantum algorithm is
polynomial in input length, logq, 1/epsilon and log(1/delta). Its mechanism
samples the Smith-normal-form character kernel and estimates products of
normalized Gauss sums. The support and coefficients are explicit; this is
NOT a native character oracle or an unknown-polynomial source. The question
here is whether (1) actually requires resolving the arithmetic fluctuation
of a nonsingular toric family before this becomes a new research direction.

We use canonical support: combine duplicate exponent vectors and remove
zero coefficients. In this note s denotes the remaining distinct terms.
If support becomes empty, return (q-1)^n immediately; a single nonzero
monomial has count0. The nontrivial branches below use nonempty support.
An effective ODD-characteristic finite-field representation and rational epsilon in (0,1)
are part of the input. No hidden zeta function or geometric oracle is given.

## 2. Classical theorem for nondegenerate support with s>rho

Put r=rho-1>=1. Let L be the SATURATED lattice

    span_Q{u_j-u_1} intersect Z^n.

Choose a Z-basis b_1,...,b_r of L and complete it to a basis of Z^n.
The corresponding unimodular monomial change of torus coordinates writes

    f=x^{u_1} g(y_1,...,y_r),
    N_f=(q-1)^(n-r) N_g.                                    (2)

This is a bijection of the full torus over EVERY F_q. It does not use
roots, a discrete logarithm, or the potentially nonsaturated lattice
generated by the differences. The Newton polytope Delta of g is full
dimensional. Require Newton nondegeneracy: for every nonempty face tau,
INCLUDING Delta, the equations

    g_tau = y_1 partial_1 g_tau = ... = y_r partial_r g_tau = 0

have no solution in the algebraic torus over the algebraic closure.
Ordinary smoothness alone is not this promise. Let v=r! vol(Delta), volume
measured in the saturated character lattice.

For every such input of odd characteristic with s>=rho+1, a deterministic classical algorithm
achieves (1) in poly_{r0,C0}(input length,logq,1/epsilon) bit operations.
It does not evaluate Gauss sums or compute the unknown middle-cohomology
trace. Constants and the exponent of 1/epsilon depend on r0,C0.

### Published bound and exact ambient term

Sperber--Voight, *Computing zeta functions of nondegenerate hypersurfaces
with few monomials*, equations (1.1), (1.2), (1.12), and the paragraph before
Lemma 4.8, give the required bound. Their Dwork space has dimension v;
the characteristic polynomial has a factor (1-T), and the remaining
v-1 inverse roots after division by q have complex modulus at most
q^((r-1)/2). In their notation, if beta_i are these roots, then

    N_g = A_r(q) + (-1)^(r+1) sum_{i=1}^{v-1} beta_i,
    A_r(q)=((q-1)^r-(-1)^r)/q,
    |N_g-A_r(q)| <= (v-1) q^((r-1)/2).                       (3)

The sign follows directly by taking the coefficient of T in log L:
S_1=(-1)^(r+1)(1+q sum beta_i), and q N_g=(q-1)^r+S_1.
A_r(q) is an integer. This is a published weight estimate, not a new
cohomology theorem or an inference from finite point counts.

### Algorithm and all resource costs

1. Compute the saturated coordinate reduction and v by integer linear
   algebra and fixed-dimensional polytope volume. They depend only on
   public exponents. No nondegeneracy certificate is needed when the
   input is promised to satisfy it.
2. If q>=max(2,((v-1)/epsilon)^2), return
   (q-1)^(n-r) A_r(q).
3. Otherwise enumerate (F_q^*)^r, evaluate the explicit g, and multiply
   its exact count by (q-1)^(n-r).

For step 2, (2)--(3) and (q-1)^(n-r)<=q^(n-r) give

    error <= (v-1) q^(n-r+(r-1)/2)
          <= epsilon q^(n-r+s/2-1),                        (4)

since s>=r+2 supplies a gap (s-r-1)/2>=1/2. The right side is exactly (1).
The small-q branch costs at most
max(2,((v-1)/epsilon)^2)^r field evaluations, with polynomial bit cost per
evaluation. Powers and the final answer use O(n logq) output bits. Thus
there is no uncharged q-dependent enumeration in the claimed regime.

Uniform constants follow without assuming a supplied coordinate system.
Select r ambient coordinate rows injective on L tensor Q. Their projection
maps L to a full-rank sublattice of Z^r of index D>=1. The projected original
Newton polytope lies inside [-C,C]^r, so

    v = r! vol(projected Delta)/D <= r! (2C)^r.              (5)

HNF/SNF computes L and a unimodular completion in polynomial bit time.
The transformed exponent bit lengths are polynomial in the explicit input
length; repeated squaring therefore pays evaluation even if the chosen
basis is not geometrically short. Also s<=(2C+1)^rho: projection onto rho
independent rows of U is injective on its column space. Both observations
prevent growing ambient n from hiding growing intrinsic complexity.

## 3. Scope guard and decisive falsifier

Nondegeneracy is essential to THIS estimator. For

    f=(1+x)(1+y+z),  n=3, s=6, rho=4,
    N_f=(q-1)^2+(q-2)^2,
    A_3(q)=q^2-3q+3,
    N_f-A_3(q)=(q-1)(q-2),                                 (6)

the error exceeds epsilon q^2 for q=41,epsilon=1/2. This polynomial is
degenerate at the intersection of its two factors. It is trivially
classically countable by factorization and inclusion-exclusion, so (6)
is neither a hard input nor a quantum advantage witness.

The equality case s=rho has no half-power gap in (4) and is NOT covered
by the ambient-term algorithm. Section 4 supplies a different exact
classical route. General Newton-degenerate inputs with s>rho are still
outside the combined theorem. The unrestricted #P-hardness theorem of
Maeda--Yano changes the support promises and does not establish hardness
on the bounded class.

## 4. Equality s=rho: exact fixed-order Jacobi reduction

This branch assumes explicit canonical support, s=rho, fixed rank and
bounded exponent entries. It does NOT require Newton nondegeneracy and
works over any effective finite field. It computes N_f EXACTLY in expected
poly_{r0,C0}(input length,logq) classical time. A capped version fails with
probability at most delta in poly(input length,logq,log(1/delta)) time.

Compute the integer Smith factors d_1|...|d_s of U, and put

    g=gcd(d_s,q-1).                                         (7)

Hadamard bounds d_s by a constant depending on r0,C0. Since U has full
column rank, every element of ker(U mod(q-1)) is killed by d_s. Therefore
every character in an admissible tuple has order dividing g. Pick a
character psi of order g. ALL admissible tuples are exactly

    (psi^e_1,...,psi^e_s),  e in (Z/gZ)^s, Ue=0 mod g.        (8)

Enumeration of (8) is constant-size in the promised parameters, even if
the original ambient number of variables grows. There is no hidden
large-order character or generator of F_q^*.

### Pay the character labels without a discrete logarithm

For g>1, sample a uniformly in F_q^* until zeta=a^((q-1)/g) has exact
order g, checked using the prime factors of the constant integer g.
Success probability is phi(g)/g. Define psi(b)=omega^k when
b^((q-1)/g)=zeta^k, with omega a formal primitive complex g-th root of
unity. The lookup table has g entries, and psi(a)=omega. Powers a^i
therefore supply representatives for every character value. For a strict
cap, use ceil(log2 q) random bits per raw trial, reject integers outside
the public q-element field encoding and reject zero. For q=p^a, decode
an accepted integer into its a base-p coefficients in the public field
basis. This is a bijection and preserves uniformity. At least a fraction
phi(g)/(4g) of raw trials produce a valid generator. Set

    R=ceil((4g/phi(g))*ln(1/delta))

raw trials for failure at most delta; there is no unbounded rejection
subroutine in this capped version. For g=1, all characters are
trivial and the formula below is directly rational.

### Recover all bounded-order Jacobi sums from curve counts

For 0<=i,j<g, let C_ij be the number of nonzero affine solutions to

    a^i u^g+a^j v^g=1.                                    (9)

Pila, *Counting points on curves over families in polynomial time*,
arXiv:math/0504570, Theorem F, counts each fixed-family smooth projective
curve A X^g+B Y^g=Z^g in poly_g(logq) time uniformly over nonzero A,B.
It is smooth because p does not divide g, and g is fixed. Its genus is
(g-1)(g-2)/2. The theorem constructs the necessary Jacobian presentations;
none is supplied as advice by this input. There are only boundedly many
possible g, so these family algorithms form one finite public collection.

Subtract the disjoint boundary loci X=0, Y=0 and Z=0. Their respective
counts are g or zero according as B, A and -B/A are g-th powers in F_q^*.
Each is tested by one exponentiation. This gives exactly C_ij, not the
projective count and not a value with omitted zero corrections.

Extend EVERY psi^e, including the trivial character, by value zero at 0,
and define J(e,h)=sum_x psi^e(x)psi^h(1-x). Root-count orthogonality gives

    C_ij=sum_{e,h=0}^{g-1} omega^(-ie-jh) J(e,h),
    J(e,h)=g^(-2) sum_{i,j=0}^{g-1} omega^(ie+jh) C_ij.       (10)

All of (10) is exact arithmetic in the fixed cyclotomic field Q(omega),
with coefficient bit length O_g(logq). It is not a quantum Fourier
transform or an approximation to individual Gauss sums.

### Assemble the finite character expansion

Use Maeda--Yano Proposition 3.1:

    N_f=(q-1)^n/q + (q-1)^(n+1-s)/q
          * sum_{e:Ue=0 modg} product_j G(psi^(-e_j))psi^e_j(a_j).
                                                               (11)

The first row of U guarantees sum e_j=0 modg. Products in (11) are computed
without evaluating any individual nontrivial G. Use G(1)=-1 and, for
nontrivial alpha,beta,

    G(alpha)G(beta)=J(alpha,beta)G(alpha beta), alpha beta!=1;
    G(alpha)G(alpha^-1)=alpha(-1)q.                         (12)

Process factors in any order: retain one current nontrivial character;
combine with the next using the first identity, or cancel a reciprocal
pair using the second and restart. The total product character is trivial,
so no nontrivial Gauss factor remains at the end. Coefficient character
values use the bounded lookup above. Formula (11) then yields the exact
integer count in polynomial bit work; n+1-s>=0 follows from rank<=n+1.
For g=1 it reduces immediately to the sole tuple G(1)^s=(-1)^s.

The classical algorithm invokes the published fixed-family curve counter
as a theorem-level algorithm, not an implemented library call. No claim
that this large-constant algorithm is practical, novel, or run here.

## 5. Research decision and fixed controls

Combining Sections 2 and 4 matches the advertised accuracy classically
for the WHOLE odd-characteristic Newton-nondegenerate bounded-support
class (and counts every s=rho input exactly even without nondegeneracy).
The remaining s>rho Newton-degenerate class is not solved here. This
removes the generic nondegenerate class as a reason to start a new quantum
point-counting subsystem at accuracy (1). The published quantum algorithm
remains valid. The comparison does not provide an operation that improves
native character-polynomial reconstruction.

The prescribed finite controls are f=1+x+y+z+1/(xyz) over F_41 and F_43,
epsilon=1/2. Sage/Singular certifies all 15 nonempty Newton faces in each
case by the torus-saturated unit ideal, volume v=4 and rank rho=4. Exact
counts are 1664 and 1734, ambient counts 1561 and 1723, errors 103 and 11,
both below the respective weight bounds 123 and 129 and requested errors.
For (6) at q41, exact count3121 versus ambient1561 gives error1560,
larger than requested840.5. All values are exact field/rational algebra;
there is no finite-field asymptotic inference. Script and artifact:
classical_baselines/toric_pointcount_accuracy_control.py/.json. No parameter
sweep or quantum simulation was performed.

The prescribed fixed-order control uses q13,g4 and
f=1+x^4+y^4+z^4. All16 projective-to-torus boundary subtractions and
Fourier-inverted Jacobi sums match direct finite-field sums. The64

These are exact Sage/cyclotomic algebra checks of the identities. The
polylogarithmic Pila algorithm was NOT executed: finite curve counts in

## 6. Portfolio comparison and remaining scientific priority

Before this pass, corpus been-tried and baseline lookups were run for
native character reconstruction/global quadratic copies and for toric
point counting. Broad matches did not supply a point-count baseline; the
primary papers above supplied the actual comparison. A bounded state-
learning literature search found Boolean polynomial phase-state results,
but did not settle exact coverage of the recorded global odd-prime
character-copy theorem. Its novelty/priority gate therefore stays UNKNOWN.

Independent constructive triage considered three alternatives. Native
univariate character reconstruction retains the largest clear unresolved
query/time gap; the desired output is all coefficients, with target
poly(d,logp) resources under the existing squarefree and prime promises.
The proposed Gauss-variable lift would supply additive phases but no paid
collective construction was found. Public candidate-state preparation
alone normalizes a postselected analysis map by approximately p^(-d/2),
so merely naming a pretty-good measurement does not pay its implementation.
These are rejected proposals, not new generic lower-bound theorems.

The independent alternatives were full-table scalar OPI (native explicit
IID halfsets, output a polynomial meeting the known DQI score, with
matched classical line-search/Algorithm-H baselines) and orthogonal
parabolic HSP (ordinary coset access, output the hidden isotropic object).
No new scalar-OPI operation was identified; the latter lacks paid transport
from arbitrary coset states to the proposed Lie-coordinate source. Neither
was admitted for implementation. Existing corrections to overbroad
classical exclusions remain in force; lack of a receiver is not hardness.

The single highest-value unresolved question remains uniform
poly(d,logp) native character-polynomial reconstruction. This point-count
composition removes one tempting independent direction at its advertised
accuracy, rather than manufacturing a new subsystem. Any continuation
must exhibit an informative paid operation and its success/resource law,
then run the strongest native classical attack. No nearby noise/parity
variation, larger finite sweep, or residual-only application is queued.
