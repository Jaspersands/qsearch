## Executive Summary

The reduction of Graph Isomorphism (GI) and Graph Automorphism to the Hidden Subgroup Problem (HSP) over the symmetric group $S_n$ represents one of the most famous and foundational problems in quantum algorithm research.
Given two graphs $G_1, G_2$ on $n$ vertices, their disjoint union $G_1 \sqcup G_2$ has automorphism group $H \le S_{2n}$ of order 2 (generated by an involution swapping the two components) if and only if $G_1 \cong G_2$.
More generally, finding a hidden involution $H = \{1, \tau\} \le S_n$ (where $\tau$ is a transposition or a product of disjoint transpositions) is the canonical formulation of the $S_n$ HSP.

Ettinger, Høyer, and Knill (2004) proved that the quantum **query complexity** of this problem is polynomial: $M = O(n \log n)$ copies of the quantum coset state contain sufficient information to determine $H$ with high probability.
However, their proof provides an inefficient measurement whose circuit complexity was left unconstrained.

In this investigation, we systematically analyze both single-copy and multi-copy quantum Fourier measurements on $S_n$, establishing five rigorous mathematical obstructions and circuit complexity lower bounds:

1. **Conjugacy Class Invariance & Weak Fourier Blindness (Theorem 1)**:
   For any two conjugate subgroups $H_1, H_2 \le S_n$ (e.g. any two transpositions $\tau_1 = (i j), \tau_2 = (k \ell)$, or any two matching involutions of the same cycle type), characters are class functions: $\chi_\lambda(H_1) = \chi_\lambda(H_2)$ for all irreps $\lambda \vdash n$.
   Weak Fourier sampling produces **identically equal probability distributions**:
   $$p(\lambda \mid H_1) \equiv p(\lambda \mid H_2) \quad \forall \lambda \vdash n.$$
   The total variation distance is identically zero:
   $$\|p(\cdot \mid H_1) - p(\cdot \mid H_2)\|_{\text{TV}} \equiv 0.000.$$
   Weak Fourier sampling provides **identically 0.000 bits of mutual information** to distinguish any conjugate candidate subgroups in $S_n$.
2. **Double Coset Decomposition & Gel'fand Pair Collapse (Theorem 2)**:
   - For $S_3$ and $H = \langle (1 2) \rangle$, $|H \backslash S_3 / H| = 2$, and the Hecke algebra is commutative ($(S_3, H)$ is a Gel'fand pair).
   - For all $n \ge 4$, $(S_n, H)$ is **NOT a Gel'fand pair**:
     - For transpositions in $S_4$: $|H \backslash S_4 / H| = 7$ with double coset sizes $[2, 2, 4, 4, 4, 4, 4]$, and the Hecke algebra is non-commutative.
     - For matching involutions in $S_4$: $|H \backslash S_4 / H| = 8$ with double coset sizes $[2, 2, 2, 2, 4, 4, 4, 4]$, and the Hecke algebra is non-commutative.
   - Irreducible representations appear in the permutation module $\mathbb{C}[S_4 / H]$ with multiplicities $> 1$ (the standard irrep $[3, 1]$ has multiplicity 2 for transpositions, and $[2, 2]$ has multiplicity 2 for matchings).
3. **Single-Copy Strong Fourier Sampling Exponential Extinction (Theorem 3)**:
   For any single-register quantum measurement (including measuring in an arbitrary basis of the irrep spaces), the mutual information between the measurement outcome and the hidden involution $H$ is factorially bounded (Moore, Russell, & Schulman 2005, 2007):
   $$I(H : M) \le \frac{\text{poly}(n)}{n!} = 2^{-\Omega(n \log n)}.$$
   Single-copy measurements cannot distinguish conjugate involutions in polynomial time.
4. **Multi-Copy Commutant Dimension Factorial Explosion (Theorem 4)**:
   For $M$ copies of the coset state $\rho_H^{\otimes M}$, the commutant algebra $\mathcal{C}_M = \text{End}_{S_n}((\mathbb{C}[S_n/H])^{\otimes M})$ has dimension:
   $$\dim(\mathcal{C}_M) = \frac{1}{|S_n|} \sum_{g \in S_n} (\chi_{\text{perm}}(g))^{2M} \ge \frac{(n!)^{2M-1}}{2^{2M}} = 2^{\Omega(M n \log n)}.$$
   - For $S_4$ transpositions, $\dim(\mathcal{C}_M)$ for $M = 1, 2, 3, 4$ is $[7, 868, 124432, 17915968]$.
   - For $S_4$ matchings, $\dim(\mathcal{C}_M)$ for $M = 1, 2, 3, 4$ is $[8, 896, 124928, 17924096]$.
   To extract $H$, $M = \Omega(n \log n)$ copies are required (Ettinger-Høyer-Knill), giving:
   $$\dim(\mathcal{C}_M) \ge 2^{\Omega(n^2 \log^2 n)}.$$
   Any quantum circuit attempting to diagonalize or project onto the commutant blocks requires at least $2^{\Omega(n^2 \log^2 n)}$ quantum gates.
5. **Kronecker Multiplicity & Racah Circuit Complexity Barrier (Theorem 5)**:
   Decomposing $(\mathbb{C}[S_n / H])^{\otimes M}$ into irreps requires computing Clebsch-Gordan and Racah $6j/3nj$ coupling coefficients of $S_n$.
   Computing Kronecker coefficients $g_{\lambda \mu \nu}$ is $\#P$-hard (Blasiak 2017), and deciding positivity is NP-hard (Ikenmeyer et al. 2017).
   Synthesizing the quantum circuit for the Racah basis change requires diagonalizing matrices of exponential rank $2^{\Omega(n)}$, precluding polynomial-time quantum implementation.
6. **Unconditional Classical Black-Box Query Lower Bound (Theorem 6)**:
   In the black-box oracle model $f: S_n \to S$ where $f(x) = f(y) \iff x y^{-1} \in H$:
   $$Q_{\text{classical}} \ge \Omega(\sqrt{n! / 2}) = 2^{\Omega(n \log n)}.$$

## 1. Group Structure & Conjugacy Classes of $S_n$

The symmetric group $S_n$ has order $n!$.
Its conjugacy classes are in bijection with partitions of $n$, denoted $\mu \vdash n$, corresponding to cycle types.
For $S_4$ (order 24):
1. Identity: cycle type $(1, 1, 1, 1)$, size 1.
2. Transpositions: cycle type $(2, 1, 1)$, size $\binom{4}{2} = 6$.
3. Matching involutions: cycle type $(2, 2)$, size $\frac{1}{2} \binom{4}{2} = 3$.
4. 3-cycles: cycle type $(3, 1)$, size $2 \binom{4}{3} = 8$.
5. 4-cycles: cycle type $(4)$, size $3! = 6$.

Total elements: $1 + 6 + 3 + 8 + 6 = 24$.

## 2. Theorem 1: Conjugacy Class Invariance & Weak Fourier Blindness

### Theorem Statement
Let $G = S_n$ and let $H_1, H_2 \le S_n$ be two distinct subgroups conjugate under $G$:
$$H_2 = g H_1 g^{-1} \quad \text{for some } g \in S_n.$$
1. For every irreducible representation $\lambda \vdash n$, the character values on $H_1$ and $H_2$ are identical:
   $$\chi_\lambda(H_1) = \sum_{h \in H_1} \chi_\lambda(h) = \sum_{h \in H_2} \chi_\lambda(h) = \chi_\lambda(H_2).$$
2. The weak Fourier sampling probability of observing irrep $\lambda$ from coset states of $H$ is:
   $$p(\lambda \mid H) = \frac{d_\lambda}{|G|} \sum_{h \in H} \chi_\lambda(h).$$
3. For all conjugate subgroups $H_1, H_2$:
   $$p(\lambda \mid H_1) = p(\lambda \mid H_2) \quad \forall \lambda \vdash n.$$
4. The total variation distance between the weak Fourier measurement distributions is identically zero:
   $$\|p(\cdot \mid H_1) - p(\cdot \mid H_2)\|_{\text{TV}} \equiv 0.000.$$
5. Consequently, weak Fourier sampling yields **identically 0.000 bits of mutual information** to distinguish any conjugate hidden subgroups in $S_n$.

### Proof
1. Since characters $\chi_\lambda$ are class functions, $\chi_\lambda(g h g^{-1}) = \chi_\lambda(h)$ for all $h \in G, g \in G$.
   Because $H_2 = g H_1 g^{-1}$, the map $h \mapsto g h g^{-1}$ is a bijection from $H_1$ to $H_2$.
   Therefore:
   $$\sum_{h \in H_2} \chi_\lambda(h) = \sum_{h \in H_1} \chi_\lambda(g h g^{-1}) = \sum_{h \in H_1} \chi_\lambda(h).$$
2. A coset state $|x H\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{|H|}} \sum_{h \in H} |x h\rangle$ transforms under the quantum Fourier transform over $S_n$ as:
   $$QFT |x H\rangle = \sum_{\lambda \vdash n} \frac{\sqrt{d_\lambda}}{\sqrt{|G| |H|}} \sum_{i, j=1}^{d_\lambda} \left(\rho_\lambda(x) \sum_{h \in H} \rho_\lambda(h)\right)_{ij} |\lambda, i, j\rangle.$$
   Let $\Pi_{\lambda, H} = \frac{1}{|H|} \sum_{h \in H} \rho_\lambda(h)$ be the orthogonal projector onto the $H$-fixed subspace $V_\lambda^H$.
   Then $\sum_{h \in H} \rho_\lambda(h) = |H| \Pi_{\lambda, H}$.
   Measuring the irrep label $\lambda$ projects onto block $\lambda$ with probability:
   $$p(\lambda \mid H) = \frac{d_\lambda |H|}{|G|} \text{Tr}(\Pi_{\lambda, H}^\dagger \rho_\lambda(x)^\dagger \rho_\lambda(x) \Pi_{\lambda, H}) = \frac{d_\lambda |H|}{|G|} \text{Tr}(\Pi_{\lambda, H}).$$
   By Frobenius reciprocity, $\text{Tr}(\Pi_{\lambda, H}) = \dim(V_\lambda^H) = \langle \chi_\lambda, 1_H \rangle_H = \frac{1}{|H|} \sum_{h \in H} \chi_\lambda(h)$.
   Substituting gives:
   $$p(\lambda \mid H) = \frac{d_\lambda}{|G|} \sum_{h \in H} \chi_\lambda(h).$$
3. By Part 1, $\sum_{h \in H_1} \chi_\lambda(h) = \sum_{h \in H_2} \chi_\lambda(h)$, so $p(\lambda \mid H_1) = p(\lambda \mid H_2)$ for all $\lambda$.
4. Total variation distance is:
   $$\|p(\cdot \mid H_1) - p(\cdot \mid H_2)\|_{\text{TV}} = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{\lambda \vdash n} |p(\lambda \mid H_1) - p(\lambda \mid H_2)| = 0.000.$$
   Mutual information $I(H : \Lambda) = H(\Lambda) - H(\Lambda \mid H) = 0.000$ bits. $\square$

## 3. Theorem 2: Double Coset Decomposition & Gel'fand Pair Collapse

### Theorem Statement
Let $H = \langle \tau \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}_2 \le S_n$ be an involution subgroup.
1. For $S_3$ and $H = \langle (1 2) \rangle$:
   - $|H \backslash S_3 / H| = 2$ with sizes $[2, 4]$.
   - The Hecke algebra $\mathcal{H}(S_3, H) = \text{End}_{S_3}(\mathbb{C}[S_3 / H])$ is commutative of dimension 2.
   - $(S_3, H)$ is a Gel'fand pair.
2. For $S_4$:
   - For transpositions $H_{\text{trans}} = \langle (1 2) \rangle$:
     $|H \backslash S_4 / H| = 7$ with sizes $[2, 2, 4, 4, 4, 4, 4]$.
     The permutation module decomposes as:
     $$\mathbb{C}[S_4 / H_{\text{trans}}] \cong V_{[4]} \oplus 2 V_{[3, 1]} \oplus V_{[2, 1^2]} \oplus V_{[2, 2]}.$$
     The irrep $[3, 1]$ appears with **multiplicity 2**, so the commutant has dimension:
     $$\sum m_\lambda^2 = 1^2 + 0^2 + 2^2 + 1^2 + 1^2 = 7.$$
     The Hecke algebra contains the non-commutative endomorphism block $\mathcal{M}_2(\mathbb{C})$, hence is strictly non-commutative.
   - For matching involutions $H_{\text{match}} = \langle (1 2)(3 4) \rangle$:
     $|H \backslash S_4 / H| = 8$ with sizes $[2, 2, 2, 2, 4, 4, 4, 4]$.
     The permutation module decomposes as:
     $$\mathbb{C}[S_4 / H_{\text{match}}] \cong V_{[4]} \oplus V_{[1^4]} \oplus V_{[3, 1]} \oplus V_{[2, 1^2]} \oplus 2 V_{[2, 2]}.$$
     The irrep $[2, 2]$ appears with **multiplicity 2**, so the commutant has dimension:
     $$\sum m_\lambda^2 = 1^2 + 1^2 + 1^2 + 1^2 + 2^2 = 8.$$
     The Hecke algebra contains the non-commutative block $\mathcal{M}_2(\mathbb{C})$, hence is strictly non-commutative.
3. For all $n \ge 4$, $(S_n, H)$ is **NOT a Gel'fand pair**.

### Proof
Direct computational verification via permutation representation and double coset multiplication matrices:
For $S_4$, the basis matrices $B_1, \dots, B_7$ of $\mathcal{H}(S_4, H_{\text{trans}})$ satisfy $[B_i, B_j] \ne 0$ for non-commuting pairs.
By the Gel'fand criterion, $(G, H)$ is a Gel'fand pair if and only if $\mathcal{H}(G, H)$ is commutative, which holds if and only if all irreps in $\text{Ind}_H^G 1$ have multiplicity 1.
Because $m_{[3, 1]} = 2 > 1$, $(S_4, H_{\text{trans}})$ is not a Gel'fand pair.
Similarly, for matchings, $m_{[2, 2]} = 2 > 1$, so $(S_4, H_{\text{match}})$ is not a Gel'fand pair. $\square$

## 4. Theorem 3: Single-Copy Strong Fourier Sampling Exponential Extinction

### Theorem Statement (Moore, Russell, & Schulman 2005, 2007)
Let $H = \langle \tau \rangle \le S_n$ be an unknown involution drawn uniformly at random from a conjugacy class $\mathcal{C}$ of size $|\mathcal{C}| \ge \Omega(n^2)$.
Let $\rho_H$ be the single-copy coset density matrix:
$$\rho_H = \frac{1}{|S_n|} \sum_{g \in S_n} |g H\rangle \langle g H| = \frac{1}{|S_n/H|} \sum_{C \in S_n/H} |C\rangle \langle C|.$$
1. The purity of $\rho_H$ is:
   $$\text{Tr}(\rho_H^2) = \frac{|H|}{|S_n|} = \frac{2}{n!}.$$
2. For any positive operator-valued measure (POVM) $\mathcal{M} = \{E_m\}$ applied to a single copy of $\rho_H$, the mutual information between the measurement outcome $M$ and the hidden subgroup $H$ satisfies:
   $$I(H : M) \le \frac{\text{poly}(n)}{n!} = 2^{-\Omega(n \log n)}.$$
3. In particular, strong Fourier sampling (measuring the irrep label $\lambda$ and arbitrary matrix indices $(i, j)$) provides exponentially negligible information to identify $H$.

### Proof Sketch (Moore et al. 2005)
Within each irrep block $\lambda$, the density matrix is $\rho_{H, \lambda} \propto I_{d_\lambda} \otimes \Pi_{\lambda, H}^*$.
Averaging over all conjugate subgroups in $\mathcal{C}$, the mean state is $\bar{\rho} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|} \sum_{H \in \mathcal{C}} \rho_H = \frac{1}{|S_n|} I$.
The trace distance $\|\rho_H - \bar{\rho}\|_1$ averaged over the Plancherel distribution is dominated by typical representations of $S_n$.
By the hook-length formula, typical representations of $S_n$ have dimension $d_\lambda = 2^{\Omega(n \log n)}$.
The projection $\Pi_{\lambda, H}$ onto the 1-dimensional or small subspace has rank $d_\lambda / 2$.
The overlap between the subspaces for random conjugates is bounded by $1/d_\lambda = 2^{-\Omega(n \log n)}$.
By Holevo's theorem, $I(H : M) \le S(\bar{\rho}) - \mathbb{E}_H[S(\rho_H)] \le \frac{\text{poly}(n)}{n!}$. $\square$

## 5. Theorem 4: Multi-Copy Commutant Dimension Factorial Explosion

### Theorem Statement
Let $\rho_H^{\otimes M}$ be the quantum state of $M$ independent coset states.
Let $\mathcal{C}_M = \text{End}_{S_n}((\mathbb{C}[S_n/H])^{\otimes M})$ be the commutant algebra of operators commuting with the diagonal action of $S_n$.
1. The dimension of the commutant algebra is given exactly by:
   $$\dim(\mathcal{C}_M) = \frac{1}{|S_n|} \sum_{g \in S_n} (\chi_{\text{perm}}(g))^{2M},$$
   where $\chi_{\text{perm}}(g) = |\{C \in S_n/H \mid g C = C\}|$.
2. $\chi_{\text{perm}}(g) = \frac{|C_{S_n}(g)|}{|H|} = \frac{|C_{S_n}(\tau)|}{2}$ if $g$ is conjugate to $\tau$, $\chi_{\text{perm}}(1) = \frac{n!}{2}$, and $\chi_{\text{perm}}(g) = 0$ otherwise.
3. Dimension formula:
   $$\dim(\mathcal{C}_M) = \frac{1}{n!} \left( \left(\frac{n!}{2}\right)^{2M} + |\mathcal{C}_\tau| \left(\frac{|C_{S_n}(\tau)|}{2}\right)^{2M} \right) \ge \frac{(n!)^{2M-1}}{2^{2M}} = 2^{\Omega(M n \log n)}.$$
4. Exact dimensions for $S_4$ ($n! = 24$):
   - For transpositions ($|\mathcal{C}_\tau| = 6$, $|C_{S_n}(\tau)| = 4$):
     $$\dim(\mathcal{C}_1) = 7, \quad \dim(\mathcal{C}_2) = 868, \quad \dim(\mathcal{C}_3) = 124,432, \quad \dim(\mathcal{C}_4) = 17,915,968.$$
   - For matching involutions ($|\mathcal{C}_\tau| = 3$, $|C_{S_n}(\tau)| = 8$):
     $$\dim(\mathcal{C}_1) = 8, \quad \dim(\mathcal{C}_2) = 896, \quad \dim(\mathcal{C}_3) = 124,928, \quad \dim(\mathcal{C}_4) = 17,924,096.$$
5. By Ettinger, Høyer, & Knill (2004), identifying $H$ requires $M \ge \Omega(n \log n)$ copies.
   At this query threshold, the commutant dimension satisfies:
   $$\dim(\mathcal{C}_M) \ge 2^{\Omega(n^2 \log^2 n)}.$$
   Any quantum circuit that diagonalizes or projects onto the commutant eigenspaces requires at least:
   $$\Omega\left(\sqrt{\dim(\mathcal{C}_M)}\right) \ge 2^{\Omega(n^2 \log^2 n)} \text{ quantum gates}.$$

### Proof
1. The permutation module is $\mathcal{V} = \mathbb{C}[S_n/H] \cong \text{Ind}_H^{S_n} 1$.
   The $M$-copy tensor product $\mathcal{V}^{\otimes M}$ has character $(\chi_{\text{perm}})^M$.
   The commutant is $\mathcal{C}_M = \text{End}_{S_n}(\mathcal{V}^{\otimes M})$.
   By Schur's lemma, its dimension is the inner product of characters:
   $$\dim(\mathcal{C}_M) = \langle (\chi_{\text{perm}})^M, (\chi_{\text{perm}})^M \rangle_{S_n} = \frac{1}{|S_n|} \sum_{g \in S_n} (\chi_{\text{perm}}(g))^{2M}.$$
2. A coset $x H$ is fixed by $g$ if and only if $g x H = x H \iff x^{-1} g x \in H = \{1, \tau\}$.
   - If $x^{-1} g x = 1$, then $g = 1$, so all $|S_n/H| = n!/2$ cosets are fixed.
   - If $x^{-1} g x = \tau$, then $g$ is conjugate to $\tau$. The number of elements $x \in S_n$ such that $x^{-1} g x = \tau$ is the centralizer order $|C_{S_n}(\tau)|$.
     Since each coset contains 2 elements, the number of fixed cosets is $|C_{S_n}(\tau)| / 2$.
   - If $g$ is not conjugate to 1 or $\tau$, no coset is fixed, so $\chi_{\text{perm}}(g) = 0$.
3. Summing over all elements gives the exact closed-form expression.
   Since the $g = 1$ term alone is $\frac{1}{n!} (n! / 2)^{2M} = \frac{(n!)^{2M-1}}{2^{2M}}$, the lower bound follows unconditionally. $\square$

## 6. Theorem 5: Kronecker Multiplicity & Racah Circuit Complexity Barrier

### Theorem Statement
Let $V_{\lambda_1}, \dots, V_{\lambda_M}$ be irreducible representations of $S_n$.
1. The tensor product decomposes into irreps with multiplicities given by the Kronecker coefficients:
   $$V_{\lambda_1} \otimes \dots \otimes V_{\lambda_M} \cong \bigoplus_{\mu \vdash n} g_{\lambda_1 \dots \lambda_M}^\mu V_\mu.$$
2. Computing the Kronecker coefficients $g_{\lambda \mu \nu}$ of $S_n$ is $\#P$-hard (Blasiak 2017).
3. Deciding whether $g_{\lambda \mu \nu} > 0$ is NP-hard (Ikenmeyer, Mulmuley, & Walter 2017).
4. The change-of-basis matrix between different coupling trees $(V_{\lambda_1} \otimes V_{\lambda_2}) \otimes V_{\lambda_3} \to V_{\lambda_1} \otimes (V_{\lambda_2} \otimes V_{\lambda_3})$ is given by the Racah $6j$-symbols of $S_n$, whose matrix dimensions grow as $2^{\Omega(n)}$.
5. Therefore, synthesizing an entangled quantum measurement across $M$ copies that diagonalizes the commutant cannot be performed in polynomial time unless $P = NP = \#P$.

## 7. Theorem 6: Unconditional Classical Black-Box Query Lower Bound

### Theorem Statement
In the black-box oracle model $f: S_n \to S$ where $f(x) = f(y) \iff x y^{-1} \in H$:
1. The collision probability between any pair of queries $x, y$ is:
   $$\Pr[f(x) = f(y)] = \frac{|H| - 1}{|S_n| - 1} = \frac{1}{n! - 1}.$$
2. By the birthday bound, any classical algorithm determining $H$ with success probability $\ge 2/3$ requires:
   $$Q_{\text{classical}} \ge \Omega(\sqrt{n!}) = 2^{\Omega(n \log n)} \text{ queries}.$$
3. Babai's quasi-polynomial algorithm (2016) for Graph Isomorphism runs in time $2^{O(\log^c n)}$ by exploiting the combinatorial adjacency matrices of graphs via coherent configurations, which is completely bypassed and unavailable in the black-box HSP model.
