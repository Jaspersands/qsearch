# Independent cyclic-support proof review

7 November 2026. Actual text reviewed: `CYCLIC_SUPPORT_MONOMIAL_DESCENT.md`,
native model and support lemma. **The support lemma is correct with its
written left-coset/action conventions.** This review proves that lemma;
it does not review the compact-coordinate resource argument, class-generator

## Precise statement and proof

Let E/Q be a finite Galois number field with group G. Let M=Z^r, with
any representation A:G -> GL_r(Z); faithfulness and positive matrix entries
are not required. On (E*)^r use

    (g star x)_i = product_j g(x_j)^(A_g[i,j]).

Let S be a finite set of rational primes containing all primes ramified in
E. Assume that, for every cyclic subgroup C <= G, the ordinary ideal class
group of E^C is generated by classes of finite prime ideals above S. Let
a_g in (E*)^r be a cocycle whose every coordinate is an E-S-unit. Then,
**for every solution** x of a_g=x/(g star x), there exists a solution x'
whose every coordinate is an E-S-unit. No archimedean hypothesis is needed.

Write m_P=(v_P(x_i))_i as a column vector, using normalized integer ideal
valuations. The automorphism convention gives

    v_P(g(x_i)) = v_(g^{-1}P)(x_i).

Consequently, for p outside S and all g in G,

    m_(gP) = A_g m_P.                                      (1)

Fix P above p, put D=G_P and m=m_P. Since E/Q is unramified at p, D is
cyclic. Equation (1) gives m in M^D, including when m has negative entries.

Set F=E^D and q=P intersect F. The extension E/F is Galois with group D.
Its decomposition group at P is D intersect G_P=D. Thus q has **exactly
one** prime above it in E, namely P. Its ramification index is one, because
p is unramified in E/Q. Its residue degree can be |D|: that factor does
not enter the extension-of-divisors valuation used here.

The ordinary class-group assumption supplies an S-supported divisor B on
F with [B]=-[q]. Hence q+B is principal. Choose t in F* with

    div_F(t)=q+B.

Both signs of the coefficients of B are allowed; t need not be integral
or positive. Pulling its divisor to E shows v_P(t)=1 and v_R(t)=0 for
every other finite prime R outside S. This is the primitive valuation step.

For **left cosets** G/D define

    z_i = product_(gD in G/D) g(t)^((A_g m)_i).              (2)

Replacing g by gd changes neither g(t) nor A_g m, since t and m are
D-fixed. Applying h star to (2) replaces each pair
(g(t),A_g m) by ((hg)(t),A_(hg)m). Reindexing left cosets proves
h star z=z exactly, with no root of unity or sign discrepancy.

At P, g(t) has nonzero valuation precisely when g^{-1}P=P, or g in D.
The identity coset contributes valuation vector m, with coefficient one.
More generally, at hP the only contributing coset is hD and

    v_(hP)(z)=A_h m=m_(hP).

There are no other valuations of z outside S except above p. Therefore
x/z is still a solution, and all its coordinates have valuation zero at
every prime above p. Perform this operation for each of the finitely many
rational primes outside S occurring in the coordinate divisors of x.
Each operation affects outside S only its own prime. The resulting x' is
the required S-unit solution. All x, m, t and intermediate corrections
are existential proof objects; the argument does not supply access to an
unknown solution.

Ordinary ideal classes suffice because arbitrary generators of principal
fractional ideals are permitted. The constructed correction is exactly
G-fixed for star, so it preserves every original equation, including real
signs and torsion. No narrow class group, Hasse principle, or saturation of
an invariant lattice occurs. The argument intentionally excludes ramified
primes: there e(P/q) could destroy the primitive valuation step.

For completeness, the permutation-lattice sentence also holds integrally.
For each cyclic C choose a Z-basis of M^C, and map one copy of Z[G/C] per
basis vector m to M by gC -> A_g m. Invariants for D are surjected onto
because the summands indexed by C=D contain D-fixed identity cosets mapping
to a basis of M^D. There are at most |G| cyclic subgroups and total source
rank at most r|G|^2. This is an ordinary permutation-lattice transfer, not
the image of sum_(d in D) A_d, which need not contain primitive invariants.

## One finite control design; not implemented

Use E=Q(alpha,omega), alpha^3=2, omega^2+omega+1=0. Label the roots
alpha_1=alpha, alpha_2=alpha omega, alpha_3=alpha omega^2, and identify
G=S_3 with their permutations. Let A be the permutation representation on
Z^3. At p=5 choose P with alpha reducing to 3 and omega a root of its
irreducible quadratic over F_5. The residue field is explicitly
F_25=F_5[omega]; Frobenius fixes alpha_1 and swaps alpha_2,alpha_3.
Thus D=< (23) >, F=E^D=Q(alpha), and q=(5,alpha-3).

Take m=e_1 and t=alpha+2. Its rational norm in F is 10. Modulo 5 its
only vanishing root factor is alpha=3, so v_q(t)=1 and its remaining
finite support is above 2. This explicit t certifies the particular
principalization needed here; the control need not compute class groups.
Take S containing 2 and 3. With c=(123), use the left-coset representatives
1,c,c^2. Formula (2) must give

    z=(alpha_1+2, alpha_2+2, alpha_3+2).

Check exact star invariance for c and (23), the residue support at P, and
the normalized valuation vectors e_i at the three primes above 5. This
also gives the explicit test solution x=z with a_g=1. The invariant lattice
and norm-image lattices are, respectively,

    M^D = {(a,b,b): a,b in Z},
    (I+A_(23)) M = {(2a,b,b): a,b in Z}.

The required m=e_1 belongs to the first and not the second. Replacing the
transfer by norm-average multiples must therefore fail the primitive
valuation-one check. Do not repair that failed operation by taking an
uncharged square root. Using A_g^{-1} instead of A_g with these coset
representatives gives the reversed-root vector and fails exact star
invariance. This single finite control would catch both errors; it would
not prove the all-field statement or execute a quantum algorithm.
