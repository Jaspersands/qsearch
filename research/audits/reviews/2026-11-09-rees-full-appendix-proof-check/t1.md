## Findings (most severe first)

**F1. MINOR (constructivity; also for the expert).** The time-out in step 2 and its definition.
Locations: `appendix-torsion-blackbox.tex` lines 392-398 (field-solver definition), 411-412
(the proposition's time claim), 424-427 (step 2), 441-446 (well-definedness and the 9 Nov
sentence); `main.tex` line 1211 ("Each call is stopped at the solver's time bound for valid
inputs").
- The time-out needs T_Solve as a *computable* function of public data. For Solve = Theorem main
  that bound is not explicit: rem:is-runtime (main.tex 674) leaves the absolute constant C_1
  uncomputed, and main.tex 222 states the running time only as polynomial. The 9 Nov sentence
  ("For Solve = Theorem main the time-out is not needed, since its query and time bounds hold for
  every oracle (Remark oblivious)") is therefore needed, and it is correct.
  - On every branch, the socle passed to Solve is an explicit F_p-Lie algebra of dimension at
    least 1 (stop rule 1) and class at most c (lem:tb-section-rank holds whatever the earlier
    calls returned; asserted in every round of all 2970 adversarial branches, see below), and
    f o sigma o iota_M has a standard oracle (one query of U_f between computing and uncomputing
    sigma o iota_M). The only invalid ingredient is the hiding property, which rem:oblivious does
    not use for the bounds.
  - Without the time-out, the claim "Every call runs within the bound T_Solve ... on every branch"
    (lines 411-412) follows from rem:oblivious, and on valid instances the time-out was never
    active.
- For Solve = part (ii) (cor:fpt-r) the proof relies on the time-out (line 444). The paper does
  not claim that cor:fpt-r's bounds hold for every oracle (not checked here; outside the range),
  and it gives the bound only as 2^{O(R log(R+2))} poly(...), with constants it does not compute.
  An algorithm with valid constants exists, so the existence statement of thm:ring-to-field
  survives; the algorithm as written needs that constant to run. rem:oblivious treats the
  Schedule-2 cut-off "4T" the same way and says so ("this paper does not compute its constant").
- The definition also says T_Solve "depends only on the dimension, log p, the label width and
  eta". Theorem main's bound depends on g_d = dim gamma_d (main.tex 192), and part (ii)'s on the
  derived dimension r_loc <= R.
  - For Theorem main, B_d is increasing and g_d <= m, which gives a bound in m alone.
  - For part (ii), a bound in the dimension alone, valid on all instances of that dimension, would
    be 2^{O(m log m)} and would lose the theorem's time bound on erroneous branches. The phrase "on
    the socles met here" (line 398) presumably lets it use R, but the definition should say so.
- Proposed text, lines 396-398: replace "within a running-time bound $T_{\operatorname{Solve}}$
  that depends only on the dimension, $\log p$, the label width and $\eta$" with "within a
  running-time bound $T_{\operatorname{Solve}}$ that is a computable function of $\eta$ and of
  public data (the structure constants of the instance and of $\mathcal{L}$, $\log p$ and the label
  width)". In lines 412 and 426, "for its dimension" and "for this dimension" become "for its
  instance" and "for this instance".
- Proposed addition after line 446: "For $\operatorname{Solve}$ = part~(ii), the time-out uses
  the bound of Corollary~\ref{cor:fpt-r}, whose constants are not computed here; an algorithm with
  valid constants exists, which is what Theorem~\ref{thm:ring-to-field} asserts."

**F3. MINOR.** `main.tex` line 1203: "On $(\mathbb{Z}/p^e\mathbb{Z})^2$ with $[x,y] = py$, the
class is $e$ while $R = 1$."
- This needs 2 <= e < p. At e = 1 the bracket is 0, so R = 0, and e < p is needed for the Lazard
  setting. Both recorded instances, (e,p) = (4,5) and (128,131), satisfy it; the macros in
  `numbers.tex` (classes 4 and 128, call caps 21, 36, 32896 and 10, readout orders 9 and 3) are
  arithmetically right. Fix: "for $2 \le e < p$".
- Related (line 1227): "but later sections can have class up to $R+1$". This is true as a bound,
  and it is attained by the Heisenberg control (Heis_3(Z/p^2)/L[p] is Heis_3(F_p), class 2 = R+1).
  It is never attained by the two [x,y] = py families. In every section S/K of those families the
  socle is abelian: for a, b in S with pa, pb in K, [a,b] = det(a,b)py, and det(a,b)py =
  alpha(pb) - alpha'(pa) lies in K. Suggest "(as for $\Heis_3(\mathbb{Z}/p^2)$, whose second
  section is $\Heis_3(\Fp)$)".

**F4. MINOR.** `appendix-torsion-blackbox.tex` lines 126-130 against line 154. The identities

X_j substituted for U and V; that algebra is neither of the two. The same universal property

Proposed text: "in every Lie algebra over $R_c$ of class at most $c$, in particular in
$\Lambda_{R_c}(X,Y)$ and in every finite Lazard ring".
