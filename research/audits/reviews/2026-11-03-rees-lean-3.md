## 1. Statements proved (label: Lean)

All three are existence statements. They check the algebra of the constructions,
not their running time, and no executable solver is extracted.

### 1.1 Witness existence at arity `κa+1` (Chevalley–Warning), `ReesSdeWitness`

- `exists_witness`: let `K` be any finite field, `κ ≥ 1` and `M > κa`. For any
  columns `λ_1, …, λ_M ∈ K^a` there is a nonzero `s ∈ K^M` with
  `∑_i s_i^κ λ_i = 0`.
  Proof: the `a` coordinate polynomials `∑_i λ_{i,j} X_i^κ` have total degree at most
  `κ` each (`coordPoly_totalDegree_le`). Their degrees sum to at most `κa < M`, so
  Mathlib's `char_dvd_card_solutions_of_sum_lt` (Chevalley–Warning) says the
  characteristic divides the number of common zeros. Zero is a common zero, so there
  are at least `p ≥ 2` of them. The hypothesis `κ | p−1` is not needed; `κ ≥ 1` is,
  because at `κ = 0` the claim is false.
- `exists_witness_zmod`: the same over `ZMod p`.
- `exists_witness_gcd`: over `ZMod p`, at exponent `d ≥ 1`, there are
  `M > gcd(d, p−1)·a` columns, and some nonzero `s` has `∑ s_i^d λ_i = 0`.
  Proof: Chevalley–Warning at `κ = gcd(d, p−1)`, then the Bezout lift of 1.3.
- `exists_witness_linear`: if `gcd(d, p−1) = 1`, any `a+1` columns admit such an
  `s`. This is part (c) of `lem:app-s2-gcd`.

This is the bound that `appendix-schedule2.tex` ("Not shown") and the exhaustive-search
arity `κ_d g_d + 1` of the end-to-end simulation (`main.tex`, "Exact end-to-end
simulation") cite as "by the Chevalley–Warning theorem".

### 1.2 The triangular two-level solver (`prop:triangular-sde`), `ReesSdeTriangular`

Abstract form (`triangular_witness`). Let `K` be a field and `V` a finite-dimensional
`K`-space with `n = dim V`. Let `λ : ι → V` be indexed by a finite type, and `D ⊆ K`
with `0 ∈ D`. Let `A, B : K → K` satisfy

- `hAB`: `ζ δ (A ζ + B δ) ∈ D` for all nonzero `ζ, δ`;
- `h1`: `A 1 + B 1 ≠ 0`.

If `(n+1)(n+2) ≤ 2|ι|`, some nonzero `x ∈ D^ι` has `∑ x_i λ_i = 0`.

The proof follows the paper's construction step by step, and each step is a named lemma:

| Paper step | Lean |
|---|---|
| A round's weights `x_i = ζ δ_i(α_n + β_{cos δ_i})` lie in `P ∪ {0}` | `roundWeight_mem` |
| …and sum to `ζ α_n e + ζ v` | `roundWeight_sum` |
| `a − dim U_k + 1` columns have a dependence `δ` with `e ∈ U_k`, scaled to `1` at one column | `exists_dependence` (with `c` the codimension: `c+1` columns, any column normalised) |
| Stop: `v ∈ U_k`, `R` at `ζ = 1` lies in `U_k`, and `−R` closes with weight `α_0+β_0 ≠ 0` | `stop_witness` |
| Descent: `τ ∈ U_{l−1} ⊕ F v_l`; peel `ζ_l`, rest lies in `U_{l−1}` | `realized_extend` (proved upward: `U ⊕ Kv` is realized on the used columns) |
| Disjoint supports add | `realized_add`, `add_mem_of_disjoint` |
| Count `∑_{k=0}^{a}(a−k+1) = (a+1)(a+2)/2` | `witness_aux` (strong induction on the codimension) |

Concrete forms:

- `triangular_sde_witness`: take `D` to be the `κ`-th powers, `κ ≥ 1`. Given `hAB` in
  the form `ζδ(Aζ+Bδ) = w^κ` and `h1`, any `M ≥ (a+1)(a+2)/2` columns in `K^a` have a
  nonzero `s` with `∑ s_i^κ λ_i = 0`. This includes taking `κ`-th roots, and
  `two_mul_triangle` checks that `(a+1)(a+2)/2` is exact.
- `triangular_sde_of_contraction` is the proposition's hypothesis in the paper's own
  form. Here `g` generates `K^×`, `κ ≥ 1`, and `α, β : Z/κ → K` with `α_0 = 1`,
  `β_0 = 0` and `(α_j + β_i)·g^{(i+j)} ∈ P ∪ {0}` for all `i, j` (as `∃ w, … = w^κ`).
  The conclusion is the same. The coset map `cos` is built from the generator
  (`exists_coset_index`), and the coset arithmetic is `coset_product`.
- `triangular_sde_zmod`: over `ZMod p` at exponent `d`, with the contraction given at
  `κ = gcd(d, p−1)`, it applies the solver and then the Bezout lift. The conclusion is
  a nonzero `s` with `∑ s_i^d λ_i = 0` from `(a+1)(a+2)/2` columns. This is the
  proposition's last sentence.
- `exists_generator`: every finite field has such a `g`.
- Non-vacuity: `contraction_three` (by `decide`) checks that `α = (1,0)`,
  `β = (0,1)`, `g = 2` is a normalised contraction at `p = 3`, `κ = 2`.
  `triangular_sde_three` then gives the instance: any `(a+1)(a+2)/2` columns in
  `F_3^a` have a nonzero `s` with `∑ s_i^2 λ_i = 0`. This is the `F_3` subset-sum
  case that `rem:triangular-scope` cites.

The Lean statement is more general than the paper's. It does not assume `κ ≥ 2`,
`κ | p−1`, `p ∉ E_2(κ)`, a prime field, or that `cos` is well defined. Those
conditions are only needed for a contraction to exist, and here the contraction is a
hypothesis.

The comparison with the written proof found no gap. It agrees with the paper on
three points:

1. In the stopping case the paper scales `δ` so that its first nonzero entry is `1`.
   Lean uses any normalised column, which works because the only use is
   `x_{i_0} = α_0 + β_0`.
2. The parenthetical "always the case once `U_k = F_p^a`" is the `c = 0` branch, where
   `v ∉ U` is impossible.
3. The column count is exactly `∑_{c=0}^{a}(c+1)`.

### 1.3 The gcd/Bezout reduction (`lem:app-s2-gcd`), `ReesSdeGcd`

- `pow_bezout_exponent`: for a prime `p` and `d ≥ 1` there is `t ≥ 1` with
  `(x^t)^d = x^{gcd(d,p−1)}` for every `x ∈ ZMod p`. This is the `t` of part (b),
  `t d' ≡ 1 (mod q')`, shifted by `q'` so that `t ≥ 1`.
- `range_pow_units_eq` is part (a): the `d`-th powers of nonzero elements are exactly
  the `gcd(d,p−1)`-th powers of nonzero elements.
- `bezout_witness` is part (b). If `u ≠ 0` and `∑ u_i^κ λ_i = 0` in any
  `ZMod p`-module, then `s_i = u_i^t` is nonzero, has the same support (`s_i = 0 ↔
  u_i = 0`), and satisfies `∑ s_i^d λ_i = 0`.
- Part (c) is `exists_witness_linear` (1.1).

## 4. What stays outside the Lean boundary

- The existence of a universal two-level contraction, i.e. `p ∉ E_2(κ)`. This covers
  the Weil and second-moment thresholds `T_A`, `T_S`, the moment bound of
  `rem:two-level-threshold-size`, and the computed sets `E_2(κ)`. The triangular
  theorem takes the contraction as a hypothesis.
- The reduction from the matrix form of a contraction (`C ∈ (Q_0^∘)^{κ×κ}`,
  `C_00 ≠ 0`, `⟨C, W⊗W⟩ = 0`) to the decoupled normal form
  `C_ij = z_i z_j(α_j+β_i)` (`lem:app-s2-normal`). Lean starts from the normal form.
- Schedule 1, the Ivanyos–Santha arity `B_d(a)` (`eq:is-arity`) and its
  deterministic run. Schedule 2 at arity `(a+1)((κ−1)a+1)`
  (`thm:power-sde`(ii), `prop:decoupled-sde`). Schedule 3 and the free-coordinate
  sampler (`thm:random-column`, `thm:free-coordinate`).
- Every running-time, Gaussian-elimination, coset-test and root-extraction cost,
  including "deterministic", "`O(a)` eliminations" and "`O(a^2)` evaluations of
  `y ↦ y^{(p−1)/κ}`". Lean proves existence and does not extract an algorithm.
- The link to the receiver. The Lean descent modules (`CentralQuotientReceiver`,
  `ReesGeneralMeasuredReceiver`) still take a valid SDE witness as an input, and
  these modules are not yet wired into them. With 1.1 alone such a wiring would give
  a non-constructive, existence-only policy, not a solver.
- Everything already listed as outside in `sec:formalization`: BCH/Lazard for
  `c ≥ 5`, preparation of the lifted source, oracle implementation, approximation
  errors, the final Fourier step and running time.

## 5. Exact statement that now holds; what is missing; gates

**Holds (Lean, standard axioms, AI-checked, pending human review):**

1. Over any finite field and for any `κ ≥ 1`, every `M ≥ κa+1` columns in `K^a`
   admit a nonzero `s` with `∑ s_i^κ λ_i = 0`. Over `ZMod p` at exponent `d ≥ 1`,
   `gcd(d,p−1)·a + 1` columns suffice.
2. Let `K` be a field whose multiplicative group is generated by `g`, and let
   `α, β : Z/κ → K` satisfy `α_0 = 1`, `β_0 = 0` and
   `(α_j+β_i) g^{i+j} ∈ (K^×)^κ ∪ {0}` for all `i, j`. Then every
   `M ≥ (a+1)(a+2)/2` columns in `K^a` admit a nonzero `s` with
   `∑ s_i^κ λ_i = 0`. Over `ZMod p` with `κ = gcd(d,p−1)`, the same holds at exponent
   `d`.
