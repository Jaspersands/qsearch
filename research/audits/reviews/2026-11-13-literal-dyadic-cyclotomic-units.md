# Explicit squareclasses for the literal dyadic cyclotomic family

2026-11-13. Independent attack on the parent's explicit constructor, using
current STATE and the one-prime ordinary/narrow proofs. No API retry,
network, Git, archives, implementation, or numerical experiment.

**Accepted, with one harmless size qualification:** the generator list
has O(m) sparse monomial terms; a fully dense coefficient-list output has
O(m^2) entries. Both are polynomial in the native dense input length.
The units, sign-rank argument, unique real-unit relation, torsion bit and
S-prime generator all work. Together with the reviewed native dyadic
local-image/evaluation interface, they give a classical polynomial-bit
Selmer-dimension algorithm for the LITERAL family below. No general
extra-S or other-conductor conclusion is asserted.

Put

    m=2^t, t>=2;       d=m/2;       r=m/4;
    f(X)=X(X^d+1),    K=Q(zeta_m), F=K^+.

The curve has genus r. Write zeta=zeta_m and

    c_a=1+zeta^a+zeta^(-a)+zeta^(2a)+zeta^(-2a),
    c_j=c_(5^j),             0<=j<r,
    pi=1-zeta.

The proposed complete basis of K(S,2), for the unique prime S={P} above
2, is

    zeta, c_0,...,c_(r-2), pi.                         (1)

When r=1 the c-list in (1) is empty, giving i and 1-i.

## 1. Field data and actual units

The polynomial `Phi_m(X)=X^d+1` is irreducible over Q and Q_2: after
X->X+1 it is Eisenstein at 2. Its polynomial discriminant has absolute
value `d^d`, so K is unramified outside 2. The same Eisenstein calculation
makes 2 totally ramified, with a unique prime P and residue degree 1.
Also `Norm_K/Q(1-zeta)=Phi_m(1)=2`, so `(pi)=P` and `v_P(pi)=1`.
K/Q is Galois of 2-power degree, and its ordinary class group is odd by
the independently proved one-prime result. This does not assert that
its class group is trivial.

For odd a, the formula can be written directly in K:

    c_a=zeta^(-2a)*(1-zeta^(5a))/(1-zeta^a).            (2)

No extension to Q(zeta_(2m)) is needed. The Laurent-polynomial expression
makes c_a integral, and it is fixed by complex conjugation. The numerator
and denominator in (2) both have K/Q norm 2, since a and 5a are prime to
m. Therefore `Norm_K/Q(c_a)=1`. Thus c_a is an actual unit of O_F, not
merely a rational expression with a possible dyadic denominator.

## 2. Odd norm and independent signatures

The real embeddings of F are indexed by odd integers `1<=a<m/2`, one
representative of each pair modulo sign. Under the embedding indexed by a,

    c_1 -> sin(5a*pi/m)/sin(a*pi/m).

The denominator is positive. In this range the numerator is negative
exactly when `m/5<a<2m/5`; it is never zero. If
`m=20q+s`, the possible residues are `s=4,8,12,16`. In all four cases the
number of odd integers in that open interval is exactly `2q+1`:
the respective first/last offsets from 4q and 8q are (1,1), (3,3),
(3,3), and (5,5). Hence

    Norm_F/Q(c_1)=-1.                                 (3)

The group `Gal(F/Q)=(Z/mZ)^*/{+1,-1}` is cyclic of order r generated by 5.
For t>=3, `v_2(5^(2^j)-1)=j+2` proves its order; no power of 5 is -1
modulo m because every power is 1 modulo 4. The case t=2 is the trivial
group. Thus the c_j are precisely all conjugates of c_1.

Encode negative signs by 1 in F_2. The signature vector of c_1 is an
element v of the regular module `F_2[C_r]`, with augmentation 1 by (3).
Since r is a power of 2,

    F_2[C_r]=F_2[T]/((T-1)^r),

and augmentation-one elements are units. Consequently the r translates
of v are a basis. The signature vectors of c_0,...,c_(r-1) are exactly
these translates, up to reversing the cyclic indexing, so they are
linearly independent.

Dirichlet gives `dim_F2 U_F/U_F^2=r`. Independent signatures therefore
prove that the c_j form a BASIS of U_F/U_F^2. In particular the signature
map is an isomorphism and every totally positive F-unit is a square of
an F-unit. This conclusion is obtained directly from the explicit c_j;
there is no circular appeal to unknown unit generators. Finally (3) is
an exact norm identity, giving

    product_(j=0)^(r-1) c_j = -1.                      (4)

## 3. The CM unit passage and its unique lost squareclass

For u in U_K, `u*conjugate(u)` is a totally positive F-unit. By the
preceding paragraph it equals v^2 for some v in U_F. Every complex
conjugate of u/v has absolute value 1. This quotient is an algebraic
integer unit, so Kronecker's theorem makes it a root of unity. Therefore

    U_K=mu_K*U_F.                                     (5)

This proves the needed CM unit-index statement; it is not presumed from
a generic circular-unit theorem. Here `mu_K=<zeta>`: an odd-order root
would introduce a ramified odd prime, and a root of order 2^(t+1) would
generate a field of twice K's degree.

Since K=F(i), the restriction kernel

    U_F/U_F^2 -> U_K/U_K^2

is exactly the line generated by -1. Indeed, writing a square root of an
F-unit as A+Bi, its square being real forces A*B=0. The unit is then a
square or minus a square in F; the square root in F is itself a unit.
Conversely -1=i^2. Equation (4), together with the c_j basis, identifies
this kernel with the all-ones vector. Hence the images of ANY r-1 of the
c_j are independent and span the real-unit image.

Equation (5) says that adjoining zeta spans all K-unit squareclasses.
Dirichlet gives `dim_F2 U_K/U_K^2=r`, so zeta contributes the remaining
independent bit. Equivalently, if zeta times a real unit were a square,
(5) would force an odd power of zeta to be real; this is impossible since
the real roots of unity are +1 and -1, both even powers of zeta for t>=2.
Thus

    zeta,c_0,...,c_(r-2)

is a complete independent basis of U_K/U_K^2. There is no additional
hidden torsion or real-unit relation.

Because `(pi)=P`, every S-unit is pi to its P-valuation times an ordinary
unit. Its valuation parity proves pi is independent of the unit basis.
Odd Cl(K) makes S-unit squareclasses equal to K(S,2), by the already
proved odd-index argument. This proves (1), with dimension r+1.

## 4. Native size and the classical Selmer assembly

Compute `a_(j+1)=5a_j mod m`. Each c_j has five Laurent monomials; reducing
exponents with `zeta^d=-1` gives coefficients of absolute value at most 5
in the native power basis. Each exponent has O(log m) bits. Listing the
sparse generators takes O(m) small-integer arithmetic operations and
O(m log m) bits; dense lists use O(m^2) constant-size entries. No huge
fundamental unit or product over all c_j is computed. Identity (4) is a
proof, not an instruction to expand that product.

The native branch algebra is Q times K, supplied explicitly by
`f=X Phi_m`; there is no unknown polynomial or integer factorization.
The rational bad-prime set is P0={2}, because `Res(X,Phi_m)=1` and
`|disc(f)|=d^d`. Thus the rational factor has squareclass basis -1,2.
The exact norms are

    Norm(zeta)=1,    Norm(c_j)=1,    Norm(pi)=2.

A basis of the global NORM-SQUARE kernel can therefore be taken as

    (1,zeta), (1,c_0),...,(1,c_(r-2)), (2,pi),          (6)

of dimension r+1. There is no quotient by diagonal rational classes.
For explicit native evaluation, if beta has degree below d, the pair
`(q,beta)` is represented in Q[X]/f by

    beta(X)+(q-beta(0))*Phi_m(X).

All representatives in (6) are integral, with constant coefficient bounds.
Their algebra norms are 1 or 4, so their nonzero component valuations at
2 have a fixed numerical bound. The reviewed native squareclass backend
therefore evaluates them in the SAME finite local frame with polynomial
precision and bit cost. No implicit unit evaluation or frame translation
is being granted for free.

There is only one real root of f, namely 0. More explicitly,
`L tensor R=R times C^r`; its norm-square kernel has zero real
squareclass coordinate. Thus (6) automatically meets the real condition.
At every odd prime the curve has good reduction and the branch algebra
is unramified. The retained odd-degree descent theorem makes the
even-valuation norm-square condition the required unramified local image.
Only the prime 2 remains to be tested.

Call the reviewed native dyadic algorithm on this SAME polynomial to
