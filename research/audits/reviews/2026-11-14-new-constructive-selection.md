## One concrete nonlinear mechanism and its native contract

Physical target: a normalized Gross–Pitaevskii/Hartree wave on a fixed-volume
three-dimensional periodic box. Let D=2^(3n) be its spatial grid size.
Input is binary grid size, rational time T, coupling g, a short explicit
Fourier description of the initial normalized wave and external potential,
a box-membership circuit for Ω, and additive scalar accuracy η. Output
I_Ω(T), the fraction of mass in Ω at time T. This is a bounded observable,
not all D amplitudes or an exponentially small transition probability.
The target is the stated D-grid equation; a continuum theorem would also
need its discretization error, rather than treating the grid as exact physics.
All Fourier lists, coefficients and membership circuits count as input;
no unknown wavefunction inverse, QRAM table or supplied nonlinear solution
is allowed. Sparse Fourier initial data can be prepared using its explicit
coefficients at polynomial cost in list size and bits; it is not free.

Proposed operation: prepare M KNOWN copies of the initial one-particle state
and evolve the symmetric product under

 H_M=Σ_a h_a+(M−1)^−1 Σ_(a<b) W_ab.

Read the one-particle Ω occupation. A quantitative mean-field limit relating
this reduced density to Hartree evolution would convert genuine many-body
unitary dynamics into the nonlinear scalar solution, without postselection
or cloning an unknown state. This is more specific than claiming that a
quantum computer directly applies a nonlinear map to one input state.
A useful endpoint requires BOTH M and the full circuit/readout cost to be
poly(n,input bits,T,1/η). Independent repetitions/estimation and all M source
preparations are charged. Native formulas for W are not a free efficient
simulation theorem at arbitrary coefficient norm.

The strongest cheap normalization check already prevents an admission for
contact interactions. Writing the continuum-grid amplitudes as
ψ_j=φ(x_j)/sqrt(D) gives Σ_j|ψ_j|²=1. The nonlinear term transforms as

 g|φ(x_j)|²φ(x_j)/sqrt(D)=gD |ψ_j|²ψ_j.

Consequently the two-particle interaction is
W=gD Π_equal, Π_equal=Σ_j |j,j><j,j|, with ||W||=|g|D.
The fixed-volume finite-difference kinetic norm also grows as Θ(D^(2/3)).
One cannot replace these by bounded constants while retaining the SAME
physical equation. Reducing g by D instead studies a vanishing-interaction
family. A standard copy/error or simulation estimate depending polynomially
on these norms does not yield a polynomial-in-n algorithm.

This is deliberately a scope test, not a gate-count lower bound. Both the
kinetic operator in momentum space and Π_equal in position space admit
fast individual phase evaluation even for large coefficients. That alone
does not pay their combined evolution error or the finite-M mean-field
approximation. Bounds using energy/Sobolev regularity might bypass worst-case
norms, but none is derived or assumed here. Bounded NONLOCAL Hartree kernels
also avoid this particular contact norm; a claim for them must retain their
actual equation and prove its own full-time/copy bound.
