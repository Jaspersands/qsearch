## 2. What is formalised

### 2.1 `BCHClassSix`

Law (`QSearch.BCHClassSix.bch`), over any field `F`:

```
x ⋆ y = (quintic law of BCHClassFive)
      - [x,[x,[y,[y,[x,y]]]]]/720 + [x,[y,[x,[y,[x,y]]]]]/240
      + ([x,[y,[y,[y,[x,y]]]]] + [y,[x,[x,[x,[x,y]]]]])/1440
```

The degree-six part was taken from `log(exp x exp y)` in the exact model. Every
denominator is of the form `2^a 3^b 5^c`, so the law is defined when `2, 3, 5 ≠ 0`,
for example over `ZMod p` with `p ≥ 7`.

Proved:

- `classSix_of_lowerCentralSeries`: from `lowerCentralSeries F L L 6 = ⊥`, every
  right-normed length-7 bracket vanishes.
- `bch_assoc_of_classSix` and `bch_assoc`: associativity from `(2 : F) ≠ 0`,
  `(3 : F) ≠ 0`, `(5 : F) ≠ 0` and the class bound alone.
- `bch_zero_left/right`, `bch_neg_left/right`, `bch_of_lie_eq_zero`, `map_bch`,
  `bch_smul_same`.
- `instGroup : Group (BCH F L hnil)`, under `[NeZero 2] [NeZero 3] [NeZero 5]`.
- `val_mul`, `val_mul_formula`, `val_one`, `val_inv`, `val_pow`, `val_zpow`.
- `lieHom`, `lieEquiv` and their `id`, `comp` and `symm` lemmas.

The method is the one of class five. Both sides of associativity are expanded by one
`simp only` into right-normed brackets in `x < y < z` and normalised. Then
`match_scalars`, `field_simp` and `ring` compare the coefficients. The rewrite set is
`jacobi3`, 6 degree-four rules, 6 degree-five rules and 28 new degree-six rules. The
irreducible words number 3, 3, 8, 18, 48 and 116 in degrees 1–6, the dimensions of the
free Lie algebra on three generators, and the model checks that they are linearly
independent. So the normal form is a basis and the comparison is complete.

Every degree-six rule is proved in Lean from one or two instances of `lie_skew x W` or
`lie_self U`, each with coefficient ±1. Two points needed care:

- **`lie_self` certificates.** Using `lie_self U` (`[U,U] = 0`, for U of degree 3)
  instead of half of `lie_skew U U` makes every certificate integral.
- **Path dependence.** Lean's `simp` closes a syntactic `[W, W]` by `lie_self` before
  expanding it with `lie_lie`. The raw degree-six forms then depend on the rewrite
  path. The model now copies this (`bw(u, u) = 0`). Before the change, one rule
  (`xyzyxz`) failed with a certificate that was correct in the model.

`BCHClassSix.lean` checks in 2 min 46 s wall time (114 s CPU) under a load average of
about 90–260 (`lake env lean`). Its `lake build` took 181 s. Proofs that are expensive
to check run under `maxHeartbeats 0` (no limit).

### 2.2 `BCHClassSixSubgroups`

This is Lazard's subgroup direction at class six over `ZMod p`, under
`[Fact p.Prime] [NeZero 2] [NeZero 3] [NeZero 5]`; `neZeroTwo/Three/Five (hp : 5 < p)`
install the last three. The structure is that of class five:

- Polynomials:
  - `commPoly`: the logarithm of `x y x⁻¹ y⁻¹`, with 18 normal-form terms of degrees 2–6.
  - `p3x, p3y, p4xx, p4yx, p4yy`, and six degree-five words `p5xxx … p5yyy`, which now
    carry degree-six tails.
  - Nine central degree-six commutators.
- `bch_comm` and the lemmas `commPoly_*`.
- Group versions `val_comm_*`.
- `bracketWord x y`: `comm x y` times powers of 2 + 3 + 6 + 9 iterated commutators of
  degrees 3–6. The coefficients up to degree 5 are those of class five. In degree 6
  they are 1/5, 9/8, −1, 47/72, 19/24, −4/3, 35/24, −4/3 and 1/5.
  `val_bracketWord : (bracketWord x y).val = ⁅x.val, y.val⁆`.
- `addWord x y`: `x * y` times powers of the 1 + 2 + 3 + 6 + 9 exact bracket monomials
  of degrees 2–6. `val_addWord : (addWord x y).val = x.val + y.val`.
- `toLieSubalgebra hnil H`: the logarithm of an arbitrary subgroup is a Lie
  subalgebra. Also `toSubgroup`, and both round trips
  `toLieSubalgebra_toSubgroup` and `toSubgroup_toLieSubalgebra`.

In two letters the normal form is `[b,a] ↦ -[a,b]`, then `jacobi4c`, then no degree-five
rule, then three degree-six rules `two6a`–`two6c`. Each of these three is proved from
one `lie_skew` instance; in degree six, 12 words `a·w` span a space of dimension 9.

`lake build` takes 459 s under load (282 s CPU). The cost is mostly in `val_bracketWord`
and `val_addWord`, which have 20 nested factors each and run under
`maxRecDepth 100000` and `maxHeartbeats 0`.

### 2.4 `ReesSdeQuadratic`: goal 3, triangular solver at κ = 2 with no hypothesis

`ReesSdeTriangular` already contained the arity statement of `prop:triangular-sde`:
from `(a+1)(a+2)/2` columns it gives a nonzero solution with entries in `P ∪ {0}`
(`triangular_witness`, `triangular_sde_witness`, `triangular_sde_of_contraction`,
`triangular_sde_zmod`). It needs the contraction as a hypothesis.
`ReesSdeWitness.exists_witness_linear` already gave the linear case: `a + 1` columns
when `gcd(d, p − 1) = 1`.

The missing piece was the contraction itself, and at κ = 2 it is now constructed:

- `nonsquare_mul_nonsquare`: in a finite field, the product of two non-squares is a
  square (proved with the quadratic character).
- `exists_consecutive_nonsquare`: over `ZMod p`, `p` odd, the least non-residue `ν`
  gives a nonzero square `μ = ν − 1` with `1 + μ` a non-square.
- `exists_quadratic_contraction`: the functions `A ζ = [ζ square]` and
  `B δ = μ·[δ non-square]` satisfy `hAB` and `h1` of `triangular_sde_witness`.
- `contraction_two`: the paper's coset form. For every generator `g` of `(ZMod p)^×`,
  `α = (1, 0)` and `β = (0, μ)` satisfy `(α_j + β_i) g^(i+j) ∈ P ∪ {0}`.
- `triangular_sde_quadratic`: for every odd prime `p`, any `(a+1)(a+2)/2` columns in
  `(ZMod p)^a` have a nonzero `s` with `∑ s_i² λ_i = 0`.
- `triangular_sde_gcd_two`: the same at every exponent `d` with `gcd(d, p − 1) = 2`.

This is the existence half of `p ∉ E_2(2)`, as the paper states it, over prime fields.
It is an existence statement; the algorithm and its running time are not formalised.

### 2.5 `ReesSdePolicy`: SDE witnesses wired into the receiver (existence level)

- `exists_witness_module`: Chevalley–Warning with label columns in any
  finite-dimensional space, transported through `Module.finBasis`.
- `exists_witness_module_gcd`: the same over `ZMod p` at any exponent `d`, via the
  Bezout lift.
- `BankSizes c L I`: every bank at stage `d + 1` has more than
  `gcd(d + 1, p − 1) · dim (γ_{d+1})^*` columns.
- `cwPolicy`: a total policy chosen with `Classical.choose`, with `cwPolicy_active` and
  `cwPolicy_sde`.
- `measured_terminal_source_cw`: the general-class measured receiver with this policy.
  The policy, `active` and `sde` inputs are replaced by `BankSizes`.
- `classSix_measured_terminal_source_cw`: its class-six instance, for an arbitrary
  subgroup `H` at the points 1, …, 6 with `p > 6`. The only inputs left are the bank
  sizes and, as in every receiver theorem here, the exact initial coset source.

Caveats:

- The bank bound uses the full dimension of `(γ_{d+1})^*`, not the label rank `g_d` of
  the paper.
- The policy is chosen, not computed.

## 5. Goal 2: is a general-class construction reachable? Not tonight

**Mathlib (v4.35.0, local copy).**

- `grep -rli "BakerCampbell\|Baker-Campbell\|Campbell" Mathlib` finds nothing, and
  `grep -rl Lazard Mathlib` finds no BCH or Lazard-correspondence material. The only
  hit is Lazard's flatness theorem in `RingTheory/Flat`.
- `Mathlib/RingTheory/Nilpotent/Exp.lean` has `IsNilpotent.exp` over ℚ-algebras only,
  with `exp_add_of_commute` for commuting elements. There is no `log`, no BCH series,
  no version over `ℤ_(p)` or over rings where only `1, …, c` are invertible, and no
  `exp`/`log` bijection.
- `Mathlib/Algebra/Lie/Free.lean` has `FreeLieAlgebra`, `lift` and
  `universalEnvelopingEquivFreeAlgebra`. Its docstring says that injectivity of
  `FreeLieAlgebra R X → FreeAlgebra R X` is not proved, because the author knows no
  proof that avoids Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt. PBW is not in Mathlib.

So nothing usable exists, and the general-class (c < p) route has to be built from
scratch.

**Plan, with the precise remaining lemmas.**

1. **L1, truncated exp/log.** Let `A` be an `R`-algebra with `(k : R)` a unit for
   `k ≤ c` and `I^{c+1} = 0`. Then `exp : I → 1 + I` and `log : 1 + I → I` are mutually
   inverse, `exp(a + b) = exp a · exp b` for commuting `a, b`, and `exp(n·a) = (exp a)^n`.
   Mathlib's ℚ-only `exp` does not cover this ring.
2. **L2, BCH is a Lie element.** In `A_c(X) = FreeAlgebra R X / (words of length > c)`,
   `log(exp x · exp y)` lies in the Lie subalgebra generated by `x, y` and equals an
   explicit Dynkin polynomial `D_c(x, y)` with coefficients in `R`. Route: the
   coproduct, the fact that `exp x` is group-like, Friedrichs' criterion, and the
   Dynkin–Specht–Wever map `θ(w) = [w_1,[w_2,…]]`. `θ(P) = n·P` on Lie elements of
   degree `n`, and `n ≤ c < p` is invertible.
3. **L3, injectivity of the free nilpotent Lie algebra into `A_c`.** Over `R` (ℤ_(p),
   or a field of characteristic 0 or above `c`), the class-`c` free Lie algebra on three
   letters embeds in `A_c`. Without PBW, use Lyndon words: standard bracketings have
   leading word equal to the Lyndon word, so the images are triangular. This turns the
   associative identity `exp(D(x,y))·exp z = exp x · exp(D(y,z))` into the Lie identity
   `D(D(x,y),z) = D(x,D(y,z))`.
4. **L4, specialisation.** For any Lie algebra `M` over `F` with `c!` invertible and
   `lowerCentralSeries F M M c = ⊥`, pass through `FreeLieAlgebra.lift` and the quotient
   by the `(c+1)`-st lower-central term. This gives `NilpotentGroupLaw F c` for every
   `c`, together with `zero_mul`, `neg_mul`, `natural` and `AdditiveOnCommuting`.
5. **L5, Lazard's subgroup direction.** For every `c < p`, find group words with
   exponents in `ℤ_(p)` whose BCH logarithms are `x + y` and `[x, y]` exactly. The proof
   is an induction on degree. The degree-`k` residual is a Lie element, so it is a
   combination of left-normed brackets. Each such bracket is the leading term of the
   logarithm of an iterated group commutator, by L2 and L3. Correct the residual with
   powers of those commutators. Then derive `SubgroupsAreSubalgebras` for every `c`.
6. **L6, assembly.** Build the `ReesGeneralLawClassC` analogue for all `c < p`.

L2, L3 and L5 are each a substantial formalisation project: Hopf-algebra and
combinatorics-on-words infrastructure that Mathlib lacks. A realistic estimate is
several weeks for an expert, not one session. It was not attempted.

**Per-class extension (route B).** The present pipeline is mechanical and could reach
`c = 7`. The degree-7 BCH coefficients have 7 in the denominator (for example
1/30240 = 1/(2^5·3^3·5·7)), so `p > 7` would be needed. There are 312 three-letter basis
words in degree 7. Associativity cost grew from about 15 s (`c = 5`) to about 114 s CPU
(`c = 6`), roughly 8× per class, so `c = 7` would take something like 15–20 min CPU and
`c = 8` hours. This covers fixed classes only, never "every `c < p`".

A reflective route (a verified normal-form evaluator run by `decide`) is also per-class.
The `native_decide` shortcut would add `Lean.ofReduceBool` and break the axiom guards.
