# Binary-Fock passive/Kerr sampling: coherent source interface

Status: CLOSED source/composition review for passive two-mode gates on all states of pair occupation at most K, including reference entanglement, using ONE common product Hermite transform and grid. This is a known-primitive composition, not novelty or a separation. The fixed-spin theorem by itself would not justify coherent control of spin/total number; the accepted construction below avoids that extrapolation. No new controls, Git, network or APIs were used.

Primary snapshots: `research/audits/sources/binary_fock_kerr/iyer.txt`/PDF (arXiv2602.15180v1) and `prakash.txt`/PDF (arXiv2610.03581v1), with the separate `iyer_source.json` and `prakash_source.json` manifests.

## What the actual source guarantees

Iyer§3.1,939–980, Lemma3.1, compares one discrete oscillator unitary with the continuum operation on EACH Fock basis input. Its grid operation is obtained by replacing xhat,phat in the same quadratic-factor product; it does not first read a total-number label. For a product of r bounded-angle factors, a common grid dimension D≥c*2^r*M suffices, with error exp(−gamma D), for total occupation M. The proof1280–1316 pays the intermediate high-energy excursions of the individual shears by successive Taylor cutoffs. Thus one must not pretend each intermediate shear conserves photon number, or simply truncate it to K. Only the complete passive operation conserves number.

Iyer Theorem4.2,1363–1396, is stronger than a basis-state preparation theorem: it explicitly quantifies over ARBITRARY normalized coefficients alpha_m in sum_(m<M) alpha_m|m>, gives the coherent Hermite map, gate complexity polynomial in log M and log(1/eta), and D=O(M^(9/4)/eta^(13/4)). Lines1378–1385 address phases explicitly: the transform's Hermite phase is +1 for every m in the Sec3 convention, rather than an arbitrary degree-dependent phase. The construction sketch1387–1396 uses coherent trial-state preparation, eigenstate filtering/fixed-point amplification, and a second phase-estimation step to erase the input degree. It is not postselected state generation. Algorithm1,1420–1444, applies the inverse QHTs. The source's full target contract Eq(11),326–329, includes a zero ancilla in the ideal output and vector-norm error on all inputs. The QHT map must be used as this coherent isometry, including its uncomputed workspace and small residual leakage; a marginal-state preparation oracle would not suffice. The original cited arXiv2510.04929 has now also been read at its formal theorem and phase/workspace proof; see the addendum below.

Prakash§6.2,2999–3031, Theorem2.1, independently states the same arbitrary-superposition guarantee, explains that the sampled states are only almost normalized, and uses a common maximum cutoff/grid when an inverse needs a larger space. Its Theorem2.2 and proof3322–3392 give an explicit OPERATOR-norm digital Gaussian-gate theorem over a rectangular Fock subspace, with the SAME operational grid throughout. Its hypothesis N=Omega(log(1/eta)) can be met by padding the cutoff; it adds only log log(1/eta) to register size. This is supporting prior-art coverage, not a reason to apply a fixed-total-spin circuit independently in different sectors.

## Coherent two-mode compilation and its error

Use q=a,b for the two physical modes. In Iyer's convention A_12=(p1*x2−x1*p2)/2 (735–776),

    a†b−b†a = −2i A_12,
    B(theta)=exp[theta(a†b−b†a)]=exp(−2i theta A_12).

Iyer Lemma2.2/Eq(46),797–833, factors exp(i phi A_12) into three mixed-coordinate shears. Thus set phi=−2theta, divide the angle into a CONSTANT number of bounded pieces, implement each shear by diagonal quadratic phases and centered QFTs, and invert ONE common pair of QHTs. Prakash Eq(31),739–746, defines BSY with the OPPOSITE sign to the native generator, so its angle must be negated. Its Hermite convention at2971ff has an extra (−1)^n; using it on both modes conjugates by (−1)^(n_a+n_b), which commutes with the passive gate. Do not mix conventions mode by mode.

A safe global angle reduction is modulo2pi. In particular B(theta+pi)=(−1)^(n_a+n_b) B(theta); reducing modulo pi while dropping that parity is wrong on a coherent superposition of different totals. The continuum factorization is an operator identity connected to identity at theta=0 and fixes the vacuum, so it does not introduce an unspecified sector-dependent metaplectic phase. Implement the displayed generator/sign convention, not merely its action on covariance matrices.

Choose a single QHT cutoff K+1 for the promised TRIANGLE n_a+n_b≤K. The number of input basis states d=(K+1)(K+2)/2 is at most(K+1)^2. Taking the same grid and unitary for all of them, a basis discretization error delta yields restricted operator error at most sqrt(d)*delta≤(K+1)delta by Cauchy–Schwarz. Thus delta=O(eta/(K+1)) suffices; the required logarithmic accuracy stays polynomial in log K and log(1/eta). QHT errors are already uniform on superpositions, so their operator errors add without another dimension factor. Sampled-Hermite near-isometry follows either from the QHT approximation or Iyer Eq(55),905–918. Forward/inverse QHT errors and the grid error telescope; e.g. assigning each QHT error eta/32 and grid column error eta/[8(K+1)] leaves a constant fraction for finite gate compilation. The reference extension follows from the restricted operator-norm bound, not an assertion about classical mixtures of fixed-number sectors.

If the stronger WHOLE RECTANGLE 0≤n_a,n_b≤K is claimed, its output can have2K photons in one mode. Use cutoff2K+1 for BOTH forward and inverse QHTs and a grid valid up to total2K. A K-only inverse is insufficient for that statement. The native total-K circuit needs only the triangular claim. No lexicographic fixed-spin map V1, spin measurement, coherent j-controlled theorem or independently chosen sector grids are required.

## Binary resources, Kerr and full-circuit error

The two-mode chirp decomposition has constant length after bounded angle reduction. Take D to be a suitable sufficiently large operational grid, with log D=O(log(K+1)+log(1/eta)). Signed grid coordinates, their products, angles and Fourier phases can be computed reversibly with polynomially many bits. Quadratic grid-operator norms are O(D), so coefficient/angle precision O(log D+log(1/eta)) suffices. The physical passive generator restricted to total at most K has norm O(K); its input angle therefore needs absolute accuracy O(eta/(K+1)). Binary rational/efficiently computable angle inputs pay that precision and any large-angle range reduction. QFT/chirp and QHT work is cleaned by inverse arithmetic/unitaries up to the included error. Grid padding/ancilla leakage is allowed only within that error; it is never postselected away.

For a local Kerr exp[2pi i*(a/b)*n(n−1)/2], b>0, compute n(n−1)/2 and a times that integer modulo b with reversible binary arithmetic. Its bit cost is polynomial in log(K+1), log(|a|+1), log b and log(1/eta). The modular exponent calculation is EXACT, but the resulting rational phase generally needs approximate synthesis over a finite universal gate set; do not call the whole arbitrary-b Kerr gate exact. Uncompute the arithmetic work. The Prakash digital-optics Corollary2.3,879–901, already states Gaussian/Kerr composition coverage; the present purely passive restriction also supplies a simpler fixed total-photon cutoff at every complete stage.

For an explicit list of G passive/Kerr stages on m modes and initial binary Fock occupations summing to K, choose per-stage clean-isometry error O(epsilon/G). Every IDEAL complete stage preserves total K, hence the local triangular promises hold along the entire ideal trajectory. Hybrid telescoping gives global vector error O(epsilon), even if finite circuits leak slightly and even with a reference; it does not require exact conservation by each digital approximation. Final number measurement has TV error at most the corresponding vector-error bound. Handle out-of-range measured padding by a fixed output/failure convention, not conditional renormalization. K=0 is trivial vacuum. Gate/workspace bounds are polynomial in the explicit list/input bits, m, log(K+1) and log(G/epsilon), with no expansion into K photons or an exponentially long amplitude table. The strongest matched classical baseline and any claimed operational advantage remain the parent's separate task.

## Original Hermite source and classical-model addendum

The original `hermite.txt` (arXiv2510.04929; adjacent primary PDF/manifest) now closes the previously explicit source-reading assumption. Formal Theorem19,1300–1320, quantifies over arbitrary normalized alpha_n, allows ANY sufficiently large common power-of-two grid M≥c*N^(9/4)/eta^(13/4), and gives polynomial-logarithmic gates. Its condition N>log(1/eta) should be paid by padding the effective QHT cutoff to N_eff=max(K+1,C log(1/eta)) (or2K+1 for the rectangle). This does not increase the physical photon promise and preserves the claimed logarithmic resource dependence.

The phase/workspace guarantee is not inferred from isolated basis preparations. Lemma25,2008–2019, explicitly says its amplification ancilla returns to|0> within error eta, which is required to avoid altering alpha_n. Lemma26 and its proof,2021–2070, replace the amplification block by a direct sum and apply the SAME Chebyshev polynomial in every degree block. With positive real marked amplitudes bounded below, this maps every target block with the same prescribed phase; different unknown overlap magnitudes do not generate arbitrary relative phases. Lemma24's application,2073–2105, implements the input-degree-controlled arithmetic and the degree-dependent QPE offsets with polynomial-logarithmic gates. Lemma27,2115–2145, then uses the known oscillator offset(n+1/2), a shifted QPE labeler and its inverse to erase the degree coherently. Algorithm2 and the theorem proof,2151–2178, combine these operations without postselection or discarded nonzero work. Residual work is included in the vector error.

Keep the Hermite sign convention explicit: Eq(1),123–132, includes(-1)^n, whereas1353–1355 expressly suppresses that factor for the Plancherel–Rotach construction. Its positive-overlap proof1491–1521 is in that suppressed-sign convention. A known parity phase on the binary degree restores whichever convention is desired before degree erasure; applying the same convention on both modes only conjugates the passive gate by total parity, which commutes with it. This corroborates Iyer's explicit fixed-phase discussion; it is not permission to ignore an arbitrary phase depending on n. For a conservative error ledger, Lemma21 Eq(45),1412–1418, bounds the high-energy tail's SQUARED norm by its tolerance parameter. Choose that internal parameter O(eta²), and budget filtering/amplification errors separately, rather than confusing it with a vector-norm eta. The grid remains polynomial in cutoff and1/eta and its logarithm remains polynomial in the claimed variables; no sharp grid exponent is needed for the native endpoint.

`devroye.txt` is Devroye–Los, *An asymptotically optimal algorithm for generating bin cardinalities*, Mathematics and Computers in Simulation228(2025),147–155 (2024 copyright), not a binary-arithmetic theorem. Its model54–59 gives exact real storage, unit-cost arithmetic INCLUDING exp/log/trigonometry, and exact independent uniform real draws. Its constant EXPECTED binomial time is uniform over parameters in THAT model and points to Devroye's1986 book; it does not pay output bits, finite precision or a bounded rejection clock. The multinomial discussion96–110 supplies the familiar sequential-binomial approach. For unconditional probabilities p_i the correct parameter is p_i/(1−sum_(j<i)p_j) after earlier counts; the printed paragraph's p_i notation must not be copied literally without this conditioning. The parent's sections3a/3b are a separate finite-BIT/capped-error derivation, not imported from this RAM bound. Their mathematical proof is being independently reviewed elsewhere.

`ct.txt`, Van den Nest arXiv0911.1624, Definition1/344–365, defines CT by BOTH efficient Born sampling and efficiently evaluable amplitudes relative to the supplied description. The precision note350–358 permits amplitude evaluation to requested exponential precision in polynomial time. Theorem3/525–527 assumes CT states and an efficiently computable sparse operator of norm at most1 and yields additive polynomial-accuracy matrix-element estimation; its overlap proof539–569 explicitly constructs bounded random variables. Its simulation discussion281–324 defines this accuracy as inverse-polynomial additive error with high confidence, not relative control of arbitrarily small probabilities or arbitrary full-output TV sampling. Thus CT does not, by itself, establish Born sampling from a coherent sum of CT branches: that is the parent's actual rejection construction, whose chi/small-probability clocks must be charged. These sources supply prior methods and comparison models; they support no novelty or unit-cost-real-to-BIT inference.
