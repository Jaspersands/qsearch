## Verdict

**Reading confirmed, with caveats** (none of the caveats changes `B_d` or the exponent 145).

1. The arity `B_d(a) = G(d,a)^{ceil(log2(d+1))}`, `G(d,a) = d^{d(d-1)/2}(a+1)^d`, is exactly the
   bound that the proof of IS Theorem 3 establishes (IS p.10, last display of Section 3.3,
   middle term of the chain). It is not the bound printed in the statement of Theorem 3 (IS p.3).
2. Remark 5.3's claim that the last inequality of IS's chain fails for large `m` is correct
   when `log` is read as `log_2` (IS do not state the base; it fails for base e or 10 as well).
   The direction is as the Remark says: the printed statement claims a *smaller* exponent of
   `m+1` than the proof gives, so the printed theorem is not implied by the proof. The paper
   uses only what the proof gives.
3. `sum_{d=2}^{9} d*ceil(log2(d+1)) = 4+6+12+15+18+21+32+36 = 144`, and with the factor `(m+t)`
   the exponent of `m` in `Q_q` is `145`. Recomputed with Python (below). Confirmed.
4. Hidden hypotheses: `d | q-1` (IS Theorem 11), handled in the paper by Lemma 5.4 via
   `kappa = gcd(d,p-1)` exactly as IS do on p.7; the final step needs van de Woestijne's
   deterministic solver (IS Fact 2, p.2-3); no condition on characteristic, field size or
   `m` beyond these. Details in Section 3 below.

## 1. What IS 2015 states and what its proof establishes (read first, independently)

### 1.1 The problem (IS p.2, Definition 1, eqs. (3)-(4))

`SDE(F_q, n, m, d)`: given `m` diagonal equations `sum_j a_ij x_j^d = 0` in `n` variables over
`F_q`, find a nonzero solution. Equivalently (eq. (4)): given column vectors
`v_1,...,v_n in F_q^m`, find `x`, not all zero, with `sum_j x_j^d v_j = 0`. So `m` = number of
equations (the Rees paper's `a`), `n` = number of variables/columns (the Rees paper's `M`).

### 1.2 The printed statement (IS p.3, Theorem 3)

Quote: "Let d be constant. For n > d^{d^2 log d}(m+1)^{d log d}, the problem SDE(F_q, n, m, d)
can be solved in time polynomial in n and log q."

The exponent on `d` is `d^2 log d` and the exponent on `(m+1)` is `d log d` (confirmed from
a 200 dpi crop of p.3). The base of `log` is not stated anywhere in the paper.

### 1.3 Ingredients of the proof (IS Section 3, pp.7-10)

(a) **Reduction to `d | q-1`** (p.7, first paragraph of Section 3). If `d` does not divide
`q-1`, put `d' = gcd(d, q-1)`; solve with exponent `d'`; the extended Euclidean algorithm gives
`t` with `t d = u(q-1) + d'`, and `(x_j^t)` solves the original system. Quote: "it is
sufficient to solve SDE(F_q, n, m, d) in the case when d divides q-1."

(b) **Classification without nonresidues** (Section 3.1, pp.7-8). Nonzero field elements are
written as `alpha = zeta_i beta^d` relative to a subgroup `H` generated by the pi-parts of the
elements seen so far (`pi` = primes dividing `d`), using Pohlig-Hellman in `H` (time polynomial
in `d` and `log q`). On failure the whole computation restarts with a larger `H`; at most
`log q` restarts, which costs a `log q` factor in time "but not in the bound on the number of
input vectors necessary for success" (p.8, lines 1-3). This is deterministic.

(c) **Lemma 12** (p.8). For `l = 1..d`, `B_l(d,m) = d^{l(l-1)/2}(m+1)^l`. From `B_l(d,m)` input
vectors one finds `d^l` pairwise disjoint index sets `J_a` (`a in {1..d}^l`) and field elements
`beta_i` with `J_{(1,...,l)} != emptyset` such that, with `w_a = sum_{i in J_a} beta_i^d v_i`,
`sum_{s=1}^d zeta_s w_{a(j,s)} = 0` for all `a` and all `j <= l`.
- Base `l = 1`: `m+1` vectors of `F_q^m` are linearly dependent (p.8, proof, first lines).
- Step `l -> l+1` (p.9, top): take `E = d^l (m+1)` blocks of `B_l` vectors; the concatenated
  vectors `W(k)` have length `d^l m < E`, so they are dependent; classify the coefficients
  `alpha(k) = zeta_r gamma(k)^d`; eq. (11). The count check: `B_{l+1} = d^l(m+1) B_l =
  d^{l(l+1)/2}(m+1)^{l+1}`. Correct. (Note `E = d^l m + 1` would already suffice for the
  dependence, so `B_l` is not tight; irrelevant here.)

(d) **Theorem 11** (p.8). Quote: "Assume that d | q-1 and put G(d,m) = d^{d(d-1)/2}(m+1)^d.
Then, given G = G(d,m) input vectors v_1,...,v_G in F_q^m, in time polynomial in G and log q,
we can find two disjoint subsets I and J of {1,...,G} with I != emptyset and nonzero field
elements gamma_j in F_q^* (j in I u J) such that sum_{i in I} gamma_i^d v_i = sum_{j in J}
gamma_j^d v_j." Proof (pp.9-10): apply Lemma 12 at `l = d` (`B_d(d,m) = G(d,m)`), restrict to
tuples that are permutations, and show `sum_{a in S_d} sgn(a) w_a = 0` (eq. (13)) as the
difference of (14) and (15); the terms with `s > 1` cancel in pairs differing by a
transposition. `I = union over even a`, `J = union over odd a`. I checked the cancellation
argument; it is correct, and `I` contains `J_{(1,...,d)} != emptyset` (identity is even).

(e) **Section 3.3, Proof of Theorem 3** (p.10). Quote: "Iterating this, using G(d,m)^l input
vectors, we obtain a vector with 2^l representations as linear combinations having pairwise
disjoint support and coefficients that are explicit dth powers. When 2^l >= d+1, we can use
Fact 2 to find field elements z_1,...,z_{d+1}, not all zero, such that z_1^d + ... + z_{d+1}^d
= 0." Multiplying the `i`-th representation by `z_i` gives the zero vector. Each level: split
`G^{l}` vectors into `G^{l-1}` groups of `G`, apply Theorem 11 to each group and then to the
`G^{l-1}` resulting vectors (p.10, second and third paragraphs); the representations
double each round (if any `J` is empty one is done immediately).

(f) **The final display** (p.10, last line of Section 3.3). Quote:
`C(d,m) <= G(d,m)^{ceil(log_2(d+1))} <= d^{d^2 log d}(m+1)^{d log d}.`
Here `C(d,m)` is "the number of vectors (variables) used by our algorithm" (p.7, line
"We will denote by C(d,m)..."). The number of rounds is `l = ceil(log_2(d+1))`, written with an
explicit base 2 here.

(g) **Fact 2** (p.2-3, van de Woestijne [28]). Quote: "In deterministic polynomial time in d
and log q we can find a nontrivial solution for a_1 x_1^d + ... + a_{d+1} x_{d+1}^d = 0."
Used with all `a_i = 1`.

### 1.4 What the proof establishes vs what is printed

- **Established by the proof:** for `d | q-1`, any `n >= G(d,m)^{ceil(log_2(d+1))}` input
  vectors suffice, with explicit nonzero scalars (products of the `gamma`'s, `beta`'s and
  `z_i`), deterministically, in time polynomial in `n` and `log q` for fixed `d`. For general
  `d`, replace `d` by `d' = gcd(d,q-1)` (and `G(d',m) <= G(d,m)`).
- **Printed (Theorem 3):** `n > d^{d^2 log d}(m+1)^{d log d}`.
- **The last inequality of the chain is false for large `m`** under any standard reading of
  `log`. With `log = log_2`: exponent of `(m+1)` on the left is `d*ceil(log_2(d+1))`, on the
  right `d*log_2 d`; the left is strictly larger for every `d >= 2`. Numerically (Python,
  below): at `d = 2` the two sides are `4(m+1)^4` and `16(m+1)^2`, equal at `m = 1` and the
  inequality fails from `m = 2` on; at `d = 4` it fails from `m = 128`; at `d = 3` from
  `m = 1468`; at `d = 5` from `m = 606078`; for `d = 6..9` the crossover is above `m = 10^7`
  (the huge `d^{d^2 log d}` constant delays it). So for the class-9 parameter range the
  proof's bound is numerically the *smaller* (better) one, but asymptotically in `m` it is the
  larger one; either way only the middle term is proved.
- Side remark (not needed by the paper): the printed statement for `d = 2, 3` is probably
  true by other means (IS Proposition 9 gives `(m+1)^2`, randomized; Proposition 10 gives
  `(9m+1)(3m+1)(m+1)`, randomized; and [15] gives `Omega(m^2)` for `d = 2`), but Section 3's
  general argument does not give it, and the general argument is the only deterministic one.

## 2. Comparison with the Rees core paper

### Theorem 5.1 (`thm:is-import`, core lines 427-430)

Statement: `d >= 1` fixed, `p` prime with `d | p-1`, `M >= B_d(a)` columns in `F_p^a`; a
deterministic algorithm outputs explicit `s_1..s_M`, not all zero, with `sum s_i^d lambda_i
= 0`, in time polynomial in `M` and `log p` (polynomial depending on `d`).

- `d | p-1` matches IS Theorem 11's hypothesis `d | q-1` (q = p). Correct.
- `M >= B_d(a)` (non-strict) matches the proof ("using G(d,m)^l input vectors"); extra
  columns get weight 0. IS's printed "n >" is strict but irrelevant. Correct.
- "explicit field elements `s_i`" (Remark 5.2(a)): IS Theorem 11 returns `gamma_j in F_q^*`
  explicitly; Section 3.3 multiplies them across rounds and by the `z_i` of Fact 2. Correct.
- "deterministic": Section 3.1 avoids nonresidues; Fact 2 is deterministic; only
  Propositions 9-10 (warm-up, Section 2) are randomized, as the paper says (core line 434;
  IS p.5, "randomization in these algorithms is only required to obtain quadratic and cubic
  nonresidues"). Correct.
- `d = 1`: `B_1(a) = (a+1)^1 = a+1`, matching IS `C(1,m) = m+1` (p.7). Correct.
- Citation locator "Theorem 11 and the proof of Theorem 3, Sections 3.1-3.3": the right
  places (pp.7-10). Fact 2 / van de Woestijne (p.2-3) is also used and is cited separately.

### Remark 5.2(b) and Lemma 5.4 (`lem:bezout`, core lines 454-461)

Matches IS p.7 (`t d = u(q-1) + d'`). The paper's own proof with Bezout coefficients `u,v`
and `u >= 0` is self-contained and correct (`s_i^d = x_i^{ud} = x_i^{kappa - v(p-1)} =
x_i^kappa` for `x_i != 0`). `B_kappa(a) <= B_d(a)` because `G(d,a)` and `ceil(log_2(d+1))`
are nondecreasing in `d`. The companion `main.tex` Remark (b) uses `t d/kappa == 1 mod
(p-1)/kappa`, which is the same identity.

### Remark 5.3 (`rem:is-printed`, core lines 445-452)

Every factual claim checks against IS p.10 (chain) and p.3 (printed statement):
- the chain is quoted correctly;
- "its Theorem 3 prints the last bound": correct;
- "fails for every d >= 2 once a is large, because d ceil(log_2(d+1)) > d log_2 d": correct,
  with `log` read as `log_2`; IS do not fix the base, so the Remark should say so;
- "for d = 2 the exponents of a+1 are 4 and 2": correct;
- "We use only the first inequality, which is what the proof establishes": correct.

One wording caveat: the referee summary framed the printed bound as "weaker than what the
proof gives". That is true numerically at every `d <= 9` and every `a` below the thresholds
above (which cover all realistic `a`), but as a statement about exponents the printed bound
is the *stronger, unproved* one. Remark 5.3 already states it the right way round. Suggested
precise sentence in Section 5 below.

### `eq:Bd`, `thm:main` and the exponent 145 (core lines 138-152, 551-555)

`B_d(a) = G(d,a)^{ceil(log_2(d+1))}` is IS's middle term with `m = a`. With `g_d <= m`,
`B_d(g_d) <= d^{d(d-1)ceil(log_2(d+1))/2}(m+1)^{d ceil(log_2(d+1))}`, and
`Q_q = c(m+t) prod_{d=2}^c B_d(g_d)`. Exponent of `m`: `1 + sum_{d=2}^c d ceil(log_2(d+1))`.

## 3. Hidden hypotheses found (all either handled by the paper or harmless)

1. `d | q-1` for Theorem 11 (IS p.8). Handled by Lemma 5.4 / IS p.7 reduction.
2. Fact 2 (van de Woestijne) for one equation in `d+1` variables, deterministic, polynomial in
   `d` and `log q` (IS p.2-3). The paper cites it; the section locator in van de Woestijne is
   flagged in the paper as unchecked (core line 433), and I did not check it either.
3. The base of `log` in Theorem 3 is unstated. Only affects the printed bound, which the
   paper does not use.
4. Time bound: IS say "polynomial in n and log q" for constant `d`; the restarts of Section
   3.1 add a `log q` factor, Pohlig-Hellman is polynomial in `d` and `log q`. The paper's
   "polynomial depending on d" is at least as cautious. No field-size, characteristic or
   `m >= 1` condition is needed (`m = 0` is vacuous: one vector in `F_q^0` is dependent).
5. Theorem 11 returns `I != emptyset` but allows `J = emptyset`, in which case a zero
   representation is found at once (IS p.10, "we are done if this is the zero vector"). The
   round count `ceil(log_2(d+1))` is therefore an upper bound. Fine.
6. `B_l(d,m)` is not tight (`E = d^l m + 1` blocks would do); the paper takes IS's numbers as
   printed, which is the conservative choice.

## 5. Proposed replacement text for Remark 5.3 (core `rem:is-printed`)

> The proof of \cite[Theorem~3]{IvanyosSantha2015} ends (Section~3.3, p.~10, last display)
> with the chain
> \[ C(d,m) \le G(d,m)^{\lceil\log_2(d+1)\rceil} \le d^{d^2\log d}(m+1)^{d\log d}, \]
> where $C(d,m)$ is the number of input vectors the algorithm uses (p.~7),
> $G(d,m) = d^{d(d-1)/2}(m+1)^d$ is the arity of \cite[Theorem~11, p.~8]{IvanyosSantha2015}
> (Lemma~12 there at $\ell = d$, with $B_\ell(d,m) = d^{\ell(\ell-1)/2}(m+1)^\ell$), and
> $\lceil\log_2(d+1)\rceil$ is the number of rounds of Section~3.3 needed for $2^\ell \ge d+1$
> disjoint representations before \cite[Fact~2]{IvanyosSantha2015} is applied. Theorem~3
> itself (p.~3) prints only the right-hand bound, with the base of $\log$ unstated. We use
> the middle term, $B_d(a) = G(d,a)^{\lceil\log_2(d+1)\rceil}$, which is what Sections~3.1--3.3
> establish. The right-hand inequality does not hold for large $m$ under the reading
> $\log = \log_2$ (nor for base $e$ or $10$), since the exponent of $m+1$ in the middle term,
> $d\lceil\log_2(d+1)\rceil$, exceeds $d\log_2 d$ for every $d \ge 2$; at $d = 2$ the two
> terms are $4(m+1)^4$ and $16(m+1)^2$, and the inequality fails from $m = 2$. Nothing in this
> paper uses the printed form. This reading of the source is by AI sessions only and has not
> been confirmed with the authors or by a human.

The companion `main.tex` Remark (c) (line 624) could carry the same page locators (p.~3 for
the statement, p.~8 for Theorem~11, p.~10 for the final display).

## 6. Reproduction

```
/opt/anaconda3/bin/python - <<'EOF'
import math
G=lambda d,a: d**(d*(d-1)//2)*(a+1)**d
B=lambda d,a: G(d,a)**math.ceil(math.log2(d+1))
print(sum(d*math.ceil(math.log2(d+1)) for d in range(2,10))+1)   # 145
print(B(2,2), 16*3**2)                                             # 324 > 144
EOF
```
