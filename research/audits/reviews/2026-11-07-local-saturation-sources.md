# Local 2-saturation: actual-source scope

Status: OPEN quantitative gate. Stoll pays an algebraic ONE-half construction, not a uniform growing-genus p-adic BIT/precision theorem. The sources checked do not yet pay the native complete wild local-image algorithm. This is a missing bound/construction, not a no-go. No controls, network, API or Git actions were used.

## Stoll's actual interface

Primary: `research/audits/sources/local_halving_saturation/stoll.txt`, author PDF adjacent, *Chabauty without the Mordell–Weil group* (author version 2016-12-07). Section5,763–806, explicitly allows computing ONE half or ALL halves over a field of characteristic different from2. Given a Mumford representation and ONE square root s of the Kummer representative in k[x]/f, Proposition5.1,807–833, recovers a half via linear congruences in u,v,w, degree bounds O(g), minimal-degree monic w, polynomial gcds and CRT. Proof835–898 establishes existence and uniqueness for that chosen s. One can solve for each possible degree of w (at most g+1) by linear algebra; no enumeration of the up-to-4^g square-root sign choices is necessary for ONE half. Square-root extraction over the explicit local étale algebra remains separately charged.

The qualification is literal:899–910 warns of precision problems when roots of a approach roots of f, when the result has lower divisor degree, or when support approaches infinity. “In practice ... fairly rarely” is empirical. It proposes another representation and Laurent-series specialization; it does not bound how many charts/parameters must be tried, their condition numbers or lost digits. The replacement formula [c−2hb−h²a,−b−ha]=[a,b] is explicit. The text says a constant h can remove common roots and “probably” arrange squarefreeness; it is not a proved bounded-precision chart theorem for every input. Proposition5.1's squarefree a and exact gcd/rank/minimal-degree decisions cannot silently be made from a fixed arbitrary p-adic truncation. Remark5.2,911–925, handles halves of P+T and P+P' under stated coprimality/degree conditions; it does not discharge these conditioning obligations.

Section3,391–396, actually computes q(P) by recursively considering ALL halves and only asserts eventual termination away from infinitely divisible points. The candidate saturation algorithm may improve this by linear relation search plus ONE half, but that improvement is a parent derivation, not Stoll's stated recursion or complexity theorem.

## What the formal-group estimates do and do not bound

Stoll413–437 fixes a proper regular curve model and the Néron model. It proves log:K_m ≅ (p^m O)^dimJ when m>e/(p−1), hence pK_m=K_(m+e). Over Q2, m=2 suffices. This is a useful uniform formal-group threshold IN NÉRON coordinates; it does not identify the input equation's differential basis with that lattice.

The exponent n_tors of J(k)[p^infinity] is introduced at459–460 as an input to Lemma3.3, not bounded by g/discriminant. Lemma3.6,519–538, introduces two further constants: a is the valuation of the first derivative c1 in Néron coordinates; a' is the p-adic valuation of the exponent of J(k)/K_mu. The resulting bound n_m e≤m+b is existential, with b depending on these and finitely many earlier annuli. Lemma3.7/Corollary3.8,539–568, gives eventual stopping once N(m)≥n_m+1 and m exceeds n_tors e+mu+e. It supplies no native polynomial bound for these constants, for the number of annuli/subdisks, or for their total arithmetic. The strong m=1/2 stopping result Corollary3.9,574–588, assumes GOOD reduction at2 and vanishing local/residue-field2-torsion; it does not apply to arbitrary wild input.

For the proposed finitely generated subgroup, the required clock is a bound on the 2-primary INDEX of the chosen branch-torsion plus near-infinity generators, not merely on dim J(Qp)/2J(Qp). A small mod2 target dimension does not bound the number of successful saturation enlargements. At2, a sufficient ledger would combine the explicit logarithmic determinant of the g chosen points in standard hyperelliptic differentials, the change-of-lattice determinant to Néron differentials, and the 2-part of J(Q2)/K2. At odd p only the finite2-primary contribution survives. Stoll's exponent n_tors and a' should not be confused with the cardinality or index; a bound on their exponents can yield a size bound only after charging the relevant number of generators/ranks.

## Existing wild sources: exact available bounds

`wild_local_selmer/liu_models.txt`899–914, Corollary1.27, proves n≤v(Delta_initial)/2 for a CHAIN of singular Weierstrass-model operations. This is genuine and applies to those operations, including residue characteristic2. It does not directly bound all regular charts, the component-group order, Néron differential index or the half-map minors. Its Artin/discriminant comparison at809–815 is expressly for characteristic(k) different from2 and cites Obus–Srinivasan; do not import that displayed inequality at the dyadic prime.

The same source2598–2645, §3.3/Proposition3.14, relates the determinant of the Néron differential lattice to the displayed hyperelliptic volume form, but explicitly assumes g=2 and adds cases/hypotheses involving minimal models. It does not supply the requested arbitrary-growing-g index bound. `liu_1996.txt`1320–1348 (Lemma13/Proposition7) bounds singular Weierstrass chain length and local multiplicities by discriminant. Its canonical differential statements1535ff are in the genus1 section, not an all-g Néron differential theorem.

`wild_local_selmer/bosch_liu.txt`109–135, Theorem1.1, computes the geometric component group as the weighted-intersection quotient of an ALREADY supplied regular model. Rational-point corrections and Lemma2.1,531–555, identify reduction surjectivity over a finite residue field. These give neither a bound on that matrix in terms of the native f nor divisor-lift/half precision. Given polynomial-size intersection data, determinant/Hadamard bounds would pay logarithmic component-group size, but supplying such data is exactly the unresolved model bound; it cannot be used circularly. `muselli_regular.txt`8–31 explicitly excludes residue characteristic2, so it cannot close the present dyadic lattice/precision gate. The existing Liu-double multiplicity estimates concern one normalized blowup and do not settle these different global bounds.

## Precisely missing lemma and bounded source requests

The source-supported conclusion is conditional: provide an explicit polynomial P in g, coefficient bits, log p and v_p(disc f) that bounds (i) the 2-index of the SPECIFIED starting subgroup, with justified standard-to-Néron differential comparison and finite reduction contribution; and (ii) the total coefficient precision and number of chart repairs for one half at the actual saturation inputs, including degenerate Mumford divisors. Give the finite-precision output invariant permitting the next iteration and an exact/certified final Kummer-label test. Then polynomially many linear relation searches and one-half operations are plausible. Neither finiteness, rare numerical failure, finite mod2 dimension nor eventual saturation supplies P. No assertion here excludes a different paid repair avoiding regular models.

If fetching one further source is useful, the existing actual bibliography identifies S.Kunzweiler–S.Wewers, *Integral differential forms for superelliptic curves*, https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.12357 (`muselli_regular.txt`2700–2701). Its dyadic hypotheses and quantitative theorem have NOT been checked; it is a source request, not evidence for the desired bound. For a discriminant/differential comparison, P.Lockhart, *On the discriminant of hyperelliptic curve*, Trans.AMS342(1994),729–752, is cited at `liu_1996.txt`1721–1722; again its applicability and BIT scope are UNKNOWN. No further paper title alone should promote this gate. The immediate decisive task remains an explicit bounded one-half chart/precision lemma and starting-index bound, not another all-halves scan or broad empirical sweep.
