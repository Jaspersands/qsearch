# Spinor-genus decision: an exact ray-class obstruction over Q

2026-11-07. CLOSED focused core review. The proposed example is valid:
two explicit indefinite ternary integral lattices have equal determinant
and the same genus, but are not integrally isometric. This refutes
replacement of the spinor ray-class obstruction by the ordinary class
group. It does not refute the full spinor-genus route or establish a
quantum advantage. No code, control rerun, network or API action was used.

Read current STATE/TRAPS and the actual frozen Kirschmer text
`audits/sources/spinor_genus_admission/kirschmer.txt`, especially
Theorem 5.4.4, Theorems 5.4.5--5.4.6, Lemma 5.4.9 and Corollary 5.4.10.
No claim is imported from the inaccessible 2024/2026 papers.

## 1. Explicit neighboring lattices

Let p be a prime congruent to 17 modulo 72. On V=Q^3 take

    Q=diag(1,p^2,-p^4),    L=Z^3,
    x=(1,0,1),            q=3.

The form has real signature (2,1). Since p=-1 modulo 9,
Q(x)=1-p^4 is divisible by 9. Also x is primitive modulo 3, and L_3
is unimodular because p is not 3. With the bilinear form v^T Q w,

    B(x,z)=z_1-p^4 z_3 = z_1-z_3 modulo 3.

Thus the usual admissible-vector 3-neighbor is

    M={z in L:B(x,z)=0 modulo 3}+Z*(x/3).

It has basis columns

    T=[[3,0,1/3],[0,1,0],[0,0,1/3]],    det T=1.

Indeed the intersection L intersect M is exactly
{z in Z^3:z_1-z_3=0 modulo 3}; it has index 3 in both L and M.
The displayed basis gives

    Q_M=T^T Q T
       =[[9,0,1],[0,p^2,0],[1,0,(1-p^4)/9]],
    det Q_M=det Q=-p^6.

Both Gram matrices are integral. For every prime ell other than 3,
T and T^(-1) have integral entries over Z_ell, so M_ell=L_ell as
lattices in this same rational quadratic space. At 3 the hypotheses
of Kirschmer Lemma 5.4.9 apply: the prime is odd, L_3 is modular
(in fact unimodular), and M is its 3-neighbor. The lemma supplies
sigma_3 in SO(V_3) with sigma_3 L_3=M_3 and

    theta(sigma_3)=3 modulo (Q_3^*)^2.

It follows that the lattices lie in the same genus. This invocation
uses an actual neighbor, not merely equal determinants.

For p=17 the exact matrices and determinant are

    Q=diag(1,289,-83521),
    Q_M=[[9,0,1],[0,289,0],[1,0,-9280]],
    det Q=det Q_M=-24137569.

## 2. Local stabilizer spinor groups

At the distinguished prime p, the orthogonal rank-one Jordan blocks
have coefficients 1,p^2,-p^4 and scale valuations 0,2,4. Since
p=1 modulo 4, -1 is a square in Q_p. Every unit-scaled norm in each
rank-one block is therefore a square in Q_p. Theorem 5.4.4 says that
the proper stabilizer spinor image is generated by even products of
these block norms. Consequently its full preimage in Q_p^* is

    theta(L_p)=(Q_p^*)^2.

At any odd ell different from p, L_ell is unimodular, so the same
theorem's modular case gives

    theta(L_ell)=Z_ell^* (Q_ell^*)^2.

No formula for theta(L_2) is needed in the obstruction below. In
particular the proof does not import a dyadic enumeration or a Beli
algorithm with an unexamined clock.

## 3. A quadratic character separates the neighbor

Use the quadratic idele-class character of Q(sqrt(p))/Q. Its finite
conductor is p because p=1 modulo 4. It is trivial at the real place
because p>0. At 2 it is also identically trivial: p=1 modulo 8 makes
p a square in Q_2, so that quadratic algebra is split. Equivalently,
the unramified local character has value (p/2)=1 on 2 and value 1
on every unit.

For clarity this character can be specified directly. At an odd
ell different from p set

    chi_ell(a)=(p/ell)^(v_ell(a)).

At p, writing a=p^r u with u a unit, set chi_p(a)=(u mod p / p).
The value on p is 1, consistent with the norm of sqrt(p) being -p
and with -1 being a local norm. Set chi_2 and chi_infinity to 1.
Quadratic reciprocity, with p=1 modulo 4 and p=1 modulo 8, shows
that the product character chi is trivial on every diagonal rational
number: check rational prime generators and -1. Thus this is an
explicit character of the idele-class quotient, not an ordinary
unramified ideal-class character.

It annihilates every local stabilizer spinor group:

* at p the spinor image consists of squares;
* at other odd primes its elements are units times squares;
* at 2 and infinity the entire local character is trivial.

Let j be the neighbor idele, with j_3=3 and j_v=1 elsewhere.
Lemma 5.4.9 and the equality M_ell=L_ell for ell!=3 show that it is
the proper-spinor comparison idele in Theorem 5.4.5. But

    chi(j)=chi_3(3)=(p/3)=(3/p)=-1,

since p=2 modulo 3 and p=1 modulo 4. Therefore j does not belong
to Q^* J^L. The real quadratic space is indefinite, so the set S
of anisotropic real places in that theorem is empty. The lattices
are in different proper spinor genera, hence cannot be properly
integrally isometric. One does not need to enumerate their classes
or compute the full ray class quotient to obtain this certificate.

## 4. Ordinary isometry and scope

In odd rank 3, -I is an integral automorphism of every lattice and
has determinant -1. Any improper integral isometry L->M could be
composed with -I to give a proper one. Thus ordinary and proper
integral isometry coincide here, and L and M are not integrally
isometric at all. This conclusion already follows from the spinor
obstruction; Corollary 5.4.10's indefinite equality of spinor genera
and classes is consistent with it but is not needed to strengthen
the nonisometry implication.

The ordinary class group of Q is trivial, while the displayed
conductor-p quadratic character detects a nontrivial spinor
obstruction. Hence a proposed decision procedure which checks the
genus and then substitutes Cl(Q) for the ray-class quotient would
incorrectly identify these lattices. The example does not assert
the exact size of the entire spinor class group, nor classify all
dyadic local images. It is a sharp falsifier of the ordinary-class-
group shortcut only. The full Kirschmer ray-class reduction remains
intact, with its native ambient-isometry, local-transporter and
growing-field resource obligations unchanged.
