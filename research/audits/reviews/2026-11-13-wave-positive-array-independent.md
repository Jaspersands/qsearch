# Positive separated arrays can have exponentially small Green Gram quotient

Date: 2026-11-13. Independent proof review against
`audits/api_inputs/2026-11-13-sparse-wave/contract.md`, after STATE/TRAPS.
No live paid report, network, API, Git, implementation, or numerical sweep.

**Disposition: proved.** For every fixed numerical separation R0 in the
contract's grid-spacing units, there are positive integer-weight sources of
polynomial explicit input size for which

    Z/(W2 Z0) <= C_R / L = C_R 2^(-n).

Here W2=sum_j w_j^2, Z=||A^(-1)f||^2, and Z0=||A^(-1)delta_0||^2.
The constants and a sufficient threshold are explicit below. Thus positivity
and fixed pairwise separation alone do not give an inverse-polynomial lower
bound on this quotient. This is a source-interface falsifier, not hardness
of sampling these sources or of the native problem.

## 1. Fixed constants and the exact Bragg-grid gap

Choose a fixed R=2^s>=max(2,R0), s>=1, and put

    d=phi(R)=R/2,
    delta=7^(-(d-1)),
    gamma=R^2 delta^2/1024.

For a primitive R-th root zeta, consider every Bragg-grid value

    b_(a,b)=-3+zeta^a+zeta^(-a)+zeta^b+zeta^(-b).

The ring Z[zeta]/(1-zeta) is F_2: its cyclotomic polynomial is
X^(R/2)+1 and takes value 2 at X=1. Every root of unity displayed above
maps to 1, whereas -3+4 maps to 1 in F_2. Consequently b_(a,b) is nonzero.
It is an algebraic integer in Q(zeta), so its norm is a nonzero integer.
Every conjugate has absolute value at most 7. Taking the other d-1 factors
in that norm proves the uniform, effective gap

    |b_(a,b)| >= delta.                                     (1)

This argument includes nonprimitive choices of a or b and the case R=2.
It needs neither exact trigonometric evaluation nor a sampled gap estimate.
Also

    R delta=2d/7^(d-1)<=2,   0<gamma<=1/256.                 (2)

The elementary inequality d<=7^(d-1) proves the first bound.

## 2. Explicit positive input and a sufficient size threshold

For n>=2 set L=2^n and

    M=ceil(8n/gamma),   K=ceil(8/gamma),   M<=Kn.

It suffices to take

    n >= n0=max(4,s,4RK).                                   (3)

Indeed 2^n>=n^2 for n>=4, whence (3) implies L>=4RKn>=4RM.
All these constants depend only on the fixed R0 through the chosen R.
No uniform polynomial dependence on an additional variable R0 is asserted.

Take the explicit source list

    x_(a,b)=(Ra,Rb),  w_(a,b)=binom(M,a)binom(M,b),
    0<=a,b<=M.

There are m=(M+1)^2 distinct sites. Every weight is a positive integer.
Since 0<=Ra,Rb<=RM<=L/4, no coordinate wraps. The minimum torus Euclidean
distance between distinct sites is at least R>=R0, and the support diameter
is at most sqrt(2)RM<L/2. The same minimum-separation claim holds in the
usual l1 and l-infinity grid metrics.

Its generating polynomial is exactly

    F(z,w)=(1+z^R)^M(1+w^R)^M.                              (4)

Vandermonde's identity and the largest-binomial-coefficient bound give

    W2=sum_(a,b) w_(a,b)^2=binom(2M,M)^2
       >=16^M/(2M+1)^2.                                    (5)

The exact coefficients require at most 2M+1 bits each. Hence m=O_R(n^2),
total weight bit length B=O_R(n^3), and the full list including two n-bit
coordinates per site has size O_R(n^3). One row of binomial coefficients
is generated by the exact recurrence

    binom(M,a+1)=binom(M,a)(M-a)/(a+1),

and then all pairwise products are written. This is polynomial BIT work
in n for the fixed R. There is no implicit exponentially large source
list or source-generation oracle. If an l1-normalized source is preferred,
dividing every coefficient by sum w_(a,b)=4^M preserves positivity,
polynomial rational bit size, and the scale-invariant quotient under review.

## 3. Uniform suppression on the actual resonant band

Let

    b(theta_x,theta_y)=-3+2cos(theta_x)+2cos(theta_y).

Choose nearest Bragg angles theta_x^0,theta_y^0 and let their circular
angular distances be d_x,d_y in [0,pi/R]. The elementary Lipschitz bound is

    |b(theta_x,theta_y)-b(theta_x^0,theta_y^0)|
       <=2d_x+2d_y.                                       (6)

If |b(theta_x,theta_y)|<=delta/4, (1) and (6) imply that at least one of
d_x,d_y is greater than delta/8. In fact max(d_x,d_y)>=3delta/16, but the
weaker bound suffices. By (4),

    |F(exp(i theta_x),exp(i theta_y))|^2
      =16^M [cos^2(R d_x/2) cos^2(R d_y/2)]^M
      <=16^M rho^M,
    rho=cos^2(R delta/16).                                 (7)

Cosine squared decreases on [0,pi/2]; (2) puts R delta/16 in [0,1/8].
The inequality sin x>=x/2 on this interval proves

    rho<=1-gamma,
    rho^M<=exp(-gamma M)<=exp(-8n)<=2^(-8n)=L^(-8).          (8)

Thus the proposed angle threshold, rho, and gamma are all valid. These
bounds hold pointwise at every angle, and in particular on the finite
Fourier grid. No continuum spectral approximation is being imported.

## 4. Plancherel bounds the source field independently of n

Use the unitary two-dimensional DFT, so the Fourier coefficient of f is
F/L (the Fourier-sign choice does not change any modulus). At finite-grid
angles theta_k=2pi(k_x,k_y)/L, write b_k=b(theta_k). Exactly,

    W2=(1/L^2) sum_k |F_k|^2,
    Z =(1/L^2) sum_k |F_k|^2/(b_k^2+L^(-2)),
    Z0=(1/L^2) sum_k 1/(b_k^2+L^(-2)).                     (9)

Split Z into |b_k|<=delta/4 and its complement. Damping gives the exact
bound |A^(-1)(k)|<=L. Equations (5), (7), and (8) therefore imply

    Z_near/W2 <=L^2(2M+1)^2 rho^M
                <=(2M+1)^2 L^(-6)<=1.                    (10)

The final inequality follows from L>=4RM, R>=2, L>=4, which in particular
imply 2M+1<=L. On the complement |b_k|>delta/4, so Plancherel directly gives

    Z_far/W2 <=16 delta^(-2).

Consequently

    Z/W2 <=16 delta^(-2)+1.                                (11)

This uses all Fourier modes, not a renormalized resonant-shell law.

## 5. Elementary finite-grid lower bound for one Green source

For each integer k_x with 0<=k_x<=floor(L/6), put theta_x=2pi k_x/L.
Then theta_x is in [0,pi/3], and there exists theta_y^* in [0,pi/3] such that

    cos(theta_y^*)=3/2-cos(theta_x),
    b(theta_x,theta_y^*)=0.

Choose k_y nearest to L theta_y^*/(2pi). Its angular error is at most pi/L;
therefore |b_k|<=2pi/L. These are distinct finite-grid points because their
k_x coordinates differ. There are floor(L/6)+1>=L/6 of them. Each term
in the last sum of (9), after multiplication by L^(-2), is at least
1/(4pi^2+1). Thus, for every L in the contract,

    Z0 >= L/[6(4pi^2+1)] >= L/390.                          (12)

This is a direct finite-grid counting proof. It requires no curvature
asymptotic, arithmetic equidistribution, exact resonant grid point, or
hypothesis about how close a preselected grid point lies to the curve.

## 6. Conclusion and precise scope

Combining (11) and (12) proves

    Z/(W2 Z0) <=390(16 delta^(-2)+1)/L,
    delta=7^(-(R/2-1)),  L=2^n,  n>=n0.                    (13)

The right side is exponentially small in n, while m, B, and the complete
explicit source description are polynomial in n. Hence no lower bound
inverse-polynomial in n,m,B can follow from positive weights and a fixed
numerical pairwise-separation promise alone.

The usual l1 source LCU success Z/(||w||_1^2 Z0) is no larger than this
quotient, and the stated Green-mixture rejection success is this quotient
divided by m. The example therefore also invalidates a universal polynomial
success assertion for those particular source-combination mechanisms.
It does not prove that the single-source Green sampler/evaluator is hard,
that every source combination is difficult, or that the positive arrays
constructed here lack a different efficient classical sampler. No full
native sampling impossibility, quantum advantage, or novelty follows.
