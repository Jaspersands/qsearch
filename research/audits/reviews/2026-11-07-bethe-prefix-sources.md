## What is already efficiently computable

Claeys et al., arXiv1501.05827, `gaudin.txt`586–608, Eqs(49)–(50), gives the overlap with one specified occupied-site configuration as the determinant representing a Cauchy permanent. Equations(48),(52)–(57),630–704, give related Borchardt/Izergin forms. For the native rational spin-half state,

    amplitude(S) = per[1/(v_a−eps_i)]_(a,i in S),
    per(C) = det(C Hadamard C)/det(C).

The denominator determinant is nonzero for distinct sites/roots avoiding poles. Thus one amplitude takes polynomial rational-bit arithmetic; the physical norm is not the permanent of that one square Cauchy matrix. Statements about generic permanent complexity in the paper must not be transferred to this special Cauchy amplitude. Polynomial amplitude evaluation alone does not sum squared amplitudes over all completions of a prefix.

Sklyanin, solv-int/9708007, `correlators.txt`274–286, Theorem2, is unambiguous: the roots MUST satisfy the Bethe equations for the highest-weight function lambda of the representation. It then gives one determinant for the norm. For the real native data, the algebraic dual agrees with the physical adjoint after the standard spin convention. The constant gamma can be included in the analytic highest-weight function:268–270 explicitly explains that the concrete lambda is immaterial to the correlator derivation beyond the algebra/vacuum relations and analyticity at the roots. Removing sites changes lambda, so the original equations do not automatically remain true. This is exactly the hypothesis that must be rechecked before using the same norm determinant for a restricted chain.

## Sklyanin's general formulas do not hide a single off-shell determinant

Section3,329–395, Theorem3/Eqs(3.5)–(3.7), gives arbitrary correlators through a sum over coordinated partitions of U,V,W. The off-shell scalar-product case W=empty still contains all singleton pairings, i.e. M! different pairings for M roots. It is an explicit identity, not a stated polynomial-time summation algorithm. Coincident U,V can be handled by analytic limits (393ff); this does not remove the partition sum by itself.

Most decisively, Section5,783–809, Theorem5/Eqs(5.9)–(5.11), states that the GENERAL scalar product is a SUM OF M! DETERMINANTS. Each matrix has size M. Its proof expressly says that only the identity-permutation term survives when U=V AND the parameters satisfy the Bethe equations; that specialization gives Theorem2. The general formula therefore does cover an off-shell norm algebraically, but its termwise evaluation is M! times polynomial work. Section6,813ff, is discussion, not another determinant or native polynomial clock. Neither the recurrence of Theorem4,645–676, nor the closed generating function is accompanied by an efficient high-degree coefficient-extraction algorithm for all growing prefixes. This source does not rescue the proposed deletion shortcut. It also does not prove that an alternative polynomial contraction is impossible.

## What the normal/dual determinant actually assumes

Claeys §IV.B/C, `gaudin.txt`704–806, compares normal and particle-hole dual descriptions of the SAME state, up to normalization. The derivation around Eqs(66)–(67),704–735, uses the eigenvalues of the conserved charges and explicitly calls this the dual representation of an eigenstate. Equations(68)–(69),747–801, compute the normal–dual overlap, then obtain the normalization from its product with the ratio of reference-state overlaps. The normal–dual overlap determinant can be evaluated when both descriptions are given. Its interpretation as the desired norm requires the common-state relation and normalization ratio; it is not a norm theorem for two arbitrary off-shell root lists. No opened theorem constructs a compatible dual description for every state obtained after arbitrary prefix projections.

The raising-operator form factor Eq(70),810ff, and discussion791–806 supply useful specified local form factors; the latter explicitly mentions a sum over determinants for further operators. They do not state a uniform polynomial cost for an extensive product of occupation/empty-site projectors. Even an empty-site prefix produces the same-root state on the remaining sites, generally off shell for their highest weight. An occupied-site projection additionally gives a sum over omitted-root states. Re-solving new Bethe equations would generally change the conditional state, so it is not a valid free repair.

## Existing preparation sources and matched baseline

`deterministic.txt`79–97 defines the prefix norm by summing squared configuration coefficients. Its actual circuit count,355–380, Eqs(3.16)–(3.18), is binomial(N,M)−1 rotations and O(binomial(N,M)) size/depth for general coefficients. The paper explicitly charges classical coefficient costs; its generic XXZ amplitude sums are not a lower bound for the native rational Cauchy amplitudes. It supplies a constructive exponential baseline, with the expected polynomial regimes when M or N−M is fixed.

`f_basis.txt`932 explicitly permits off-shell rapidities. Its long-unitary blocks are indexed by root subsets, with binomial(M,r)-dimensional sectors (Table2,1335–1353). Lines1354–1371 explicitly discuss scalar-product/Gram construction, their growing classical storage and Cholesky cost; the unitary formula does not make those matrices small. These are resource statements about that construction, not lower bounds on all circuits or all Gaudin states. The paper concerns XXZ Bethe circuits; taking a rational/Gaudin limit still requires its actual normalization and limiting construction, not a free cross-model theorem.

For this native input, a completely matched classical baseline computes every needed configuration amplitude with the Cauchy determinant and explicitly sums over binomially many completions. A hypothetical efficient exact prefix oracle would also give efficient sequential classical sampling, so recursive coherent preparation would not by itself establish a computational advantage. Exact rational prefix values have polynomial bit length using a common product of the supplied nonzero rational denominators; the missing gate is their polynomial-time evaluation and coherent reversible use, not merely storage of the answer.

Conclusion: the exact missing operation remains a uniformly polynomial-bit evaluation of conditional prefix norms/probabilities for the supplied on-shell parent state after arbitrary extensive occupation/zero measurements, with correct conditional states and normalization. None of these four actual formulas closes it. The proposed direct use of the parent's global Gaudin determinant is refuted if the independent exact counterexample stands. Broader arbitrary-prefix algorithms, other preparation methods and hardness remain OPEN; no exponential F-basis matrix is promoted into a general circuit lower bound.
