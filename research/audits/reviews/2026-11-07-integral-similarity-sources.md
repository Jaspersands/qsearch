## Local freeness and normalization

BHJ Proposition6.5,690–699, tests local freeness through the finite algebra Lambda/pLambda, its radical, a product of finite-field matrix algebras, and linear algebra; a chosen finite coordinate vector lifts through the expanded lattice basis. Its omega_i therefore has polynomial bit size. It is invertible in A because it generates the full localized lattice. Its inverse is an expanded rational matrix computed before any PIP/unit output. Corollary6.6,701–711, uses expanded pseudo-HNF/SNF for Hom and End, not large unit coordinates.

Proposition6.8,727–750, uses CRT beta_i and xi=sum beta_i omega_i^-1 nu_i. The implicit nu_i can be made explicit without new arithmetic: form the denominator ideal J_i={c in O: X omega_i^-1 c subset Lambda} by rational linear equations/HNF. Its localization at p_i is O_p_i, so J_i is not contained in p_i. Select an element of an expanded Z-basis outside p_i. This gives polynomial-height nu_i. CRT and a polynomial number of rational matrix operations then give expanded polynomial-height xi. Local surjectivity at the conductor primes implies A xi=A, hence xi is invertible; no generic singularity trial is hidden. If beta was computed before normalization, update beta to beta*xi and undo xi on the right exactly once in the final generator.

AppendixA1836–1929 supplies the weak-approximation probability bound uniformly in field degree: the retained random success probability is at least exp(-3C/2) for fixed C. Capping independent trials costs O(log(1/eta)); exact ideal tests detect failures. This is not an unbounded random clock.

## Finite SL lift and compact residues

BHJ §7,985–1047, reduces SL_n(O/g) to elementary matrices by finite-ring row operations and CRT. Lemma7.7/Proposition7.8,1050–1126, explicitly lift each elementary matrix in a nice order, using the expanded coprime ideal representative and scalar supplied by AppendixA. Theorem7.9,1131–1140, changes back using the small nice-order conjugation. There are polynomially many small elementary factors, so their expanded product has polynomial bit size. Rank one has trivial SL and introduces no exception. Algorithm8.3 Step11 is consequently a finite-residue operation followed by a small lift; it never row-reduces beta or a huge unit over the number field.

Step10,1271–1292, obtains a center unit epsilon from a COMPLETE unit generating set and finite abelian-group arithmetic. Lemma6.3,658–665, lifts it by a diagonal matrix in nice coordinates. Keep epsilon as a power product and its inverse syntactically; do not expand it. Finite group relation solving gives polynomial-bit exponents because the explicitly represented finite quotient has polynomial logarithmic order. Finite unit-group generation, discrete logarithms, and factoring remain charged as in Theorem8.4; they are not supplied for free by the compact-output convention.

A necessary representation repair is retained: final local integrality never licenses inverting nonunit power-product leaves. For a compact scalar, factor the explicit modulus into prime ideals, remove each expanded atom's local valuation using a small uniformizer, represent its remaining local unit with a denominator outside that prime, and use binary modular powering. The binary total valuation decides zero/nonintegrality; this works at ramified/dyadic primes too. The supplied residue_lemma packet states this correctly. For coordinate sums after conjugation, individual terms may be fractional. The PIP scalar's principal fractional ideal a*b is expanded, and units have ideal O; hence one can choose a single polynomial-bit integer D clearing all coefficient ideals. Reduce the integral terms modulo the correspondingly enlarged finite lattice D*gM, add, then divide D through the exact isomorphism D*M/D*gM -> M/gM. Never invert D modulo g, and never call a general compact-sum integrality/zero oracle. Denominator, modulus and lattice bit sizes remain polynomial.

## Boundary and exact references

The frozen exponential-output family remains decisive against expanded-output polynomial bit complexity. The construction above preserves a polynomial-size arithmetic DAG/power-product description. Final radical lifting, determinant magnitude, NO certification and integrality are separate obligations in the parent argument; this source review supplies no arbitrary arithmetic-circuit PIT or general compact determinant test. A failed capped primitive is ABORT, not mathematical NO. Mathematical NO in Algorithm8.3 is restricted to failures of maximal-order principality, local freeness, or finite norm-image membership computed from complete correct primitive data (1172–1200). This is a bounded-error correctness event, not a claim that every returned NO has an independent deterministic certificate. All primitive error probabilities must be union-bounded over their polynomial number of calls.
