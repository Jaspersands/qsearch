## What Mascot actually proves

*Hensel-lifting torsion points on Jacobians*, mascot.txt52–59, explicitly requires a prime of GOOD reduction of the supplied model and p different from ell; it also assumes a known local L-factor and a coprimality condition isolating the desired torsion representation. Remark1.5,127–128, expressly says that good reduction justifies its use of J(Zq/p^e). This is not an arbitrary Néron-model assertion silently valid for a singular reduction of y²=f.

The p-adic matrix convention in Remark2.4,237–246, requires distinct evaluation points modulo p and integral matrices whose basis columns/equation rows remain independent modulo p. Theorem2.23,875–880, is a precise no-loss linear algebra theorem: rk_k(A)=rk_residue(A mod pi) is required. The proof883–907 uses that equality to identify the mod-pi^e kernel with the reduction of the true integral kernel. Remark2.25,910–914, gives O(n^omega) operations in the finite coefficient ring; this is a ring-operation cost, with extension degree and precision still charged. Howell form itself does not decide an unknown characteristic-zero rank after rank drops modulo p. For example, 1x1 matrices 0 and p^E agree modulo p^E but have different characteristic-zero kernels. This elementary ambiguity explains why the hypothesis cannot be discarded; it is not a counterexample to Mascot's stated theorem.

Section4,1036–1055, starts with an already valid point W of J(Zq/p^e) and precomputed Jacobian data to precision at least2e. The membership computation1088–1135 uses a rank-r minor invertible MOD p, obtained because the reduction represents a point of the smooth special-fiber Jacobian. The Schur complement D−CA^-1B and its linear deformation then lift W to SOME valid point modulo p^(2e). Algorithm9 does not solve [2]Q=P for a specified non-torsion P. Its finite-precision and O(g^(omega+3)) ring-operation statements,1280–1299, are useful under the preceding data/rank hypotheses; they do not construct those data for arbitrary bad reduction or bound nonunit minors in native f.

Section5,1312–1314, explicitly assumes m coprime to p to obtain the unique m-torsion lift. Algorithm10,1331–1364, uses a CRT multiplier N with p^e dividing N and N=1 modulo m. At m=p=2 these requirements are incompatible. This is an exact source applicability failure, not merely a missing constant. Its method of correcting a lifted torsion point is also a different problem from finding a half of a specified non-torsion point. The chart variant Algorithm11 retains the prime-to-p hypothesis and assumes the chart differential has full rank modulo p (1442–1443,1484–1505). The source explicitly notes that this immersion can fail, with retries/test (1533–1541); the empirical rarity does not supply a native worst-case chart clock. No dyadic bad-reduction theorem follows by substituting m=2 in either variant. The formal logarithm needed in the parent algorithm should retain its independently proved safe K2 threshold at2; no additional dyadic logarithm claim is imported from this source.

## The second Mascot paper and the original KM interface

*Moduli-friendly Eisenstein series over the p-adics and the computation of modular Galois representations*, mascot_moduli.txt116–146, Definition2.1.1, defines its p-adic Makdisi model with GOOD reduction, unramified Qq, distinct special-fiber evaluation points, and a section basis that remains a basis after reduction. It includes local L-factor data. Section5.1,1155–1163, further requires p not dividing6,ell,N or|H|; it explains good reduction, étaleness of ell-torsion and the Eisenstein-series reason for these restrictions. This is constructive modular good-prime model machinery, not a replacement model or half algorithm at an arbitrary native bad prime.

Khuri-Makdisi, *Linear algebra algorithms for divisors on an algebraic curve*, frozen `surface_torsion_selection/khuri_makdisi_divisors.txt`82–129, supplies O(g^4) FIELD-operation Jacobian/Riemann–Roch algorithms with an explicit section multiplication map. Lines98–108 distinguish construction of that initial field description from later group operations. Lines234–262 use subspaces/Grassmannians rather than exponentially many Plücker coordinates. These are genuine efficient exact-field representations and avoid a general elimination oracle. They contain no bound for p-adic precision loss, bad integral reduction, or inversion of multiplication by2 at2. Working over Qp as a field does not itself pay the finite-precision implementation.

No opened theorem or cited passage supplies the requested arbitrary-bad-reduction finite-precision half interface. Closely related exact bibliography entries in mascot.txt are KM07, *Asymptotically fast group operations on Jacobians of general curves* (2248–2250); Hess, *Computing Riemann–Roch spaces in algebraic function fields and related topics* (2238–2240); and Storjohann–Mulders, *Fast algorithms for linear algebra modulo N* (2295ff). They are leads for their stated algebraic operations, not source-checked bad-reduction precision theorems; no further fetch is required to establish the present source boundary. A field-operation complexity or finite-ring Howell algorithm must not be promoted into that missing theorem.

## Newly fetched differential source: scoped disposition

Kunzweiler–Wewers, *Integral differential forms for superelliptic curves*, `local_halving_saturation/differentials.txt`113–116, Assumption1.4, requires the exponent n invertible in O_K. Theorem1.5(a),130–137, repeats this; the proof138–166 uses tame/Kummer covers and tame quotient singularities. Thus its hyperelliptic n=2 construction excludes residue characteristic2. Remark1.6,168–175, generalizes only to monodromy of order PRIME TO the residue characteristic. The basis theorem4.14,1420–1435, explicitly invokes Theorem1.5 in its proof and does not remove that global hypothesis. The general definition/identification of integral differentials with Néron invariant differentials,70–79, is not an independent quantitative dyadic discriminant/lattice bound. This disposition supersedes any suggestion that the paper's title alone closed the earlier dyadic source request; the parent's new index proof avoids needing that computation.

The remaining constructive obligation is precise: convert the actual native saturation inputs into a finite, polynomial-bit p-adic representation; produce ONE Q with [2]Q=P to sufficient certified accuracy; preserve an invariant for subsequent additions/halvings and Kummer labels; and bound all rank/degree/chart decisions and lost digits by an explicit polynomial clock. The sources provide useful exact-field and GOOD-reduction primitives, but no theorem with this complete arbitrary-bad-reduction/dyadic contract. This review neither asserts impossibility nor reopens the now-closed iteration/index bound.
