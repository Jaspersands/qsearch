# Gauss–Legendre scalar source and per-index clock

**Checked scope.** For binary N>=1, an index 1<=j<=N and precision p>=1,
one can compute the j-th Gauss–Legendre angle/node and its positive weight
to p-bit accuracy in poly(log N,p) bit operations, independently of the
other roots. The weight can also be obtained to relative error 2^-p.
The proof below makes the scalar clock explicit enough to avoid importing
an all-N table, a double-precision O(1) routine, or an unexplained root seed.
It does not construct a coherent Gauss–Legendre transform or its norm/error
compiler, and says nothing about Hahn or Clebsch–Gordan transforms.

Source: Johansson–Mezzarobba, arXiv1802.03948v2, retained as
`sources/2026-11-13-binary-recoupling/johansson_mezzarobba.txt`.
Locators count newline-delimited physical lines. The extra estimates below
are derived here from its displayed equations, rather than attributed to
its theorem labels. No web documentation or finite controls were used.

## 1. Actual asymptotic formula and source boundary

Source Sec.4.1, Eqs.(17)–(21), LF507–539, gives, for 0<theta<pi and K>=1,

 P_n(cos theta)=sqrt(2/(pi sin theta))
   *sum_(r=0)^(K-1) C_(n,r) cos((n+r+1/2)theta-(r+1/2)pi/2)/sin(theta)^r
   +xi_(n,K),

 C_(n,r)=Gamma(r+1/2)^2 Gamma(n+1)
          /[pi*2^r*Gamma(n+r+3/2)*Gamma(r+1)],
 C_(n,0)=Gamma(n+1)/Gamma(n+3/2),
 C_(n,r)/C_(n,r-1)=(2r-1)^2/[4r(2n+2r+1)],
 |xi_(n,K)|<2 sqrt(2/(pi sin theta))*C_(n,K)/sin(theta)^K,
 C_(n,K)<=K!/[pi sqrt(n)(2n)^K]  (n,K>=1).

In particular the leading coefficient is
sqrt(2/(pi sin theta))*Gamma(n+1)/Gamma(n+3/2), asymptotic to
sqrt(2/(pi n sin theta)). A numerical prefactor without its coefficient
normalization is not safe to import. LF547–551 explicitly warns that a
different asymptotic series can converge to twice P_n when summed indefinitely.

Theorem1 (LF130–142) concerns all roots when p is proportional to n and
does not itself supply the requested binary-degree oracle. The source
explicitly distinguishes fixed-precision O(1) claims (LF82–88). Sec.5.2
(LF793–799) states individual-root cost softO(p)*log(n)^O(1) via bit-burst
and Newton, with a sketch of its uniform coefficient bound. Sec.7.2
(LF1012–1019) describes an indexed rigorous routine but imports Petras for
its seed. Neither import is needed for the polynomial clock below.

## 2. Uniform scalar evaluation with a fixed polynomial cap

Reflect theta to [0,pi/2] using P_n(-x)=(-1)^n P_n(x) and the corresponding
derivative sign (-1)^(n+1); handle n=0 and theta=0 explicitly. For required
absolute precision r set q=r+64 ceil(log2(n+2))+64 and L=64(q+1).
Use overlapping branches:
the endpoint method is allowed when n*theta<=2L, and the asymptotic method
when n*theta>=L. A coarse enclosure decides a branch without an exact
comparison at the boundary. This also applies to angles given as rational
multiples of pi, as used in root bisection below. Supplied angle-description
bits are charged; those used below have O(log N+p) bits.

For the endpoint branch, source Eq.(27), LF632–656, is the exact polynomial

 P_n(cos theta)=sum_(r=0)^n binom(n,r)binom(n+r,r) u^r,
 u=-sin(theta/2)^2.

Put A=sqrt(n(n+1)|u|)<=sqrt(2)L. Each absolute term is at most
A^(2r)/(r!)^2, and their sum is at most exp(2A). After
K=ceil(16(L+q+2)) terms, either the polynomial has terminated or the next
term is <=(e*A/K)^(2K)<2^-K and subsequent absolute ratios are <=1/16.
Thus the tail is <2^(1-K). This proves a cap O(q) on term count and O(q)
cancellation bits; it does not rely on the source's heuristic method selector.
Compute the combined terms by Eq.(28), not by materializing degree-n arrays.

For the asymptotic branch, n sin(theta)>=2L/pi>L/2. Taking K=q+2 in
the displayed remainder bound gives

 |xi| <= [2 sqrt(2)/pi^(3/2)]/(sqrt(n sin theta))
             * (K/(2n sin theta))^K < 2^-q.

The combined coefficient recurrence has small, decreasing terms in this
range. Absolute precision remains valid even at an exact zero of P_n;
there is no relative-error condition on this oscillating sum. Trigonometric
phase evaluation needs only O(log n) additional argument precision.

There is no hidden exact binomial of length n in C_(n,0). One elementary
finite-bit implementation evaluates log Gamma twice by Stirling with
remainder, shifting each positive argument up to at least 8J, J=q+8:

 log Gamma(X)=(X-1/2)log X-X+log(2pi)/2
     +sum_(r=1)^J B_(2r)/[2r(2r-1)X^(2r-1)]+R_J,
 |R_J|<=|B_(2J)|/[2J(2J-1)X^(2J-1)] < 2^(4-9J).

Here Euler–Maclaurin gives
R_J=-(1/(2J))*integral_0^infty Btilde_(2J)(t)/(X+t)^(2J) dt.
The inequality follows from |Btilde_(2J)(t)|<=|B_(2J)| and
|B_(2J)|<=4(2J)!/(2pi)^(2J), with X>=8J; undo at most 8J shifts by
the Gamma recurrence. Bernoulli numbers through index 2J have polynomial
bit length and are generated by their finite rational recurrence. Large
log-Gamma terms require O(log(n+2)) extra absolute working bits before
subtraction. Elementary log/exp/sin/cos and square roots admit ordinary
range-reduced polynomial-bit series algorithms. Thus all these operations
have polynomial, not degree-n, cost. This supplies a simple polynomial
bound; no quasi-linear precision claim is needed.

Term recurrences use integers of O(log n+log q) bits and at most polynomial
working precision; even exact rational intermediate representations have
polynomial length. An overlarge fixed working cap 2^20(q+1)^3 bits suffices:
there are O(q) recurrence steps, each crude amplification is at most
(2n^2+2) times a polynomial in q, so their total logarithmic amplification
is O(q(log n+log q)), beneath this cap. The displayed truncation remainders
are charged separately. For derivatives one may use source Eq.(5), LF201–209,
away from the endpoint, and the direct endpoint recurrence Eq.(30),
LF670–687, otherwise. In the former region sin(theta)>=L/(2n), so the division
by sin(theta)^2 loses only O(log n) bits. A direct differentiated endpoint
sum has the same term cap and an additional O(log n) magnitude budget.
This proves poly(log n,p) absolute evaluation of P_n and P_n' throughout
the required interval. When the supplied angle is rounded, the source
global derivative bounds (Prop.3, LF238–246) pay another O(log n) guard.

## 3. Index-certified brackets, without all-root isolation

Write nu=N+1/2 and order theta_j increasingly, so x_j=cos(theta_j)
decreases. For j=1,...,floor(N/2), set

 a_j=(j-1/2)pi/nu,  b_j=j*pi/nu.

On these endpoints sin(theta)>=1/nu. The K=1 relative remainder divided
by its leading amplitude is exactly bounded by
1/[2(2N+3)sin(theta)]<1/4. The two leading cosines are opposite and have
magnitude 1/sqrt(2). Hence each interval has a root. Disjointness,
reflection, and the odd-N root at pi/2 account for all N simple roots.
Their angular separation is at least pi/(2nu), and

 |theta_j-(j-1/4)pi/nu|<=pi/(4nu)                 (all j).

These rational multiples of pi are stored exactly as rational turn
coordinates; bisection need not round the initial bracket endpoints.

## 4. Uniform weights and a quantitative root stopping rule

Put y(theta)=sqrt(sin theta)P_N(cos theta). Its equation is
y''+Q y=0, Q=nu^2+1/(4sin(theta)^2). For
E=y'^2+Q y^2, E'=Q' y^2. On the positive half Q decreases. Consequently

 E_center<=E(theta)<=E_center*Q(theta)/Q_center.

Throughout every bracket nu sin(theta)>=1, hence Q/Q_center<=5/4.
Let a_m=binom(2m,m)/4^m. At the center E=(nu^2+1/4)a_m^2 for N=2m,
and E=N^2 a_m^2 for N=2m+1. The elementary product recurrence gives
1/(4m)<=a_m^2<=1/(m+1) for m>=1; N=1 is direct. Therefore
N/2<=E_center<=2N. Source Eq.(3), LF38–47, now gives at a root

 w_j=2 sin(theta_j)/E(theta_j),
 (4/5)sin(theta_j)/N <= w_j <= 4 sin(theta_j)/N.

In particular w_j>=1/(2N^2); this also pays reciprocal and square-root
precision if those are later requested as scalar arithmetic.

For an effective bisection stopping rule define the continuous Prüfer phase
by y=R sin(phi), y'=nu R cos(phi), phi(0+)=0. Direct differentiation gives
phi'=nu+sin(phi)^2/[4nu sin(theta)^2]. Parity and root counting give
phi(pi/2)=nu*pi/2+pi/4. Thus

 phi(theta)=nu*theta+pi/4-I(theta),
 0<=I(theta)<=cot(theta)/(4nu)<=1/4

throughout a bracket. There |phi-j*pi|<=pi/4+1/4<pi/2 and
E0=y'^2+nu^2 y^2>=(4/5)E>=2N/5. Since phi'>=nu and
|sin z|>=2|z|/pi for |z|<=pi/2, this yields

 |P_N(cos theta)| >= (sqrt(N)/4)|theta-theta_j|.

Evaluate the midpoint with absolute error delta=2^(-t-5). A certified sign
halves the bracket. If the computed magnitude is at most delta, the true
root is within 8delta/sqrt(N), so stop with that certified enclosure.
Otherwise O(t+log N) halvings suffice. Exact-zero midpoints cause no issue.

For p-bit nodes and relative p-bit weights take, for example,
t=p+32 ceil(log2(N+2))+32. Prop.3 gives |P_N''|<=N^4 and |P_N'|<=N^2;
the weight bounds give |P_N'(x_j)|>=sqrt(N/2), and sin(theta_j)^2>=nu^-2.
These bounds show that root rounding, derivative evaluation and division
in the weight formula require only the stated O(log N) guards. Symmetry
handles the other half and the central odd root exactly. All loops have
explicit polynomial caps and rational turn coordinates have O(t+log N)
bits. No inverse-gap, conditioning, table, or supplied-root advice remains
at this scalar interface. Combining this routine with Section2 also evaluates
P_k(cos(theta_j)) for binary k<=N using only polynomial additional precision,
since |P_k'|<=k^2. The coherent transform is a separate obligation.
