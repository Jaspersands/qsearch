Scope, cost and pre-registered rules for the kappa = 8 direct (C1) sampler (topic note section 12.14; mechanical item
9). Written 2026-11-11 10:50:08, before any kappa = 8 class-value run and before item 8 (kappa = 7, 24 primes) is scored.
Nothing here is started; the sampler itself is not written (only the two scoping timers below exist).

WHAT IT MUST SAMPLE. The (C1) measure of mc_classes.py: uniform on pairs (S, T), S a k-set, T a k-subset of one
S-bucket, i.e. ind(s_j - t_i) = a_j + b_i (mod k) for all i, j. Uniform on unordered pairs = uniform on ordered
sequences, since each pair gives k!^2 orderings. For each configuration it must output mc_classes.py's per-configuration
Q = P Hc P^T over z in P^1 minus S u T, so that the existing estimators (k6_spike_survey.analyse, tail_index.py) apply.

WHY uniform-S sampling cannot. At k = 8, lambda = 0.18 (p ~ 3.8e5) a uniform S has ~5.7e-5 configurations
(M lambda^k / k!, M = k^(k-1)), so 6000 configurations need ~1e8 S at ~30 ms each: ~900 core-h per prime.

METHOD: sequential importance sampling. Order s1, t1, s2, t2, ..., sk, tk. A new s must satisfy
ind(s - t_i) - ind(s - t_1) = ind(s_1 - t_i) - ind(s_1 - t_1) for the t_i already chosen, and a new t the mirror
condition. Each step picks uniformly among the unused candidates; the weight W is the product of the candidate counts
(0 at a dead end). E[W] = number of ordered (C1) sequences, so N1 = E[W] (p+1)/(p(p^2-1)), and class values are
weighted means (self-normalised). Configurations are independent, so batches need no S-clustering correction.

COST: about 1.5 core-h per prime for an ESS of 6000 (about 13000 weighted configurations), on this loaded machine

  The per-run limit of 2 core-h may need nconf lowered to 4000 effective; the rule does not depend on nconf.
