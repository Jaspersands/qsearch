2026-11-10T02:16  Written before the runs below finished. This is a post-hoc pattern in the 17 survey primes
1531-1753 (k6_spike_survey.py output at 02:16) plus the earlier S-batched runs; it is not yet a tested claim.

Hypothesis H. At kappa = 6, B_D spikes need ind(-1) = 0 (mod 6), i.e. p = 1 (mod 12).

Heuristic mechanism. Take a configuration that is partly symmetric under a split involution conjugate to x -> -x.
The contributions of z and -z to the class values then differ by the phases exp(2 pi i j ind(-1)/6):
- if ind(-1) = 0, the phases are all 1 and the contributions add;
- if ind(-1) = 3, the odd characters j change sign and the contributions partly cancel.
N1/mu1 is a count, not a phase sum, so it should not depend on ind(-1).

Seen so far:

  are 1 (mod 12). The pre-fix lambda = 0.5 spike 3889 is also 1 (mod 12).
- The 9 survey primes that are 7 (mod 12) have B_D at most 1.00 (1627).
- The N1/mu1 spikes 1531 (4.11) and 1627 (2.64) are 7 (mod 12).
- If class did not matter, the top 4 of 17 would all fall in the class of 8 with probability C(8,4)/C(17,4) = 0.03.
  Four residue symbols were looked at, so that is about 0.1 after the look-elsewhere correction.

2026-11-10T02:24  Correction, appended; the predictions above are unchanged. The heuristic mechanism above is wrong.
The class modes R, C are sum-zero, and count vectors are taken up to a cyclic shift of the values
(mc_classes.tables). For a configuration symmetric under x -> -x, U(-z) is a permutation of U(z) plus a constant
vector; the constant is h + d, where d = beta(-t) - beta(t) does not depend on t. So z and -z contribute equally
whatever h is. No mechanism for H is known; H is an empirical pattern only.
