# Joint-charge randomized eigenpath tracking

Status: CLOSED conditional source/composition review. The proposed randomized dephasing removes classical target-charge computation under the explicit simple-branch, joint-distance and efficient-charge promises below. It does NOT prove the unresolved Gaudin joint-gap bound or an unconditional polynomial algorithm for arbitrary binary-close rational sites. No controls, Git, network or external services were used.

## Dimension-free joint-charge construction

Assume m≥1 explicitly simulable commuting Hermitian C1 charges Q_i(s), s in[0,1], and a target joint eigenline v(s), initially a known product state. Its joint eigenvalue vector must be at Euclidean distance at least kappa>0 from every other joint eigenspace throughout the path. Degeneracy entirely within the complement is harmless. Write L²=sum_i sup_s||Q_i'(s)||². These are explicit promised/computed bounds, not an oracle for target eigenvalues q_i(s).

At one mesh point take independent uniform t_i in[0,T], T=4sqrt(m)/kappa, and apply the product of exp(-it_i Q_i). In a simultaneous eigenbasis its target–other multiplier is product_i exp(i d_i T/2)sinc(d_i T/2), up to the harmless sign convention for d_i. At least one |d_i|≥kappa/sqrt(m), hence |d_i|T/2≥2 and that factor's magnitude is at most1/2. Independent repetition k times makes the maximum cross multiplier c≤2^-k. No individual charge gap or target eigenvalue is needed.

Let R be that exact randomized channel, P the target projector and Q=I−P. The ideal comparison channel is M(rho)=P rho P+R(Q rho Q). It is CPTP, fixes the target, and retains arbitrary complement evolution. If p=Tr(P rho) and b=P rho Q, positivity gives ||b||_1≤sqrt(p(1−p)). The crossblock multiplier acts by a diagonal contraction D with ||D||≤c, so

    ||(R−M)(rho)||_1 ≤ 2c sqrt(p(1−p)) ≤ c.

This proves the claimed dimension-free bound, rather than summing absolute errors over exponentially many eigenvectors. The same estimate can be extended with a passive reference by trace-norm Cauchy–Schwarz; no such extension is needed for known-input state preparation.

Choose parallel-transport phase for v. Differentiating each eigen-equation and projecting on a complementary joint eigenvector w_j gives d_i,j <w_j|v'>=−<w_j|Q_i'|v>. Summing squared magnitudes first over i then over j yields kappa²||v'||²≤sum_i||Q_i'||²≤L². Thus ||v'||≤L/kappa, with no calculation of q_i or q_i'. Uniform mesh spacing1/R gives ideal Zeno fidelity at least1−L²/(kappa² R), by Lemma1/product overlaps. Full trace norm to the endpoint pure state is therefore at most2L/(kappa sqrt(R)).

For example, R=max(1,ceil(16L²/(kappa² epsilon²))) gives ideal error≤epsilon/2. Taking k=ceil(log2(4R/epsilon)) makes total replacement error R*2^-k≤epsilon/4 by CPTP contractivity. This leaves epsilon/4 for finite implementation. L=0 is the trivial constant-line case; using the displayed positive mesh guard remains safe.

## Finite clocks and required scope guards

There are I=R*k*m exponential simulations and bounded evolution time k*m*T per mesh step. Use a rational T between4sqrt(m)/kappa and a constant multiple, e.g.4ceil(sqrt(m))/kappa. Let A_tot=sum_i sup_s sum_a|c_i,a(s)| for the explicit Pauli expansions Q_i=sum_a c_i,a P_i,a. Round each uniform time to a b-bit uniform grid. Coupling it to the continuous time changes the total channel by at most2R*k*T*A_tot*2^-b. Choose 2^b≥16R*k*T*A_tot/epsilon, giving error≤epsilon/8. Approximate each resulting exponential with operator-norm error at most epsilon/(16I), including coefficient, simulation and gate-synthesis errors; their channel telescoping costs at mostepsilon/8. All errors now sum to at mostepsilon. These are finite bounds, not precision-until-success loops.

For the explicit polynomial-size Pauli charges, a conservative product formula already gives polynomial gate cost in T, coefficient one-norms and reciprocal requested accuracy; elementary rotation synthesis requires polynomial bit precision. More efficient simulation may improve that upper bound but is unnecessary to close this conditional composition. A polynomial native BIT/gate claim requires these numerical resource bounds (including L/kappa and T*A_tot) polynomial in the stated input and1/epsilon. Distinct rational sites alone permit exponentially small separations and exponentially large coefficients; their binary encoding does not establish these bounds. The dependence here is polynomial in1/epsilon, not logarithmic precision.

Use an interpolation that actually preserves charge commutativity: in Lutz's family, vary g with the fixed distinct sites, including the derivative of the chosen g(s). Arbitrarily interpolating unrelated endpoint operators is outside the theorem. Magnetization is conserved by every exact Gaudin charge. One may restrict the joint-gap promise to the starting magnetization sector if every IDEAL operation preserves it and all finite-simulation leakage is charged by the same trace-error budget; alternatively retain the stronger full-space joint-gap promise. The cross-sector bound does not settle same-sector separation. Lutz's parent-Hamiltonian gap is kappa², whereas the random-time and derivative bounds here use the UNSQUARED kappa.

The target is defined by continuation from the specified initial product label. Mapping an arbitrary supplied endpoint Bethe root list/eigenstate label back to that initial branch is a separate task; it is not made free by deleting q(s). The output guarantee is for the AVERAGED randomized preparation density matrix. It gives neither a guarantee for every sampled time sequence nor a clean coherent preparation unitary, inverse, target reflection or controlled eigenstate oracle. No outcome is postselected and the same evolving register is used throughout. Subject to these guards the proposed composition is accepted; the full native Gaudin gap/parameter promise remains conditional, with no novelty or separation claim.
