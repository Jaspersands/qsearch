## 2. What is formalised

### 2.1 `BCHClassSeven`

The law `QSearch.BCHClassSeven.bch` is the sextic law of `BCHClassSix`, written out
term by term, plus `z7 x y`. In right-normed words (`xxyyxy` = `[x,[x,[y,[y,[x,y]]]]]`):

```
z7 =  (xxxxxxy + yyyxxxy)/30240 - (xxxyyxy + yyyyyxy)/30240
    + (xxxxyxy + xxyyyxy + xyyyyxy + yxxyyxy)/10080
    - (xyyxxxy + yxxxxxy + yyxxxxy + yyyxyxy)/10080
    + xyxxxxy/5040 - yxyyyxy/5040 + xyyxyxy/2520 - yxxxyxy/2520
    + xyxxyxy/1120 - yxyxyxy/1120
```

**Denominators and invertible primes.** The degree-7 denominators are 1120, 2520,
5040, 10080 and 30240. Their lcm is 30240 = 2^5·3^3·5·7, which is also the lcm of
every denominator of the whole law in associative form. So the law needs
`(2 : F) ≠ 0`, `(3 : F) ≠ 0`, `(5 : F) ≠ 0` and `(7 : F) ≠ 0`, and nothing more.
Over `ZMod p` this means `p > 7`, so `p ≥ 11`.

Proved:

- `classSeven_of_lowerCentralSeries`: from `lowerCentralSeries F L L 7 = ⊥`, every
  right-normed length-8 bracket vanishes.
- `bch_assoc_of_classSeven` and `bch_assoc`: associativity, from
  `2, 3, 5, 7 ≠ 0` and the class bound alone.
- `bch_zero_left/right`, `bch_neg_left/right`, `bch_of_lie_eq_zero`, `map_bch`,
  `bch_smul_same`.
- `instGroup : Group (BCH F L hnil)`, under `[NeZero 2] [NeZero 3] [NeZero 5] [NeZero 7]`.
- `val_mul`, `val_mul_formula`, `val_one`, `val_inv`, `val_pow`, `val_zpow`.
- `lieHom`, `lieEquiv` and their `id`, `comp` and `symm` lemmas.
- Helper lemmas, reused by the subgroup file: `z7`, `bch_eq_six_add`, `bch_shape`,
  `z7_central`, `z7_peel_left/right`, `six_central_left/right`, `defect`,
  `smooth7_ne_zero`.

**Change of method.** The class-six method, one `simp only` over both sides of
associativity, failed at class seven. After 1562 s wall time (934 s CPU) the build
stopped with "`simp` failed: maximum number of steps exceeded". The cost came from
`z7 (bch x y) z`: the 34-term polynomial `bch x y` sits in up to six slots, and its
bracket terms die only once they reach length 8. So associativity is now proved in two
parts.

1. **Reduction.** `z7` is central (`z7_central`). It ignores any summand `t • [p,q]`
   in either argument (`z7_peel_left/right`, applied after `bch_shape`, which writes
   `bch x y` as `x + y + Σ tᵢ • [pᵢ, qᵢ]`). A central summand passes through the
   sextic law (`six_central_left/right`). With `bch = BCHClassSix.bch + z7`
   (`bch_eq_six_add`), both sides become the sextic law plus `z7` terms.
2. **`defect`.** In class seven,
   `B6(B6(x,y),z) + z7(x,y) + z7(x+y,z) = B6(x,B6(y,z)) + z7(y,z) + z7(x,y+z)`,
   where `B6` is `BCHClassSix.bch`. This identity is checked by the class-six method:
   - one `simp only` (with `maxSteps := 100000000`) brings both sides to right-normed
     brackets in `x < y < z`;
   - `match_scalars`, `field_simp` and `ring` compare the coefficients;
   - the rewrite set is `jacobi3`, 6 degree-four, 6 degree-five, 28 degree-six and 36
     new degree-seven rules.

The irreducible words number 3, 3, 8, 18, 48, 116 and 312 in degrees 1–7. These are
the dimensions of the free Lie algebra on three generators, and `rules7.py` checks
that the words are linearly independent. So the normal form is a basis and the
comparison is complete. Every degree-7 rule is proved in Lean from one, two or three
`lie_skew` instances with integer coefficients. One rule, `xxzyxyz`, needs coefficient
2, which the generator writes as a repeated summand. All rules passed on the first
compile.

### 2.2 `BCHClassSevenSubgroups`

This is Lazard's subgroup direction at class seven over `ZMod p`, under
`[Fact p.Prime] [NeZero 2] [NeZero 3] [NeZero 5] [NeZero 7]`.
`neZeroTwo/Three/Five/Seven (hp : 7 < p)` install the last four. The structure is that
of class six, with three changes.

- **Words.**
  - The nine degree-6 commutators used now carry degree-7 tails (defs `p6…`).
  - The 18 iterated commutators `C(l, C6)` of those nine are central, and each one's
    logarithm is a single degree-7 basis word (`commPoly_<5 letters>`).
  - `bracketWord x y` is `comm x y` times powers of 2 + 3 + 6 + 9 + 18 commutators.
    The coefficients up to degree 6 are those of class six; the degree-7 ones are
    −1/6, −37/24, 187/120, −9/16, … (listed in `words7.py`).
  - `addWord x y` is `x * y` times powers of 1 + 2 + 3 + 6 + 9 + 18 exact bracket
    monomials.
  - `val_bracketWord : (bracketWord x y).val = ⁅x.val, y.val⁆` and
    `val_addWord : (addWord x y).val = x.val + y.val`.
- **Staged proofs.**
  - `val_bracketWord` and `val_addWord` are each a chain of `rw` steps through 38
    and 39 lemmas `bwStep k`, `awStep k`. Each lemma passes from the exact normal-form
    logarithm after k − 1 factors (`bwStage`, `awStage`) to the one after k.
  - The `awStep` lemmas drop `z7` first (`z7_bracket_right`), so their `simp` runs on
    the sextic law.
  - `bch_comm` uses the reduction of §2.1 and an intermediate normal form
    `commStage1` of `log(x y x⁻¹)`.
  - The monolithic class-six `bch_comm` was too slow here: the first build was still
    inside it after 2 h 05 min wall time (78 min CPU) and was stopped.
- **Two-letter normal form.** `[b,a] ↦ -[a,b]`, `jacobi4c`, the three degree-6 rules
  `two6a`–`two6c` reused from `BCHClassSixSubgroups`, and no degree-7 rule: the 18
  words `a·w` are independent.

`toLieSubalgebra hnil H` (the logarithm of an arbitrary subgroup is a Lie subalgebra),
`toSubgroup`, and both round trips are stated and proved as in class six.

## 3. Coefficient cross-check

The degree-7 coefficients were computed by four methods independent of the
generator's right-normed form, and compared word by word in the free associative
algebra on `x, y`:

| Method | Script | Result |
|---|---|---|
| (A) The Lean law: degree ≤ 6 terms + `Z7`, expanded | `model7.py` | reference |
| (B) `log(exp x exp y)` by truncated series | `model7.py`, `cross7.py` | = (A) in degrees 1–7 (2, 2, 6, 4, 30, 28, 126 words) |
| (C) Dynkin's explicit formula, `Σ (-1)^(n-1)/n · [x^r1 y^s1 …]/((Σ rᵢ+sᵢ) Π rᵢ! sᵢ!)` with right-nested brackets; no exp/log | `dynkin7.py` | = (A) in degrees 1–7 |
| (D) Varadarajan's recursion in Sage 10.9's free Lie algebra (Lyndon basis), lifted to the free algebra | `sage_bch7.py` → `sage_bch7.json` | = (A) in degrees 1–7 |
| (E) Exact `log(exp X exp Y)` for 3 random strictly upper-triangular 8×8 rational matrices (class ≤ 7, so the truncation is exact) | `dynkin7.py`, `cross7.py` | = (A) and = (C) |

`check_lean_def7.py` closes the loop. It parses the text of `def bch` in
`formal/Formal/BCHClassSeven.lean` itself, expands it to 198 associative words, and
finds them equal to Dynkin's formula through degree 7. Run with argument `6`, it
checks `BCHClassSix.bch` against Dynkin through degree 6, with 72 words, all equal.
The class-six review left that check open.

**Side finding: a bug in Sage 10.9's `bch_iterator`.** The library routine
`sage.algebras.lie_algebras.bch.bch_iterator` is wrong from degree 6 on.

- Its degree-6 term has coefficient −61/8640 on `[x,[x,[x,[x,[x,y]]]]]`. This is
  impossible: the part of BCH linear in `y` is `ad_x/(1 − e^{−ad_x}) y`, and B₅ = 0.
- Its value also disagrees with the exact matrix logarithm.
- The cause is the Bernoulli cache test `if p <= len(norm_ber): norm_ber.append(...)`.
  It appends B₂/2! again, so from degree 6 on, B₂/2! is used where B₄/4! is meant.
- `sage_bch7.py` writes the same recursion out with the Bernoulli numbers indexed
  correctly; that is method (D).

This bug was not reported upstream, because the session had no network access.
