## Printed292 (PDF zero-based10 / one-based11): ambiguous forms
Image: bosma-page-images/pdf-page-010-printed-292.png

"The class of the form P in 3.4 is only determined up to composition with classes from Cl[2], and all we know is that the form P found by the algorithm lies in one of these classes. Even when Q is in the trivial class, there is no guarantee that P will be in the trivial class."

For an odd prime p dividing D, with p^k || D, the displayed ideal and form are:

$$I_p=\mathbf Z\cdot p^k+\mathbf Z\cdot(D+\sqrt D)/2,$$

$$Q_p=\begin{cases}
(p^k,p^k,(p^k-D/p^k)/4)&\text{if }D\equiv1\pmod4,\\
(p^k,0,-D/(4p^k))&\text{if }D\equiv0\pmod4.
\end{cases}$$

For D equiv0 mod4, with 2^t || D and t>=2:

$$Q_2=\begin{cases}
(2,2,(4-D)/8)&\text{if }D\equiv-4\pmod{16},\\
(2^{t-2},0,-D/2^t)&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}$$

For D divisible by32 the further displayed form is:

$$Q'_2=(4,4,1-D/16).$$

The source defines S_0 from the Q_p by leaving out Q_2 for D equiv4 mod16, and including Q'_2 for D equiv0 mod32.

## Printed293 (PDF11 / physical12): positive-discriminant relation and Lemma3.5
Image: bosma-page-images/pdf-page-011-printed-293.png

"For this reason, we have to start our algorithm for D>0 with a generating set S_0 for C[2], not an F_2-basis. This difference between the real and the imaginary case accounts for the slightly larger running times of the algorithm for positive D. The relation between the generators of C[2] for D>0 can be obtained as a by-product of the computation of C. From the relation we can determine the sign of the norm of epsilon_D without explicitly computing epsilon_D."

The pairing displayed as(3.2) is:

$$\mathcal C/2\mathcal C\times\mathfrak X_D/\langle\chi_d\rangle\longrightarrow\mathbf F_2.$$

Lemma3.5. Let G be a finite abelian2-group, X a basis for its group of quadratic characters, and suppose there is a disjoint union

$$B=\bigcup_{j=1}^N A_j$$

of finite sets A_j subset G such that:

(a) G[2] is generated by the elements 2^{j-1}a_j with a_j in A_j and j in{1,...,N};

(b) the matrix

$$(\chi(a))_{\chi\in X,a\in B}$$

is a non-singular square matrix.

The printed conclusion is that B maps to an F_2-basis for G/2G, the elements in A_j have exact order2^j in G, and the natural map

$$\prod_{j=1}^N(\mathbf Z/2^j\mathbf Z)^{A_j}\xrightarrow{\sim}G$$

is an isomorphism of groups.

## Printed294 (PDF12 / physical13): stored invariants and initialization
Image: bosma-page-images/pdf-page-012-printed-294.png

The numbered stored data after i steps are:

1. A disjoint union B_i=union_{j=1}^i A_j of finite sets of forms A_j, together with a collection of forms S_i such that C[2] is generated by elements

$$2^{j-1}[a_j]\quad(a_j\in A_j),\qquad2^i[s]\quad(s\in S_i).$$

2. A subset X_i subset X of characters such that

$$(\chi(a))_{\chi\in X_i,a\in B_i}$$

is a non-singular square matrix.

"Initialization. At the initial stage i=0, we have B_0=X_0=emptyset and our set S_0 of ambiguous forms that meets the non-empty requirement(1)."

## Printed295 (PDF13 / physical14): induction and exact termination
Image: bosma-page-images/pdf-page-013-printed-295.png

The character matrix at stage i is displayed as:

$$M_i=(\chi(a))_{\chi\in X,a\in B_i\cup S_i}.$$

After compositions with B_i, the source makes all characters in X_i vanish on S_i. It chooses A_{i+1} subset S_i whose columns X(a) form a basis of the resulting space V, and chooses Y_i subset X such that

$$(\chi(a))_{\chi\in Y_i,a\in A_{i+1}}$$

is non-singular. It sets X_{i+1}=X_i union Y_i. After further compositions with A_{i+1}, all characters in X vanish on the remaining forms. Division-by2 algorithm3.4 is applied to those modified forms to obtain S_{i+1}.

The two displayed sets in the invariant argument are:

$$\{2^i[s]:s\in S_i\},$$

$$\{2^i[a_{i+1}]:a_{i+1}\in A_{i+1}\}\ \cup\ \{2^{i+1}[s_{i+1}]:s_{i+1}\in S_{i+1}\}.$$

"Termination. The algorithm terminates at stage i if we have X_i=X. This is bound to happen as we have X_i=X if and only if C is annihilated by2^i."

"We conclude that after N steps, with2^N the exponent of C, we have found a basis B=union_{j=1}^N A_j for C that satisfies the conditions of lemma3.5."

## Printed296 (PDF14 / physical15): extra real relation and represented coordinates
Image: bosma-page-images/pdf-page-014-printed-296.png

"In the real case D>0, we have to work with an extra generator in S_0. At the final stage N, we compute from S_{N-1} a set A_N that completes our basis B and a single form s_D in2C to which we can apply3.4 to find the single element of S_N. As[s_D] is divisible by2 in a group of exponent2^N, we have2^{N-1}[s_D]=0."

"We can, at the cost of a little extra administration, carry not only the set S_i along at stage i, but also for each s in S_i the representation of2^i[s] in terms of our original2-torsion generators in S_0."

The coordinate recovery passage displays:

$$c=c_0=b_0+2c_1,\qquad c_i=b_i+2c_{i+1}\quad(i=0,1,\ldots,N-1),$$

$$c=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}2^i b_i.$$

## Printed308 (PDF26 / physical27): Theorem6.7, including parity branch
Image: bosma-page-images/pdf-page-026-printed-308.png

The preceding displayed last-row identity is:

$$M^T(0,0,1)=(p,q,r)=(a_2b_3-a_3b_2,\ a_3b_1-a_1b_3,\ a_1b_2-a_2b_1).$$

Theorem6.7. Let Q be a primitive binary quadratic form of EVEN discriminant D, and suppose there are M in SL_3(Z) and a ternary form Q_1 satisfying

$$Q_1(x,y,0)=Q(x,y),\qquad Q_1^M=\Phi=y^2-2xz.$$

Let(p,q,r)=M^T(0,0,1) be the last row of M, and define:

$$P=\begin{cases}
px^2+2qxy+2ry^2&\text{if }p\text{ is odd},\\
rx^2-2qxy+2py^2&\text{if }p\text{ is even}.
\end{cases}$$

"Then P is a primitive form of discriminant D, and we have2[P]=[Q] in Cl(D)."

The proof displays:

$$pa_1+qa_2+ra_3=pb_1+qb_2+rb_3=0,$$

$$\alpha=\Phi(a_1,a_2,a_3)=a_2^2-2a_1a_3,\qquad\gamma=\Phi(b_1,b_2,b_3)=b_2^2-2b_1b_3.$$

## Printed309 (PDF27 / physical28): completion of the halving identity
Image: bosma-page-images/pdf-page-027-printed-309.png

The two displayed bilinear matrices are:

$$\begin{pmatrix}s_1&s_2&s_3\\t_1&t_2&t_3\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}b_3&b_2&2b_1\\a_3&a_2&2a_1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}2b_3&b_2&b_1\\2a_3&a_2&a_1\end{pmatrix}.$$

The displayed discriminant is4(q^2-2pr)=D, and the final identity is:

$$\Phi^*(p,q,r)=-q^2+2pr=(Q_1^*)^{M^*}(p,q,r)=Q_1^*(0,0,1)=\det Q=-D/4.$$

"Theorem6.7 shows that we can divide[Q] in Cl(D) by2 if we can complete Q to a ternary form Q_1 of determinant-1 and find the transformation matrix M that reduces Q_1 to Phi=y^2-2xz. Both of these steps can be efficiently performed whenever Q is in the principal genus. This finishes the description of the division-by2-algorithm3.4."
