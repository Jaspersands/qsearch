## Verdict

**Yes, under an explicit quadratic-Hecke GRH assumption, the isolated gluing gate has a polynomial-bit solution.** Replace the random-one-prime search by a batch of small primes that split in both relevant quadratic extensions. Their **collective span**, rather than any one prescribed ray class, supplies the common value.

This gives polynomially many, polynomial-size support enlargements, using only the stipulated complete squareclass routine, polynomial local arithmetic, and charged integer factorization. It does **not** establish the separate tower recursion or repair Appendix A’s analysis of arbitrary random prime selection.

Without GRH, the same search is a constructive terminating algorithm, but the polynomial clock proved below is unavailable.

---

## 1. Fixed modulus and the precise GRH assumption

Write \(K=F_h\), \(N=[K:\mathbf Q]\), and
\[
q=\langle a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4\rangle,\qquad a_i\in K^\times.
\]
All complexity statements below include the supplied field tables, coefficient coordinates, support, and expanded outputs.

Enlarge the initial finite support \(S\) to contain:

* every prime above \(2\);
* the supplied tower support;
* every prime occurring in the numerator **or denominator** of an \(a_i\).

This enlargement is polynomial-size. For example, write \(a_i=\alpha_i/t_i\), with \(\alpha_i\in\mathcal O_K\), and factor the polynomial-bit integers \(t_i\) and \(N_{K/\mathbf Q}(\alpha_i)\). Those factorizations and subsequent prime-ideal decompositions are charged.

Put
\[
d_1=-a_1a_2,\qquad d_2=-a_3a_4,\qquad
M=K(\sqrt{d_1},\sqrt{d_2}).
\]
Then \(M/K\) is abelian of degree at most \(4\), unramified outside \(S\).

For \(\mathfrak p\in S\), set
\[
k_{\mathfrak p}=2v_{\mathfrak p}(2)+1,\qquad
\mathfrak m_0=\prod_{\mathfrak p\in S}\mathfrak p^{k_{\mathfrak p}},
\]
and let \(\mathfrak m\) include every real place. Thus
\[
1+\mathfrak p^{k_{\mathfrak p}}\subset K_{\mathfrak p}^{\times2}.
\]
Every quadratic character unramified outside \(S\), including those defining \(M\), has conductor dividing \(\mathfrak m\).

Define the charged parameter
\[
\Lambda=2+N+\log |D_K|+\log N\mathfrak m_0.
\]
Integer bit lengths can replace these logarithms in the implementation.

The modulus has polynomial bit length. In particular, its entire dyadic contribution satisfies
\[
\sum_{\mathfrak p\mid2}(2v_{\mathfrak p}(2)+1)\log N\mathfrak p
\le 3N\log2.
\]
The real part has at most \(N\) places. Neither calculation requires enumerating local squareclasses.

### Analytic assumption

Assume GRH for the primitive Hecke \(L\)-functions of \(K\) associated with **every character of order at most \(2\) whose conductor divides \(\mathfrak m\)**, including the trivial character.

This is stronger than assuming GRH only for \(\zeta_K\) or the two visible quadratic extensions. No Artin-holomorphy conjecture is needed: all characters used below are abelian Hecke characters.

## 2. The small-splitting-prime lemma

Let
\[
G=\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak m}(K)/2\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak m}(K).
\]
This group is used **only in the proof**; the algorithm never computes it.

Let \(H\subseteq G\) be generated by the classes of all primes outside \(S\) splitting completely in \(M\). Let \(H_X\) use only such primes of norm at most \(X\).

### Lemma

Under the stated GRH, there is an absolute effective constant \(C\) such that
\[
H_X=H
\quad\text{for}\quad
X=(C\Lambda)^8.
\]
The exponent \(8\) is deliberately nonoptimal.

### Proof

Suppose \(H_X\ne H\). A character
\[
\chi:G\longrightarrow\{\pm1\}
\]
then exists that is trivial on \(H_X\) but not on \(H\). Let \(K_\chi/K\) be its quadratic class field.

Because \(\chi\) is nontrivial on some completely split prime of \(M\), \(K_\chi\) is not contained in \(M\). Consequently,
\[
E=MK_\chi
\]
has an automorphism \(g\) that is the identity on \(M\) and nontrivial on \(K_\chi\).

Crucially,
\[
[E:K]\le8,\qquad [E:\mathbf Q]\le8N.
\]
Every character of \(E/K\) has conductor dividing \(\mathfrak m\). The conductor-discriminant formula therefore gives
\[
\log |D_E|
\le 8\log |D_K|+7\log N\mathfrak m_0.
\]

Here is the effective-prime input being used. A deliberately weakened form of the GRH Hecke prime-ideal estimate gives, for these abelian extensions,
\[
\left|
\sum_{\substack{N\mathfrak p\le x,\ \mathfrak p\notin S\\
                 \operatorname{Frob}_{\mathfrak p}=g}}
       \log N\mathfrak p
-\frac{x}{[E:K]}
\right|
\le
C_0\sqrt{x}
 \bigl(\log|D_E|+[E:\mathbf Q]\log(2x)\bigr)^2
+\log N\mathfrak m_0,
\]
with an absolute effective \(C_0\).

This follows from the GRH explicit formula for the Hecke characters of \(E/K\), character orthogonality, and removal of prime powers. Excluding \(S\) costs at most \(\sum_{\mathfrak p\in S}\log N\mathfrak p\). The displayed bound is intentionally weaker than optimized effective Chebotarev estimates.

For \(x=(C\Lambda)^8\), with sufficiently large absolute effective \(C\), the main term exceeds the error. Hence there is a prime \(\mathfrak p\notin S\) with
\[
N\mathfrak p\le X,\qquad
\mathfrak p\text{ split completely in }M,\qquad
\chi(\mathfrak p)=-1.
\]
That contradicts the definition of \(\chi\).

The prescribed Artin data here consist of at most **three quadratic bits**: split in the two visible quadratic extensions and nontrivial in one separating quadratic extension. The relative field degree is at most \(8\), not the degree of the full ray class field. ∎

This does **not** assert that a prime which solves the original problem by itself has density at least \(1/8\). It proves that small splitting primes collectively generate the necessary subgroup.

## 3. Why this span supplies the desired \(b\)

The isotropy promise supplies some nonzero common value \(b_0\). Indeed, when both binary summands are anisotropic, any nonzero isotropic vector gives
\[
b_0=a_1x_1^2+a_2x_2^2
   =-a_3x_3^2-a_4x_4^2\ne0.
\]
Binary-isotropic cases are immediate.

At a prime \(\mathfrak p\notin S\), all \(a_i\) are units and the residue characteristic is odd. The necessary local equations are
\[
(d_1,b_0)_{\mathfrak p}=1,\qquad
(d_2,b_0)_{\mathfrak p}=1.
\]
If \(v_{\mathfrak p}(b_0)\) is odd, these equations force both \(d_1\) and \(d_2\) to be local squares. Thus:

> Every outside-\(S\) prime occurring with odd valuation in \(b_0\) splits completely in \(M\).

Write the outside-\(S\) ideal of \(b_0\) as
\[
(b_0)_{S^c}=J I^2,
\]
where \(J\) is the squarefree product of those odd-valuation primes. Its class lies in \(H\).

By the lemma, there is a product \(J_X\) of completely split primes of norm at most \(X\) with
\[
[J]=[J_X]\quad\text{in }G.
\]
By the definition of the ray class group modulo squares,
\[
JJ_X^{-1}=A^2(c),
\]
where \(A\) is prime to \(S\), while
\[
c\equiv1\pmod{\mathfrak p^{k_{\mathfrak p}}}
\quad(\mathfrak p\in S),
\qquad c>0\quad\text{at every real place}.
\]
Therefore \(c\) is a local square at every place in \(S\), including the real places, and
\[
(b_0/c)_{S^c}=J_X(IA)^2.
\]

Let
\[
Q_X=\{\mathfrak p\notin S:
       N\mathfrak p\le X,\ \mathfrak p\text{ split completely in }M\},
\qquad T=S\cup Q_X.
\]
Then \(b_0/c\in K(T,2)\), with the same local squareclasses as \(b_0\) at \(S\).

At the added primes, both \(d_i\) are local squares, so their local equations are automatic. Elsewhere outside \(S\), even valuations make the equations automatic. Hence \(K(T,2)\) contains a feasible common-value squareclass.

**Neither \(b_0\), \(c\), nor a ray-class principal generator is computed.** Their role is to prove consistency of a linear system. The stipulated expanded \(K(T,2)\) basis supplies the actual polynomial-bit representative.

## 4. Explicit algorithm

The following replaces step (5i) of Algorithm 1.

1. Construct the charged support \(S\) above and compute \(d_1,d_2\).
2. For cutoffs \(X=2,4,8,\ldots\):
   * Enumerate rational primes \(p\le X\).
   * Decompose \(p\mathcal O_K\).
   * Retain prime ideals \(\mathfrak p\notin S\) with \(N\mathfrak p\le X\) for which both \(d_1,d_2\) are squares in the residue field.
   * Set \(T=S\cup Q_X\).
   * Obtain the stipulated complete basis
     \[
     \kappa_1,\ldots,\kappa_k
     \quad\text{of }K(T,2).
     \]
   * Solve Koprowski’s sign/Hilbert-symbol system, using the original finite places \(S\):
     \[
     (d_1,b)_{\mathfrak p}=(a_1,a_2)_{\mathfrak p},\qquad
     (d_2,b)_{\mathfrak p}=(-a_3,-a_4)_{\mathfrak p}.
     \]
     Include the prescribed real-sign rows.
3. On success output
   \[
   b=\prod_{j=1}^k\kappa_j^{x_j}.
   \]

Rows for the added primes would be zero equations, so omitting them changes nothing.

The lemma proves success by
\[
X\le 2(C\Lambda)^8.
\]
Doubling avoids needing a numerical value for \(C\) in the implementation.

Once \(b\) is obtained, invoke the two stipulated ternary solvers and check their expanded witnesses exactly. In Algorithm 1’s nontrivial branch, the normalizing coordinates are nonzero because the corresponding binary subforms are anisotropic.

## 5. Cost accounting

This is a polynomial-bit **gluing reduction**, with the squareclass and ternary backends charged separately as stipulated.

### Support and prime search

There are at most \(N X\) prime ideals above rational primes \(p\le X\). Thus the strategy makes
\[
O(N\Lambda^8)
\]
individual support additions, or \(O(\log\Lambda)\) batches.

Every added prime has
\[
\log N\mathfrak p=O(\log\Lambda).
\]
Its ideal representation, and the complete enlarged support, have polynomial bit length.

There is **no prime oracle or assumed prime-sampling distribution**. Sieve/enumeration, prime-ideal decomposition, and both residue square tests are paid. Enumeration is polynomial because \(X\) itself—not merely \(\log X\)—is polynomial in the charged parameters.

### Ray class and field degree

No ray class group, class group, class field, or principal generator is constructed. They occur only in the completeness proof.

The analytic fields have absolute degree at most \(8N\), even when \(K/\mathbf Q\) is nonnormal. No absolute Galois closure is used. Their logarithmic discriminants are bounded explicitly above.

### Signs and dyadic arithmetic

No sign pattern or dyadic residue pattern is sampled by rejection.

* At most \(N\) real-sign rows are evaluated.
* Dyadic Hilbert symbols use polynomial-time local arithmetic, not enumeration of all units modulo \(\mathfrak p^{2e+1}\).
* The dyadic precision parameter \(2e+1\) is \(O(N)\); additional valuation and coordinate-conversion precision is charged to the expanded inputs.
* Exact real-sign computation uses polynomial-bit algebraic isolation precision.

Thus no concealed \(2^{-r_1}\) sign loss or exponentially small dyadic acceptance probability occurs.

### Basis, factoring, and output

There are \(O(\log\Lambda)\) complete-basis calls on polynomial-size supports. The matrix has at most
\[
2|S|+N
\]
rows. Under the stipulated polynomial expanded-output guarantee, multiplying the selected basis representatives produces a polynomial-bit \(b\).

All numerator and denominator norm factorizations needed for support construction or subsequent verification are on polynomial-bit integers and remain explicitly charged. Factoring only the original discriminant is not sufficient.

This construction uses explicit classical data; it assumes no quantum state preparation or compact-output interface. Composing the charged factorization calls with quantum factoring would not establish a speedup beyond that resource.

## 6. What remains unconditional, and what the shortcuts actually fail to prove

### Unconditional termination

Without GRH, the doubling algorithm still terminates: the unknown \(b_0\) has finite odd support, and every prime in that support splits in \(M\). Eventually the enumeration includes it.

What is not proved unconditionally here is a polynomial cutoff for the relevant split-prime span, or a different polynomial-time procedure producing a sufficient support. The lemma’s uniform small-generator statement is a sufficient condition for this method, not a proved necessary condition for every possible construction.

### Appendix A’s bit-complexity inference

If \(S\) contains a prime of norm \(P\), the inverse product in Appendix A can already contain a factor \(P\). That is exponential in \(\log P\). This invalidates the claimed polynomial-bit inference; it does **not** prove that Algorithm 1 is slow.

### Exact counterexample to local-only approximation and rank-only clocks

Over \(\mathbf Q\), take
\[
q=\langle1,1,-3,-7\rangle,\qquad S=\{2,3,7\}.
\]
It is isotropic at \((1,3,1,1)\), with common value \(b_0=10\).

The original \(S\)-singular system is not solvable:

* positivity and the sum-of-two-squares conditions at \(3,7\) leave only the squareclasses \(b=1,2\);
* at \(7\),
  \[
  (-21,b)_7=1\quad(b=1,2),\qquad (3,7)_7=-1.
  \]

Nevertheless, \(5\) splits in both \(\mathbf Q(i)\) and \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-21})\), and adding \(5\) permits \(b=10\).

For weak approximation, put
\[
t=1177=11\cdot107,\qquad b=10t.
\]
Since \(t\equiv1\pmod{168}\) and \(t>0\), \(b\) has the same local squareclasses as \(10\) at \(2,3,7,\infty\). But \(v_{11}(b)=1\), so
\[
(-1,b)_{11}=-1.
\]
The first ternary form is not globally isotropic. Matching the original local data did not control new support.

For rank-only reasoning, append arbitrarily many primes \(\ell\equiv1\pmod{168}\). Each contributes a zero local column at the original places, and the system remains unsolvable. Hence a bound on matrix rank alone does not bound the number of arbitrary support additions.

These examples falsify those shortcuts, not a polynomial algorithm or a complexity separation.

---

**Conclusion:** The auxiliary-prime/local-gluing gate is conditionally closed by a uniform, degree-polynomial small-splitting-support construction under the stated quadratic-Hecke GRH. The squareclass backend, expanded-output guarantees, and ternary backend remain assumptions; their growing-tower recursion is not solved by this result.
