## 1. Cheapest falsifiers—and a genuine reconstruction obstruction

Interpret the branch-orbit bound as
\[
\left|\bigcup_{\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbf Q}/\mathbf Q)}\sigma(B)\right|\le r.
\]
This is the interpretation compatible with the source’s genus estimate. Merely bounding the number of conjugate **sets** would not bound \(|B|\).

Twists do **not** disprove a bound for stable Faltings height or the stable Arakelov invariants: they are geometrically isomorphic. Likewise, changing coordinates on the target \(\mathbf P^1\) is not an intrinsic counterexample.

There is, however, an actual obstruction to bounding divisor-support heights **in the supplied model** using only \(d\) and \(h_B\).

### Counterfamily for model-independent reconstruction height

For positive squarefree integers \(a\), consider
\[
E_a:\quad y^2=a\,x(x-1)(x-2),\qquad f_a=x.
\]
These are connected degree-two covers with
\[
B=\{0,1,2,\infty\},\qquad h_B=\log 2.
\]
Over \(\overline{\mathbf Q}\), \(y\mapsto y/\sqrt a\) identifies every \(E_a\) with \(E_1\). Thus their stable Faltings heights and delta invariants are constant.

For any affine point \(Q=(x,y)\) outside the branch points,
\[
a=\frac{y^2}{x(x-1)(x-2)}.
\]
Elementary logarithmic-height inequalities give
\[
\log a\le 2h(y)+3h(x)+3\log 2
       \le 5h([x:y:1])+3\log 2.                 \tag{1}
\]

Let \(u\ne0\) be a class in \(E_a[3]\). A degree-zero divisor supported entirely on the three finite branch points and the origin represents a class in \(E_a[2]\), hence cannot represent \(u\). Therefore **every** divisor representing \(u\) contains a point satisfying
\[
h(Q)\ge \frac{\log a-3\log2}{5}.                \tag{2}
\]

Consequently, the source-style assertion that every support point of a chosen torsion representative has height bounded solely by \(\operatorname{poly}_r(d,1+h_B)\) is false in arbitrary supplied coordinates—even for \(d=2\), fixed branch locus, and fixed torsion order.

**Scope of this counterfamily:** it does not refute the intrinsic Arakelov bound, nor polynomial time in the full input length. Here the input model itself has height \(\Theta(\log a)\). One may normalize the model, but constructing that normalization and retaining its arithmetic descent data costs \(\Theta(\log a)\) bits. This is not branch-coordinate rescaling: the branch coordinate \(x\) stays unchanged.

It also pinpoints the issue in the last step of surface Theorem 4.5: a bound on \(h(f(Q))\) does not yield a bound on the supplied-coordinate height \(h(Q)\) from the degree of \(f\) alone. Model/inverse-map height data must be paid.

## 2. What the supplied Arakelov theorem actually certifies

Restrict to genus \(g\ge1\), as in the quoted Arakelov theorems. Riemann–Hurwitz gives \(g=O_r(d)\).

Writing
\[
\kappa_r=45r^3 2^{r-2}r!,
\]
Theorem 6.0.4, with \(X=\mathbf P^1\), yields bounds of the form
\[
\max\{0,h_{\rm Fal}(Y),|\delta_{\rm Fal}(Y)|\}
 \le C_r d^7 \exp(\kappa_r h_B).                \tag{3}
\]
The power \(7\) absorbs the displayed \(g^2d^5\) term. Theorem 6.0.6 instead packages the branch dependence into
\[
d^{\,O_r(1+h_B)}.                              \tag{4}
\]

Put \(n=\lceil\log_2p\rceil\). Under the stated promises
\[
d=\ell^{O(1)},\quad \ell=O(n),\quad h_B=O(n),
\]
the available certificates are respectively
\[
n^{O(1)}e^{O_r(n)}
\quad\text{and}\quad
e^{O_r(n\log n)}.
\]
Neither is polynomial in \(n\).

This is a failure of the **available uniform upper-bound certificate**, not a lower bound on the actual heights or runtime. For fixed characteristic-zero \(X\), the branch data are constant, so this objection does not contradict the stated fixed-\(X\) theorem.

### A proved special case

If the actual branch set has at most three points, a Möbius transformation puts it inside \(\{0,1,\infty\}\). Then \(\deg_B(Y)\le d\), and supplied Theorem 1.1.1 gives
\[
\max\{0,h_{\rm Fal}(Y),|\delta_{\rm Fal}(Y)|\}=O(d^7),
\]
independently of the original branch coordinates.

This is a genuine complete subcase, but not a result for the moving, larger branch configurations needed for general quintic surfaces.

## 3. A useful complete reduction: branch arithmetic is logarithmic

There is an elementary normalization lemma that removes one possible source of exponential loss.

Choose three branch points and normalize them to \(0,1,\infty\). Each remaining coordinate is a cross-ratio \(c_i\), satisfying
\[
h(c_i)\le 4h_B+\log4.                           \tag{5}
\]
The normalization lies in the field generated by all conjugate branch points, whose degree is at most \(r!\): its Galois group acts faithfully on that finite set.

Let \(\Gamma\) denote the finite normalized branch coordinates, including \(0,1\). Over a number field \(K\) containing them, define
\[
\Lambda(B)=1+\frac1{[K:\mathbf Q]}\sum_v n_v
\left[
 \sum_{c\in\Gamma}\log^+|c|_v+
 \sum_{\{c,c'\}\subset\Gamma}\log^+|(c-c')^{-1}|_v
\right].
\]
By the product formula,
\[
\Lambda(B)
=1+\sum_{c\in\Gamma}h(c)+
       \sum_{\{c,c'\}\subset\Gamma}h(c-c').
\]
Using (5) and \(h(c-c')\le h(c)+h(c')+\log2\), one obtains, for example,
\[
\boxed{\Lambda(B)\le 8r^2(1+h_B).}              \tag{6}
\]

Thus averaged logarithmic branch sizes and logarithmic collision depths are already polynomially controlled, with explicit dependence. Only the branch-coordinate field—not the cover’s field of definition—is controlled by the \(r!\) bound.

The missing geometric estimate must depend polynomially on these **logarithmic** collision depths. An estimate involving inverse separation itself, or \(\exp(c\Lambda)\), reproduces the unwanted exponential height dependence.

### Precise unresolved intrinsic comparison

A sufficient direct-branch replacement for the Belyi step is:

> **Logarithmic branch–Arakelov comparison \(\mathsf A_r\).**  
> Produce explicit, effectively computable constants \(C_r,a_r,b_r\) such that every connected degree-\(d\) cover under the branch-orbit promise satisfies
> \[
> \max\{0,h_{\rm Fal}(Y),|\delta_{\rm Fal}(Y)|\}
> \le C_r d^{a_r}\bigl(1+\log d+\Lambda(B)\bigr)^{b_r}.       \tag{7}
> \]
> All constants must be uniform over the cover and moving branch configuration.

Equation (6) would immediately turn (7) into the requested intrinsic polynomial bound. Neither the supplied fixed-branch theorem nor the alternative mentioned in Remark 6.0.5 proves (7): the latter retains exponential dependence on degree.

I do **not** derive (7) here, and the excerpts do not establish it. A decisive intrinsic counterfamily would have to violate every fixed polynomial in \(d\) and \(1+h_B\); twists and target-coordinate changes do not do that.

## 4. Exactly what is additionally needed for reconstruction

Even granting (7), one has not completed the surface height gate.

### A degree bound can be proved separately

Suppose \(E\subset\operatorname{Jac}(Y)[\ell]\) is Galois-stable of dimension \(s\). It becomes rational over an extension \(L/K\) with
\[
[L:K]\le |\mathrm{GL}_s(\mathbf F_\ell)|<\ell^{s^2}.
\]
Choose a point \(Q\) in a nonbranch fiber of \(Y\to\mathbf P^1\). After another extension of degree at most \(d\), \(Q\) is rational.

Over the resulting field \(M\), a class \(x\in E\) is represented by a degree-zero line bundle. Riemann–Roch gives an effective divisor \(D_x\) of degree \(g\) such that
\[
x=[D_x-gQ].
\]
Each support point has degree at most \(g\) over \(M\), hence at most
\[
gd\,\ell^{s^2}
\]
over \(K\).

This repairs the bare inference “the class has polynomial field degree, therefore suitable support points do.” It does **not** bound their heights, construct small sections efficiently, or justify forming a common splitting field of all supports. Separate existence of low-degree and low-height representatives does not establish a representative satisfying both bounds simultaneously.

### Required additional source assumption

The following is a precise sufficient reconstruction obligation, rather than an assumption of a supplied small Jacobian embedding.

> **Uniform constructive compact reconstruction \(\mathsf R_{r,s}\).**  
> Input: explicit equations and cover/action maps for \(Y\), an explicitly encoded number field \(K\), \(\ell\), and the isolated reduction of the Galois-stable rank-\(s\) subspace \(E\) at a good auxiliary prime.  
> Let \(\mathcal S\) charge the complete arithmetic encoding, including field degrees, coefficient lengths, basis/discriminant data, and modular input. Put
> \[
> T=d+\ell^s+\mathcal S+1+h_B.
> \]
> From these inputs—not from characteristic-zero torsion advice—construct:
> 1. compact Riemann–Roch/Khuri–Makdisi data and evaluation recipes of polynomial encoding size;
> 2. the representatives needed during lifting, with simultaneously polynomial field-degree and logarithmic-height bounds, or an equivalent compact representation avoiding explicit supports;
> 3. a \(K\)-rational evaluator
>    \[
>    \alpha:\operatorname{Jac}(Y)\dashrightarrow\mathbf A^1
>    \]
>    finite and injective on \(E\), with polynomial-time evaluation at the required precision and an explicit bound
>    \[
>    h(\alpha(x))\le C_{r,s}T^A\qquad(x\in E).
>    \]
> Construction, denominator losses, separation tests, and necessary field extensions must all have polynomial bit cost.

The model-size term is necessary by the counterfamily in §1. Alternatively, the lemma may construct a normalized low-height model, but must charge the normalization and descent maps.

This is the additional source assumption needed at Theorem 4.5 and Lemma 6.7, Step 6, together with the separating-map step in Theorem 4.4.

Why stable Faltings height alone does not supply it:

* The displayed theta estimate becomes polynomial once \(g\) and the required Arakelov quantities are controlled. That is only one component.
* Theorem 4.5 also uses Green-function bounds, the integral
  \[
  \int_Y\log(1+|f|^2)\,\mu_Y,
  \]
  intersection estimates, and conversion back to an explicit model. Their uniform constants and effective choices must be supplied.
* The Green-function statement must identify the finite one-sided or regularized quantity used. An absolute supremum of a logarithmic Green kernel across its diagonal is not finite.
* Theorem B.4 states polynomial dependence only in its last three parameters. It does not state polynomial dependence in growing genus or embedding dimension. B.3 already contains exponential-in-\(g\) factors.
* A full \(4\Theta\) embedding has \(4^g\) coordinates. It cannot silently serve as polynomial-size data when \(g=\operatorname{poly}(\ell)\).

Accordingly, the needed source strengthening is a **uniform effective version of the cited CE inequalities and Abel/divisor construction for the actual compact choices made by the algorithm**, or a direct proof of \(\mathsf R_{r,s}\) that bypasses them. Merely proving \(h_{\rm Fal}(Y)=\operatorname{poly}(d,h_B)\) is insufficient.

## 5. Proof/resource ledger and conditional completion

Let \(n=\lceil\log_2p\rceil\).

| Item | Charged bound or obligation | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Native input | A homogeneous quintic in four variables has \(56\) coefficients; reading all coefficients and \(p\) costs \(O(n)\) bits | Paid |
| Characteristic-zero lift | Lift coefficients to \(0,\ldots,p-1\); height \(O(n)\). Smooth reduction gives a smooth generic fiber | No characteristic-zero advice needed |
| Branch data | Fixed orbit bound and \(h_B=O(n)\) are the stated premises; computing the pencil/discriminant data must also be charged | Premise/construction obligation |
| Cover | \(d=\ell^{O(1)}\), \(g=O_r(d)\) | Geometric bounds; explicit model bit-size still needs proof |
| Relevant torsion | \(s=O(1)\), hence \(|E|=\ell^s\); do not enumerate all \(\ell^{2g}\) Jacobian torsion points | Uses the advertised fixed-rank construction |
| Heights | Current certificate is exponential in \(h_B\); \(\mathsf A_r\) is missing | Not uniformly certified |
| Compact reconstruction | Must satisfy \(\mathsf R_{r,s}\), without a free embedding or torsion representative | Missing |
| Semistable extension | May be used analytically with normalized inequalities; an exponential-degree extension may not be constructed for free | Distinction required |

In particular, stable height does not pay for the primitive-element, normalization, and explicit cover-model phases. Their equation lengths and bit complexity must themselves be polynomial in \((\ell,n)\). An \(\exp(d)\) naive-height bound is not adequate.

### Explicit precision accounting

For clarity take \(K=\mathbf Q\). Let \(N=|E|=\ell^s\), and suppose \(\mathsf R_{r,s}\) gives \(h(\alpha(x))\le H_\alpha\). Then
\[
F_E(Z)=\prod_{x\in E}(Z-\alpha(x))\in\mathbf Q[Z],
\]
and the elementary coefficient-height bound gives
\[
h(F_E)\le N(H_\alpha+\log2).                    \tag{8}
\]
Thus a logarithmic numerator/denominator bound is polynomial when \(H_\alpha\) is polynomial.

After accounting for denominators and valuation losses, rational reconstruction is certified by precision satisfying
\[
e\log P>2h(F_E)+\log2.                          \tag{9}
\]
The charged precision is \(e\log P\), multiplied by the relevant field degrees and arithmetic costs—not merely the symbol \(e\). Over a number field, its basis, degree, and denominator costs must likewise remain polynomial.

Equations (8)–(9) show exactly why a constructive scalar-evaluation height bound suffices for the Mascot reconstruction stage.

### Auxiliary prime

The source’s auxiliary counting cost depends on the **numerical value** of \(P\). Hence \(P\) must be polynomial in \((\ell,n)\), not just have polynomial bit length.

A sufficient certificate is a computable nonzero bad-reduction integer \(\mathfrak D\) with polynomial logarithmic size. A prime avoiding \(\mathfrak D\ell p\) then exists below
\[
O\!\left(\log|\mathfrak D|+\log\ell+n+1\right)
\]
by the elementary prime-product bound. It can be found by scanning primes and testing divisibility; factoring \(\mathfrak D\) is unnecessary. Producing \(\mathfrak D\) and proving it covers the required bad primes remain charged obligations.

A fixed-\(X\) assertion “\(P=O(\ell)\)” or “for sufficiently large primes” is not, by itself, a uniform statement for changing lifts \(X\).

### Exact native output

A smooth quintic surface has \(b_2=53\). Consequently \(P_2\) has \(54\) integer coefficients, with
\[
|[T^k]P_2|\le \binom{53}{k}p^k.
\]
Its complete binary output has \(O(n)\) bits. A CRT modulus exceeding \(2^{54}p^{53}\) suffices, obtainable using primes \(\ell=O(n)\), subject to uniformly controlled exclusions.

Therefore, **conditional on** \(\mathsf A_r\), \(\mathsf R_{r,s}\), polynomial-size cover construction, and uniform treatment of exceptional primes, the advertised arithmetic workflow has the required polynomial resource scaling. None of these costs may be hidden as characteristic-zero preprocessing.

No quantum state-preparation oracle or coefficient-access oracle has been credited here. The quantum construction’s preparation and execution costs are not supplied in these excerpts and are not independently audited.

## 6. Scoped disposition

* **Intrinsic sharper Arakelov inequality:** not established by the supplied results and not refuted here. Equation (7) states the precise missing logarithmic-degeneration comparison.
* **Divisor-height bound solely in \(d,h_B\), in arbitrary supplied coordinates:** false, by the infinite family \(E_a\). A model-height term or a constructively paid normalization/descent statement is indispensable.
* **End-to-end native classical bound:** not certified. Beyond intrinsic heights, the exact missing source assumption is uniform constructive compact reconstruction \(\mathsf R_{r,s}\), together with polynomial-size construction of its input data.
* **Comparison with the stated paid quantum algorithm:** no classical match has been established by this audit, but neither hardness nor a quantum speedup follows from the gap.

No tools, network access, interpreter execution, or finite experimental controls were used. The counterfamily and partial lemmas above are mathematical deductions; the uniform comparisons are explicitly identified proof obligations.
