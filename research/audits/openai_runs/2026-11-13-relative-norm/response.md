## Result

Under the stated one-prime Galois \(2\)-extension hypotheses, the sequence **is exact**:
\[
0\longrightarrow F(S,2)/\langle[d]\rangle
\longrightarrow L(S,2)
\xrightarrow{\,N_{L/F}\,}
\bigl(F(S,2)\cap N_{L/F}L^\times\bigr)
\longrightarrow0.
\]

The computational qualification is substantial. A relative conic point has a **fractional ideal content**, not generally an element gcd. Its numerator’s outside-\(S\) parity is exactly that content’s parity. Odd class order makes the required correction exist, but does not compute it.

The precise extra operation is to produce
\[
\boxed{\quad \mathfrak c=(b)\mathfrak h^2
       \quad\text{in }\mathcal O_{F,S},\quad}
\]
including the element \(b\), not merely an abstract square root of \([\mathfrak c]\). Dividing the conic numerator by \(b\) then gives the desired lift. No polynomial field-arithmetic/factoring implementation of this operation in growing degree follows from oddness alone.

## 1. First falsifier: elementwise primitive is not ideal-primitive

A genuinely ideal-primitive point does satisfy the appropriate relative numerator lemma. The false step is assuming that clearing denominators and removing common **element divisors** produces such a point.

Here is a symbolic counterexample inside the promised family.

Set
\[
F=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{229}),\qquad
t=\frac{1+\sqrt{229}}2,\qquad t^2=t+57,
\]
and
\[
d=-107-15t=-\frac{229+15\sqrt{229}}2,\qquad
L=F(\delta),\quad \delta^2=d.
\]
Take \(S_{\mathbf Q}=\{2,229\}\).

These fields satisfy the required ramification and Galois promises:

* \(\mathcal O_F=\mathbf Z[t]\) and \(\operatorname{disc}(F)=229\).
* \(N_{F/\mathbf Q}(d)=229\). The nontrivial automorphism of \(F/\mathbf Q\) extends by
  \[
  \delta\longmapsto \frac{\sqrt{229}}{\delta}.
  \]
  Its square sends \(\delta\) to \(-\delta\), so \(L/\mathbf Q\) is cyclic of degree four.
* Since
  \[
  \frac{t^2-d}{4}=41+4t,
  \]
  the element \((t+\delta)/2\) satisfies
  \[
  u^2-tu+41+4t=0,
  \]
  whose relative discriminant is \(d\). Thus \(L/F\), and hence \(L/\mathbf Q\), is unramified outside \(229\), including at \(2\).

Consider
\[
\mathfrak q=(3,t),\qquad \mathfrak q'=(3,t-1),\qquad
\rho=6-t.
\]
Then
\[
N(\rho)=-27,\qquad (\rho)=\mathfrak q^3.
\]
The ideal \(\mathfrak q\) is not principal. Here is a short proof, without a class-number computation. The unit
\[
\varepsilon=t+7=\frac{15+\sqrt{229}}2
\]
has norm \(-1\). If an algebraic integer \(\gamma\) had absolute norm \(3\), multiplication by a power of \(\varepsilon\) would arrange
\[
|\gamma|,\ |\gamma'|\leq \sqrt{3\varepsilon}.
\]
Writing \(\gamma=a+bt\), this gives
\[
|b|
=\frac{|\gamma-\gamma'|}{\sqrt{229}}
\leq \frac{2\sqrt{3\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{229}}<1,
\]
because \(12\varepsilon<229\). Hence \(b=0\), contradicting \(|N(\gamma)|=3\).

Moreover, \(2\) is inert and the prime above \(229\) is generated by \(\sqrt{229}\). Both inverted prime ideals are principal, so \(\mathfrak q\) remains nonprincipal in \(\mathcal O_{F,S}\).

Now take
\[
X=198+29t,\qquad Y=18,\qquad Z=-228-35t.
\]
The identities
\[
\frac{81}{\rho}=-3(t+5),\qquad d\rho=213+32t
\]
give
\[
X=\frac{81}{\rho}+d\rho,\qquad
Z=\frac{81}{\rho}-d\rho.
\]
Consequently
\[
X^2-dY^2=Z^2,\qquad
\beta=X+Y\delta=\frac{(9+\rho\delta)^2}{\rho}.
\]

Nevertheless,
\[
(X,Y,Z)=\mathfrak q.
\]
Indeed, \(Y=18\) restricts common prime divisors to primes above \(2\) and \(3\). At \(2\), \(X\bmod2=t\neq0\); at \(\mathfrak q'\), \(X\) is a unit; and at \(\mathfrak q\),
\[
v_{\mathfrak q}(X)=v_{\mathfrak q}(Z)=1,\qquad
v_{\mathfrak q}(Y)=2.
\]

Thus the three integral coordinates have **no common nonunit element divisor**: such a divisor would generate a proper principal ideal containing the maximal ideal \(\mathfrak q\), forcing \(\mathfrak q\) to be principal. Their integer coefficient gcd is also \(1\).

But \(d\) is a unit at \(\mathfrak q\), and \(L/F\) is unramified there. Therefore, for every \(\mathfrak P\mid\mathfrak q\),
\[
v_{\mathfrak P}(\beta)=1.
\]
So
\[
[\beta]\notin L(S,2),
\]
despite the elementwise primitivity and square norm.

This is not confined to the trivial norm target: replacing
\[
(X,Y,Z,a) \quad\text{by}\quad (dY,X,Z,-d)
\]
gives numerator \(\delta\beta\), the same bad outside-\(S\) parities, and the nontrivial norm squareclass \([-d]\).

Two qualifications matter:

* Multiplying by the rational ideal norm \(3\) does not repair the example: it toggles parity at both \(\mathfrak q\) and \(\mathfrak q'\).
* This particular example is easy to repair:
  \[
  \frac{\beta}{\rho}
  =\left(\frac{9+\rho\delta}{\rho}\right)^2.
  \]
  It falsifies free gcd normalization, **not** the existence of a lift or the possibility of an efficient algorithm.

## 2. Exact sequence: maps and complete kernel

Write
\[
R=\mathcal O_{F,S_F},\qquad T=\mathcal O_{L,S_L},
\]
where the two sets consist of all primes above the specified rational set. Define
\[
U=F(S_F,2),\qquad W=L(S_L,2),
\]
and
\[
V=\{[a]\in U:a\in N_{L/F}L^\times\}.
\]
This last condition is independent of the representative because every \(F\)-square is a relative norm.

### Radicand valuations

Every prime \(\mathfrak p\notin S_F\) is unramified in \(L/F\). Since
\[
2v_{\mathfrak P}(\delta)
=e(\mathfrak P/\mathfrak p)v_{\mathfrak p}(d),
\]
we have \(v_{\mathfrak p}(d)\) even. Thus \([d]\in U\).

In particular, if an integral radicand is already ideal-squarefree outside \(S_F\), its outside-\(S_F\) valuations must all be zero. **Producing** such a global representative is not automatically free; this is addressed below.

### Inclusion and norm

Inclusion sends \(U\) into \(W\). Its kernel is exactly \(\langle[d]\rangle\): if
\[
q=(x+y\delta)^2\in F^\times,
\]
then \(2xy=0\), so \(q\) is either an \(F\)-square or \(d\) times one.

For \(\alpha\in W\),
\[
v_{\mathfrak p}(N\alpha)
=\sum_{\mathfrak P\mid\mathfrak p}
 f(\mathfrak P/\mathfrak p)v_{\mathfrak P}(\alpha)
\]
is even outside \(S_F\). Hence norm maps \(W\) into \(V\).

### Complete norm kernel

Suppose \(N\alpha=c^2\) with \(c\in F^\times\). If \(\alpha=-c\), its class already comes from \(F\). Otherwise put
\[
q=\operatorname{Tr}_{L/F}(\alpha)+2c.
\]
Then \(q\neq0\) and
\[
(\alpha+c)^2=q\alpha.
\]
Thus \([\alpha]=[q]\) in \(L^\times/L^{\times2}\).

For \(\mathfrak P\notin S_L\), unramifiedness gives
\[
v_{\mathfrak p}(q)+v_{\mathfrak P}(\alpha)
=2v_{\mathfrak P}(\alpha+c).
\]
Therefore \(v_{\mathfrak p}(q)\) is even and \([q]\in U\). The special case \(\alpha=-c\) gives the same conclusion directly.

So the kernel is exactly \(U/\langle[d]\rangle\). There is no additional sign, narrow-class, torsion, or Pell term.

## 3. The correct relative numerator lemma

Take \([a]\in V\) and any nonzero relative conic point
\[
X^2-dY^2=aZ^2,\qquad X,Y,Z\in F.
\]
Necessarily \(Z\neq0\). Set
\[
\beta=X+Y\delta,\qquad N\beta=aZ^2.
\]

Neither the coordinates nor \(a,d\) need be integral \(S\)-units.

### Weighted fractional content

Define fractional \(R\)-ideals
\[
\mathfrak A=\prod_{\mathfrak p\notin S_F}
 \mathfrak p^{v_{\mathfrak p}(a)/2},
\qquad
\mathfrak D=\prod_{\mathfrak p\notin S_F}
 \mathfrak p^{v_{\mathfrak p}(d)/2}.
\]
Thus
\[
\mathfrak A^2=(a)R,\qquad \mathfrak D^2=(d)R.
\]
Define
\[
\boxed{\quad
\mathfrak c=(X)R+(Y)\mathfrak D+(Z)\mathfrak A.
\quad}
\]

This weighted content avoids silently principalizing \(\mathfrak A\) or \(\mathfrak D\). If \(a,d\) are already \(S\)-units, it is simply \((X,Y,Z)R\).

**Content-parity lemma.** There is an integral \(T\)-ideal \(\mathfrak B\) such that
\[
(\beta)T=(\mathfrak cT)\mathfrak B^2,
\]
and
\[
N_{L/F}\mathfrak B
=\mathfrak A(Z)R\,\mathfrak c^{-1}.
\]

**Proof.** Fix \(\mathfrak p\notin S_F\), and write
\[
v_{\mathfrak p}(d)=2k,\qquad
v_{\mathfrak p}(a)=2\ell,
\]
\[
m=v_{\mathfrak p}(\mathfrak c)
=\min\{v_{\mathfrak p}(X),\,
       v_{\mathfrak p}(Y)+k,\,
       v_{\mathfrak p}(Z)+\ell\}.
\]
Using a local uniformizer, normalize these three weighted coordinates to an integral primitive triple. The normalized coefficients \(a,d\) are units.

The normalized \(X,Y\) cannot both vanish modulo \(\mathfrak p\): the equation would then force normalized \(Z\) to vanish too.

* If \(\mathfrak p\) is inert, the residue-field independence of \(1,\sqrt d\) gives
  \[
  v_{\mathfrak P}(\beta)=m.
  \]
* If \(\mathfrak p\) splits, both conjugate numerators cannot have valuation greater than \(m\), because \(2\) and the normalized \(d\) are units. Their minimum valuation is \(m\), while their sum is
  \[
  2\ell+2v_{\mathfrak p}(Z).
  \]
  Hence both valuations are congruent to \(m\pmod2\), and neither is below \(m\).

This proves the ideal-square factorization. Taking ideal norms gives
\[
(a)(Z)^2
=\mathfrak c^2(N\mathfrak B)^2
=\mathfrak A^2(Z)^2.
\]
Uniqueness of ideal valuations yields the displayed formula for \(N\mathfrak B\). ∎

Thus the exact numerator criterion is
\[
[\beta]\in W
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
v_{\mathfrak p}(\mathfrak c)\text{ is even for every }\mathfrak p\notin S_F.
\]
Unit content is sufficient. Elementwise primitivity is not.

### Odd class order completes the existence proof

By the supplied one-prime theorem, \(\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal O_F)\), and hence its quotient \(\mathrm{Cl}(R)\), has odd order. Therefore multiplication by \(2\) is bijective on \(\mathrm{Cl}(R)\).

Choose a fractional ideal \(\mathfrak h\) with
\[
2[\mathfrak h]=[\mathfrak c].
\]
There exists \(b\in F^\times\) satisfying
\[
\mathfrak c=(b)\mathfrak h^2.
\]
Then
\[
\gamma=\frac{\beta}{b}
\]
satisfies
\[
(\gamma)T=(\mathfrak hT\,\mathfrak B)^2,
\qquad
N\gamma=a\left(\frac Zb\right)^2.
\]
Thus \([\gamma]\in W\) lifts \([a]\).

This proves surjectivity and the stated short exact sequence. In particular,
\[
\dim_{\mathbf F_2}W
=\dim_{\mathbf F_2}U-1+\dim_{\mathbf F_2}V.
\]

Only oddness of the **base** \(S\)-class group was needed here. No principalization in \(L\), and no conversion of \(\gamma\) into an actual \(S\)-unit, is required.

## 4. The exact missing effective operation

The necessary interface is:

> **Principalization modulo ideal squares.**  
> Input: an explicitly represented fractional \(R\)-ideal \(\mathfrak c\).  
> Output: \(b\in F^\times\) and a fractional \(R\)-ideal \(\mathfrak h\), with an exact certificate
> \[
> \mathfrak c=(b)\mathfrak h^2.
> \]
> For this application, it need only handle contents produced by the conic calls.

An abstract class-square root is insufficient: the principal relation’s **generator \(b\)** is what changes the numerator.

This operation precisely captures the algebraic obstruction. Without assuming odd class order, the same construction gives a well-defined map
\[
\partial:V\longrightarrow \mathrm{Cl}(R)/2\mathrm{Cl}(R),
\qquad
[a]\longmapsto[\mathfrak c]\bmod2,
\]
with
\[
\ker\partial=N(W).
\]

To check independence of the chosen conic point, take two norm preimages. Their quotient has square norm, so the full-field kernel identity above says that its squareclass comes from \(F^\times\). Their parity-content ideals therefore differ by a principal ideal times an ideal square.

Consequently one has, more generally, exactness through \(V\) in
\[
0\to U/\langle[d]\rangle\to W\to V
\xrightarrow{\partial}\mathrm{Cl}(R)/2\mathrm{Cl}(R).
\]
No surjectivity onto the last group is asserted.

Here the obstruction group vanishes. **Its vanishing is not an algorithm for producing its witnesses.**

For example, if \(m\) is the odd order of \([\mathfrak c]\) and
\[
\mathfrak c^m=(z),
\]
then
\[
\mathfrak c=(z)\left(\mathfrak c^{(1-m)/2}\right)^2.
\]
But neither \(m\) nor \(z\) is supplied. Even if \(m\) were known in binary, expanding a nonunit integral ideal power \(\mathfrak c^m\) has an output-size bill proportional to \(m\log N\mathfrak c\). Repeated squaring does not remove that bill.

Likewise, the promise that \(\mathfrak D\) is principal—because \(\mathfrak D^2=(d)\) and the class group is odd—does not provide a generator allowing a free global squarefree-radicand normalization. The weighted-content construction deliberately avoids that preprocessing.

## 5. Conditional construction and resource accounting

Assume only the following **conditional conic interface**:

* on a solvable input \(X^2-dY^2=aZ^2\) over an explicitly represented \(F\), return exact coordinates with \(Z\neq0\);
* its search time, output height, factoring requirements, and any internal precision costs remain separately charged.

No relative-conic clock is asserted.

Given such a point and a principalization-modulo-squares interface, the lift is explicit:

1. Verify the conic equation exactly.
2. Form \(\mathfrak A,\mathfrak D,\mathfrak c\).
3. Obtain and verify \(\mathfrak c=(b)\mathfrak h^2\).
4. Output
   \[
   \gamma=(X+Y\sqrt d)/b.
   \]

Fractional ideal sums can form the content without factoring \(Z\). This does **not** compute \(b\). If subsequent operations factor the content or norms of returned coordinates, those are new factoring inputs and must be charged; they are not automatically among the inherited coefficient factorizations.

The arithmetic wrapper is polynomial in the **total encoded input and returned-data lengths**, assuming the requisite field/ideal arithmetic interfaces. That is not a polynomial bound in native input size until bounds are supplied for:

* the conic point;
* \(b\) and the ideal certificate;
* any compact-to-explicit conversion;
* field/tower presentations, embeddings, maximal-order data and prime descriptions not already supplied.

A complete basis follows algebraically: take a complement to \([d]\) in \(U\), together with lifts of a separately obtained basis of \(V\). Applying norm proves independence and spanning, using the complete kernel above.

### A legitimate alternative: enlarged lower-field support

There is a useful, precise reduction—not a free normalization.

Let \(S_F'\) contain \(S_F\) and the support of \(\mathfrak c\). If a complete explicit basis of \(F(S_F',2)\) is already available, consider the parity map
\[
F(S_F',2)\longrightarrow
\mathbf F_2^{\,S_F'\setminus S_F}.
\]
Oddness of \(\mathrm{Cl}(R)\) makes this map surjective. Binary linear algebra therefore produces a product \(b\) of basis representatives whose parity equals that of \(\mathfrak c\). This is exactly the required correction. The final lifted class still belongs to the **original** \(L(S_L,2)\).

This reduction costs polynomial arithmetic in the supplied basis and its explicit output sizes. But obtaining the enlarged-support basis is an additional global task. The old \(F(S_F,2)\) basis alone cannot correct the content: all its elements have zero outside-\(S_F\) parity.

Thus lower-field recursion is algebraically legitimate, including temporary support enlargement. A native polynomial-bit recursion still needs a bound on the resulting calls, support growth and accumulated coordinate heights.

## Conclusion and decisive obligations

**Proved:** the exact sequence, its complete kernel, the weighted-content parity lemma, and existence of every lift under the stated odd-class promise.

**Falsified:** replacing rational gcd normalization by elementwise relative primitivity, or by multiplication by the rational norm of the content ideal.

**Unresolved effective interface:** produce the principal-modulo-square certificate
\[
\mathfrak c=(b)\mathfrak h^2
\]
with a proved native polynomial bit bound using only the claimed resources. Any proposed implementation is falsified by a failed ideal identity, a wrong norm squareclass, or an odd outside-\(S\) valuation in its returned lift.

Tower recursion therefore survives algebraically. An ordinary relative conic solver plus odd class order does **not by itself** establish the classical polynomial match. Conversely, this missing constructive proof is not evidence of hardness or a quantum speedup.
