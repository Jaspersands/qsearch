# One-prime two-class coverage and a ramified-support counterexample

2026-11-13. Independent focused review of the two new algebraic claims in
`audits/openai_runs/2026-11-13-general-two-coverage/response.md`, read in
full. STATE/TRAPS and the current global composition were consulted. No
Git, network, external API, archives, numerical sweep, or shared-note edit.

**Decision:** both claims are correct at their stated scope. The first
removes the class-generator GRH requirement for the specified Galois
2-power branch fields. The second genuinely defeats using only the native
bad-prime support as a general two-primary source. Neither supplies a
general unconditional source or a quantum-advantage result.

## 1. The special-base-Q odd-class-number proof

Let K/Q be finite Galois, with degree a power of 2, and with at most one
finite ramified rational prime p. Let C be its ORDINARY ideal class group.
For proof only, take the maximal ordinary unramified elementary 2-extension
M/K, corresponding under class field theory to C/2C. This extension is
canonical under automorphisms of K; hence M/Q is Galois, with a finite
2-group Gamma. Write H=Gal(M/K). No Hilbert class field is constructed by
the algorithm.

M/Q is unramified away from p at finite primes. Let I be inertia at one
prime of M above p. If I were proper, choose a maximal subgroup N of Gamma
containing I. In a finite 2-group, every maximal subgroup is normal of
index 2. It therefore contains every conjugate of I. The quadratic field
M^N/Q would be unramified at p and at every other finite prime.

That is impossible: every nontrivial quadratic number field has a
fundamental discriminant of absolute value greater than 1. Thus I=Gamma.
Since M/K is unramified, its relative inertia group is `I intersect H=1`.
It follows that H=1, so `C/2C=0` and C has odd order. If no finite prime
ramifies, the same quadratic-quotient argument first gives K=Q.

This also proves that the sole possible ramified finite prime is totally
ramified, with just one prime above it, whenever the extension is
nontrivial. Thus no separate nonsplitting hypothesis is missing in this
special base-Q argument. It would be incorrect to delete that hypothesis
from a more general Iwasawa statement over arbitrary base fields: the
argument here specifically uses the absence of finite-unramified quadratic
extensions of Q. The class-number result is known mathematics, not a
novelty claim. This proof does not rely on the report's cyclic-cubic-163
example, which is not re-audited here.

## 2. Exactly what this pays in the native Selmer composition

Let S contain the primes of K above the native rational set
`P0={p:p divides 2 disc(f)}`. It may contain many primes in addition to the
single prime ramified in K/Q: cross-resultants between distinct branch
factors can enlarge P0. This causes no difficulty. Since C is odd, its
quotient by the classes of S is odd as well.

The natural map is an isomorphism

    O_(K,S)^*/O_(K,S)^{*2}  ~=  K(S,2).

Injection is immediate: a field square root of an S-unit is an S-unit.
For surjection, if alpha has even valuations outside S, let a be the ideal
with half those outside-S valuations. Then `2[a]` lies in the subgroup
J generated by S. The quotient C/J is odd, so `[a]` itself lies in J.
Choose an S-supported b in its class and `(beta)=a b^-1`; then
`alpha/beta^2` is an S-unit. The witnesses a,b,beta are used only to prove
completeness. They are not uncharged computed generators.

Consequently the source is literally `T=S`, with no extra-prime parity
rows for this factor and no randomized class-generator coverage step.
This does NOT claim that the full class group is trivial or generated by
S. It also does NOT compute a complete S-unit basis for free.

The remaining operation is exactly the already reviewed complete compact
S-unit computation, including the torsion squareclass bit. The retained
actual Biasse--Song Theorem 1 has polynomial dependence on degree,
log discriminant, |S| and the largest logarithmic prime norm, without a
GRH premise. Its separate class-group-generation application is where
GRH entered; see the retained `global-primary.md` source packet, physical
lines 28--31 and 191--196. The native maximal-order, compact evaluation,
norm/sign, real-image and all-prime local-image interfaces remain the same
ones checked in the general composition.

These costs stay native: factor the original polynomial and discriminant,
compute its orders and prime decompositions, and take all primes over P0.
There are at most `deg(K)*|P0|` of them, and
`log Norm(P)<=deg(K)*log p`. Both are polynomial in native input length;
there is no enumeration up to the numerical value of a large p. The
complete unit call pays arbitrary additional S-primes, not just the one
ramified prime.

Therefore, on every native odd-degree monic squarefree input whose branch
fields are all of the following types, the reviewed global quantum
Selmer-dimension composition is unconditional:

* Q, handled directly;
* Galois 2-power extensions of Q with at most one finite ramified prime,
  handled with T=S;
* the cubic fields covered by the separately retained unconditional cubic
  source, using THAT source to choose T, which need not equal S.

The field properties restrict the correctness domain; no class groups,
units, normal closures or field automorphisms are supplied as advice. The
explicit family below has directly recognizable membership. This review
does not add a general family-recognition algorithm or re-prove the cubic
source. The output is the exact Selmer dimension with bounded error
polynomial in native input bits and log(1/eta). Global compact-unit
completeness remains probabilistic, so this is not promoted to an
abort-only global guarantee. No GRH class-generator step remains on this
domain; no general unconditional source is inferred outside it.

## 3. The quadratic native counterexample

Take distinct primes `p,q congruent 1 (mod 8)` with `(p/q)=1`. Quadratic
reciprocity gives `(q/p)=1`. Set

    K=Q(sqrt(pq)),       M=Q(sqrt(p),sqrt(q)).

The three quadratic discriminants are p, q and pq, so the biquadratic
discriminant formula gives

    Delta_K=pq,         Delta_M=p*q*pq=(pq)^2.

The tower discriminant identity therefore gives relative discriminant 1:
M/K is unramified at every finite prime. Both fields are totally real,
and every real place splits, so this is an ORDINARY unramified quadratic
extension, not merely a narrow-class construction. It is nontrivial
because distinct p and q give a degree-four biquadratic field.

The local Frobenius claims hold exactly:

* At p, q is a square in Q_p by its nonzero quadratic residue and Hensel's
  lemma. Thus `K_P=Q_p(sqrt(p))`, and adjoining sqrt(p) already splits the
  relative quadratic algebra. The unique prime above p splits in M/K.
* At q the same argument, with p and q interchanged, applies.
* At 2, both p and q are squares in Q_2 because they are 1 modulo 8.
  Hence `K tensor Q_2=Q_2 times Q_2` and
  `M tensor Q_2=Q_2^4`. Each of the two primes of K over 2 splits in M/K.

Thus the nontrivial Artin character `psi:Cl(K)->{+1,-1}` is trivial on
EVERY prime above 2pq, including every prime ramified in K/Q. Their class
span has even index. In fact `Cl(O_(K,S))` has nonzero 2-torsion, so the
standard exact sequence shows that S-unit squareclasses strictly miss
part of K(S,2). This is a failure of the proposed source, not merely a
failure of one attempted proof of its completeness.

The native polynomial is

    f=x(x^2-pq),        disc(f)=4(pq)^3.

It is monic and squarefree, with branch algebra Q times K and native
bad-prime support exactly {2,p,q}. The corresponding elliptic equation
has discriminant `16 disc(f)=64(pq)^3`, so no other finite prime is bad.
For the explicit witness p=17,q=89, reciprocity and `89=4 (mod 17)` give
`(17/89)=(4/17)=1`; both primes are 1 modulo 8. No class-number, Pell,
exceptional-zero or numerical computation is needed for this witness.

For completeness, an odd rational prime ell not dividing pq, with p and q
nonsquare modulo ell splits in K but has nontrivial relative Frobenius in
M/K; mixed signs give an inert K-prime whose relative Frobenius is trivial.
This matches the report's double-nonresidue description. It supplies no
claim that a harmful exceptional zero actually exists.

The counterexample does not by itself prove that a final Selmer dimension
computed from an incomplete source is wrong: subsequent norm/local
conditions could remove omitted classes. It refutes the general source
identity and odd-index coverage needed for the stated completeness proof.
It is not hardness or an impossibility theorem for other classical sources.

## 4. Cyclotomic specialization and the classical boundary

For `f_k=x(x^(2^k)+1)`, k>=1, the nontrivial branch factor is
`Phi_(2^(k+1))`, so its field is `Q(zeta_(2^(k+1)))`, of degree 2^k,
Galois and ramified only at 2. Its discriminant has absolute value
`2^(k*2^k)`, and the cross-resultant with x is 1. Thus the literal native
family has P0={2}, and T=S applies. Its complexity is polynomial in the
native degree/coefficients, not in the short exponent k alone.

This family needs a serious classical circular-unit comparison. In the
literal family the only extra S-direction is especially simple:
`Norm(1-zeta)=2`, so `1-zeta` generates the unique prime over 2. A complete
classical mod-square source still needs the relevant circular-unit index
and construction theorem, including its 2-primary index and torsion.
Odd ordinary class number alone is not that unit theorem. For other
polynomial presentations, extra native S-primes must also be paid.
Accordingly this review establishes no advantage for the cyclotomic family
and does not infer free S-unit generators for arbitrary enlarged S.
