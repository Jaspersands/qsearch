## Result and interface

**The classical rounding-and-validation module can be completed.** It produces one labeling with probability \(1-o(1)\), conditional on the supplied mass-matrix guarantee and its charged construction.

There is one essential data-flow requirement:

> First attenuate and split the original graph. Then compute \(R\), and all candidates derived from it, using only the first two resulting arrays. Use the third array only for validation.

An already-fixed \(R\) computed from the unsplit graph cannot simply be declared independent of a subsequently manufactured validation array. The construction below invokes the supplied \(R\)-producing algorithm after preprocessing; it does not reprove that algorithm or its mass lemma.

Fix a known rational constant
\[
0<\eta\le \min\{\eta_{p,k},1\}.
\]
The required matrix interface is a real PSD trace-one \(R\), with
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(P_LR)\ge\eta
\]
except with probability \(\delta_R(n)=o(1)\) on the balanced-label event. Entrywise approximations to \(R\) of accuracy \(O_{p,k}(1/n)\) suffice. Their production and access are charged below.

All label-dependent objects in the proof are analysis-only.

## 1. Attenuation and the exact three-way channel

Write
\[
p=2d,\qquad M=\binom np,\qquad
D=\binom{\ell}{d}\binom{n-\ell}{d}.
\]

For every labeling \(z\),
\[
|f_z(E)|\le 1.
\]
Indeed, Hölder gives
\[
\sum_{a=1}^k\prod_{i\in E}
  \left|\mathbf 1[z_i=a]-\frac1k\right|
\le
\left(1-\frac1k\right)^p+(k-1)k^{-p}\le1.
\]

### Known-signal attenuation

Let \(C_R\) be a fixed sufficient frontier constant for the supplied matrix producer, and set
\[
K=4C_R.
\]
Assume the original signal satisfies
\[
\epsilon^2D\ge K\ell.
\]

Choose the largest integer \(j\ge0\) for which
\[
4^{-j}\epsilon^2D\ge K\ell,
\]
and put
\[
\beta=2^{-j}\epsilon.
\]
Then, exactly,
\[
K\ell\le\beta^2D<4K\ell. \tag{1}
\]

For each edge, independently retain the original bit with probability \(2^{-j}\), otherwise replace it by an independent \(\operatorname{Bern}(q)\) bit. The resulting bit has conditional law
\[
\operatorname{Bern}\bigl(q+\beta f_x(E)\bigr).
\]

This attenuation uses a dyadic probability, so it requires no irrational parameter or non-dyadic Bernoulli sampler. Selecting \(j\) uses exact rational comparisons. At the frontier in (1), \(\beta\to0\); hence \(\beta\le q/2=1/8\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).

### Exact routing law

Set
\[
m=\lceil\log_2 n\rceil,\qquad t=\frac1m,\qquad s=1-2t.
\]
For \(n\ge16\), \(s\ge1/2\).

For each attenuated edge bit \(Y\), independently choose
\[
J\in\{0,1,2\},\qquad
\Pr(J=0)=s,\quad \Pr(J=1)=\Pr(J=2)=t.
\]
Place \(Y\) in output coordinate \(J\), and fill the other two coordinates with independent \(\operatorname{Bern}(q)\) bits.

Call the three frozen arrays \(Y^{(0)},Y^{(1)},Y^{(2)}\). Their marginal signal strengths are exactly
\[
s\beta,\quad t\beta,\quad t\beta.
\]
Moreover,
\[
(s\beta)^2D\ge \frac14K\ell=C_R\ell, \tag{2}
\]
so the first array remains above the assumed matrix-construction threshold.

The classical construction may ignore \(Y^{(1)}\). It may instead use it for a separately specified guide, provided the matrix guarantee applies to that use.

## 2. Exact per-edge chi-square calculation

Fix the labels and one edge. Put
\[
\delta=\beta f_x(E),\qquad
(w_0,w_1,w_2)=(s,t,t).
\]
Let \(P_\delta\) denote the routed three-bit law, and let
\[
Q_\delta=\bigotimes_{j=0}^2\operatorname{Bern}(q_j),
\qquad q_j=q+w_j\delta
\]
be the product of its exact marginals. Write \(v_j=q_j(1-q_j)\).

The following identity is exact:
\[
\boxed{
\chi^2(P_\delta\|Q_\delta)
=
\delta^4\sum_{i<j}\frac{w_i^2w_j^2}{v_iv_j}
+
4\delta^6\frac{w_0^2w_1^2w_2^2}{v_0v_1v_2}.
} \tag{3}
\]

To prove it, under the routed law,
\[
\mathbb E(Y_j-q)=w_j\delta,
\]
while every product of two or three distinct coordinates centered at \(q\) has expectation zero. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(Y_i,Y_j)=-w_iw_j\delta^2,
\]
and
\[
\mathbb E\prod_{j=0}^2(Y_j-q_j)
=2w_0w_1w_2\delta^3.
\]
Expand \(dP_\delta/dQ_\delta\) in the orthonormal product basis generated by
\[
\frac{Y_j-q_j}{\sqrt{v_j}}.
\]
The singleton coefficients vanish because the marginals match. Squaring the pair and triple coefficients gives (3).

When \(|\delta|\le1/8\),
\[
q_j\in[1/8,3/8],\qquad v_j\ge v_*:=7/64.
\]
Also
\[
\sum_{i<j}w_i^2w_j^2=2s^2t^2+t^4\le3t^2.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\chi^2(P_\delta\|Q_\delta)
\le
\left(\frac3{v_*^2}+\frac1{16v_*^3}\right)\delta^4t^2
<300\,\delta^4t^2. \tag{4}
\]

Thus the proposed fourth-order, \(t^2\)-weighted bound is valid. The outputs are not exactly independent; their dependence is accounted for by (3).

### Whole-input and conditional-label TV

Conditional on \(x\), different edges remain independent. Hence
\[
1+\chi^2(P_x\|Q_x)
=\prod_E\bigl(1+\chi^2(P_{\delta_E}\|Q_{\delta_E})\bigr),
\]
where \(Q_x\) is the experiment with three mutually independent arrays, conditional on \(x\), at strengths \(s\beta,t\beta,t\beta\). Therefore
\[
\operatorname{TV}(P_x,Q_x)
\le
\frac12\sqrt{\exp(300M\beta^4t^2)-1}
=:\tau_n. \tag{5}
\]

This bound is uniform in \(x\). Keeping the same IID label prior gives the joint bound
\[
\operatorname{TV}\bigl((x,Y^{(0)},Y^{(1)},Y^{(2)})_{\rm routed},
                       (x,Y^{(0)},Y^{(1)},Y^{(2)})_{\rm product}\bigr)
\le\tau_n. \tag{6}
\]

The joint-label statement is important: recovery success depends on the latent labels.

For fixed \(p=2d\) and the stipulated growing polylogarithmic \(\ell\),
\[
D=(1+o(1))\frac{\ell^d n^d}{(d!)^2}.
\]
Using (1),
\[
M\beta^4t^2
=O_{p,k}\!\left(t^2\ell^{\,2-p}\right)=o(1), \tag{7}
\]
so \(\tau_n=o(1)\). In fact,
\[
\tau_n=O_{p,k}\!\left(t\ell^{\,1-d}\right).
\]

We henceforth prove recovery in the conditional-product experiment and transfer the **entire algorithm once** through (6). There is no per-query or per-call resampling.

## 3. Gap-free extraction of a constant-dimensional subspace

Use the IID event
\[
\mathcal B_n=
\left\{\max_a\left|\frac{n_a}{n}-\frac1k\right|
                  \le n^{-1/4}\right\}.
\]
Its failure probability is at most
\[
2k e^{-2\sqrt n}.
\]
For sufficiently large \(n\), this event implies \(n_a\ge n/(2k)\).

The tighter balance condition is needed later for rounding. The weaker lower bounds alone do not justify the proposed \(1/k+o(1)\) baseline.

Let \(W\) have columns \(w_a\), and let \(r=k-1\). On \(\mathcal B_n\),
\[
WW^T=\sum_a w_aw_a^T\ \succeq\ \frac{n}{2k}P_L. \tag{8}
\]
Indeed, for \(u\perp\mathbf1\) in color space,
\[
u^TW^TWu=\sum_a n_a u_a^2\ge\frac{n}{2k}\|u\|^2.
\]

Set
\[
\alpha=\frac{\eta}{8r},\qquad e=\frac{\alpha}{4}.
\]
Compute an orthogonal \(Q\) and diagonal \(H\) such that
\[
\|R-QHQ^T\|_{\rm op}\le e. \tag{9}
\]
A bounded rational implementation without an eigengap is given in Section 7.

Let \(V\) be spanned by columns of \(Q\) whose diagonal entries in \(H\) are at least \(\alpha\), and write \(P=P_V\). If \(m_V=\dim V\), then
\[
1\ge\operatorname{Tr}(PR)\ge m_V(\alpha-e),
\]
so
\[
m_V\le\frac1{\alpha-e}\le\frac2\alpha=:m_0. \tag{10}
\]

Also, since \(\|R\|\le1\),
\[
R\preceq (1+2e)P+(\alpha+e)I.
\]
Thus
\[
\eta
\le (1+2e)\operatorname{Tr}(P_LP)+r(\alpha+e),
\]
which implies
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(P_LP)\ge\eta/2. \tag{11}
\]

Combining (8) and (11),
\[
\sum_a\|Pw_a\|^2
=\operatorname{Tr}(PWW^T)
\ge\frac{\eta n}{4k}.
\]
Some color \(a\) therefore satisfies
\[
\|Pw_a\|\ge\frac{\sqrt\eta}{2k}\sqrt n. \tag{12}
\]

No separation between eigenvalues of \(R\), or between an eigenvalue and the cutoff, was used.

## 4. A constant list containing a correlated labeling

Set the rational constants
\[
a=\frac{\eta}{4k},\qquad L_0=\frac2a.
\]
Since \(\eta\le1\), the right side of (12) is at least \(2a\sqrt n\).

Take an \(a\)-net covering the unit sphere of \(V\). Its size is a constant depending only on \(k,\eta\). For exact rational implementation, it is sufficient to use net points of norm at most one rather than exactly one.

For example, in the computed orthonormal coordinates of \(V\), use the grid
\[
h\mathbb Z^{m_V}\cap\{c:\|c\|\le1\},
\qquad h=\frac{a}{4m_0}.
\]
It covers the unit sphere within distance \(a\): shrink a unit vector by \(1-a/2\), then round its coordinates to the grid. The rounding error is at most \(a/8\), and the rounded point remains in the unit ball.

Let \(J_0\) be a fixed upper bound on the number of candidates, for instance
\[
J_0\le \left(1+\frac{8m_0}{a}\right)^{m_0}. \tag{13}
\]
This is independent of \(n\).

For the color in (12), a net point \(v\) satisfies
\[
\|v\|\le1,\qquad \langle v,w_a\rangle\ge a\sqrt n. \tag{14}
\]
The net covers both signs; no sign or color oracle is used.

### Clipping

For each net point, define
\[
u_i=\sqrt n\,v_i,\qquad
g_i=\operatorname{clip}(u_i,[-L_0,L_0]).
\]
Since \(\sum_i u_i^2\le n\),
\[
\sum_i |u_i-g_i|
\le\frac1{L_0}\sum_i u_i^2\le\frac n{L_0}.
\]
Using \(|w_a(i)|\le1\), (14) gives
\[
\frac1n\sum_i g_iw_a(i)\ge a-\frac1{L_0}=\frac a2. \tag{15}
\]

### Randomized labeling

Independently over vertices, set
\[
h_i=\frac{1+g_i/L_0}{2}.
\]
Output label \(1\) with probability \(h_i\); otherwise output a uniform label in \(\{2,\ldots,k\}\).

Fix any permutation mapping output label \(1\) to planted color \(a\). Its expected agreement is exactly
\[
\frac{1-n_a/n}{k-1}
+\frac{k}{k-1}\frac1n\sum_i h_iw_a(i).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\mathbb E[\mathrm{agreement}]
=
\frac1k+
\frac{k-2}{2(k-1)}
  \left(\frac{n_a}{n}-\frac1k\right)
+
\frac{k}{2(k-1)L_0}\frac1n\sum_i g_iw_a(i). \tag{16}
\]

Define
\[
G=\frac{k a^2}{8(k-1)},\qquad
\gamma=\frac G4=\frac{\eta^2}{512k(k-1)}. \tag{17}
\]
The last term of (16) is at least \(G\). On \(\mathcal B_n\), for sufficiently large \(n\), the imbalance loss is at most \(G/4\).

Implement \(h_i\) by a dyadic \(\widehat h_i\) satisfying
\[
|\widehat h_i-h_i|\le \min\{G/4,n^{-4}\}.
\]
This reduces expected agreement by at most \(G/4\). Thus the implemented rounding has expected agreement at least \(1/k+G/2\). Hoeffding gives
\[
\Pr\{\mathrm{agreement}<1/k+\gamma\}
\le e^{-nG^2/8}. \tag{18}
\]

The good net index and its planted color are fixed by \(x,R\), before the rounding coins. They need not be identified by the algorithm. Thus, conditional on the mass and balance events, the finite list contains a labeling of permutation-invariant agreement at least \(1/k+\gamma\), except with the probability in (18).

## 5. Native validation: mean, collision correction, and variance

Work temporarily in the conditional-product experiment. All candidates depend only on the first two arrays and independent rounding coins. Conditional on those objects and \(x\), the validation array is independent Bernoulli data at strength
\[
\beta_v=t\beta>0.
\]

For a candidate \(z\), compute
\[
S_z=\sum_{E\in\binom{[n]}p}f_z(E)\bigl(Y^{(2)}_E-q\bigr).
\]
Define
\[
C_{ab}
=\frac1n\sum_i
 \left(\mathbf1[z_i=a]-\frac1k\right)
 \left(\mathbf1[x_i=b]-\frac1k\right),
\qquad
A_z=\sum_{a,b}C_{ab}^{\,p}.
\]

Because \(p\) is even, \(A_z\ge0\).

### Exact collision bound

Expanding the mean,
\[
\mathbb E S_z=\beta_v\sum_E f_z(E)f_x(E).
\]
For each pair \(a,b\), put
\[
u_i^{ab}
=\left(\mathbf1[z_i=a]-\frac1k\right)
 \left(\mathbf1[x_i=b]-\frac1k\right).
\]
The unrestricted ordered sum is
\[
\sum_{i_1,\ldots,i_p}\prod_{j=1}^p u_{i_j}^{ab}
=\left(\sum_i u_i^{ab}\right)^p=n^pC_{ab}^p.
\]
There are at most \(\binom p2 n^{p-1}\) ordered tuples with a repeated index, and every summand has absolute value at most one. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\left|
\mathbb E S_z-\frac{\beta_v n^p}{p!}A_z
\right|
\le
\frac{\beta_v k^2\binom p2}{p!}\,n^{p-1}.
} \tag{19}
\]

This bound requires neither balanced \(z\) nor balanced \(x\).

### Variance

Conditional independence of validation edges gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}(S_z)
=\sum_E f_z(E)^2\operatorname{Var}(Y^{(2)}_E)
\le\frac M4
\le\frac{n^p}{4p!}. \tag{20}
\]

For analysis only, normalize
\[
T_z=\frac{p!S_z}{\beta_v n^p}.
\]
Then
\[
|\mathbb ET_z-A_z|\le\frac{B_{\rm col}}n,
\qquad B_{\rm col}=k^2\binom p2, \tag{21}
\]
and
\[
\operatorname{Var}(T_z)
\le\frac{p!}{4\beta_v^2n^p}. \tag{22}
\]

The algorithm need not perform this normalization.

## 6. Selecting one candidate and converting its score to agreement

The centered confusion matrix has exact zero row and column sums:
\[
\sum_a C_{ab}=0,\qquad \sum_b C_{ab}=0. \tag{23}
\]
For every permutation \(\pi\),
\[
\sum_a C_{a,\pi(a)}
=
\frac1n\#\{i:x_i=\pi(z_i)\}-\frac1k. \tag{24}
\]
Thus no balance assumption is needed in the score-to-agreement conversion.

### A good labeling has a constant score parameter

If \(z\) has agreement at least \(1/k+\gamma\), (24) shows that some entry of \(C\) is at least \(\gamma/k\). Consequently,
\[
A_z\ge A_0,\qquad A_0:=\left(\frac{\gamma}{k}\right)^p. \tag{25}
\]

Take
\[
n\ge \frac{8B_{\rm col}}{A_0}.
\]
Then the collision error in (21) is at most \(A_0/8\). By (22), Chebyshev and a union bound over the at most \(J_0\) candidates,
\[
\Pr\left\{\exists z:\ |T_z-\mathbb ET_z|>A_0/8\right\}
\le
\frac{16J_0p!}{A_0^2\beta_v^2n^p}. \tag{26}
\]
On the complementary event,
\[
|T_z-A_z|\le A_0/4
\]
for every candidate.

Select the candidate \(z_*\) with largest observed \(S_z\), breaking ties deterministically. If the list contains a candidate satisfying (25), then
\[
A_{z_*}\ge A_0/2. \tag{27}
\]

### A large score parameter forces permutation-invariant agreement

From (27), some entry of \(C\) has absolute value at least
\[
u=\left(\frac{A_0}{2k^2}\right)^{1/p}.
\]
If that entry is negative, its row sum being zero implies that some positive entry is at least \(u/(k-1)\). Thus
\[
m_+:=\max_{a,b}C_{ab}\ge\frac{u}{k-1}. \tag{28}
\]

Fix an entry \(C_{a_0b_0}=m_+\). Average \(\sum_aC_{a,\pi(a)}\) over permutations conditioned on \(\pi(a_0)=b_0\). The sum of the submatrix omitting row \(a_0\) and column \(b_0\) is \(m_+\), by (23). Hence the average is
\[
m_++\frac{m_+}{k-1}=\frac{k}{k-1}m_+.
\]
Some permutation therefore achieves at least that value. By (24) and (28),
\[
\max_\pi\frac1n\#\{i:x_i=\pi(z_{*,i})\}
\ge\frac1k+c,
\]
where the explicit sufficient constant
\[
\boxed{
c=
\frac{k}{(k-1)^2}
 \left(\frac{A_0}{2k^2}\right)^{1/p}
=
\frac{\gamma}{(k-1)^2(2k^2)^{1/p}}
>0.
} \tag{29}
\]

This proves the required conversion to **one** output labeling, rather than a list or an expected overlap.

### Validation has sufficient signal

At (1),
\[
\beta_v^2n^p
=t^2\beta^2n^{2d}
=\Theta_{p,k}\!\left(t^2n^d\ell^{\,1-d}\right)
=n^{d-o(1)}. \tag{30}
\]
Thus the validation failure bound (26) is \(o(1)\), despite \(t=1/\lceil\log_2n\rceil\).

## 7. Finite precision, exact-channel implementation, and finite clocks

### 7.1 A gap-free rational diagonalization

It suffices to obtain a symmetric dyadic \(A\) with
\[
|A_{ij}-R_{ij}|\le \frac{e}{2n}.
\]
Then
\[
\|A-R\|_F\le e/2,\qquad \|A\|_F\le2.
\]
This requires \(O_{p,k}(\log n)\) bits per entry.

A bounded Jacobi procedure gives rational orthogonal \(Q\) and diagonal \(H\) with
\[
\|A-QHQ^T\|_F\le e/2.
\]

Here is a finite-precision justification, not an eigengap assumption. Let
\[
F(A)=\sum_{i\ne j}A_{ij}^2.
\]
An exact Jacobi rotation at a largest off-diagonal entry reduces \(F\) by at least the fraction \(2/[n(n-1)]\). Approximate that rotation by an **exactly orthogonal rational rotation** within operator error \(\zeta\). Such rotations are obtained from rational \(u\) using
\[
\cos\theta=\frac{1-u^2}{1+u^2},\qquad
\sin\theta=\frac{2u}{1+u^2}.
\]
For \(\|A\|_F\le2\), the perturbation changes the next potential by at most \(16\zeta\). Hence
\[
F_{j+1}
\le\left(1-\frac2{n(n-1)}\right)F_j+16\zeta.
\]
Taking, for example,
\[
\zeta=\frac{e^2}{128n^2}
\]
and a fixed cap \(O(n^2\log(1/e))\) makes \(F\le e^2/4\).

The procedure uses exact rational comparisons and arithmetic; a final rational residual check certifies the required bound. Approximate diagonal entries are compared exactly to the rational cutoff \(\alpha\). No eigenvalue needs to be resolved more accurately than its distance from another eigenvalue.

For fixed \(\eta,k\), each rotation has \(O(\log n)\)-bit rational parameters. Keeping common denominators bounds all intermediate bit lengths by a polynomial in \(n,\log n\). Consequently this is deterministic polynomial **bit time**, not merely an arithmetic-operation bound.

### 7.2 Exact probabilities versus bounded fair-bit sampling

There is a genuine distinction here.

A fixed bounded number of fair bits cannot realize probability \(1/m\) exactly when \(m\) is not a power of two. Therefore an exact non-dyadic channel on every branch, with a deterministic finite fair-bit budget and no failure branch, is impossible.

The high-probability recovery target permits the following exact-with-abort implementation:

* Sample a uniform integer in \(\{0,\ldots,m-1\}\) by rejection from \(\lceil\log_2m\rceil\) fair bits.
* Reserve \(L_{\rm cap}=O_{p,k}(\log n)\) independent attempts per edge.
* Use the first accepted value.
* If every attempt is rejected, abort and output a fixed labeling.

Each attempt accepts with probability at least \(1/2\). Choose the cap so that the probability of any routing-sampler abort is at most \(n^{-8}\). Conditional on no abort, the route choices have the **exact** required independent uniform law. This event is independent of the input graph and labels.

The same bounded method supplies uniform labels in \(\{2,\ldots,k\}\). Bernoulli probabilities \(\widehat h_i\) are dyadic and use fixed-length bit blocks. The null probability \(q=1/4\) uses exactly two fair bits.

Thus:

* the channel proved in Sections 1–2 is exact;
* the finite-clock machine either implements that channel or enters an explicitly charged \(o(1)\)-probability failure branch;
* no non-dyadic probability is silently treated as a unit-cost exact coin.

### 7.3 Precision in clipping and scores

The rational net vectors and the rational orthogonal basis give rational \(v_i\). Compute \(\sqrt n\) and the clipped probabilities to the accuracy needed for
\[
|\widehat h_i-h_i|\le\min\{G/4,n^{-4}\}.
\]
The required output precision is \(O_{p,k}(\log n)\) bits. Arithmetic on the rational basis has the polynomial intermediate lengths just described.

Validation scores can be accumulated **exactly**. Write
\[
F_z(E)=\sum_a\prod_{i\in E}\bigl(k\mathbf1[z_i=a]-1\bigr),
\qquad f_z(E)=\frac{F_z(E)}{k^p}.
\]
Selecting the largest \(S_z\) is equivalent to selecting the largest integer
\[
\sum_E F_z(E)\bigl(4Y^{(2)}_E-1\bigr). \tag{31}
\]
For fixed \(p,k\), its bit length is \(O_{p,k}(\log n)\). There is no floating-point score comparison and no precision requirement involving a random score gap.

### 7.4 Fixed randomness and all branches

All random blocks can be sampled once and frozen for the execution. This is not a claim that the TV bound holds after conditioning on every individual random tape.

The preprocessing route and attenuation coins must not become extra observations for candidate generation. Materializing the three arrays and discarding those coins enforces the needed interface. Subsequent computation factors through:

* the first two frozen arrays for \(R\) and candidate generation;
* independent rounding coins;
* the third frozen array for scoring.

No later access to the raw graph is allowed to influence candidate selection outside this interface.

Use deterministic caps for:

* the supplied matrix producer;
* rational diagonalization;
* all rejection samplers;
* the net size, bounded by \(J_0\);
* every scan and score computation.

On abort, invalid numerical certificate, zero selected dimension, excessive selected dimension, or a fixed small-\(n\) guard, output a specified fixed labeling. No branch tests the latent mass or latent agreement.

## 8. Native bit complexity

Let \(b\) be the bit length of the rational signal parameter. The matrix producer must supply the requested \(O(1/n)\) entry accuracy within its independently charged bound
\[
T_R=n^{\ell+O_p(1)}\operatorname{poly}(\text{inputbits}).
\]
The new signal supplied to that producer has \(b+O(\log n)\) bits.

The additional work is explicitly classical:

* Read the \(M\) original edge bits.
* Generate and write the three \(M\)-bit arrays.
* Generate \(O(M\log n\log\log n)\) routing bits under the above caps, plus attenuation and null bits.
* Read \(n^2\) matrix entries at the requested precision.
* Perform polynomial-bit-time rational diagonalization.
* Construct at most \(J_0\) length-\(n\) candidates.
* Scan all \(M\) validation edges for each candidate and accumulate (31).
* Write \(n\lceil\log_2k\rceil\) output bits.

A deliberately loose elementary bound for the added bit work is
\[
O_{p,k}\!\left((n^{10}+n^{p+2})
 \operatorname{poly}(b+\log n+\log\ell)\right). \tag{32}
\]
This allows straightforward rational arithmetic and non-oracular array access; optimizing the exponent is unnecessary here.

Consequently the complete classical pipeline has the claimed conditional bound
\[
\boxed{
n^{\ell+O_p(1)}\operatorname{poly}(\text{inputbits}).
}
\]
There is no free projector access, implicit planted validation sample, QRAM, or uncharged state preparation. The preparation of \(R\) remains included in \(T_R\).

## 9. Overall success bound and remaining boundary

For sufficiently large \(n\), the failure probability is at most
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\delta_R(n)
+2k e^{-2\sqrt n}
+e^{-nG^2/8}\\
&\quad+
\frac{16J_0p!}{A_0^2\beta^2t^2n^p}
+\frac12\sqrt{\exp(300M\beta^4t^2)-1}
+O(n^{-8}).
\end{aligned} \tag{33}
\]
Every term tends to zero by the supplied matrix guarantee, (7), and (30). The output therefore satisfies
\[
\max_\pi\frac1n\#\{i:z_i=\pi(x_i)\}
\ge\frac1k+c
\]
with probability \(1-o(1)\), where \(c\) is given in (29).

### What this proves—and what would invalidate the composition

**Proved here:** the gap-free numerical reduction, constant-list rounding, exact routing law and chi-square identity, joint-label TV transfer, collision-corrected validation, selection of one labeling, agreement conversion, and finite-clock postprocessing costs.

**Still supplied externally:** a matrix producer meeting its constant-mass, requested-precision, high-probability, and all-branch runtime guarantees at the attenuated training strength in (2). No claim about that pending proof is promoted here.
