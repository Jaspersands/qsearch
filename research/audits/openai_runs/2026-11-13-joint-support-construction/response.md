## Outcome: obstruction to the transfer repair, not to polynomial support itself

The ordered rectangles have an exact cyclic cokernel, not merely an occasional index defect. The natural signed-unordered-pair repair fixes the two-cycle control but leaves every odd-cycle defect unchanged.

More strongly, a small-input family forces **factorial auxiliary degree for any full-local-kernel repair using field transfers or signed-permutation source tori**. Polynomial-degree single-relative-norm source tori do not evade this obstruction either. This identifies a specific unpaid operation beyond the already-paid S-unit endpoint.

All calculations below are symbolic; no Sage run or source access is claimed.

## 1. Ordered rectangles: exact norms, wrong integral image

For a field factor \(F\) of
\[
A\otimes A\otimes B\otimes B,
\]
the proposed alternating product of four relative norms does preserve both exact norms. After applying \(N_{L/A}\), the two terms with each fixed \(A\)-embedding cancel by norm transitivity. The analogous cancellation holds over \(B\). There is no sign or archimedean qualification.

The defect is integral valuation generation.

Take
\[
X=Y=\mathbb Z/n,\qquad \mathrm{Frob}(i,j)=(i+1,j+1).
\]
The \(L\)-prime orbits are
\[
Z_r=\{(i,i+r):i\in\mathbb Z/n\}.
\]
Each column of the norm matrix has weight \(1\) in both rows. Consequently the joint valuation kernel is
\[
K_n=\{(a_0,\ldots,a_{n-1})\in\mathbb Z^n:\sum a_r=0\}.
\]

Identify this with the augmentation ideal
\[
I=(t-1)R,\qquad R=\mathbb Z[t]/(t^n-1).
\]
For an ordered rectangle with
\[
a=x_1-x_0,\quad b=y_1-y_0,\quad r=y_0-x_0,
\]
its Frobenius-orbit transfer has **per-point**, not total-orbit, column
\[
t^r(1-t^b)(1-t^{-a}).
\]
Thus all columns lie in \(I^2\). Taking \(a=b=1\) and varying \(r\) generates \(I^2\), so
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{image}(\text{ordered rectangles})=I^2.}
\]

Moreover,
\[
\boxed{I/I^2\simeq\mathbb Z/n.}
\]
Indeed, modulo \(I^2\), write \(t=1+\epsilon\); then \(\epsilon^2=0\) and \(n\epsilon=0\). The quotient is detected exactly by
\[
\mu(a)=\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}r\,a_r\pmod n.
\]

Therefore:

* \(n=2\): image \(=2\mathbb Z(1,-1)\), index \(2\).
* \(n=3\): image generated by
  \[
  (2,-1,-1),\quad(-1,2,-1),\quad(-1,-1,2),
  \]
  with index \(3\). In the kernel basis
  \(u=(1,-1,0),v=(0,1,-1)\), the first two columns give
  \[
  \begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},
  \]
  whose Smith invariants are \(1,3\).
* For every \(n\), the missing quotient has order exactly \(n\).

Replacing \(I^2\) by its saturation \(I\) would therefore introduce an unpaid multiplicative division operation.

## 2. Signed unordered pairs do not repair odd cycles

The natural signed-pair source has cocharacter lattice
\[
P=\bigwedge\nolimits^2\mathbb Z[X]\otimes
  \bigwedge\nolimits^2\mathbb Z[Y],
\]
with transfer induced by
\[
e_i\wedge e_j\longmapsto e_i-e_j
\]
in each factor. These are the signed-permutation lattices arising from unordered pairs with their quadratic norm-one orientation character.

For \(n=2\), each exterior-square generator changes sign under Frobenius, so their tensor is invariant. Its image is the primitive vector \((1,-1)\). This genuinely removes the factor \(2\). For quadratic \(A,B\), the corresponding concrete operation is \(z/\bar z\) from the third quadratic subfield of \(L\); both required norms are exactly \(1\).

For **odd \(n\)**, however, Frobenius cannot reverse an unordered pair: such a reversal would have order \(2\). Every signed-pair orbit can therefore be oriented consistently around its entire Frobenius orbit. Its transfer column is already an ordered-rectangle column. Conversely, ordered columns descend to these signed columns.

Hence
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{image}(\text{signed unordered pairs})=I^2
       \quad(n\text{ odd}).}
\]
Even granting complete access to the source tori’s local points does not repair this.

### Explicit field control

Let
\[
A=\mathbb Q(\alpha),\quad \alpha^3+\alpha^2-2\alpha-1=0,
\]
\[
B=\mathbb Q(\beta),\quad \beta^3-3\beta+1=0.
\]
These are the real cubic subfields of the seventh and ninth cyclotomic fields, with discriminants \(49\) and \(81\). They are cyclic, distinct, and linearly disjoint.

At \(2\), their reductions are respectively
\[
T^3+T^2+1,\qquad T^3+T+1,
\]
both irreducible. Thus \(L\) has exactly the two-three-cycle local configuration above. Signed pairs miss \((1,-1,0)\).

The missing direction is not fictitious globally. The automorphisms
\[
\alpha\mapsto\alpha^2-2,\qquad
\beta\mapsto\beta^2-2
\]
are the Frobenius generators at \(2\). Let \(C_0\subset L\) be the fixed field of their diagonal product. It is obtained as a fixed subspace inside the explicit degree-nine algebra \(L\), without a larger closure. It has degree \(3\), splits completely at \(2\), and
\[
AC_0=BC_0=L.
\]
For a generator \(\sigma\) of \(\operatorname{Gal}(C_0/\mathbb Q)\),
\[
y(z)=z/\sigma(z)
\]
satisfies
\[
N_{L/A}y(z)=N_{L/B}y(z)=N_{C_0/\mathbb Q}y(z)=1.
\]
Choosing \(z\) with valuations \((1,0,0)\) at the three primes above \(2\), by finite approximation, gives a primitive difference valuation for \(y(z)\).

This is an actual missing-direction operation for this control—not root extraction and not a proof of the handbook’s general normal-case assertion.

## 3. A factorial obstruction to polynomial-degree transfer repairs

The following obstruction addresses growing cyclic correspondences, rather than just the two finite controls.

Let \(\ell\ge5\) be prime. Take degree-\(\ell\) fields \(A,B\) whose normal closures have groups \(S_\ell\) and are linearly disjoint. Thus the action on their embeddings is
\[
G=S_\ell\times S_\ell.
\]
Normal closures are used here only to describe the action; none is constructed.

Let
\[
T=\ker\!\left(
\operatorname{Res}_{L/\mathbb Q}\mathbb G_m
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Res}_{A/\mathbb Q}\mathbb G_m
\times\operatorname{Res}_{B/\mathbb Q}\mathbb G_m
\right).
\]
Its cocharacter lattice is
\[
K=\{M\in\mathbb Z^{\ell\times\ell}:
       \text{every row and column sum is zero}\}.
\]

Consider arbitrary algebraic multiplicative transfers
\[
h_i:U_i\longrightarrow T
\]
whose source cocharacter lattices are signed-permutation lattices, of combined rank \(D\). This includes ordinary auxiliary-field multiplicative groups and the signed unordered-pair sources.

**Claim.** If \(D<\ell!\), then at every good prime whose Frobenius is a pair of \(\ell\)-cycles, their valuation images lie in \(I^2\), not all of \(I\).

### Proof

Conjugate the two cycles to the same shift and write
\[
C=\langle(c,c)\rangle.
\]

**First, nonzero \(C\)-invariant vectors require large global orbits.**  
A vector \(v\in K^C\) is a circulant matrix
\[
v_{ij}=a_{j-i},\qquad \sum a_r=0.
\]
If \(v\ne0\), its columns are all distinct: equality of two columns would make \(a\) periodic under a nonzero translation, hence constant because \(\ell\) is prime.

No column is the negative of another either. Iterating such a translation \(\ell\) times, with \(\ell\) odd, would give \(a=-a\), hence \(a=0\).

It follows that the stabilizer of \(\{v,-v\}\) in \(G\) projects injectively into either \(S_\ell\): an element fixing one side cannot permute distinct columns, or match them to their negatives. Therefore
\[
|\operatorname{Stab}_G\{v,-v\}|\le\ell!,
\qquad
|G\cdot\{v,-v\}|\ge\ell!.
\]

**Second, a small signed-permutation source cannot supply such a vector.**  
The image \(w\) of a signed basis vector has a global orbit up to sign of size at most \(D\). Thus, if \(D<\ell!\), a nonzero such image cannot belong to \(K^C\). Its \(C\)-orbit consequently has length \(\ell\).

**Third, every source valuation orbit produces a trace.**  
A positive-sign Frobenius orbit contributes
\[
w+cw+\cdots+c^{m-1}w.
\]
For nonzero \(w\), its length \(m\) must be divisible by \(\ell\). Its image is therefore a multiple of
\[
N_C(w)=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}c^jw.
\]
Negative-sign source orbits have no nonzero invariant valuation.

Finally, \(K\) is generated integrally by elementary rectangles. The calculation in Section 1 therefore gives
\[
N_C(K)=I^2\subsetneq K^C=I.
\]
This proves the claim.

The argument also applies when auxiliary fields are not contained in the normal closure: the orbit of a basis image in \(K\) still cannot exceed the auxiliary signed-basis orbit.

### These are genuinely small-input fields

This is not a degree-only bound hiding enormous input coefficients.

Choose distinct primes \(r,s>\ell\) of size \(O(\ell)\). By coefficientwise CRT, choose monic degree-\(\ell\) polynomials which:

* are irreducible modulo \(2\);
* modulo \(r\), factor as one irreducible quadratic and \(\ell-2\) distinct linear factors;
* for the first polynomial, split squarefreely modulo \(s\);
* for the second, are congruent modulo \(s^2\) to
  \[
  T^2\prod_{i=1}^{\ell-2}(T-i)+s.
  \]

Their coefficients have \(O(\log\ell)\) bits. Irreducibility gives transitivity; prime degree gives primitivity; the transposition supplied at \(r\) then forces \(S_\ell\). For completeness, the normal subgroup generated by conjugate transpositions has graph components forming blocks; primitivity forces one component, and those transpositions generate \(S_\ell\).

At \(s\), the first discriminant has valuation \(0\), while the second has valuation \(1\). Their quadratic sign fields are therefore distinct. The normal-subgroup structure of \(S_\ell\) then implies that the two splitting fields intersect trivially.

Discriminant bounds give polynomial logarithmic discriminants. Maximal-order bases and multiplication tables can be represented with polynomially many bits, using overorder indices bounded by these discriminants. The same holds for \(L\).

Thus original explicit input length can be polynomial in \(\ell\), whereas \(\ell!\) is superpolynomial. Chebotarev supplies infinitely many primes with the required pair of cycles, outside any finite support and all auxiliary ramification. Only qualitative existence is used; no GRH or effective prime bound is needed for this obstruction.

### Single-relative-norm sources do not evade it

Suppose a repair instead uses polynomial-degree sources
\[
U=\operatorname{Res}_{F/\mathbb Q}
       \operatorname{Res}^{\,1}_{E/F}\mathbb G_m
\]
and torus homomorphisms \(U\to T\).

The supplied single-norm argument gives integral local valuation generation by paired transfers from factors of \(E\otimes_F E\), of total degree at most \([E:\mathbb Q]^2\). Composing those transfers with \(U\to T\) would give polynomial-degree **field** transfers generating the same local image. The preceding obstruction applies.

This concerns homomorphic transfer repairs, not arbitrary adaptive algorithms using a norm solver. A genuinely different nonlinear operation is not ruled out.

## 4. The minimal unpaid mathematical operation

Ordinary class generators only principalize a divisor **after it has been lifted through the transfer lattice**. They cannot manufacture the missing \(\mu=1\) class. Enlarging binary exponents cannot change \(\mu=0\), and adjoining finitely many support primes leaves infinitely many obstructed Frobenius primes outside support.

Consequently a polynomial invariant-surjective permutation/signed-permutation resolution is not merely unproved: the family above rules it out.

What remains unpaid is **global joint-torus divisor correction beyond this architecture**. A sufficient uniform formulation is
\[
H_S=\mathcal O_{L,S}^{*}\,T(\mathbb Q),
\]
where
\[
H_S=\{x\in L^*:
 N_{L/A}x\in\mathcal O_{A,S}^{*},\
 N_{L/B}x\in\mathcal O_{B,S}^{*}\}.
\]
This equality says exactly that both norms of every eligible element can be preserved while removing its outside-\(S\) valuations. For the native instance, only the specified target fiber needs this conclusion.

A replacement must therefore either:

1. supply nonmonomial torus arithmetic that realizes the residual classes, with a proved polynomial support/principalization bound; or
2. prove a direct global support theorem showing why eligible global divisors can be corrected without full local-kernel generation.

Such a theorem must state its actual field, discriminant/conductor and GRH hypotheses. A Bach bound for ordinary field class groups does not automatically establish either alternative. Restricting a field source by new joint norm constraints simply introduces another torus whose support completeness must be proved.

This obstruction does **not** establish nonsolvability of any target pair, failure of polynomial support itself, or hardness. Individual norm solvability is nowhere substituted for simultaneous solvability.

## 5. Endpoint, BIT accounting, and classical control

Let \(M\) be the complete original input length, including all order tables, embeddings and expanded targets.

If a valid support were supplied by a proved construction, the remaining paid composition would require:

* polynomially many support primes, each of polynomial bit length;
* charged reading, integer factoring and prime decomposition in the supplied maximal orders;
* target support obtained by clearing denominators separately in \(A,B\), then factoring those denominators and the norms of the resulting integral targets—not merely the norms of \(a,b\);
* polynomial total auxiliary degree, representation size and logarithmic discriminant;
* complete compact \(S\)-units, including full torsion, with the endpoint’s state preparation, precision, amplification and finite clocks charged;
* exact atom norms to **both** fields, followed by the paid full paired relation lattice and integer preimage solve.

The native output remains a polynomial-size power product of expanded small \(L\)-atoms with binary exponents. Its two norm equalities can be verified without expanding the powers. Positive verification is separate from support completeness; a sound **NO** still requires the missing support theorem and complete source generation.

No new quantum state preparation or coherent Frobenius oracle is supplied here. Therefore no original-input polynomial total clock is established.

The matched classical baseline is complete classical S-unit/PIP/class-group computation, including index-calculus implementations, followed by the same exact paired relation/SNF endpoint. On the cyclic controls, direct subfield/Hilbert-90 operations are an additional stronger comparison. The documented simultaneous-norm routine remains relevant, but its normal-case completeness assertion is not used as a theorem here. Neither failed transfers nor finite controls establish a quantum speedup.

### Precise falsifiers

* Ordered \(n=3\) columns, expressed in the full kernel basis, must have Smith invariants \(1,3\).
* The natural signed-pair source must have \(\mu=0\) for every odd \(n\). A genuine \(\mu\ne0\) column would falsify the stated image calculation.
* Under the \(S_\ell\times S_\ell\) hypotheses, a signed-permutation source of rank \(<\ell!\) giving nonzero \(\mu\) would falsify the orbit/trace proposition.
* A proposed replacement fails this review if it substitutes saturation, an uncharged root, factorial-degree cycle fields, or an unproved complete-support theorem for a new source torus.

**Bottom line:** the missing operation is not more S-unit computation. It is polynomial-cost global control of the residual joint-torus divisor classes, outside the field/signed-pair/single-norm transfer architecture disproved above.
