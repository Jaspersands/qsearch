**Disposition.** A written composition exists that removes the GRH class-generator premise of the accepted 2026-11-13 global composition on this subfamily. Everything below is a deduction from the supplied texts plus standard genus theory, except where marked ASSUMPTION / SOURCE GAP. No novelty, speedup, hardness or separation is claimed. The local-image primitive is a conditional premise and is not re-audited.

## 0. Statement achieved (exact)

**Family.** Monic squarefree f ∈ Z[x], odd degree n = 2g+1, coefficient bit length B, such that every irreducible factor f_i of f over Q has degree d_i ∈ {1,2,3}. (Odd n forces at least one odd-degree factor.) Let h = ⌈log₂(2|disc f|)⌉ = O(n(B+log n)), L = Q[x]/(f) = ∏ K_i, K_i = Q[x]/(f_i).

**Output.** The integer dim_{F₂} Sel^(2)(Jac(y² = f)/Q).

**Conditional premises (only these).**
(P1) The supplied uniform complete local-image primitive at every p | 2 disc f, with efficient common-frame comparison, exactly as in the accepted review §5–§7 (not re-audited here).
(P2) Biasse–Song corrected v2 Theorem 1 with §8.1/Prop. 7 exact compact recovery and Algorithm 3 basis completion, at failure parameter μ with log(1/μ) cost (checked by the parent).
(P3) Shor integer factoring, verified classically (Las Vegas).
(P4) The retained unconditional fixed-odd-degree class-generator source (ODD_GALOIS §6, n=3, n!=6), applied per cubic factor K_i to its explicit maximal order.
(P5) Bosma–Stevenhagen 1996 (B–S) as a random-polynomial-time algorithm in log|D| given the factorization of D, with output represented by binary quadratic forms, plus the cited Lagarias [8] polynomial-time ternary reduction bound (SOURCE GAP flagged in §3.6).

**Claim.** Under P1–P5 there is a bounded-error quantum algorithm which, for EVERY f in the family and every η ∈ (0,1), returns the exact integer with probability ≥ 1−η in time polynomial(n, B, log(1/η)). GRH is used nowhere. This is a worst-case-over-inputs statement; probabilities are over the algorithm's own randomness and the quantum primitives.

**Not claimed.** Mordell–Weil rank, points, covering curves; any classical lower bound; any speedup over classical class/unit arithmetic; any statement for factors of degree ≥ 4 or for even n; that any incorrect run is detectable.

## 1. Input processing and native maximal orders (all charged)

1. Factor f over Q classically (polynomial in n, B); obtain monic integral f_i of polynomial encoding size.
2. Shor-factor |disc f| (P3), verify by multiplication and primality tests. Since disc f = ∏ disc(f_i) ∏_{i<j} Res(f_i,f_j)², every prime dividing any disc(f_i) is found; hence the squarefree factorization of each disc(f_i) is available. P0 := rational primes dividing 2 disc f; |P0| ≤ h.
3. Maximal orders O_{K_i}: degree 1 trivially Z; degree 2 explicitly Z[(d_i+√d_i)/2] with fundamental discriminant d_i computed from the factored disc(f_i) (d_i = disc K_i divides disc f_i, so all its primes lie in P0); degree 3 via Poteaux–Weimann Theorem 1 given the squarefree factorization of disc(f_i), with the polynomial-BIT consequence as justified in the accepted review §1. These are the native maximal orders used by every later step; no abstract field isomorphism is introduced.
4. S_i := all prime ideals of O_{K_i} above primes in P0, decomposed in the maximal order (finite-algebra decomposition; for degree 2 explicit; for degree 3 as in the retained source). |S_i| ≤ d_i |P0|.

Bit sizes: log|d_i| ≤ h; log|disc K_i| ≤ h for all i; number of field factors ≤ n.

## 2. The two-primary interface: proved lemmas

Fix one field K = K_i, S = S_i, and a finite prime set T ⊇ S. Write ⟨T⟩ ⊆ Cl(K) for the subgroup generated by the classes of T, and K(S,2) := {α ∈ K^*/K^{*2} : v_P(α) even for all P ∉ S}. Let V: U_T/U_T² → F₂^{T∖S} be valuation parity at T∖S.

**Lemma A (odd index suffices).** If Cl(K)/⟨T⟩ has odd order, then the natural map ker V → K(S,2) is an isomorphism of F₂-vector spaces.

*Proof.* Well-defined: a T-unit has zero valuation outside T, and elements of ker V have even valuations at T∖S, so their classes lie in K(S,2). Injective: if u ∈ U_T and u = x² with x ∈ K^*, then every valuation of x outside T is zero, so x ∈ U_T and u is trivial in U_T/U_T². Surjective: let α ∈ K(S,2). Write (α) = b·a², where b is supported on S and a is the fractional ideal with v_P(a) = v_P(α)/2 for P ∉ S and v_P(a)=0 for P ∈ S. In G := Cl(K)/⟨T⟩ we have 2[a] = [(α)] − [b] = −[b] = 0 because b is T-supported. G has odd order, so multiplication by 2 is injective on G and [a] = 0 in G, i.e. [a] ∈ ⟨T⟩: a = (x)·c with c a T-supported fractional ideal and x ∈ K^*. Then u := α/x² has (u) = b·c², a T-supported ideal, so u ∈ U_T; its valuations at T∖S are those of c² (b is S-supported), hence even; and u ≡ α mod squares. The ideals a, b, c and x are proof-only witnesses; the algorithm never constructs them. ∎

**Lemma B (odd index ⇔ spanning mod 2).** For a finite abelian group G and subgroup H: G/H has odd order iff H + 2G = G.
*Proof.* G/H is a finite abelian group; it has odd order iff multiplication by 2 is surjective on it, i.e. iff 2(G/H) = G/H, i.e. H + 2G = G. ∎

**Lemma C (strict projects to ordinary safely).** Let π: Cl⁺(K) → Cl(K) be the surjection from the narrow to the ordinary class group, and let H⁺ ⊆ Cl⁺(K) be generated by the narrow classes of T, so π(H⁺) = ⟨T⟩. If H⁺ + 2Cl⁺(K) = Cl⁺(K), then ⟨T⟩ + 2Cl(K) = Cl(K), hence Cl(K)/⟨T⟩ has odd order.
*Proof.* Apply π, which is surjective and additive. Then Lemma B. ∎

Consequences. (i) We need NOT determine the sign of the norm of the fundamental unit, the kernel of π, or any relation among ambiguous forms; the direction strict ⇒ ordinary is the safe one, and the converse is never used. (ii) Full generation of Cl(K) (the GRH premise of the accepted review §4) is strictly stronger than what Lemma A needs. (iii) If the classes of T span Cl⁺(K)/2Cl⁺(K), Lemma A applies. This is a constructive sufficient condition; it is NOT an equivalence between Selmer dimension and class-group computation.

With Lemma A substituted for "T generates the class group" in the accepted review §4, every later step of that review (norm rows, atoms/parities, compact localization, real image, rank formula, majority vote) is unchanged, because those steps only use (a) a complete basis of U_T/U_T², (b) the binary kernel of V, and (c) T ⊇ S.

## 3. Degree-2 factors: strict 2-Sylow generators from B–S, converted to prime support

Let K = Q(√d), d the fundamental discriminant, t := number of distinct prime divisors of d, factorization of d known (§1).

### 3.1 Standard genus-theory facts used (not from the OCR)
(G1) dim_{F₂} Cl⁺(K)/2Cl⁺(K) = t − 1 (Gauss: the number of genera of forms of fundamental discriminant d is 2^{t−1}; B–S §3 cite Cox [4] for this material).
(G2) The genus characters χ_p (odd p | d) together with the 2-power-conductor character (χ_{−4}, χ_8 or χ_{−8} according to d) span Hom(Cl⁺(K)/2Cl⁺(K), F₂), with exactly one relation; the pairing Cl⁺/2Cl⁺ × (genus characters) → F₂ is perfect.
(G3) The value of a genus character on the class of a primitive form Q equals its value on any integer represented by Q and coprime to the character's conductor.
(G4) A primitive form represents an integer coprime to any prescribed modulus; concretely, for each prime q, at least one of Q(1,0)=a, Q(0,1)=c, Q(1,1)=a+b+c is prime to q, so a CRT-combined (x,y) mod 2|d| gives m = Q(x,y) coprime to 2d of polynomial size.
