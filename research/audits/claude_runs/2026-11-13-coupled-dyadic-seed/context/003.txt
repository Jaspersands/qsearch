# Dyadic ternary spinor generators: actual-source and resource audit

Status: CLOSED local source/compilation review. For a rank3 lattice at a specified dyadic prime of the native expanded number field, Beli's formulas can be compiled in polynomial BIT resources using the explicit small-rank construction and squareclass linear algebra below. ## Exact mathematical interfaces

Beli594–605 fixes a dyadic local field F, ring O, uniformizer pi and e=ord(2), with quadratic defect and N(a)=Norm(F(sqrt(a))/F). At615–621 the quadratic norm group is explicitly the kernel of the Hilbert character (a,−). Section1.3 defines the binary invariant a(L)=det(L)/q(x)^2 modulo unit squares at831–858. Definition3,1001–1025 (printedp29), gives G(a) through valuation/defect cases, cyclic squareclasses, subunit groups and quadratic norm groups; Lemma1.3.7,1027–1046, identifies it with the proper binary spinor-norm group. Retain the exceptional hyperbolic class G(−1/4)=unit squareclasses: it is not the whole F*/F*². This exception is a local unit-squareclass condition, not literal equality of two expanded global field elements.

Section1.5 Theorem1,2283–2304 (printedp70), applies to PROPERTY-A lattices. For a ternary good BONG with norms a1,a2,a3 and Ri=ord(ai), propertyA is R1<R3, by Lemma1.4.3(i),1839–1850. The theorem expresses theta as G(a2/a1) G(a3/a2) times the subunit group with exponent floor((R3−R1)/2). Use Beli's convention at606–610: exponent0 gives ALL squareclasses, not merely units. If R1=R3, the lattice lacks propertyA; section1.7,3413ff, says its group is units or all squareclasses. Theorem3,3610–3678 (printedpp112–113), decides containment in units using the two adjacent G groups and the parity condition (R2−R1)/2=e modulo2. The adjacent containment already forces the relevant differences even. Thus the ternary case requires only two binary-group formulas, one subunit factor or a units/full decision, not a search through a squareclass group. These are proper-rotation spinor groups, matching Kirschmer's theta(Aut+) convention.

## Good BONG is a short construction, not an unspecified long search

Definitions7/8,1706–1718 and1831–1835, define maximal norm splitting and good BONG. Lemma1.4.5,2000–2032 (printedpp60–61), explicitly repairs a pair of modular components by replacing the binary span Ox+Oy with O(x+z)+Oy, where z is a norm generator of the other component, and taking its orthogonal complement. The second case is obtained by dualization. Lemma1.4.6,2033–2054, starts from unary/modular-binary components and proves that each repair preserves all scales/ranks and other component norms while decreasing the NUMBER of bad component indices. Hence at most t repairs, t≤3 here. This is stronger than a merely decreasing valuation invariant. It supplies an executable bounded sequence once elementary lattice operations are paid.

For completeness, those elementary operations require no local representation oracle in rank3. Pick an entry of minimum Gram valuation. A diagonal minimum splits a unary line. Otherwise a minimal off-diagonal pair has both diagonals of larger valuation, so its2x2 determinant has precisely twice that minimum valuation and gives a modular binary block. Projection through this block yields the complement. This produces at most3 components. The norm ideal of a component is generated by q(e_i) and2B(e_i,e_j); its minimum valuation is attained by a basis vector, or by e_i+e_j when the cross term is strictly smaller than every diagonal. Thus a norm generator comes from at most6 candidates. Binary BONGs follow by choosing that generator and projecting the other vector. Dualization/complements use only matrices of size at most3. No p-adic square root, binary norm equation search, large residue enumeration or huge-unit elimination is hidden in this construction.

All these vectors/Gram matrices can be retained as exact elements of the ORIGINAL number field K. A constant number of3x3 rational-field operations and a bounded number of repairs preserve polynomial expanded coefficient height. Valuations of the resulting nonzero entries/minors are bounded by their expanded numerator/denominator norm heights, hence polynomial numerically in the native field/table/coefficient bits. This pays both valuation tests and any powers of pi used to normalize ai. It does not presume a primitive local irreducible polynomial or a local splitting field as advice.

## Compile norm/subunit groups without enumerating2^[F:Q2]

Let nloc=e*f≤[K:Q], with f the residue degree. Work in expanded finite rings O_K/P^m, not an enumerated list. Obtain a small pi in P outside P² by choosing an element of an expanded Z-basis of P not contained in P². A residue-field F2-basis b1,...,bf has polynomial-size lifts. The squareclasses are generated by

    pi, and 1+pi^j*b_l, 1≤j≤2e, 1≤l≤f.

Indeed U_j/U_(j+1) is the additive residue field, the residue multiplicative group has odd order and contributes no squareclasses, and U_(2e+1) consists of squares by Hensel's inequality. There are1+2ef=O([K:Q]) listed elements. No claim that all listed elements are independent is needed. For r≥1, the subunit group U_r F*² is spanned by the same generators with j≥r; r>2e is trivial. Beli's exponent0/full-group convention and its unit-only factors are treated separately.

A nondegenerate Hilbert-pairing matrix on the complete generator list gives all F2 relations and squareclass coordinates. Choose independent columns; their classes form a basis. Obtain any new class's coordinates by its pairing vector. A norm group N(a) is one linear kernel, intersections are linear intersections, and group products are linear spans. A unit's defect is the greatest r≤2e for which its class lies in U_r F*², or infinity for the trivial class. Consequently the G-case tests require no search over2^f residue elements or2^(nloc+2) squareclasses. Returning products of basis generators gives explicit polynomial-height representatives.

The needed symbol algorithm is actually source-paid: `research/audits/sources/global_isotropy/voight.txt`1226–1228, Theorem6.1, gives deterministic polynomial-time Hilbert symbols for number-field input, without fixed-degree restriction. Its bit encoding is109–136; its dyadic Algorithms6.2–6.6,1235–1349, increase valuation through at most2e levels and use residue Frobenius/linear equations, not brute-force residue searches (Remark6.4 explicitly contrasts a possibly nonpolynomial brute-force method). Thus e,f and residue arithmetic are charged uniformly. The modulus O_K/P^(2e+1) has polynomial logarithmic size. Exact field operations above avoid unstable approximate Gram pivots; after extracting the known valuation, unit squareclasses need only this bounded RELATIVE precision. No arbitrary-accuracy local square-root-output theorem is imported from Hilbert-symbol decision.

## Native boundary and remaining obligations

The local call assumes P has been found/factored and the supplied expanded maximal-order table is available. The native algorithm must charge integer factoring, prime decomposition, finite residue-field construction and all exceptional primes; they are not hidden advice. Standard HNF/finite-algebra operations have polynomial bit size in those expanded data, and any randomized factoring clock/failure budget remains charged. Output is a generating set modulo local squares, exactly what Kirschmer Theorem5.4.6(3),4519ff, requests, rather than explicit local rotation matrices. His actual5.4.4/4450–4454 only points to [Bel03] for dyadic computations; it does not itself prove the uniform-BIT compilation above.

The source therefore supports a constructive positive local gate after this explicit compilation; absence of a polynomial-complexity sentence in the thesis is not an exponential obstruction. Conversely, source formulas alone do not establish the full native decision theorem. Rational/common-ambient isometry, local integral transporters with known support, ray-class/unit completeness and global error clocks remain distinct obligations owned by the parent review. No novelty, speedup, hardness or full implementation is inferred.
