## Verdict

I could not produce a counterexample, and I believe the theorem is **correct as stated**. Every step of the proposed proof checks; the only places a counterexample could hide are listed below, and each is closed by a proved deduction. The conclusion is modest (an existence statement about rational norms, nothing integral), and that is exactly why it holds.

## Counterexample search (what would have to fail)

A counterexample needs $x\in L^*$ with $N(x)=a\in\mathbb Z[S^{-1}]^*$ such that no $y\in L^*$ with $N(y)=1$ has $\operatorname{div}(y)=\operatorname{div}(x)$ outside $S$. The proof produces such $y=\prod_j y_j(b_j)$. The possible failure points are:

1. The outside-$S$ divisor of $x$ at $p$ is not in the lattice generated by pair-syzygies.
2. A pair-syzygy is not realized by an actual prime of some $E_j$ with the claimed multiplicities.
3. $y_j(b_j)$ introduces new valuations outside $S$ (from $A_j$).
4. $N(y_j(b_j))\neq 1$, or sign/infinite-place drift.
5. A non-Galois subtlety in the tensor decomposition.

Each is checked and closed below. I also sanity-checked $L=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-5})$, $S=\{2,5\}$ (class group of $E_j=L$ generated by the prime over 2): the rational $S$-unit norms from $L^*$ are exactly $2^{2a}5^b$, all realized by $S$-units $2^a(\sqrt{-5})^b$ — consistent, not evidence.

## Verification

**(i) Prime/orbit correspondence and multiplicities.** For $p\notin S$ (so unramified in $L$), $L\otimes_\mathbb Q\mathbb Q_p=\prod_i L_{P_i}$ with $L_{P_i}=\mathbb Q_{p^{f_i}}$. Then
$$(L\otimes_\mathbb Q L)\otimes\mathbb Q_p=\prod_{i,k}\mathbb Q_{p^{f_i}}\otimes_{\mathbb Q_p}\mathbb Q_{p^{f_k}}=\prod_{i,k}\ \prod^{\gcd(f_i,f_k)}\mathbb Q_{p^{\operatorname{lcm}(f_i,f_k)}}.$$
Hence the primes $Q$ of $\prod_jE_j$ over $p$ are indexed by $(i,k)$ and $g=\gcd(f_i,f_k)$ copies; $Q$ lies over $P_i$ via $\iota_1$ and over $P_k$ via $\iota_2$ (the two tensor slots), with relative residue degrees $\operatorname{lcm}/f_i=f_k/g$ and $f_i/g$. So $N_{E_j/\iota_1L}(Q)=\iota_1(P_i)^{f_k/g}$, $N_{E_j/\iota_2L}(Q)=\iota_2(P_k)^{f_i/g}$, and the transported difference is $(f_k/g)P_i-(f_i/g)P_k$ as claimed. This is purely local and uses no Galois closure; the Frobenius-orbit language is just a restatement. $E_j$ is unramified over $p$, but this is not even needed—only the norm multiplicities are used. Diagonal pairs $i=k$ give $0$; harmless.

**(ii) Lattice generation.** Let $K=\ker\big((f_1,\dots,f_r):\mathbb Z^r\to\mathbb Z\big)$ and $M\subseteq K$ the sublattice generated by $s_{ik}=(f_k/g)e_i-(f_i/g)e_k$. Fix a prime $\ell$ and $i$ with $v_\ell(f_i)$ minimal. Then $v_\ell(f_i/\gcd(f_i,f_k))=v_\ell(f_i)-\min(v_\ell f_i,v_\ell f_k)=0$, so for $a\in K$ one subtracts $\big(a_k/(f_i/g)\big)s_{ik}\in\mathbb Z_{(\ell)}s_{ik}$ for each $k\ne i$ to kill all coordinates $k\neq i$; the remainder is $a_i'e_i\in K$, forcing $a_i'=0$. Thus $M_{(\ell)}=K_{(\ell)}$ for all $\ell$, hence $M=K$ (finitely generated $\mathbb Z$-modules with $M\subseteq K$ and equal localizations are equal). Proved. The $\mathbb Z$-linear combination then lifts to an **integral** divisor $D_j$ on each $E_j$, supported outside $S$, with $\sum_j\big(\iota_1^{-1}N_{\iota_1}D_j-\iota_2^{-1}N_{\iota_2}D_j\big)=\operatorname{div}(x)|_{\text{outside }S}$. (At $p\notin S$ the coefficient vector of $\operatorname{div}(x)$ lies in $K$ because $N(P_i)=p^{f_i}$ and $v_p(a)=0$.)

**(iii) Principalization and support control.** The hypothesis gives $A_j$ supported over $S$ with $D_j-A_j=(b_j)$ (fractional ideals; sign convention irrelevant). $N_{E_j/\iota_\nu L}$ maps a prime of $E_j$ over $p\in S$ to a power of a prime of $L$ over $p\in S$, and $N(\operatorname{div} b)=\operatorname{div}(N b)$. So $\operatorname{div}(y_j(b_j))$ agrees with the paired pushforward of $D_j$ outside $S$. Hence $x'=x/\prod_jy_j(b_j)\in O_L[S^{-1}]^*$.

**(iv) Norm, sign, infinite places.** $N_{L/\mathbb Q}\circ\iota_\nu^{-1}\circ N_{E_j/\iota_\nu L}=N_{E_j/\mathbb Q}$ (transitivity through the isomorphism $\iota_\nu:L\cong\iota_\nu L$), so $N(y_j(b_j))=1$ exactly, and $N(x')=N(x)=a$ **with sign**. No local or archimedean condition is imposed or needed: the theorem never asserts which $a$ are norms, only that an existing global preimage can be replaced by an $S$-unit preimage.

**(v) Non-Galois.** Nothing uses normality: $\iota_1,\iota_2:L\to E_j$ are injective since $L$ is a field and $E_j\neq0$; the local decomposition in (i) is an identity of étale $\mathbb Q_p$-algebras. The "Galois closure" in the proposal is cosmetic.

## Precise statement that holds

Let $L/\mathbb Q$ have degree $n$, $S$ a finite set of rational primes containing all primes ramified in $L$, $L\otimes_\mathbb QL=\prod_jE_j$. Assume $\mathrm{Cl}(E_j)$ is generated by classes of primes of $E_j$ over $S$, for every $j$. Then for every $a\in\mathbb Z[S^{-1}]^*$ (including $a<0$):
$$a\in N_{L/\mathbb Q}(L^*)\iff a\in N_{L/\mathbb Q}(O_L[S^{-1}]^*).$$
Moreover, for every $x\in L^*$ with $N(x)=a$ there exists $y\in L^*$ with $N(y)=1$ and $x/y\in O_L[S^{-1}]^*$. Enlarging $S$ preserves the hypothesis, so any target $a\in\mathbb Q^*$ is covered by adding $\operatorname{supp}(a)$ to $S$.

**Relative version (follows verbatim).** $K$ a number field, $L/K$ of degree $n$, $S$ a finite set of primes of $K$ containing those ramified in $L$, $L\otimes_KL=\prod E_j$, $\mathrm{Cl}(E_j)$ generated by primes over $S$. Then $N_{L/K}(L^*)\cap O_K[S^{-1}]^*=N_{L/K}(O_L[S^{-1}]^*)$. The local computation uses only that $\mathfrak p\notin S$ is unramified in $L$, which is all that was used.

## What does NOT follow

- No criterion for *whether* $a$ is a norm; no Hasse-type statement.
- No integral preimage in $O_L$, no control of the preimage's ideal, no "integral norm solving."
- The class-group hypothesis is a proof device; it is not computed by the algorithm but must be *guaranteed*, which currently requires GRH (Bach-type bound). Unconditionally the theorem is vacuous without a bound on $S$.

## Proof obligations / falsifiers for the downstream resource claims (not verified here)

1. **Discriminant bound**: $\operatorname{disc}(O_L\otimes_\mathbb ZO_L)=D_L^{\,n}\cdot D_L^{\,n}=D_L^{2n}$ (trace form of a tensor product is the tensor of trace forms); the maximal order of $\prod E_j$ contains it, so $\prod_j|D_{E_j}|\le D_L^{2n}$. This is a correct deduction. Each $|D_{E_j}|\le D_L^{2n}$ follows.
2. **Generator bound**: a GRH bound of the form "$\mathrm{Cl}(E_j)$ generated by primes of norm $\le c\log^2|D_{E_j}|$" gives $S\supseteq\{p\le c(2n\log D_L)^2\}\cup\{p\mid D_L\}\cup\operatorname{supp}(a)$, of polynomial size. The constant ($12$ in Bach's classical form, $48$ as attributed to BS §8.2) must be checked against the cited source; I cannot verify it. Only the *existence* of some explicit polynomial bound matters.
3. **Falsifiers for the pipeline**: (a) the primes of $L$ above $S$ number up to $n|S|$ — the $S$-unit computation must accept this; (b) membership of $a$ in $N(O_L[S^{-1}]^*)$ is a $\mathbb Z$-linear problem with torsion ($\pm1$) — straightforward but must be charged; (c) no computation in any $E_j$ is required by the theorem, so the degree-$n^2$ fields never enter the algorithm — any claimed cost that depends on computing in $E_j$ is unnecessary, and any claimed avoidance of GRH is unjustified.

**Falsifier for the theorem itself** (which I expect cannot be met): exhibit $L,S$ satisfying the hypothesis and $x\in L^*$ with $N(x)\in\mathbb Z[S^{-1}]^*$ whose outside-$S$ divisor class is not of the form $\sum_j(\iota_1^{-1}N_{\iota_1}-\iota_2^{-1}N_{\iota_2})(\text{principal divisor of }E_j)$ modulo primes over $S$. By (i)–(iii) this would contradict the lattice lemma or the class-group hypothesis.
