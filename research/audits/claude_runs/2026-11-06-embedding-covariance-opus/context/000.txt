    T_j(c)=(1-zeta^(-j))*tau(chi^j)*L(1,bar chi^j)/2.      (2)

## 4. Uniform exact fields and compatible embeddings

The actual Tal TR26-211 Theorem1.5 pays exact Jacobi sums uniformly in
poly(ell,logp). Its repeated sums give the binary sums by successive
ratios. Its compact outputs are not assumed already expanded: a binary
sum is integral with every conjugate of magnitude sqrtp, and a repeated
j-fold sum has magnitude p^((j-1)/2). Certified embedding/coefficient
rounding as in(10) therefore gives polynomial-BIT expanded coefficients.
To obtain OUR chi(g)=zeta, use the theorem's compatible reduction
prime zeta->r^(-1), since its convention is chi(x)->x^(-k). The LOCAL
prime in(7) is instead zeta->r. Do not conflate these two source roles.

no classical discrete logarithm is assumed.

Put J_a=J(chi^a,chi),1<=a<=ell-2, P_1=1,
P_j=product_(a<j)J_a and A=pP_(ell-1). Construct

    E=Q(zeta)[t]/(t^ell-A),   tau_j=t^j/P_j,
    sigma(t)=zeta^(-1)t,
    eta_0=(-1+sum_(j=1)^(ell-1)tau_j)/ell,
    eta_i=sigma^i(eta_0).                                 (8)

At the prime(zeta-r,p), each J_a reduces to a sum of a polynomial of
positive degree below p-1, namely x^(ak)(1-x)^k, so vanishes. Because

interpretation eta_i are the genuine Gaussian periods and generate K.
The sum of the tau_j is1+ell*eta_0, not eta_0 itself.

The eta_i form an INTEGRAL NORMAL BASIS of O_K. Their embedding matrix
is circulant with Fourier eigenvalues-1 and the nonzero tau_j. Its

eta_i*zeta^a,0<=a<=ell-2, is an integral basis of O_E, because the two
field discriminants p^(ell-1) and ell^(ell-2) are coprime.
