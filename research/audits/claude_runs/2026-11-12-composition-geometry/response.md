### 2. Torsion-freeness and H¹(X,F_ℓ)=0 directly in characteristic p (proved, no lift)

For Λ=Z/ℓ, ℓ≠p: P³∖X is affine of dimension 3, so by Poincaré duality and Artin vanishing H^i_c(P³∖X,Λ)=0 for i<3, hence H^i(P³,Λ)→H^i(X,Λ) is an isomorphism for i≤1 and injective for i=2 (weak Lefschetz with torsion coefficients). ### 4. Full rank and primitivity for every ℓ (proved)

**Lemma A (local).** Let z∈Z, δ_z the vanishing cycle, I_z inertia. In the Picard–Lefschetz exact sequence for a node on a curve (Λ=Z/ℓ, ℓ≠p, p≠2),
0→H¹(X_z,Λ)→H¹(X_u,Λ)→⟨·,δ_z⟩→Λ→H²(X_z,Λ)→H²(X_u,Λ)→0,
irreducibility of X_z gives H²(X_z,Λ)=Λ(−1) and H²(X_z)→H²(X_u) an isomorphism (degree). Consequently R¹π_*Λ = j_*j^*R¹π_*Λ on P¹. (This is the exact place where irreducibility of singular fibres is used; a two-component nodal fibre has δ=0 in H¹ and trivial local monodromy.)

**Lemma B (global).** Let S⊂V:=H¹(X_u,F_ℓ) be the span of the δ_z. By Lemma A and nondegeneracy of the Weil pairing on V, S^⊥ = {γ : ⟨γ,δ_z⟩=0 ∀z} = V^{π₁(U,u)} = H⁰(U,F) = H⁰(P¹,j_*F) = H⁰(P¹,R¹π_*F_ℓ). In the Leray spectral sequence of π: X̃→P¹, the edge map H¹(X̃,F_ℓ)→H⁰(P¹,R¹π_*F_ℓ) has cokernel inside d₂: E₂^{0,1}→E₂^{2,0}=H²(P¹,F_ℓ); but π has sections (the 45 exceptional curves), so π^*: H²(P¹)→H²(X̃) is split injective and d₂=0. Hence H⁰(P¹,R¹π_*F_ℓ) is a quotient of H¹(X̃,F_ℓ)=0, so S^⊥=0 and **S=V, rank 62, for every ℓ≠p**. This replaces "vanishing-cycle sheaf has full rank 62 because H¹(X)=0" by a proof whose only inputs are H¹(X,F_ℓ)=0, irreducible singular fibres, and a section. (It also shows E_{Z_ℓ} = ker(Gysin: H¹(X_u)→H³(X̃)(1)) = H¹(X_u,Z_ℓ) since H³(X̃,Z_ℓ)=0, so the source's V coincides with H¹(X_u,F_ℓ).)

### 7. From transvections to Sp₆₂(F_ℓ): what is proved and what is not

By Grothendieck, π₁^t(U_{F̄_p}) is topologically generated by the σ_z, so G is generated by the 220 transvections of Lemma A, and G⊂Sp(V) by Weil-pairing invariance.

**Decomposition lemma (proved).** Let W⊂V be a nonzero G-stable subspace and I_W={z: δ_z∈W}. For γ∈W, σ_z(γ)−γ = −ε⟨γ,δ_z⟩δ_z∈W, so either δ_z∈W or ⟨W,δ_z⟩=0. By Lemma B, I_W≠∅. Put W′=span{δ_z: z∈I_W}, W″=span{δ_z: z∉I_W}. Then W′⊥W″, W′+W″=V, hence V=W′⊕W″ is an orthogonal decomposition into G-stable nondegenerate subspaces, and W⊇W′. Thus G is irreducible iff the set of vanishing cycles cannot be partitioned into two nonempty mutually orthogonal subsets — equivalently iff the δ_z lie in a single G-orbit up to sign. **Conjugacy of vanishing cycles is therefore not merely sufficient but equivalent to irreducibility here.**

**Primitivity (proved; fills the gap in the source's Theorem 3.5 proof).** Hall's theorem requires irreducible, primitive, generated by transvections, ℓ odd. Suppose V=V₁⊕…⊕V_k, k≥2, permuted by G. A transvection T=1+c⟨·,δ⟩δ with T(V₁)=V₂≠V₁ forces V₂⊂V₁+⟨δ⟩, so dim V₁=dim V₂=1 and the T-orbit of V₁ consists of ℓ lines (T has order ℓ) in a 2-dimensional space, which cannot be in direct sum for ℓ≥3. Hence every generating transvection fixes every block, so G does, contradicting irreducibility. So irreducible + generated by transvections ⇒ primitive for ℓ≥3, and Hall's theorem applies verbatim (ℓ>53≫5). Thus:

**Conditional theorem.** If the δ_z are conjugate up to sign under π₁(U_{F̄_p}) (or under π₁(U_{Q̄}) for the lifted pencil, given §6), then G=Sp₆₂(F_ℓ) for every ℓ≠p, ℓ≥5, uniformly in the quintic, with no exceptional ℓ.

**The gap (concrete).** Conjugacy is a theorem for the *generic* Lefschetz pencil (Katz, SGA 7, via irreducibility of the dual hypersurface X̌⊂P̌¹⁹ of the 3-Veronese image; X̌ is a hypersurface since r=220>0) and for pencils whose parameter line L lies in a certain dense open subset of the Grassmannian of lines, because the special-pencil monodromy is only a normal subgroup of the universal one with quotient a quotient of π₁(open set). The candidate's certificate checks only the Lefschetz conditions: L meets X̌ transversally at 220 smooth points and misses Sing(X̌). To close the gap one must either (a) prove that this transversality already implies surjectivity π₁(L∖X̌)→π₁(P̌¹⁹∖X̌) — in characteristic 0 this is a Zariski–Lefschetz theorem for complements of a stratified hypersurface with L transversal to all strata (a line disjoint from Sing X̌ and transversal to X̌_reg is stratified-transversal, and a generic flag L⊂P²⊂…⊂P̌¹⁹ through L stays transversal by the usual dimension count), after which transitivity of π₁(P̌¹⁹∖X̌) on meridians of the irreducible X̌ gives conjugacy; this route is standard but the candidate must cite the exact statement and must transfer via §6, which then becomes load-bearing; or (b) certify membership of L in Katz's open set by an additional fixed-degree elimination, which the candidate neither describes nor costs. Neither is done in the supplied text. No counterexample is claimed; a decomposable pencil would require two mutually orthogonal families of nodes, which I cannot exclude or exhibit from the certificate.
