**Verdict: the displayed quantitative claim is correct as stated.** Both inequalities follow from three facts I can prove directly; no cited theorem is needed.

**1. Tensor multiplicity / irrep membership (proved).** For |S| = w, |S⟩ is a weight vector of ∧^w C^n with extremal weight ε_S (Weyl orbit of the highest weight ω_w = (1^w,0^{n−w})). In ∧^w ⊗ ∧^w the weight 2ε_S arises only from ε_S + ε_S, so its weight space is one-dimensional, spanned by |S⟩⊗|S⟩. Since 2ε_S is Weyl-conjugate to 2ω_w, the irrep V_{(2^w)} (Cartan product, two columns of length w) contains that weight; hence |S⟩⊗|S⟩ ∈ V_{(2^w)}, and V_{(2^w)} has multiplicity one (each copy would contribute an independent vector of weight 2ε_S). Pieri gives the full multiplicity-free decomposition ⊕_{k} V_{(2^{w−k},1^{2k})}, but only the one-dimensionality of the 2ε_S weight space is needed.

**2. Dimension (proved by hook–content).** Cells (i,1): content 1−i, hook w−i+2; cells (i,2): content 2−i, hook w−i+1. Product = [n!/(n−w)!]·[(n+1)!/(n+1−w)!] / [(w+1)! w!] = D²(n+1)/[(w+1)(n−w+1)] = D2. Checks: w=0 or w=n gives 1; n=2,w=1 gives 3 = dim Sym²C²; n=4,w=2 gives 20, and 20+1 = 21 = dim Sym²(C⁶). Symmetric under w ↔ n−w.

**3. Twirl and measure normalization (proved).** With Haar U, E_U [∧^w(U)^{†⊗2} |S⟩⟨S|^{⊗2} ∧^w(U)^{⊗2}] is supported in V_{(2^w)}, commutes with the (multiplicity-free) action, has trace 1, so equals Π_{(2^w)}/D2. Inverse-invariance of Haar makes the U vs U† twirl immaterial. Therefore

E_U Σ_S p_S(U)² = (D/D2)·‖Π_{(2^w)}(ψ⊗ψ)‖² ≤ D/D2,

with equality iff ψ is a Slater determinant. Consistency controls: lower bound 1/D (Cauchy–Schwarz) ≤ D/D2 = (w+1)(n−w+1)/[(n+1)D] holds; w=1 reproduces the exact Haar value E Σ_i |u_i|⁴ = 2/(n+1).

**4. Second inequality (proved).** For every fixed U and |A| ≤ R, Σ_{S∈A} p_S ≤ √R·(Σ_S p_S²)^{1/2}, so the event {max_A Σ_A p_S ≥ 1−ε²} is contained in {Σ_S p_S² ≥ (1−ε²)²/R}; Markov gives ≤ R·D/[D2(1−ε²)²]. The max is legitimately inside the probability because the containment is deterministic. Union bound over L passive frames V_l: V_l^†U is Haar whenever (V_l) is independent of U, including randomized lists; conditioning on the list and summing gives the factor L. For w = (n−1)/2 the bound is L·R·(n+3)/[4(1−ε²)² D] with D = binom(n,(n−1)/2) = 2^{Ω(n)}·n^{−1/2}, so polynomial L, R fail with probability 1 − 2^{−Ω(n)}.

**Counterexample search: none found.** Degenerate cases (w ∈ {0,1,n}, n=2) are tight or exact. The only way to break the bound is to leave its hypotheses: (a) frames that are not passive Slater bases — for a general orthonormal basis {f_S} of ∧^w the twirl of ψ⊗ψ has components in smaller irreps (e.g. V_{(1^{2w})} ≅ ∧^{2w}, one-dimensional at w = n/2), and f_S⊗f_S need not avoid them, so the D/D2 bound genuinely fails for active/Bogoliubov frames; (b) frames correlated with U.
