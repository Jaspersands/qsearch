## 6. Uniform compact precision and local arithmetic

Full unit rank is d=ell-1. For any nontrivial totally real unit, the norm
of u²-1 implies a conjugate has |log|u||>=log2/2. In the first d log
coordinates, the minimum sup norm is therefore>1/(4d): otherwise the
last log, minus their sum, also has absolute value<=1/4. Minkowski's cube
bound gives R_U>=(4d)^(-d). If a full-unit log matrix has entries bounded
by C>=1, its inverse row-sum norm is bounded by

    K0=d! C^(d-1) (4d)^d.

For target logs bounded by D1>=1, coordinate size is at most K0*D1.
Choose entrywise log precision, for example,

    epsilon <=1/[16 K0(1+d*K0*D1)].                      (11)

Matrix perturbation then leaves each coordinate within1/4 of its exact
integer, so rounding is justified. logK0=O(d logd+d logC) is polynomial.
This pays sigma-action coordinates and the integer Hilbert90 solve even
with a poorly conditioned returned unit basis.

In degree N=ell(ell-1), norm bounds bound the logarithms of a nonzero
rational leaf by a polynomial in N, its coefficient height and the
integral-basis embedding height. A compact product's resulting bound
may be numerically large, but its BIT length and the precision overhead
log(sum|exponents|) are polynomial. The known-generator calculations
here allow arbitrary polynomial BIT precision; no quantum Gauss or
Monte Carlo L1 estimator is asked for exponentially small error.

For local reduction put pi=tau(bar chi), v_P_L(p)=ell. For a small
rational leaf A/D with A integral and D>0, compute t=v_p(D) and

residue and valuation are

    lc(A)/[(D/p^t modp)*c_p^t],       k_A-ell*t.            (12)

Only polynomial leaf-height valuations and ideal powers occur. Binary
