# Cyclic support for multiplicative Galois descent

7 November 2026. Constructive candidate; support proof and compact-coordinate
resource obligations are under independent review. No complete algorithm,
novelty, classical separation or quantum execution is yet claimed.

## Native question and selection

Input: an explicit MAXIMAL-order basis/table for a Galois number field E/Q
of degree N, its N automorphisms G, integer matrices A_g in GL_r(Z) forming
a representation (binary entries), and nonzero EXPANDED elements a_gi in E.
All field/group/matrix data are charged input. The promise is that the
vectors a_g form a cocycle for the semilinear monomial action

    (g star x)_i = product_j g(x_j)^(A_g[i,j]),
    a_gh = a_g * (g star a_h).

Output NO or a COMPACT vector x in (E*)^r satisfying a_g=x/(g star x) for
every g. This is explicit rational-point/trivialization of a torus torsor
given by splitting-field descent data. E is SUPPLIED and charged; this is
not a polynomial algorithm for an arbitrary succinct torus whose splitting
field may have factorial degree. No target point, unit/class data or
auxiliary fields are supplied. Expanded point coordinates are not promised.

Target: bounded-error quantum polynomial BIT time in this explicit input,
under GRH. The proposed new operation is a uniform polynomial prime-support
reduction using every cyclic decomposition subgroup. Existing complete
quantum S-unit arithmetic would then supply the witnesses; no new quantum
primitive is proposed.

STATE/TRAPS and corpus been-tried/baselines were checked first. Independent
alternatives: sparse-circuit interpolation has a classical low-order-root
CRT method; thermal Pauli purification yields (rho+rho^2)/(1+Tr rho^2), not
a normalized square; central-simple-algebra splitting is already covered
by Kutas--Montessinos2307.00261v3. Character reconstruction has high external
value but supplied no new paid uniform-degree operation. These comparisons
do not prove any broad impossibility. The present question is selected for
a specific constructive route to the full native endpoint.

Prior art to retain: Magma's SUnitCohomologyProcess and classical Galois
cohomology/S-unit methods; Kutas--Montessinos uses bounded tensor powers for
central simple algebras. Qualitative torus resolutions and torsor theory
are established. Exact priority and overlap of the present quantitative
bound remain under source review. A field's explicit splitting description
must not be confused with an inexpensive construction of that field.

## Support lemma

Let S contain every rational prime ramified in E and every finite prime
support of the coordinates a_g. Suppose ordinary class groups of E^C are
generated by primes above S for EVERY cyclic subgroup C of G. Then any
solution x admits a solution x' all of whose coordinates are E-S-units.

Proof candidate. For p outside S choose P of E above p. Its decomposition
subgroup D is CYCLIC, since p is unramified. Let m=(v_P(x_i)) in Z^r.
The equation and unit cocycle give A_h m=m for every h in D.
Put F=E^D and q=P intersect F. Since E/F is Galois with group D and P has
decomposition group D, q has only P above it, with ramification index1.
Using the ordinary class-support hypothesis, choose t in F* with

    div_F(t) = q + a divisor supported above S.

It may be fractional. Define, independently of coset representatives,

    z_i = product_(g in G/D) g(t)^((A_g m)_i).

Both t and m are D-fixed. Reindexing shows h star z=z for every h in G.
At P only the identity coset contributes, so v_P(z)=m. At hP the valuation
is A_h m, which is exactly v_(hP)(x) by the original descent equation.
At other rational primes outside S it is zero. Thus x/z remains a solution
and removes the entire outside-S orbit over p. Repeat over the finitely
many outside-support primes of x. The x,t,m,F,z are PROOF OBJECTS only.
No algorithm reads the valuations of an unknown solution.

Equivalently, cyclic fixed-lattice bases induce a polynomial-rank sum of
permutation lattices mapping onto every cyclic invariant sublattice. No
enumeration of all subgroups, saturation by division, or Hasse principle
is used. Archimedean sign conditions are left inside the exact equation.

## Paying S

For F=E^C, the discriminant tower formula gives
|D_F|^[E:F] <= |D_E|. The verified GRH class-generator constant48 from
Biasse--Song2510.02280v2 Section8.2 therefore permits

    B0 = 48*max(1,bit_length(|D_E|))^2.

Include all rational primes <=B0 and all divisors of D_E and the input
coordinate supports. To pay those supports, CLEAR DENOMINATORS of each
a_gi: beta=d*a_gi integral, then factor d and |Norm(beta)|. Factoring
Norm(a_gi) alone is WRONG: valuations at distinct primes above the same
rational prime can cancel. All these integers have polynomial bit length.
Use charged checked Shor factoring and maximal-order prime decomposition.
The S list is polynomial numerically; no E^C or class group is computed.

## Endpoint still to verify

Compute a complete compact S-unit basis of E and torsion generator zeta
of order w. Every g preserves this group. Write

    g(epsilon_j) = zeta^(T_g[j]) product_l epsilon_l^(B_g[l,j]),
    g(zeta)=zeta^(k_g),
    a_gi = zeta^(v_g[i]) product_j epsilon_j^(U_g[i,j]).

For x_i=zeta^(t_i) product_j epsilon_j^(Z[i,j]), the equations are

    Z - A_g Z B_g^T = U_g,
    t - A_g(k_g t + Z T_g) = v_g (mod w).

Once these EXACT coordinates are paid, one integer HNF/SNF computation
decides feasibility and supplies polynomial-bit exponents. Output remains
a compact product. The main unresolved resource obligation is computing
these coordinates and certifying equality from compact atoms in polynomial
BIT time, including all torsion and conditioning; a real-log solve alone
is not sufficient. A complete-basis source event must pay NO answers.

Strong classical baselines: same support reduction plus classical S-units;
ordinary/permutation-action Hilbert90 is already classical linear algebra;
quadratic norm-torus examples admit conic methods. LLL is useful only under
its actual approximation/decoding conditions. No failure of one classical
method implies hardness. Classical compact product equality and known
relation algorithms must be checked before introducing a quantum subroutine.

Cheapest falsifiers: failure of primitive invariant lifting at an unramified
cyclic decomposition group; a second prime above q invalidating the local
construction; wrong monomial/coset orientation; cancellation hidden by using
only a coordinate's rational norm; missing torsion; exponentially ill-paid
coordinates; or an existing theorem already providing this entire endpoint.
No implementation is admitted until core and coordinate obligations close.
