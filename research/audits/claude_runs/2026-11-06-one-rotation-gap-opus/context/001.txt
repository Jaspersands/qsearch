Proof. The lower bound follows from a property of independent variables with finite support. For each variable, consider
the smallest positive probability of any of its values. A nonzero function of degree at most ℓ in these variables is
nonzero with probability at least the product of the ℓ smallest of these probabilities, using all variables when there are
fewer than ℓ. Here, degree counts the distinct variables with nonconstant basis elements in a product-basis monomial.
  To prove this property, choose a variable in a monomial of highest degree and the nonconstant basis element
appearing on that variable in the chosen monomial. Its coefficient is a nonzero function of degree at most ℓ − 1 on
the other variables. By induction, this coefficient is nonzero with at least the required product probability on the
remaining variables. Whenever it is nonzero, the full function is nonzero on at least one possible value of the chosen
variable. Multiplying these two probability bounds proves the claim.
  A Boolean quadratic has degree at most two in these variables, which gives the lower bound in Eq. (G2). We attain
the bound by multiplying affine indicators of the least-probable Bell labels on the two qubits with smallest ηi . For
n = 1, a single indicator suffices. Finally, the affine relabeling induced by a Clifford preserves both the polynomial
degree and the violation probability.
