                                                                                  Abstract
                                                     Let Z be a noetherian integral excellent regular scheme of dimension 2.
                                                 Let Y be an integral normal scheme endowed with a ﬁnite ﬂat morphism
                                                 Y → Z of degree 2. We give a description of Lipman’s desingularization
                                                 of Y by explicit equations, leading to a desingularization algorithm for Y .

1    Introduction
The existence of the desingularization of excellent surfaces (reduced noetherian
excellent schemes of dimension 2) is well known from the theoretical point of
view. However, in practice, the implementation of an algorithm of desingular-
ization in full generalities is rather complicated, see [3] and [7] for an overview
of various approaches. In view of applications in arithmetic geometry, a lot of
work also have been done for smooth projective curves C over a discrete val-
uation ﬁeld K. The aim is then to ﬁnd a projective regular scheme over the
valuation ring OK of K, with generic ﬁber isomorphic to C, see [8], [15] and the
references therein, when C is a cyclic cover of P1K , mostly assuming the residue
characteristic of K to be prime to the order of the cover. For hypersurfaces
in the aﬃne plane A2 over OK , see also [5] with some powerful applications in
number theory.
    In this work we deal with a special type of surfaces for which Lipman’s
method works very well and is completely explicit.
Theorem 1.1 (Lipman, [17]). Let Y be a noetherian integral normal excellent
surface. Consider a sequence of birational morphisms
                        ... → Yn → · · · → Y1 → Y0 := Y                       (1.1)
where each Yi+1 → Yi is the normalization of the blowing-up of Yi along some
singular closed (reduced) point. Then the sequence is necessarily ﬁnite.
    The surfaces we are interested in are double covers of regular surfaces. For
simplicity we only work with noetherian excellent schemes (e.g., algebraic vari-
eties over a ﬁeld, schemes of ﬁnite type over Dedekind domains of characteristic
0 or complete discrete valuation rings, see [9], §8.2.3).
Deﬁnition 1.2 Let Z be an integral noetherian regular scheme. We deﬁne a
double cover of Z as a reduced scheme Y endowed with a ﬁnite ﬂat morphism
of degree 2
                                 ψ : Y → Z.
We call Y a normal double cover of Z if moreover Y is integral and normal.
Example 1.3 Let C be a hyperelliptic curve over Q, with the canonical degree
2 morphism C → P1Q . Let Z = P1Z and let W be the normalization of Z in the
function ﬁeld K(C) of C. Then Z is a regular excellent surface, and W → Z is
a normal double cover of Z. The surface W is a so called Weierstrass model of
C. It is singular in general. A resolution of singularities on W gives a projective
regular model of C over Z.
Example 1.4 More generally, let Z be an integral noetherian excellent surface.
Let L be a (possibly inseparable) quadratic extension of the function ﬁeld K(Z)
of Z and let Y be the normalization of Z in L. Then Y → Z is a normal
double cover. Indeed, Y → Z is ﬁnite because Z is excellent. The scheme Y a
noetherian integral normal surface, hence Cohen-MaCaulay, hence ﬂat over Z
because the latter is regular.

regular model over R of a smooth curve ZK over K := Frac(R) and YK → ZK

    Our initial motivation is to give an easily implementable algorithm to ﬁnding
a desingularization of the surface W in Example 1.3, in the spirit of Tate’s
algorithm for elliptic curves [21]. Indeed, a regular model of C over Z allows to
compute some important arithmetic invariants of C as its Artin conductor, the
Tamagawa number of its Jacobian J(C) as well as the (ﬁnite part of) L-function
of J(C). Applications will be given in [13]. An implementation in PARI/gp [18]
by B. Allombert is ongoing.
    Now let us describe the content of this paper. In § 2, we introduce our
multiplicity λp (Y ) for any double cover Y → Z of regular scheme and for any
point p ∈ Y . This invariant already appeared in various works, when Z is a
smooth surface over a perfect ﬁeld, or in the context of Hironaka’s characteristic
polyhedra (see e.g. [4], §5). In our setting it is particularly simple to deﬁne.
    Normalized blowing-ups are deﬁned in § 3 as blowing-ups followed by a nor-
malization. These are the morphisms appearing in the top line of Sequence (1.1).
We prove the main theorem 3.4 about the description of a sequence of normal-
ized blowing-ups. The equation of each Yi is given in Proposition 3.3.
    In § 4, we give a bound on the sum of the multiplicities running through
the closed points of the exceptional locus of the normalized blowing-up Y1 →
Y (Theorem 4.6). Finally in § 5 we apply the above results to describe a
desingularization algorithm for the double covers of regular excellent surfaces.
   The general setting in this work is the following: Z is an integral noetherian
excellent regular scheme, Y → Z is a double cover. No assumption are made on
the residue ﬁelds of Z. Except in § 2, Z will be of dimension 2.


2     The multiplicity λp (Y )
The multiplicity of Y at a closed point p is a local invariant depending only
on OY,y . Let A be an integral noetherian regular ring. Let B = A[y] be an
A-algebra with y 2 + ay + b = 0 for some a, b ∈ A. We will deﬁne a multiplicity
λm (B) ∈ N for any maximal ideal m of A. It is a measure of singularity of B at
the maximal ideals lying over m.

2.1    Deﬁnition and basic properties for local rings
Let us ﬁrst recall some elementary facts on regular rings. Let m be a maximal
ideal of A, let d = dim Am and k = A/m. Consider
                           Grm (A) = ⊕ℓ≥0 mℓ /mℓ+1 .
It is canonically a homogeneous k-algebra. As A is regular, it is isomorphic to
Symk (m/m2 ), the polynomial ring in d variables over k.
    For any element non zero element a ∈ A, ordm (a), or simply ord(a) if m
is implicitly given, is the supremum of the integers n such that a ∈ mn . It

                                        4

depends only on the ideal aA. By convention ord(0) = +∞. We have ord(ab) =
ord(a) + ord(b) for a, b ∈ A \ {0}. As Grm (A) is an integral domain, ord extends
to a valuation on Frac(A).

Deﬁnition 2.1 Keep the above notation. If ordm (a) = ℓ 6= +∞, the initial
form of a is the image of a in mℓ /mℓ+1 . The latter can be viewed as the k-
vector space of homogeneous polynomials in d variables and of degree ℓ.

Lemma 2.2. Let R be a noetherian ring. Let D be an R-algebra, free of rank
2 as R-module. Then there exists y ∈ D such that D = R ⊕ Ry, and we have
for some a, b ∈ R
                             y 2 + ay + b = 0.
Moreover, any other z such that D = R[z] satisﬁes z = uy + v with u ∈ R∗ and
v ∈ R.
Proof. Locally on Spec R, R is a direct summand in D ([2], II, §3, Proposition
6 with M = R and N = D), then apply op. cit., Cor. 1 to Proposition 12
with M = D and N = R, which implies that R is a direct summand in D. The
existence of the equation on y and the statement on z are obvious.

duced A-algebra, free of rank 2 as A-module. Write B = A[y] with

                                y 2 + ay + b = 0

for some a, b ∈ A.
    Fix a maximal ideal m ⊂ A. For each generator y as above, denote by

                       λm (y) = min{2 ordm (a), ordm (b)}.

Q≥0 considered by [4], §5, in a much more general context involving Hironaka’s
characteristic polyhedra. We use the notation λm (B) to keep coherent with our
previous work [12], Déﬁnition 10 (with r = 1).

Lemma 2.7. Let R be an integral normal domain, let D = R[T ]/(T 2 + aT + b).
(1) The ring D is not integral if and only if T 2 + aT + b is reducible in R[T ].
(2) The ring D is non-reduced if and only if T 2 + aT + b = (T + c)2 for some
    c ∈ A.
Proof. The “if” part in both statements are obvious. Let us prove the converse.
Let K = Frac(R). As D is ﬂat over R, D → D ⊗R K is injective. If D is non-
integral, then D ⊗R K is non-integral and we have T 2 +aT +b = (T +c1 )(T +c2 )
for some c1 , c2 ∈ K. This implies that the ci ’s are integral over R, hence belong
to R. The proof is similar for the non-reduced case.

Remark 2.8 Keep the notation of Proposition 2.6. We have ∆B/A = 0 if and
only if B is integral and Frac(B)/Frac(A) is an inseparable extension.

Proposition 2.9. Keep the notation of Deﬁnition 2.3.
(1) The Artinian ring B/mB is reduced if and only if λm (B) = 0. If k := A/m
    is perfect or of char(k) 6= 2, then this is equivalent to A → B étale above m.
(2) The ring B is regular at all maximal ideals lying over m if and only if
    λm (B) ≤ 1.
(3) If λm (B) ≥ 1, then there is only one maximal ideal n ⊂ B lying over m,
    A/m → B/n is an isomorphism and we have Bn = Bm .
Proof. By Lemma 2.5 we can suppose A is local. Suppose char(k) = 2 (otherwise
the proof is easier). Let λm (y) = λm (B).
    If λm (B) = 0 and ordm (a) = 0, then A → B is étale and B is regular.
Suppose λm (B) = 0 and ordm (a) > 0. Then B/mB ≃ k[T ]/(T 2 + b̄). If
T 2 + b̄ ∈ k[T ] is reducible, there exists α ∈ A∗ such that b − α2 ∈ m. This
implies that λm (B) ≥ λm (y + α) > 0. Contradiction. So B/mB is a radicial
quadratic extension of k and B is regular.
    If λm (B) ≥ 1, then a, b ∈ m and B/mB ≃ k[T ]/(T 2). This implies that B is
local with maximal ideal n, not étale over A, and we have k ≃ B/n. The equality
Bn = Bm comes from the fact that for any u ∈ B \ n, the norm NB/A (u) ∈ A\ m.
    Finally when λm (B) ≥ 1, we have y ∈ n and the relation y 2 + ay + b = 0
implies that B is regular if and only if b ∈/ m2 , equivalently, λm (B) = 1.

3.6    Simultaneous resolution of singularities
Let f : X → Y be a ﬁnite morphism of integral noetherian excellent surfaces.
S. Abhyankar [1] asked for the existence of resolutions of singularities X̃ → X
and Ỹ → Y such that f extends to a ﬁnite morphism X̃ → Ỹ . He proved
that the answer is no if deg f > 3, and is positive when f is a Galois cover
of degree ≤ 3 of algebraic surfaces over a ﬁeld of characteristic diﬀerent from
deg f . For normal surfaces ﬁbered over a discrete valuation ring, similar results
are obtained in [14], §7 without assumption on the residue characteristics.
Corollary 3.12. Let f : X → Y be a ﬁnite morphism of degree 2 of integral
noetherian excellent surfaces. Then there are resolutions of singularities X̃ → X
and Ỹ → Y such that f extends to a ﬁnite morphism X̃ → Ỹ .
Proof. Replacing Y with a resolution of singularities of Y and then X by the
normalization of Y in K(X), we can assume that Y is regular. Then X → Y is
a normal double cover (Example 1.4). Applying Theorem 3.4 to X → Y then
yields a simultaneous resolution of singularities.

5.3     Desingularization algorithm
Let Z be an integral noetherian excellent regular surface. Let Y → Z be a
double cover. We work locally on Z and suppose Z = Spec A is aﬃne.
   (1) Find the normalization of Y (§ 5.1), then suppose Y is normal.
   (2) Find the singular points of Y (§ 5.2).



                                               29

   (3) Fix a singular point lying over a closed point q0 ∈ Z. Compute λq0 (Y )
using Lemma 2.10.
   (4) Consider the normalized blowing-up Ỹ → Y along the point p0 lying
over q0 . Use the aﬃne equation of Ỹ given Proposition 3.3 to ﬁnd the singular
points of Ỹ lying over p0 .
   (5) Repeat the above process for all singular points of Y . At the end we get
a new normal double cover of an integral noetherian excellent regular surface.
   (6) Go back to (2).
