**Candidates compared (two, then one selected)**

- B (rejected): classical-input realisation of the growing-degree hidden-polynomial-graph problem (circuit for h(t,y)=g(y−Q(t)), g injective). It inherits the repo's unpaid growing-degree solver/readout and has no clear external customer; nothing new to disprove in one pass.
- A (selected): exact middle-cohomology factor of a smooth surface over a prime field, attacked not by a "spectral operation on H²" but by a **mod-ℓ Schoof-type reduction to curve zetas of ℓ-torsion covers**, where the only quantum step is the known genus-polynomial curve-zeta algorithm (Kedlaya, remembered as quant-ph/0411623 — unverified). Overlap risk flagged in (4).

**(1) Problem / input / verification output**
Input: prime p, degree d fixed (canonical case d=4, K3 surface), a smooth surface X⊂P³_{F_p} given by its equation, plus a classically chosen Lefschetz pencil (base-point blow-up X̃→P¹, fibers plane curves of genus g=(d−1)(d−2)/2, singular set S, |S|=d(d−1)²).
Output: P₂(X,T)∈Z[T] (degree b₂=22 for d=4), the full H² Frobenius characteristic polynomial.
Verification: integrality, functional equation, q-Weil bounds, and for the finitely many k where brute force is affordable, Tr(Frob^k)=#X(F_{p^k})−1−p^{2k}. (Honest: no short certificate; exact verification is as hard as the problem.)
External value: zeta/Picard-number/Tate-conjecture data for K3 surfaces at large p, where current practice is p-adic (cost poly(p) or ~p^{1/2+o(1)}; Abbott–Kedlaya–Roe, Harvey, Costa–Harvey–Kedlaya — remembered, unverified).

**(2) Coherent source and new identity**
Coherent source: for each small prime ℓ≠p, the F_ℓ-local system F=R¹π_*F_ℓ ≅ J[ℓ] (fiber Jacobian ℓ-torsion) on U=P¹−S, with geometric monodromy G⊂Sp_{2g}(F_ℓ) and Galois closure cover Z_ℓ→U, [Z_ℓ:U]≤|Sp_{2g}(F_ℓ)|=O(ℓ^{g(2g+1)}) (ℓ²¹ for g=3). The quantum oracle actually invoked: Kedlaya-type computation of the integer zeta functions of the compact curves Z̄_ℓ/H for cyclic H⊂G, genus O(ℓ^{g(2g+1)}|S|)=poly(log p) for fixed d.
Identity to establish (the "new operation"): define, for an F_ℓ[G]-module M with compatible Frobenius, L_U(M)=∏_i charpoly(Frob|H^i(Ū,M))^{(−1)^i}. Long exact sequences with Frob-equivariant connecting maps and cd(Ū)=1 make L_U multiplicative, hence a function on K₀(F_ℓ[G]). Artin's induction theorem (unverified recall) gives [V]=Σ_H a_H[F_ℓ[G/H]] (H cyclic, a_H∈Q), and F_ℓ[G/H]-cohomology is H^*(Z̄_ℓ/H minus cusps, F_ℓ), whose Frobenius char poly is the mod-ℓ reduction (with the explicit Tate-twist by p^{−1}) of the integer zeta of Z̄_ℓ/H plus explicit cusp permutation factors. Therefore charpoly(Frob|H¹(Ū,J[ℓ]))^m = ∏_H (explicitly corrected zeta of Z̄_ℓ/H mod ℓ)^{m a_H}, uniquely solvable in F_ℓ[T] by unique factorisation. Then P₂ mod ℓ follows from H¹(P¹,j_*J[ℓ]) (parabolic image, local Picard–Lefschetz data at S, algebraic classes from the pencil) and torsion-freeness of H²(X,Z_ℓ) (Lefschetz hyperplane). CRT over O(log p/log log p) primes ℓ=O(log p) with |coeff|≤C(22,i)p^i recovers P₂ exactly.

**(3) Resource target and baseline**
Quantum: fixed d=4; per ℓ: classical construction of J[ℓ] division equations over F_p(t) and the cyclic-quotient covers (poly(ℓ^{O(1)}), Schoof–Pila-style over a function field — paid, exponent unverified); one Kedlaya run per cyclic subgroup class (poly(ℓ) of them), each poly(genus, log p)=poly(log p). Total poly(log p) with a large but fixed exponent. Growing d is NOT claimed: cover degree is exponential in g², so advantage is only in log p at fixed d.
Classical strongest baseline (unverified recall): p-adic/controlled-reduction methods ~p^{1/2+o(1)} to poly(p) for fixed d; naive counting p^{22}; no known classical poly(log p) algorithm for generic quartic K3 (the classical analogue of this route needs zetas of genus-(log p)^{21} curves, exponential for Schoof–Pila).

**(4) Single decisive unpaid proof obligation**
Prove the identity in (2) *with arithmetic Frobenius*: the covers Z_ℓ/H must be F_p-rational (H normalised by the arithmetic monodromy Γ⊇G), the Brauer-character/Artin argument must be run on Γ-sets, and the parabolic/cusp/bad-fiber corrections must be shown to be determined by data computable classically from the pencil. Prerequisite admission check by parent: whether arXiv2511.02262 ("surface P¹") already executes exactly this torsion-cover reduction for arbitrary-genus Lefschetz pencils; if yes, candidate A is subsumed and no candidate survives.

**(5) Cheapest falsifier**
Exact classical test, g=1, ℓ=2, G⊂GL₂(F₂)≅S₃: the identity predicts charpoly(Frob|H¹(Ū,E[2])) = L(curve {x³+a(t)x+b(t)=0} minus bad points, F₂) / L(U,F₂). Take a rational elliptic surface over F₅ or F₇ (e.g. y²=x³+t x+(t³+1)), compute L(E/F_q(t),T) mod 2 by exhaustive point counts over F_{q^k}, k≤deg, and compare after the explicit parabolic correction. Disagreement kills the identity (and hence the candidate) at zero quantum cost; agreement advances to ℓ=3 (|GL₂(F₃)|=48, cyclic-quotient covers via the 3-division field) and then a genus-2 or genus-3 pencil. Analytic falsifier: exhibit two pencils with identical corrected cover zetas but different P₂ mod ℓ (would show the corrections are not zeta-determined).

If (4)'s admission check fails or the ℓ=2 test fails, the honest state is: no classical-input candidate survives, and the least speculative new operation to attack is precisely this mod-ℓ parabolic L-function identity, not a generic spectral/PGM tool.
