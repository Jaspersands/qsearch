# Exact excerpt from PRIME_INDEX_FACTORIAL_RESIDUE_NORMALIZATION.md Section5

Choose any nonzero unit-coordinate vector v. The vectors
v,Sv,...,S^(ell-2)v form a rational basis, since Phi_ell is irreducible.
The inverse map1->v,zeta->Sv identifies U with a fractional ideal D_U
of R. Thus C corresponds to the PRINCIPAL fractional ideal J=I*D_U.
Its presentation has polynomial bit size. This does not make an arbitrary
compact generator safe to expand: eta^(2^B) in Z[zeta_5] is the explicit
counterexample to that shortcut.

Find a compact generator a of J using the existing S-unit theorem in
Q(zeta), with S its factored ideal support, then solve the valuation
vector of J. To find this support, first choose a known integer D0 with
D0*J integral; factor BOTH D0 and the integer norm N(D0*J), then decompose
those rational primes in R. Factoring the rational norm N(J) alone is
insufficient: a quotient of distinct equal-norm primes can have norm1.
Shor and finite-field ideal factorization pay this corrected step.
Principality is guaranteed by(3), not
supplied as a hidden generator. An exact ideal check below certifies a
successful output; capped retries amplify this stage separately.

### Balance height, without solving full-unit BDD

Let m=(ell-1)/2 and use the explicit circular units
u_i=(1-zeta^i)/(1-zeta),2<=i<=m. Their m-coordinate logarithmic columns
B form a basis of the zero-sum real subspace. Every entry has absolute
value<=logell. The underlying circulant log matrix has nonprincipal
Fourier eigenvalues of magnitude sqrtell*|L(1,psi)|/2 for the even
characters psi modulo ell. All nonreal psi have the retained effective
inverse-log lower bound. If the real even quadratic character exists,
use its SEPARATE class formula

    L(1,psi)=2hR/sqrtell >=2log((1+sqrt5)/2)/sqrtell.

Thus all relevant eigenvalues are bounded below by an effective absolute
constant c0>0. The difference matrix with columns e_i-e_1 has least
singular value1; consequently B has least singular value at least c0.
For ell3 the zero-dimensional balancing step is empty.

For the known compact a, put mu=log|N(J)|/(ell-1). Express
Log(a)-mu*1 in B to coordinate error<=1/4 and round to integers. Divide a
by the resulting product of u_i to get a'. Residual coordinates have
absolute value<=3/4, hence

    |log|sigma(a')|| <= |mu|+(3/4)(m-1)logell.              (9)

This is ordinary basis rounding to get ANY generator of controlled BIT
height. The index of circular units in full units is irrelevant; there
is no closest-unit requirement and no h^+ coset enumeration. Large
compact exponents enter the required precision through their bit lengths.
No blanket inverse-log lower bound for REAL Dirichlet characters is used.

Take a known integer D0 with D0*J contained in R, from its fractional-ideal
presentation. Then b=D0*a' is integral. Do not multiply leaf denominators
to huge powers. Equation(9) bounds all embeddings by
M=D0*exp(|mu|+O(ell logell)); logM is polynomial in the original input.
Writing b=sum_(j=0)^(ell-2)b_j*zeta^j, with b_(ell-1)=0, gives

    b_j=(1/ell)sum_(a=1)^(ell-1)
            (zeta^(-aj)-zeta^a)*sigma_a(b),   |b_j|<=2M.  (10)

Evaluate these KNOWN compact embeddings by certified log-magnitude and
phase arithmetic, to absolute error<1/8, and round the b_j to integers.
Precision is polynomial in logM, leaf heights and log(sum|exponents|).
Even very large intermediate magnitudes need no expanded intermediate
products. Check exactly that(b/D0)=J. Map this polynomial-BIT expanded
R-generator back to U; it yields the required compact c0 using ordinary
binary exponents. An arbitrary PIP answer is not used without this repair.

The log-basis argument is independently reviewed. Its purpose differs
from [CDPR Theorem4.1](https://sites.cc.gatech.edu/fac/cpeikert/pubs/logunit.pdf),
which recovers a prescribed short generator only for a circular-unit
multiplier, then discusses h^+ coset enumeration for full units.
