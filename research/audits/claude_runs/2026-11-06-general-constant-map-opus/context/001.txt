Input: F=Q(T_1,...,T_s)[Y]/f, f monic irreducible of CHARGED numerical Y-degree delta. k nonzero u_i in canonical Y-power basis; coefficients of f and u_i are explicit quotients of sparse integer polynomials in T, with binary exponents and coefficients. Input length L includes delta,s,k and all sparse data. No inverses, factors or constants supplied.

Desired lemma: there is a randomized polynomial(L)-time algorithm choosing an integer m with NUMERICAL phi(m)<=poly(L) and exponents a_j modulo m such that all coefficient denominators are nonzero at T_j=zeta_m^a_j and every u_i becomes a unit in A=Q(zeta_m)[Y]/f_specialized. This can be checked exactly in polynomial time if m and phi(m) are polynomial. Specialized coefficients then have polynomial bits because roots of unity are periodic and sparse sums remain small. Irreducibility of f_specialized is NOT required. The determinant/norm of each u_i must be nonzero after specialization. Those norm polynomials have polynomial-size determinant circuits and bounded SYNTACTIC degree in the input monomials, but may have exponentially many monomials and exponentially large numerical T degree.

Why useful: Alg={e in Z^k: product u_i^e is algebraic over Q} can plausibly be computed by the existing differential congruence/LLL machinery. If the desired lemma holds, Lenstra-Silverberg relations of the original small specialized tuple, intersected with Alg, would recover full Rel. Numerical degree of A is paid. Ordinary rational specialization such as T=2 can expand 2^N to exponential bit length; huge-order roots of unity can create exponential field dimension. Neither is allowed for free.

Cheapest attacks: binomial T^N-1 with N divisible by many small orders; norms/resultants whose cyclotomic factors are much richer than their sparse inputs; repeated roots and specialization zero divisors. Efficient CIT at a GIVEN binary order m does not prove a low-degree good m exists. No polynomial small-order hitting theorem is assumed.
