# Structured EDCP: Fourier Erasure And Its Classical Byproduct

Date: 2026-09-27. LOCAL DERIVATION / REVIEW PENDING.

This analyzes an explicit decoder attempt: compute the total frequency,
Fourier-measure the old coefficient registers, and retain the frequency
register for correction or a more elaborate quantum measurement. It does
not supply a decoder, a general quantum lower bound, or a novelty claim.
Production wiring and routine test execution remain Gemini's work.

## 1. Outcome

The simple final-Fourier version is exactly local Fourier readout followed
by a random classical mask. More strongly, in the current wide-Gaussian
regime the ENTIRE post-measurement classical-quantum state is close to a
classically sampled mixture of known Fourier-basis states. This comparison
survives arbitrary subsequent quantum channels, not only linear corrections.

It does NOT make an arbitrary quantum postprocessor classical. It reduces
this particular source of post-measurement coherence to quantum computation
on classical local-readout data. A quantum lattice decoder on such data
would still be a substantial algorithm; the erasure step does not provide it.

An intermediate result updates the earlier narrow-width classical baseline:
the SAME 4,3,3-block references in the new hardness-linked source lanes also
have enough information for unrestricted classical ML decoding. That ML
search is not known to be efficient. Information sufficiency is not the
desired computational separation.

## 2. Exact Circuit Identity

Let G=Z_Q^D, D=d*L, N=Q^D, and let v be the public row vector with entries
v_(l,i)=a_l*q^i mod Q. No unit-label or surjectivity promise is required.
Use any normalized amplitude vector g(c), even complex, and define

    f(c)=v dot c mod Q,
    |psi_s>=sum_c g(c)*omega^(s*f(c)) |c>,
    hat_g(p)=N^(-1/2)*sum_c g(c)*omega^(-p dot c).

Compute f into a fresh register u, QFT the c registers with the negative-
sign convention, and measure them, retaining the outcome y in G. The
unnormalized residual vector is

    a_y^s(u)=N^(-1/2)*omega^(s*u)
                         *sum_(f(c)=u) g(c)*omega^(-y dot c). (1)

Its squared norm p_y is independent of s. Keeping y COHERENT instead of
measuring it is a different procedure and is outside the result below.

For analysis, QFT the residual register too. Its unnormalized amplitudes are

    b_y^s(z)=Q^(-1/2)*hat_g(y+(z-s)*v),
    Pr[y,z|s]=(1/Q)*|hat_g(y+(z-s)*v)|^2.                  (2)

Thus the full measured law is obtained exactly by

    sample p from the original local Fourier readout of |psi_s>;
    sample z uniformly and independently in Z_Q;
    set y=p-z*v; return (y,z).                              (3)

No secret is needed to perform this postprocessing. The relevant sampler
for p on retained classical input is described in
`STRUCTURED_EDCP_CLASSICAL_READOUT_SIMULATION.md`; its entire ledger remains.
For physical direct-phase preparation the internally known fresh tags also
specify each product state's local Fourier law directly.

A classically chosen linear phase correction exp(-2*pi*i*gamma(y)*u/Q)
changes the final outcome to z-gamma(y). The choice of gamma can be nonlinear
or randomized; its computational cost is still charged. This establishes
the exact measured comparator without guessing any lattice alias.

Discarding y instead gives the reduced state diag(r), where
r(u)=sum_(f(c)=u)|g(c)|^2. It is independent of s. Merely deleting the
measurement record cannot produce the desired coherent phase state.

## 3. A Bound On The Full Residual Quantum State

Define the cq state AFTER the analytic final QFT by

    rho_s=sum_y |y><y| tensor |b_y^s><b_y^s|.

The vectors are unnormalized. Let D_z dephase the residual register in its
z basis, keeping y and its Born weights. The state D_z(rho_s) is exactly
generated by (3), preparing the known computational basis state |z>.
Apply an inverse QFT to this prepared register if a later circuit expects
the original u representation.

Put nu(p)=|hat_g(p)|^2, and define the UNCAPPED overlap sum

    A(v)=sum_(r=1)^(Q-1) B_nu(r*v),
    B_nu(w)=sum_p sqrt(nu(p)*nu(p+w)).                      (4)

For every classical secret s,

    D_trace(rho_s,D_z(rho_s)) <= min(1,A(v)/2).             (5)

Proof: for each y, split its off-diagonal density matrix into two-by-two
Hermitian pieces indexed by unordered pairs z,z'. Each piece contributes
|b_y^s(z)*b_y^s(z')| to half the trace norm. Sum the triangle bound, substitute
(2), then translate y. Each nonzero difference r=z'-z occurs Q times, giving
exactly A(v)/2. This argument uses neither a most-likely-alias oracle nor
Gaussian positivity; those enter only when bounding (4) for our source.

An alternative diagnostic is

    D_trace(rho_s,D_z(rho_s)) <= sqrt(2*e_ML(v)),
    e_ML(v)=1-sum_y max_z |b_y^s(z)|^2.

Here e_ML is the optimal error for the classical channel with a uniformly
random shift along v. It need not be efficiently computed. Equation (5) is
the stronger useful analytic bound in our regime, and avoids introducing
a decoder into the simulator.

CAUTION: ML failure is at most min(1,A), but that CAPPED failure bound cannot
be halved to obtain (5). At v=0,Q=5, ML failure is 4/5 and residual dephasing
distance is also 4/5. The raw A=4 gives a valid bound; halving min(1,A) would
incorrectly give 1/2.

The approximation holds for a classically indexed secret ensemble, including
classical side records under the specified joint-source contract. It is not
a diamond-norm approximation on arbitrary coherent superpositions of secrets.

## 4. Why The Wide Gaussian Has A Small Overlap Sum

Use the EXACT periodized scalar amplitude from the local-readout note:

    h(j)=exp(-pi*j^2/sigma^2),
    g_Q(x) proportional to sum_(k in Z) h(x+kQ),
    mu_Q(x)=g_Q(x)^2,
    g(c)=product_i g_Q(c_i).

Its normalized Fourier amplitudes are strictly positive. Parseval therefore
gives, for the product law,

    B_nu(w)=sum_c |g(c)|^2*omega^(w dot c) >=0.             (6)

This is the pure-state OVERLAP, not its square. For uniform independent
labels a_1,...,a_L, character orthogonality implies

    E_a A(v)=sum_(t|Q,t>1) phi(t)*b_t^L,
    b_t=Pr[E(C)=0 mod t], C_i iid with law mu_Q.             (7)

This identity was already established for the classical ML baseline. Its
old sufficient numerical bound assumed sigma<=d and must not be reused at
the much wider source widths. The ideal-dual geometry from
`STRUCTURED_EDCP_IDEAL_COLLISION_CORE.md` supplies the missing replacement.

Write s_G=sigma/sqrt(2), beta=(sqrt(d)/s_G)^d, and
alpha=1+2^(-2d). For the unwrapped coefficient probability D_(Z,s_G), the
same maximum-mass proof, not just its collision corollary, gives

    b_t^infinite <= alpha*max(1/t,beta).                    (8)

### Periodization Before The Large Divisor Sum

Let B=floor((Q-1)/2)>0 and set

    T=sigma^2/(pi*B)*exp(-pi*B^2/sigma^2),
    epsilon_wrap=min(1,2*T+T^2).

The scalar amplitude tail is at most T, and the earlier periodization proof
gives TV(mu_Q, D_(Z,s_G) mod Q)<=epsilon_wrap. Consequently

    b_t <= b_t^infinite+d*epsilon_wrap,
    b_t^L <= (b_t^infinite)^L+L*d*epsilon_wrap.              (9)

The latter uses probabilities and telescoping, not an expansion of an
uncapped upper bound. Summing the error over all divisors costs as much as
Q*L*d*epsilon_wrap. A small unscaled TV number alone would not justify the
exponentially large sum. Do not round this term to zero.

For odd q, Q has exactly one factor 2. Let G denote at least one odd label,
p_G=1-2^(-L), and h=L-1>=1. The odd-divisor sum is bounded by

    S_h <= exp((1+1/(h-1))/(2d)^h)-1, h>=2;
    S_1 <= exp(H_(r_max)/(2d))-1,
    r_max=floor(floor(log2 Q)/floor(log2(2d+1))).

These are the earlier actual-prime-structure bounds. With
b_par=sum_x (-1)^x*mu_Q(x)>=0, equations (7)-(9) give

    E[A(v)|G] <= A_bound,
    A_bound=b_par^d+(2/p_G)*[alpha^L*(S_h+Q*beta^L)
                                     +Q*L*d*epsilon_wrap]. (10)

The pure parity term has exponent d, NOT the collision bound's 2d.
The paired odd/even orders use b_(2t)<=b_t and phi(2t)=phi(t) for odd t.
For even q omit parity and use factor 1 instead of 2/p_G.

One may bound b_par by min(1,b_infinite+2*epsilon_wrap), where for s_G>=1,
b_infinite<=4*exp(-pi*s_G^2/4). The law in (10) is the exact PERIODIZED
Gaussian; the much smaller practical source truncation is charged afterward
as a state/channel error, not multiplied by Q in the divisor sum.

The same calculation proves

    E[ML failure|G,s] <= min(1,A_bound),
    E[D_trace(rho_s,D_z(rho_s))|G] <= min(1,A_bound/2).      (11)

These hold for every secret s. ML still ranges over Q possibilities, and
its computational cost remains exponential without a new algorithm.

## 6. What This Does And Does Not Simulate

Suppose the actual input source, with its allowed retained classical records,
is within Delta_src of the ideal periodized ensemble. Dephasing is a channel,
so a triangle inequality gives an additional 2*Delta_src when comparing the
actual erasure output with its own dephased version. A sampler for actual
local readout within delta_readout then realizes (3) with that same error.

With a public G rejection flag and a fixed residual state on rejected
branches, one safe unconditional bound is

    simulation error <= min(1,A_bound/2)
                           +2*Delta_src+delta_readout.      (13)

Cap at one if desired. This deliberately does not exploit the extra p_G
factor. Normalized postselected guarantees require their conditioning loss.
Delta_src includes matrix/hash, phase, truncation, periodization and quantum
implementation errors. delta_readout includes the physical-noise and finite
classical-sampler errors. The 0.001 phase budget alone is not either complete
ledger. Fresh selectors/tags must be discarded as required by the joint
direct-source contract; retaining them requires a new joint approximation.

Every later quantum channel, including a y-dependent nonlinear unitary,
can be applied to the simulator's known-basis mixture with the same trace
error. Its gate cost and any hard quantum computation remain. This is NOT
a classical simulator for that arbitrary channel, nor for the original
unmeasured phase states. It is a reduction to quantum processing of the
already-classical local-readout data.

A tempting escape is a chirp exp(i*h(f(c))) before copying/measuring. It
pulls through exactly to exp(i*h(u)) on the residual register and commutes
with the coefficient measurement. Even nonlinear h only conjugates the
known-state comparator; it does not escape (13). Do not demand that the
conjugated mixture still be diagonal in the OLD Fourier basis.

Genuinely fiber-dependent operations on c, a different coefficient
measurement basis, or keeping y coherent are outside this argument. They
need explicit circuits and matched classical comparisons. A claim based on
cheap arithmetic must specify why its operation cannot be moved to the
already-classicalized side of the measurement.

### Partial Erasure Has An Exact Factorization

Partition the original PRODUCT source into retained coordinates S and measured
coordinates R, with f=f_S+f_R. Compute f into u and Fourier-measure only R.
The known controlled subtraction

    |c_S>|u> -> |c_S>|u-f_S(c_S)>

commutes with measuring R and undoes the contribution copied from S. The
result is EXACTLY the original pure phase state on S tensor the full
Fourier-erasure cq output of R. No Gaussian assumption, unit labels, or
high-information premise is needed for this factorization. It relies on
the pre-copy source being a product across S|R.

Equivalently, after a QFT on u the residual vectors factor as

    Q^(-1/2)*sum_z hat_g_R(y+(z-s)*v_R)
                        |z> tensor |psi_(s-z)^S>.

The controlled phase omega^(z*f_S(c_S)) restores |psi_s^S> independently
of z, even while z is coherent. This is the same known arithmetic undoing,
not a new decoder.

Therefore the partial version has a comparator consisting of the retained
S quantum source, classical local readout of R, and a uniform mask, with
error bounded by A(v_R)/2 before source/implementation losses. The original
controlled addition can be restored afterward. The overlap bound must be
for R ALONE. Small ambiguity for the entire batch does not bound A(v_R).
At the finite 4,3,3 references, simply deleting one block does not inherit
the information certificate. No all-partial-measurements no-go is claimed.

As a scope control, one unit-labeled block whose coefficient support lies
within balanced base-q digits can be inverted from its frequency on that
support. It may then admit efficient nonlinear measurement corrections or
direct coherent erasure. That does not invert the many-to-one weighted
multi-block map, and its overlap sum need not be small. Any quantum advantage
using the retained S states still needs its actual subsequent joint operation.

## 7. Falsification Checks And Literature Scope

- 72 full complex circuit identities used (q,d,L)=(2,2,2),(3,2,1),
  (3,2,2),(4,2,1), two label sets, widths 0.8,1.4,3 and secrets 0,1,Q-1.
  Maximum amplitude residual was 2.30e-16. They also checked nonlinear
  classical choices of a linear correction, secret-independent y marginals,
  and the secret-free reduced state after discarding y.
- 24 exact finite-matrix cq trace comparisons verified (5) and the weaker
  posterior bound. They also checked local-ML <= optimal conditional quantum
  readout <= original optimal covariant readout. No scalable optimizer was
  assumed in these small references.
- A separate 24 Gaussian/complex-amplitude controls verified the direct
  overlap trace bound, including nonunit and zero labels.
- 44 divisor mass controls and twenty complete label-ensemble averages
  checked (7)-(10); maximum divisor-identity residual was 3.56e-15. Seed
  20260928 was used for randomized controls. These finite checks are not
  empirical recovery at the growing source dimensions.
- Twelve frequency-phase pull-through comparisons had discrepancy below
  8.78e-17. A zero-label Q=5 control had dephasing distance 0.8, correctly
  blocking a width-only claim.
- Eighteen partial-erasure factorizations at Q=5,10, two coordinates on
  each side, three random label/amplitude sets and secrets 0,1,Q-1 agreed
  within 3.93e-17, including arbitrary complex product amplitudes.
- A chirped Q=5 amplitude g(c)=exp(2*pi*i*c^2/5)/sqrt(5) has dephasing
  distance 0.8, while wrongly substituting pure-state overlap into (4)
  gives zero. Its Fourier amplitudes are not positive. This falsifies that
  substitution, not the general overlap bound or the pull-through rule.
- Six growing formula references checked (10)-(12), with periodization
  evaluated in log space and positive small-error bounds retained.

[Luongo et al., Lemma 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.20167v1) implements
measurement-based uncomputation of a computable garbage function while
retaining its input. It does not supply weighted-preimage erasure from a
many-to-one output alone. Conversely,
[Lindzey--Rosmanis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.07336) study black-box injective
index erasure. Their query lower bound is not a lower bound for our explicit
modular homomorphism. The bounds here are derived for the actual circuit.
