## Question, Input And Algorithmic Claim

Does the nilpotent upper bound extend to a permutable hidden subgroup in
a nonnilpotent ambient group without supplying the hidden normal core?
This directly addresses the independently posed permutable-H question in
[Morales2608.05321v2, Introduction](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05321v2).
The corpus been-tried lookup for permutable/Sylow/solvable HSP produced no
matching construction. A baseline lookup had no catalog match, which is
not a claim of classical hardness. Targeted primary-literature searches
did not identify this exact composition; priority remains UNKNOWN.

Input is a uniquely encoded finite group G given by generators and coherent
public multiplication/inversion, and ordinary coherent f with

    f(x)=f(y) iff x^-1 y belongs to H,

where H is PERMUTABLE in G. Label width and all oracle costs are charged.
Output is generators of H in original encodings. There is no supplied
hidden subgroup, hidden core, Lie chart or Fourier transform on G.

The general reduction pays for one public Sylow subgroup for each prime
dividing |G|, then runs the reviewed p-group algorithm on each. Its total
number of nil-2 HSP calls is at most (log2|G|)^2. With the public IMS
preprocessing, its bounded-error quantum running time is polynomial in

    input size + nu(G) + log(1/epsilon),

where nu(G) is the least degree bound for faithful permutation
representations of all nonabelian composition factors. In particular:

- For all uniquely encoded SOLVABLE groups, nu(G)=1, giving a uniform
  polynomial-time upper bound without class, exponent or prime restrictions.
- Explicit permutation-group inputs also have polynomially bounded nu(G).
  The degree is charged as part of their input; this is not an algorithm
  for arbitrary hidden subgroups of permutation groups.
- For arbitrary uniquely encoded finite G, retain the displayed nu(G)
  cost, or an independently paid polynomial public-Sylow interface. Do
  not replace this by an unconditional polynomial-in-encoding claim.

The prior nilpotent theorem is the core component. Neither general
solvable-group normalizers nor a Fitting-subgroup algorithm is required.
Those would be unnecessary new subsystems.

## 1. The Sylow Intersection Is Complete

Let P be ANY Sylow p-subgroup of the finite G. Since H is permutable,
HP is a subgroup and |HP|=|H||P|/|H intersect P| divides |G|. Taking
p-adic valuations gives

    v_p|H| + v_p|G| - v_p|H intersect P| <= v_p|G|.

Since H intersect P is a p-subgroup of H, equality is forced:

    |H intersect P| = p^(v_p|H|).

Thus H intersect P is a FULL Sylow p-subgroup of H. It is also permutable
inside P: for C<=P, (H intersect P)C=(HC) intersect P is a subgroup.
No conjugate of P has to be searched for or aligned with hidden data.
Neither G nor H needs to be nilpotent for this lemma.

The assumption is stronger than merely permuting with Sylows: the latter
would prove the order identity, but would not supply the full permutability
promise needed by the p-group solver on H intersect P.

## 2. Recovery Without Commuting Pieces

Compute generators of one P_p for each prime p dividing |G|. Restrict the
SAME original f to P_p. Apply the p-group theorem of
PERMUTABLE_NILPOTENT_HSP.md Sections1-4, returning generators of
H_p=H intersect P_p. Public group elements are already original encodings.

Let K be generated by the union of these outputs. Then K<=H. For each p,
H_p<=K implies that the full p-part of |H| divides |K|. Hence |H| divides
|K|, and K=H. This is generation, not a direct-product decomposition;
the chosen H_p need not commute or be normal in H.

If |P_p|=p^b_p, at most sum_p b_p^2 <= (log2|G|)^2 nil-2 calls are used.
Only the p-group algorithms compute normalizers; all their ambient groups
remain p-groups. Public preprocessing never queries hidden H. Amplify and
cap its calls and those of the subsolvers under a global error budget.
The coordinate/quotient compilation and source costs are as in the core
note; no recursive quotient-depth overhead is introduced here.

## 3. Primary Public-Access Contract

[Ivanyos--Magniez--Santha, quant-ph/0102014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0102014)
defines nu(G) in Section3. Theorem4(v) includes Sylow construction among
the public group tasks. Corollary5 replaces its classical number-theoretic
premises with quantum order finding and elementary-abelian membership.
Theorem6 pays those premises with unique encodings, yielding time
polynomial in input size plus nu(G). The same interface computes |G|;
quantum factoring supplies its prime divisors. This is not an assumed
efficient QFT on G, nor does it invoke a hidden normal-core theorem.

The source explicitly gives nu(G)=1 for solvable groups and a polynomial
bound for permutation-group inputs. No such bound is asserted here for
every finite-field matrix group or arbitrary opaque finite group.

Morales Section4 assumes a structured quasi-Hamiltonian input and uses its
normal Sylow direct product. Section1 here instead needs only the hidden
H to be permutable, and Section2 uses generation rather than commutation.
Holt--Subramanian2609.38085v2 normal-core/near-Hamiltonian results retain
their QFT and Baer-index premises; they do not directly pay this input.
These source-scope checks are bounded, not a comprehensive novelty review.

## 4. Classical Baselines And Falsifiers

The strongest immediate classical check is the existing public group
machinery: on explicit permutation inputs much of it is already classical.
It computes the Sylows, not H intersect P from an arbitrary hiding oracle.
Inside the probes, known abelian/normal/nil-2 quantum algorithms are the
comparators. The usual abelian oracle collision obstruction is inherited;
no new classical lower bound on a native nonabelian problem is established.

A sharp rejected extension is S3 with H a reflection and P a different
order2 Sylow. H intersect P is trivial, despite 2 dividing |H|. Here HP
is not a subgroup and H is not permutable. Thus the reduction does not
solve unrestricted solvable HSP or the dihedral hidden-reflection problem.

A positive scope control is G=M27 x S3 with H=HM x S3, where HM is the
retained nonnormal permutable order3 subgroup of M27. H is permutable as
an inverse image, nonnormal and nonnilpotent. Its intersections with public
Sylow3/Sylow2 subgroups have orders9/2 and generate H of order18. They do
not commute. This tests the generation step omitted by a direct-product
argument. Exposed H is used only as a falsifier, never as algorithm input.
