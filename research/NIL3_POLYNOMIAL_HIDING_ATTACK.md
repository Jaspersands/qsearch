# Classical Attack On Polynomial-Valued Hiding Functions

**Status: scoped classical algorithm with exact symbolic and charged
value-only controls, now extended to bounded nilpotency class. Not a
dequantization of arbitrary-label HSP.**

The class-three case below remains the worked example. The section
Fixed-Class Extension supplies the wider promise, proof and implementation.

Before searching for an application of the proposed class-three quantum
receiver, test the most tempting one: a low-degree polynomial evaluator
whose fibers are cosets. In the regime below, this application is already
classically tractable. Increasing its dimension or varying the hidden
generator would not create a quantum advantage.

This note adds a constructive baseline, not a generic no-go claim.
Derivative-based symmetry testing is established methodology, not
claimed novel here. For the abelian translation case,
[Dvir, Oliveira and Shpilka, Theorem 1.6 and Section 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1401.3714)
give efficient shift-equivalence algorithms and relate invariant shifts
to directional derivatives in sufficiently large characteristic.
The derivation below writes the corresponding right-action equations
for this repository's explicit class-three BCH model.

## Input And Claim

Let `G` be the exponent-`p` BCH group of a public `d`-dimensional Lie
algebra of class at most three, `p>3`. The classical algorithm can query

```
F : Fp^d -> Fp^s
```

in public logarithmic coordinates. Each coordinate of `F` is promised
to be a polynomial of total degree at most `e`, with

```
p > 2*(e+1).
```

The field values must be accessible. Arbitrary coset names, an unknown
output hash, quantum coset samples alone, or a low-degree polynomial
appearing only AFTER quantum Fourier sampling do not meet this promise.
The algorithm does not infer a degree bound from a finite sample.

Define the global right-action stabilizer

```
K = { exp(X) : F(BCH(z,X))=F(z) for every z in Fp^d }.
```

There is a classical randomized algorithm recovering an additive basis
of `log(K)` in time polynomial in `d,s,e,log p,log(1/delta)`. If `F`
actually separates the `gH` cosets, then `K=H`, and this is a classical
HSP solver for this input class. If it does not separate those cosets,
the algorithm recovers its global stabilizer, not an imagined hidden
subgroup.

`gH` is a left coset in group-theoretic terminology; it is the orbit
under RIGHT multiplication. The orientation is explicit below.

## Infinitesimal Equations

Differentiate the right-action path `z(t)=BCH(z,tX)` at zero. The public
vector field is

```
V_X(z) = X + [z,X]/2 + [z,[z,X]]/12.

D_X F(z) = gradient(F)(z) . V_X(z).
```

The term `-[tX,[z,tX]]/12` in BCH is quadratic in `t`, so it does not
contribute to this first derivative.

`D_X F` is LINEAR in the unknown coordinates of `X`, despite the
nonabelian action. Its total degree in `z` is at most `e+1`.
Thus the polynomial identities `D_X F=0` form a homogeneous linear
system on only `d` unknowns.

These are fields generated by right multiplication. Do not confuse them
with the left-multiplication path `BCH(tX,z)`, whose half-bracket has the
opposite sign. The fixture has a nonnormal stabilizer and the wrong-side
system returns only zero.

## Why Derivatives Are Sufficient Here

Necessity: if `exp(X)` stabilizes `F`, then all its powers do too. For
fixed `z`, the polynomial

```
q_z(t)=F(BCH(z,tX))
```

has degree at most `2e`, since the BCH path is quadratic in `t`.
It is constant on all `p` field elements. Since `2e<p`, it is formally
constant. Therefore `D_X F(z)=0` at every field point. A polynomial of
total degree at most `e+1<p` vanishing everywhere on `Fp^d` is the zero
polynomial, yielding the coefficient identities.

Sufficiency: associativity gives the flow identity
`BCH(BCH(z,tX),uX)=BCH(z,(t+u)X)`. Consequently
`q_z'(t)=(D_X F)(BCH(z,tX))`. If the identities hold, this derivative
vanishes. Since `deg(q_z)<p`, `q_z` is constant, proving invariance.

The degree/characteristic restriction cannot be dropped from this
argument. At characteristic five, `F(z)=z_0^5` has zero formal derivative
but equals the NONCONSTANT function `z_0` on field points. The test
suite refuses this input instead of returning the whole group.

The kernel is a vector space closed under the Lie bracket, as expected
for the logarithm of the subgroup stabilizer. Exponentiating a basis
provides subgroup generators. The algorithm never needs the hidden
subgroup's order to be `p`.

## Value-Only Algorithm

Dense coefficients permit exact coefficient collection and row reduction.
The implemented reference does that, but the stronger baseline does
NOT read coefficients and does NOT interpolate all `binom(d+e,e)`
monomials.

At a uniformly random `z`, compute every partial derivative from the
chosen-query lines `F(z+t*e_j)`, `t=0,...,e`. Share the `t=0` query.
The exact derivative weights are

```
w_0 = -sum_(i=1)^e 1/i,
w_t = (-1)^(t+1) * binom(e,t)/t,  1<=t<=e,
```

all modulo `p`. Therefore each point costs `1+d*e` vector-valued
evaluator queries, not one free gradient query.

For each output coordinate `a`, add the row

```
(D_(e_1)F_a(z), ..., D_(e_d)F_a(z)).
```

The kernel of all collected rows always contains the true stabilizer.
It can only have extra spurious directions, never lose true directions
under the stated promises and exact arithmetic.

Let `r<=d` be the rank of the full coefficient system. If the sampled
rank is smaller than `r`, choose in the proof a direction killed by the
current rows but not by all coefficient rows. At least one component of
`D_X F` is then a nonzero polynomial of degree at most `e+1`. A fresh
uniform point detects it with probability at least

```
1-(e+1)/p > 1/2.
```

This is the polynomial zero bound; the direction is only a proof witness,
not advice to the algorithm. It can depend on earlier samples.

Use `d` blocks of `ceil(log2(d/delta))` points each. A deficient block
fails to raise rank with probability at most `delta/d`. A union bound
over the blocks gives total error at most `delta`.
The fixed schedule is therefore

```
T = d*ceil(log2(d/delta))
queries = T*(1+d*e).
```

The returned nullspace is exact with that probability under independent
uniform sampling. Fixed seeds are finite reproducibility controls, not
randomized guarantees against an adversary who knows the sample list.
The live artifact explicitly distinguishes these two meanings.

Runtime is polynomial even when `e` grows polynomially with the input,
provided the degree, characteristic, evaluator cost and output-length
promises are charged. This is stronger than a fixed-degree dense
interpolation baseline.

## Fixed-Class Extension

2026-10-21. Increasing the nilpotency class does not rescue the
polynomial-valued application. Let the public Lie algebra have class
at most c, with p>c. Metabelianness is NOT needed for this classical
argument. The exact right-multiplication field is

```
V_X(z) = sum_(r=0)^(c-1) B_r(1)/r! * ad(z)^r X
       = X + [z,X]/2 + ad(z)^2 X/12 - ad(z)^4 X/720 + ... .
```

Here B_1(1)=+1/2; using B_1(0) would reverse the action convention.
All denominators are invertible for p>c. One way to derive the formula
is to left-trivialize the derivative of exp(z(t))=exp(z)exp(tX):
`((1-exp(-ad(z)))/ad(z))*z'(0)=X`, and invert the finite operator series.
The usual characteristic-zero BCH identity is reviewed in
[Zhang's BCH notes, Section 5](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~rzhang/files/BCHFormula.pdf).
Here its finite truncation is reduced modulo p; no analytic convergence
or numerical differentiation is used.

There is NO new ad-cubed term at class four. The first correction to
the old class-three field is the displayed negative ad-fourth term at
class five. Omitting it can wrongly remove a genuine stabilizer
direction, not merely worsen sample complexity. A regression exhibits
exactly that failure.

Set

```
D_c = max(1, 2*floor((c-1)/2)),
E = e-1+D_c for e>0, and E=0 for e=0.
Require p > max(c, e*D_c, 2*E).
```

The BCH path BCH(z,tX) has t-degree at most D_c. To see this, each
surviving Lie word has length at most c. Apart from the linear term tX,
it contains z. A word containing at least two z's has t-degree at most
c-2. The one-z terms of t-degree r are scalar multiples of ad(tX)^r z;
their coefficients vanish for odd r>1 by the Bernoulli identity. This
gives D_c, including c=1,2. The vector field has z-degree at most D_c
as well. The bound need not be tight for an individual algebra.

Consequently D_X F has total degree at most E and F(BCH(z,tX)) has
t-degree at most e*D_c<p. The necessity and sufficiency proof above
now applies unchanged. A deficient sampled kernel is detected at a
fresh uniform point with probability at least 1-E/p>1/2. The same
schedule T=d*ceil(log2(d/delta)) and T*(1+d*e) charged vector queries
therefore recover the global stabilizer with error at most delta.
All arithmetic, public bracket operations and output length are charged;
the bound is polynomial for fixed c and polynomially bounded e. It is
not a claim about evaluators with exponential degree in their input size.

`sampled_stabilizer(..., class_bound=c)` implements this extension in
the existing module. Before querying F, it computes the public lower
central series by spanning brackets of a standard basis with each
current ideal basis. It rejects an understated class bound. This takes
at most c*d^2 public bracket evaluations, plus field row reduction, and
does not enumerate p^d group elements. These calls are recorded
separately from chosen evaluator queries. Bilinearity and Jacobi remain
Lie-presentation promises, and the semantic degree of F is still not
certified. Standalone `bounded_class_right_translation_field` trusts
its class bound; the sampled solver is the checked entry point.

Independent matrix exp/log derivative controls cover classes 2 through 7.
For recovery controls use the standard semidirect Lie algebra
`[X,Y_j]=Y_(j+1)` for 0<=j<c-1, with abelian Y ideal; its class is c.
It embeds in strictly upper-triangular (c+1)-by-(c+1) matrices. For a
hidden direction h with h_X=1, write z=(x,v). The canonical section
`exp(z)exp(-x*h)` has X coordinate zero and Y coordinate

```
F_j(x,v) = sum_(k=0)^j x^k/(k+1)! * (v_(j-k)-x*h_(j-k)).
```

This is a degree-at-most-c vector that separates the genuine cyclic
cosets, used only as a negative control. Actual matrix products verify
the section and action; the attack gets only its value callback, never
h or coefficients. Recovery passes at (c,p)=(4,11),(5,23),(6,29) and
(5,2^127-1). The last uses arbitrary-precision field row reduction;
it says nothing about unrelated machine-word quantum backends.

Thus polynomial-valued hiding functions in this regime fail the
application filter for the entire fixed-class metabelian proposal, not
only class three. A low-degree phase appearing after quantum processing
still does NOT imply the necessary chosen-value access to the original
evaluator. The broad opaque-label proposal remains open.

## Decisive Existing-Family Control

Use the repository's non-split free class-three Lie algebra on two
generators, dimension five, at `p=11`, with

```
X=(1,2,3,4,5).
```

For this NEGATIVE control only, define the polynomial evaluator by taking
the canonical cross-section representative

```
F(z) = BCH(z, -z_0*X), with its identically zero first coordinate omitted.
```

It has total degree three and separates the actual `g<exp(X)>` cosets.
Indeed right multiplication shifts `z_0` by the same group parameter,
so the representative is unchanged; conversely equal representatives
imply the same coset. This is a structured labeling of the SAME group
problem used by the constructive work, not a newly invented advantage
oracle.

Both the exact coefficient system and the value-only attack recover
the subgroup. The latter sees only a callback returning four field
elements, uses 75 random points and 1200 charged queries, and agrees
with the exact rank-four coefficient calculation. Tests also cover the
trivial stabilizer, a three-dimensional derived stabilizer, the full
group, and the wrong action orientation.

No quantum-versus-classical runtime claim is inferred from these finite
counts. The relevant result is the polynomial classical algorithm and
its precise access model.

## What This Kills And What It Does Not

Reject these as evidence of a new quantum speedup:

- polynomial-valued bounded-class hiding functions in the stated regime;
- a small arithmetic-circuit evaluator with polynomial degree that
  otherwise meets the same promises;
- relabeled finite instances that expose the polynomial coordinates
  and compare only against collision search.

Do NOT infer:

- a classical algorithm for arbitrary opaque HSP labels;
- that a polynomial phase `lambda.Q` means the original oracle is
  polynomial-valued;
- a lower bound or positive advantage in small characteristic;
- a general polynomial-equivalence, code-equivalence or lattice solver;
- that this derivative strategy is a new independent breakthrough.

The public type checks catch obvious non-field outputs. They cannot
certify the semantic degree promise of a numeric hash or opaque integer
encoding. Violating that promise invalidates the guarantee.

## Research Decision

The bounded-class quantum composition remains a candidate worth proof and
prior-art review. But "apply it to explicit low-degree polynomial hiding
functions" is no longer a credible route to a new speedup under these
conditions: a stronger classical method wins at the asymptotic level.

A useful external reduction must expose an efficiently computable
coset invariant without giving this bounded-degree field-valued access,
or establish another precise reason this attack is inapplicable.
Simply composing an arbitrary hash onto the polynomial does not help
when the classical adversary has the underlying polynomial or circuit.
No such useful external reduction has been established.
