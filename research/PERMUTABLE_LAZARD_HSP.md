Can a promise on the hidden subgroup replace the fixed-class requirement?

HK=KH for every subgroup K of G. Only H has this promise; G need not be
quasi-Hamiltonian. Output generators for H in the original coordinates.

Let b=sum_i e_i, so log2|G|=b log2p. The proposed bounded-error algorithm
assumes 0<epsilon<1 and returns immediately for b=0. Otherwise it
uses at most b additive-HSP calls and polynomial classical/public quantum
work in the explicit input size and log(1/epsilon). Charge original f gates
and label width. There is no p-sized enumeration, fixed-class exponent,
supplied hidden ideal, arbitrary-group Fourier transform or Rees cover.
The promise need not be efficiently recognizable and is not certified by

An applicable stronger-than-necessary quantum baseline is

Classically, supplied Lie coordinates do not expose an arbitrary hiding

## 1. The Elementary Group Lemma

For ANY subgroup S<=G and K<=S,

    (H intersect S) K = (HK) intersect S.

Indeed, hk lies in S iff h lies in S, since k lies in S. Permutability of H
makes HK a subgroup, so the right side is a subgroup. Thus H intersect S is
permutable in S. This does NOT assert that the intersection of two
permutable subgroups is permutable in all of G.

If S is a finite p-group generated by elements of order p, every permutable
J<=S is NORMAL in S. For each such generator x, J<x> is a p-group and
[J<x>:J] is at most p. A subgroup of index p in a finite p-group is normal.
Consequently x normalizes J, and the generators give J normal in S.

In particular this applies when S has exponent p. It does not apply to
arbitrary cyclic subgroups of order p^e by replacing their index with p.

## 2. A Paid Characteristic Subgroup

In the supplied Lazard model define

    S = exp(L[p]),       L[p]={x : p*x=0}.

The kernel is obtained by additive linear congruences. Bilinearity makes it
an ideal. Lazard powers satisfy (exp x)^p=exp(px), so S is exactly the set of
elements killed by p, a characteristic subgroup of exponent p. Its additive
Fp coordinates and bracket are explicit; its class is at most c.

The preceding lemma proves

    H1=H intersect S is normal in S.

Let s=L[p] and h1=log(H1), an ideal of s. Passing to the Lie and BCH group
quotients shows, for x,y in s,

    BCH(-x,y) in h1  iff  x-y in h1.

Thus the SAME restricted ordinary oracle is an abelian HSP oracle on
(s,+)=Fp^r. Prepare a uniform additive address, evaluate f on its linear
embedding in original L, and Fourier sample. A nullspace computation
recovers an additive basis of h1. No nonlinear coset-label transformation
is needed. If H1=1, then H=1: a nontrivial element of any finite p-group H has
a nontrivial power of order p. If H1 is nontrivial, H normalizes H1 because
S is characteristic. It is NOT necessary that H1 be normal in all of G.

## 3. Normalizer, Quotient And Recursion

The additive-HSP output is already a full vector-space basis of h1. If a
normal-HSP backend were used instead, its group generators would require
Lie closure; their initial linear span alone is insufficient. The public
normalizer is

    N_G(H1)=exp(I),       I={x in L : [x,h1] subset h1}.

Proof: conjugation is exp(ad x). If ad x preserves h1, its exponential does.
Conversely, if the exponential preserves h1, its truncated logarithm does
too; that logarithm is ad x. All denominators are units because c<p.
Thus computing I is a system of linear congruences and submodule membership
conditions, solved by Smith/Hermite normal form. No group-sized search for
normalizing elements is needed. The subring h1 is an ideal of I.

Replace L by I/h1 and H by H/H1. Compute additive invariant factors and
the induced bracket explicitly. A canonical additive representative map
into I supplies a public section into G. The restriction of f descends
through that section since H1 is known to lie in H. Compose sections to
the ORIGINAL input rather than nesting oracle calls. Each local f query
uses one original f query and polynomial public computation/cleanup.

Permutability survives restriction to any subgroup containing H and
quotient by a normal subgroup contained in H. Therefore H/H1 is permutable
in N_G(H1)/H1. Nilpotency class does not increase, so the new class is still
less than p. Its log_p order drops by at least one. There are at most b
additive-HSP calls. Lift returned quotient generators, adjoin those of H1,
and unwind to generators in the original coordinates.

## 4. Uniform Arithmetic And Error Accounting

The algorithm needs no BCH evaluation: f is already addressed by original
Lazard coordinates. Every operational map is additive linear algebra,
bracket/idealizer computation, additive normal-form representatives, or an
abelian QFT. BCH/Lazard correspondence proves correctness of the coset and
normalizer identities. A separate opaque group encoding would require a
paid conversion and is outside this statement. Compute/copy/uncompute
pays for reversible public embeddings and sections.

SNF descriptions of submodules, ideals and quotients have polynomial bit
size in b logp. Record the inclusion/projection/representative maps at each
stage. A quotient basis without its original-coordinate lift is insufficient.

At a stage of additive dimension r take r+t Fourier samples, where
t=ceil(log_p(2b/epsilon)). Conditional on correct previous stages, ideal
samples are uniform on h1^perp and failure to span has probability at most
1/((p-1)p^t)<=epsilon/(2b). There are at most b stages, r<=b, hence at most
b*(b+t) original f queries. Allocate the remaining epsilon/2 to complete
preparations/QFT instruments, using polynomial precision. The classical
run has a fixed b-stage cap and rejects malformed coordinate data or a
sampled candidate that is not an ideal of the current socle before forming
I/h1; no incorrect history launches unbounded computation. This is a
bounded-error output theorem, NOT a Las Vegas completeness test:
checking that returned generators satisfy f(h)=f(1) alone cannot certify
that they generate all H. No small branch is renormalized in the argument.

## 5. Nonvacuity And Falsifiers

For odd p take L0=Z/p^2*a plus Z/p*b with [a,b]=p*a. It has class2<p.
H=exp(<b>) has order p and is not normal, since conjugation by exp(a)
sends b to b+p*a. Its p-torsion subgroup exp(<p*a,b>) is abelian, so the
additive-HSP call can recover this nonnormal H after restriction.

This is the standard modular group of order p^3. H is permutable: it
normalizes every subgroup because conjugation by exp(b), with a suitable
choice of sign, is the power automorphism g -> g^(1+p) or g^(1-p).
Direct product with an exponent-p group U preserves that power identity.
Taking U=UT_(c+1)(Fp), 3<=c<p, gives growing-class ambient groups that are
not quasi-Hamiltonian, while H remains permutable and nonnormal. This is a
scope witness, not a new hard native application or a benchmark algorithm.

Cheapest negative controls:

- An arbitrary nonnormal line in the exponent-p Heisenberg group is NOT
  permutable. The additive-HSP restriction is invalid without the promise.
- Even in the modular example, applying additive HSP to ALL of L fails:
  x and x+y differ by y in log H, but BCH(-x,x+y)=y-(p/2)x is outside log H.
  The socle restriction is essential; permutability does not mean normality.
- Outside the Lazard regime, the elements killed by p need not form a
  subgroup. In D8, the involutions and identity are not closed. Although
  the subgroup generated by all order-p elements still satisfies the group
  lemma, computing that subgroup in a general input is not granted here.
- Correct individual source marginals are not ordinary f access. The existing
  copy-transport result does not automatically implement the imported
  additive hiding ORACLE from copies.

Independent final review accepted the explicit-coordinate claim. The focused
Sage/GAP control classical_baselines/permutable_socle_controls.sage passed:
the modular order27 subgroup is nonnormal but permutable and normal in its

## 6. Further Access Deduction: The Public Power Map

Even without given Lie coordinates, an abstract class-c<p finite p-group
satisfies

    g^p=h^p  iff  g^-1 h in Omega1(G).

Proof in its abstract Lazard ring: exp(x)^p=exp(px); equality of powers is
x-y in L[p]. Since L[p] is an ideal, this is equivalent to equality in the
BCH quotient, hence to the stated group-coset condition. This power-fiber
fact is prior mathematics: [ISS, Section3, Lemma3](https://arxiv.org/html/0707.1260)
also invokes Hall's Theorem12.4.4 for it.

Operationally g->g^p takes O(logp) public multiplications and needs no
hidden input or Lie conversion. In a unique encoding it is therefore a
normal-HSP oracle for Omega1(G). IMS Theorem8 pays its recovery. The original
f restricted to that subgroup then hides a NORMAL H1, again handled by IMS.
This removes the torsion-access obstacle in a proposed black-box extension.

ISS Section3 supplies polynomial normalizers with refined polycyclic
presentations AND efficient collection. It does not promise growing-class
classical collection. The retained QUOTIENT_BRIDGE_AUDIT.md proposes paying
collection with IMS nice-factor quantum computations, and coherent canonical
coordinates with retained-input cleanup. The following composition resolves

### One Original-Group Invariant

Maintain B<=H<=S<=G in ORIGINAL encodings, with B normal in S. Initially
B=1 and S=G. At every round work directly with Q=S/B. Apply IMS quotient
preprocessing to this original pair, never to recursively nested group
wrappers. Fixed representative generators give a public forward section
sigma:Q->S by evaluating their normal words in G. Canonical quotient
coordinates are obtained by the paid nice-factor routine.

1. Implement P(q)=q^p with canonical Q output, by original-group powering
   followed by canonical quotient coordinates. By Section6 this is an
   ordinary normal-HSP oracle for T=Omega1(Q). Recover generators for T
   with IMS Theorem8. T is characteristic and has exponent p.
2. The oracle f(sigma(q)) hides H/B. On T it hides K=(H/B) intersect T,
   which is NORMAL in T by Section1. Invoke normal HSP again, using the
   same coherent ordinary f through the paid section. No additive chart
   of T is needed in this implementation.
3. If K=1, return the stored generators of B: any nontrivial H/B would
   have an element of order p in T. Otherwise let J<=S be the full inverse
   image of K, generated by B and the returned representatives of K.
4. H normalizes J because H/B normalizes its intersection with characteristic
   T. Compute N_S(J) by the paid normalizer routine directly in original S,
   and replace (S,B) by (N_S(J),J).

Permutability is preserved on restriction and quotient; c<p is inherited
by all subgroups and quotients. The original-group invariant is therefore
preserved on every correct branch. Each nonterminal step increases |B|
by at least p. There are at most log_p|G| rounds (use ell as a known safe
cap); the terminal case S=B requires no further HSP call. Returned elements
are stored as actual original encodings, not recursively expanded words.

### Polynomial Clock And Precision

The public group and subgroup encodings remain bounded by the original
input width, with polynomially many generators and coordinate entries.
All normal-HSP, public-presentation and normalizer calls have polynomial
clocks under the verified interfaces. Cap expected-time preprocessing and
amplify it with independent coins. Allocate a global error budget across
the polynomial number of classical and quantum calls. This is a bound on
the probability of the first incorrect stage, not postselection on hidden
success events.

If T0 bounds the number of compiled coherent canonical-coordinate calls,
choose per-call worst-input error delta<=(epsilon/(16T0))^2. The retained-input
bound and a hybrid give at most epsilon/4 total channel error from those
calls; amplify classical preprocessing and the imported HSP routines within
the remaining budget. Their logarithmic confidence costs remain polynomial.
Set fixed stage/time/output-size caps, including on incorrect histories.
Direct preprocessing on S/B is essential: nesting a fresh coordinate oracle
inside every older quotient would not justify this polynomial claim.
