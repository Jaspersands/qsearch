## Parametric Mathematical Family

For every prime p>3 let L have basis x,y,z,u,v and nonzero brackets

    [x,y]=z, [x,z]=u, [y,z]=v.

This is the free nilpotent Lie algebra of class three on two generators.
Its derived algebra is span(z,u,v); its third lower-central space is
span(u,v). The corresponding BCH group has exponent p and order p^5.
Take the diagnostic hidden line h=span(x+u). It is a Lie subalgebra, is not
normal, and is not invariant under the ordinary base grading: dilation by
s sends x+u to sx+s^3u, which is outside h when s^2 != 1.

The positive lower-central Rees cover has dimension ten, with basis

    tx,ty,tz,tu,tv, t^2z,t^2u,t^2v, t^3u,t^3v.

Its only positive-order brackets are

    [tx,ty]=t^2z,
    [tx,tz]=t^2u, [ty,tz]=t^2v,
    [tx,t^2z]=t^3u, [ty,t^2z]=t^3v.

Evaluation at three distinct nonzero field points is a Lie homomorphism
in each slot. An invertible Vandermonde matrix in L/h shows that the
common hidden preimage is precisely span(tx+tu). Notice that tu is a
GRADE-ONE coordinate, not the t^3u coordinate. Erasing the latter does not
erase the former. At the final additive quotient the full tilted line is
recovered, not just its image in L/[L,L].

### Not Abelian-By-Cyclic

Any codimension-one ideal of L contains [L,L]=span(z,u,v). It also contains
a nonzero ax+by modulo [L,L]. The bracket of this element with z is au+bv,
which is nonzero. Hence every codimension-one ideal is nonabelian.

Consequently the exponent-p group has no abelian normal subgroup with
nontrivial cyclic quotient: that quotient would have order p and the
normal subgroup would correspond to such a codimension-one ideal. The
group itself is nonabelian, so the trivial-quotient option also fails.
It is thus outside the direct abelian-by-cyclic group family of the older
diagonal-equation application. This does NOT rule out other reductions.

It is also not quasi-Hamiltonian. If all subgroups permuted, the cyclic
order-p subgroups generated by exp(x) and exp(y) would have a product
subgroup of order p^2, hence commute, contrary to [x,y] != 0.

These are deductions about the PARAMETRIC family with growing p. The
single p=7 control below is also a bounded-prime instance and is therefore
within the scope of the older bounded-prime result. A finite test cannot
establish a new asymptotic complexity separation.

### Matched Classical Oracle Comparison

For this same parametric BCH group, allow every noncentral order-p
subgroup as the hidden subgroup. A one-dimensional ideal in a nilpotent
Lie algebra is central: each restricted adjoint is a nilpotent scalar,
hence zero. Thus all these hidden lines are nonnormal, and their number is

    N = (p^5-p^2)/(p-1) = p^4+p^3+p^2.

Any two intersect only at the identity. Their public structure does not
defeat the following standard collision lower bound. It is NOT a new
lower-bound technique; compare
[Childs, Theorem 5.1](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~amchilds/qa/qa.pdf).

Choose H uniformly and assign distinct random labels to its left cosets.
Couple any adaptive classical algorithm to a transcript giving fresh
random distinct labels at distinct query points until an H-collision.
This ideal transcript is independent of H. A pair of distinct query
points has a nonidentity group difference belonging to at most one
candidate H. Conditional on the ideal transcript, at most Q(Q-1)/2
candidates can cause a first collision. Outside that event, the
algorithm's output can be correct for at most one further candidate.
Averaging also over its internal randomness gives

    Pr(success) <= min(1, [Q(Q-1)/2 + 1]/N).

Repeated queries add nothing. Constant success therefore requires
Omega(sqrt(N))=Omega(p^2) queries. This bound allows unrestricted public
group computation and chosen queries, not merely random samples.

The ordinary birthday baseline matches this exponent: O(p^2) uniformly
chosen distinct group inputs yield a same-coset pair with constant
probability, and its nonidentity difference generates the order-p H.
There are p^4 cosets, each containing p elements; the exact no-collision
probability after Q distinct queries is

    product_{j=0}^{Q-1} [(p^4-j)*p/(p^5-j)],  for Q<=p^4.

Thus this is a Theta(p^2) classical QUERY comparison. The proposed Rees
theorem would give polynomial time in log(p) for this fixed-dimensional
family, relative to the imported scalable arithmetic. The new code does
not establish that theorem or execute that algorithm. More fundamentally,
the random-label oracle premise does not supply an efficiently evaluable
non-oracle application. An explicitly revealed quotient map may be easy
classically. Neither this familiar oracle separation nor the finite
control resolves that application gap.

## What The Program Actually Checks

All computations use F_7 and Q[zeta_7]; there are no floating phases.

1. Verify the base BCH formula against multiplication of truncated
   noncommutative exponentials on all basis pairs and 256 seeded pairs.
   The embedding uses the free associative algebra through degree three,
   not the same bracket implementation as the BCH code.
2. Check the cover Jacobi identity on all basis triples. Check evaluation
   and dilation bracket preservation on every basis pair and every nonzero
   field scale. Bilinearity/trilinearity extend these finite basis checks
   to all elements of this particular algebra.
3. Compute the simultaneous evaluation preimage by field nullspace
   elimination, obtaining exactly span(tx+tu). Fewer or repeated
   evaluation points leave extra hidden directions and are rejected.
4. Remove the central coordinates in order t^3v,t^3u,t^2v,t^2u,t^2z.
   This uses two cubic and three quadratic scalar cancellation stages.
   It is a bounded scalar-flag diagnostic, not the general fixed-class
   vector-SDE implementation.
5. At each stage form the full two-sided measurement Pi rho Pi in the
   sparse regular group algebra, derive its probability from its trace,
   and normalize only positive branches. The conditional moments are
   computed from inverse-index coefficients, not inserted from the
   proposed branch formula. Values are then losslessly encoded as powers
   of zeta_7; absent entries remain exact zeros.
6. For EVERY positive label tuple of length d+1 find one nonzero witness
   and compose the actual conditional moment tables. Each record must
   give exactly the indicator of HZ. This is equality of sparse functions
   on the ENTIRE group, not a random selection of nonzero entries. The
   number of group elements is not the number explicitly enumerated.
7. Check selected address-kernel entries under both the zero-coordinate
   section and a nonlinear section. These are explicit ENTRY checks of
   the full normalized quotient density, not normalized states on the
   selected address panel. The full moment equality supplies the general
   kernel identity within this finite control.
8. In the final additive group, compute all 7^5 Fourier probabilities
   from the exact group-algebra coefficients and recover the annihilator.
   This computes a distribution; it does not sample a quantum circuit.

The main five stages cover 5,831 complete label records, one witness per
record, plus one all-active witness per stage. This is not every possible
witness. All six nonzero dilation
scales are separately included in the transformed-moment tables. Labels
equal to zero and discarded zero-scale slots are included. An additional
H-containing-center control has only label zero with probability one;
forbidden branches are never normalized.

The regular representation is faithful. Sparse coefficient equality is
therefore exact operator equality even though a dense matrix on the
7^10-element cover is not built. This does not make the control scalable
to arbitrary hidden subgroups: enumerating h and scalar witnesses is
permitted here ONLY because these are explicit proof fixtures.

## Resource And Failure Accounting

One terminal state under this diagnostic scalar schedule consumes
4*4*3*3*3=432 lifted states. Each lift uses three independent original
sources, so that is 1,296 original inputs before final Fourier repetitions.
Discarded zero-scale slots still count. No inverse hidden-source circuit
is used. This ledger is NOT a claim that the general fixed-class procedure
uses this constant number of sources: growing central ranks require the
published vector solver or a separately priced scalar recursion.

Negative controls reject:

- substituting linear cancellation for cubic cancellation;
- too few or repeated Rees evaluation points;
- zero being passed as an invertible dilation;
- an empty/all-zero merge witness;
- coordinate subtraction while the quotient is still nonabelian;
- reversing the left-coset difference convention;
- using base graded dilations on the nonhomogeneous hidden line.

The chosen linear-but-not-cubic witness has labels (1,2), scales (1,3):
1+2*3=0 in F_7, but 1+2*3^3=6. Its surviving central phase is detected
exactly. The cubic terms in BCH are independently nonzero in the test.

## Literature Scope, Checked 2026-10-24

- [Ivanyos--Sanselme--Santha](https://www.irif.fr/~santha/Papers/iss12.pdf)
  covers arbitrary nilpotency class at most two and supplies the
  fixed-class outer reduction used by the draft. This family has class
  three, so the direct nil-2 theorem does not settle it.
- [Ivanyos--Santha, Proposition 7 and Corollary 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.09016)
  treats the indicated HSP family in elementary-abelian-by-order-p
  semidirect products at bounded class. Its diagonal-system solver is
  also an explicit dependency of the Rees manuscript. The family above
  is not of that semidirect-product type. No novelty is claimed for the
  solver, polynomial-phase cancellation, or hidden polynomial algorithms.
- [Van de Woestijne's scalar diagonal solver](https://www.opt.math.tugraz.at/~cvdwoest/maths/issac2005.pdf)
  is the primary arithmetic source behind the scalar primitive. Its
  deterministic polynomial dependence on degree and log(field size)
  is stated in the original paper, not inferred from this F_7 brute-force
  control. Its implementation and internal proof were not reproduced here.
- [Imran--Ivanyos](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08376)
  gives the constant-class, bounded-prime result. The proposed Rees scope
  difference is allowing the primes to grow, not solving fixed p=7.
  Their state-conversion machinery is prior art, not a new contribution.
- [Morales, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05321v1)
  treats specified scalar-action semidirect products and quasi-Hamiltonian
  groups under stated input promises. The direct group families do not
  contain the one above, for the elementary reasons just proved.
- [Imran, October 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28368v2)
  announces recovery of hidden parabolic subgroups of GL_n over finite
  chain rings. Its abstract is a different hidden-subgroup promise, not
  a theorem for every subgroup of every constant-class nilpotent group.
  Only the abstract was used here; no claim about all possible reductions
  through that paper follows.

Searches included Rees/homogenization with HSP, fixed-class nilpotent HSP,
and recent nilpotent-HSP papers. No identical full theorem was located.
This is a LIMITED novelty check, not proof of originality. In particular,
being outside the directly stated families does not exclude an unpublished
result, an overlooked algorithm, or an efficient indirect reduction.

## Adversarial Conclusion And Next Work

theoretical contribution if correct and new, even without an immediate
cryptographic application. It would NOT by itself solve growing-class
dihedral HSP, graph isomorphism, lattice problems, or establish Shor-level
practical impact. Do not require a fabricated application to justify
