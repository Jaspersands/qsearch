## 1. Function Field, Squareclasses, and Constants

Write `F=F_p(x)`, `A(x)=product_{i=0}^{12}(x-i)`, and `K=F(y)`, with
`y^2=A(x)`. Trial division independently verifies that `p=100003` is prime.
The 13 roots of `A` are distinct; in odd characteristic the smooth projective
hyperelliptic model has genus 6 and a unique rational infinity.

The classes of `x-i` are independent in `F*/F*^2`: a nonempty product has odd
valuation at each of its included finite branch places. Passing from `F` to
`K` introduces precisely one relation. Indeed, if `a in F*` is a square
`(r+s*y)^2` in `K`, the coefficient of `y` is `2rs=0`. Thus either `a` is a
square in `F`, or `a/A` is. This proves that the kernel on squareclasses is
generated by `A`, rather than merely assuming that the apparent product
relation is the only one.

Consequently the 13 classes span dimension 12 over `K`, and adjoining their
square roots gives an elementary abelian extension of degree `2^12`.

On the base curve,

```text
div(x-i) = 2 P_i - 2 infinity.
```

Every place has even valuation of each radicand. Since the residue
characteristic is odd, the corresponding quadratic local extension is either
split or unramified. No branch-place exception is being suppressed here:
even valuation does not necessarily mean splitting, but it does exclude
quadratic ramification. Composita of these unramified extensions stay
unramified.

At infinity, `t=y/x^7` is a uniformizer and `(x-i)t^2` has residue 1.
Hensel lifting gives a square root in the local field for each radicand.
Thus the full extension splits at infinity, not merely each of a set of
unrelated abstract quadratic fields. Equivalently, compatible local roots
define the trivial decomposition group in the compositum.

For each of the eleven supplied values, all 13 differences are nonzero
squares; all 143 checks were independently repeated. There are two distinct
rational `y` values, and the same local argument proves splitting at each
of the 22 affine places. Complete splitting leaves rational places upstairs,
so the full constant field remains `F_p`. A nontrivial constant extension
would preclude such degree-one places. Hence there is no hidden constant
extension behind the degree or genus calculation.

## 2. Infinite Tower and Parameter Accounting

Global class-field theory makes this split elementary extension a quotient
of the `S`-class group, giving `d_2 Cl_S >= 12`. The unit group has free rank
22 and constants contribute one mod-2 dimension, so `d_2 O_S^* = 23`.
Schoof's criterion has threshold `2+2*sqrt(d_2 O_S^*+1)`. The margin is
`(12-2)^2-4*24=4>0`; the application is valid over this odd prime field,
despite the paper's title mentioning `F_2`.
[Schoof, Theorem 2.3](https://www.mat.uniroma2.it/~schoof/f2.pdf).

There are arbitrarily large finite subextensions. Finite 2-group subgroup
chains supply intermediate dyadic degrees. Starting with a fixed finite
subextension such as `X8` or the full Kummer extension still leaves an
infinite extension above it; one can select a cofinal nested chain containing
it. The phrase "every degree" must not be confused with "every cover of that
degree contains the chosen subextension."

All selected places split in every tower level. Hurwitz and pullback give:

```text
g = 5e+1;       deg I = e;        deg D = 22e;
K_X = div(pi^*(dx/y)) = 10I;
G = 10I+3R;    d = 10e+3 = 2g+1;
ell(G) = 5e+3; ell(2G) = 15e+6.
```

Here `K_X=10I` is an actual choice of canonical divisor, not just a degree
calculation. Evaluation on `L(G)` is injective since `22e>10e+3`. Evaluation
on `L(2G)` is injective for dyadic `e>=4` since `22e>20e+6`.

The needed multiplication result holds at the endpoint: divisors of degrees
at least `2g+1` and `2g` have surjective section multiplication. In particular
`L(G)^t=L(tG)` for all positive integers `t`. Evaluation therefore identifies
the square and cube with the evaluations of the full corresponding spaces.
[Couvreur, Marquez-Corbella, Pellikaan, Theorem 5 and Corollary 6](https://arxiv.org/html/1401.6025v3#S2.SS2).

I checked the HTML inequalities: the PDF text extraction sometimes renders
`>=` as `>`. There is no endpoint gap at `d=2g+1`.

The point-count argument for rational gonality is sound: an `F_p`-defined
nonconstant map to `P^1` has at most its degree times `p+1` rational points
upstairs. Thus the lower bound `ceil(23e/(p+1))` applies to **rational**, not
automatically geometric, gonality.

## 3. Conditional Refined E Bound

This section checks the AG and arithmetic input, not USD correctness.
For a nonzero evaluated `f in L(G)`, let `Z` be its evaluation zero divisor
and `z=deg Z`. Injectivity and the divisor bound give `z<=d`. Since
`deg(2G-Z)>=d=2g+1`, Riemann-Roch gives

```text
dim ev_D L(2G-Z) = W-z,       W=15e+6.
```

Consequently the shortened-square expression is bounded by

```text
sum_{v != 0} p^(W-z(v))/(p-1)^(m-z(v))
 <= (p^k-1) p^W/(p-1)^m
 < p^(9-2e) (1-1/p)^(-22e)
 < p^9 (2/p^2)^e.
```

The last step uses `(1-1/p)^22 >= 1-22/p > 1/2`. At `e=8` the coarse bound
is `256/p^7<1`. This proves the advertised arithmetic for all dyadic `e>=8`;
the independent exact-rational checks at 8, 16, and 32 are controls only.
The expression must not be substituted for the complete published quantum
failure bound without the separately reviewed reduction. The source's
conditional wording is appropriate.

## 4. Degree-Eight Function and Differential

The classes `x(x-1)`, `x(x-2)`, `x(x-3)` are independent modulo the
13-factor relation: their branch masks span the eight even subsets of
`{0,1,2,3}`, none of whose nonzero elements is the full 13-element mask.
Thus this is genuinely a degree-eight extension over `K`, of genus 41.

The quotient `w1*w2/x` is regular at finite places. Its square is
`(x-1)(x-2)`, so it is integral over `F_p[x]`; alternatively at `x=0`
the two numerator valuations cancel the denominator valuation exactly.
One must use the normalized smooth model here, not the singular affine
equations as a substitute for it.

With `x=u^-2` and the positive infinity branches, independently multiplying
binomial series over the rationals gives

```text
f = -66 + 62x - 36w1 + 18w2 - 45 w1*w2/x + w3
  = (-27/32) u^8 + O(u^10).
```

The coefficients through this order have denominators invertible modulo
100003. The leading coefficient reduces to 28125, not zero. At the other
seven infinities the order is exactly -2, with leading coefficients
`-2,54,52,162,160,36,34` in the sign order recorded in the JSON. Hence
`f in L(2I-9R0)`: the required order at `R0` is 7, because `2I` already
contributes coefficient 2 there. Actual order 8 leaves one extra order.

Let `H=product_j(x-x_j)`. All selected finite points are unramified for `x`,
and the eleven roots are distinct, so

```text
div(H) = D-22I;
div(dx/y) = 10I;
div(omega), omega=dx/(yH), = 32I-D.
```

The second identity includes the finite hyperelliptic branch points: there
the zero of `dx` cancels that of `y`. At infinity its order is 10. Pullback
along an unramified cover adds no different divisor.

It follows that `div(f*omega)>=30I+9R0-D=3G-D`. At `P` in the evaluation
set, `x-x(P)` is a uniformizer and the residue is exactly
`f(P)/(y(P)*H'(x(P)))`, with no missing factor of 2 or sheet multiplicity.
The residue theorem applied to products with `L(3G)` proves orthogonality.

The residue map on `Omega(3G-D)` is injective. A differential in its kernel
has no pole at any evaluation point and lies in `Omega(3G)`, which is zero
because `3d>2g-2`. Thus nonzero `f*omega` cannot give an all-zero vector.

For `rho:X->X8`, pullback yields
`div(rho^*f)+2I>=9 rho^*R0>=9R` when `R` lies above `R0`. The same
differential proof applies on `X`. Locally, residues are unchanged under
these unramified rational lifts; the vector is not formed by summing or
tracing fibres. The checked full support of the explicit degree-eight
residue vector therefore also persists.

## 5. Any Annihilator, Full Rank, and Every Syndrome

Set `V=ev_D L(G)` and `U=V^{*3}`. Normal generation supplies the crucial
equality `U=ev_D L(3G)`. AG evaluation/residue duality identifies `U^perp`
with the residue code of `Omega(3G-D)`.

Here is the distance argument without a black-box distance assertion. For a
nonzero residue vector, write `T` for its support. Its differential has at
most simple poles at `T`, is regular at the other evaluation points, and
has divisor at least `3G` elsewhere. Taking degrees gives

```text
2g-2 >= 3d-|T|,
|T| >= 3d-2g+2 = 20e+9 > 10e+3 = d.
```

If `B` restricted to `T` had deficient row rank, a nonzero function in the
complete `L(G)` row space would vanish at all points of `T`. Its zero
divisor there would have degree greater than `d`, impossible. This proves
full row rank for **every** nonzero annihilator, not just the analytic one
or a random draw. A first nonzero nullspace basis vector is sufficient.

For completeness, the solver algebra can be checked directly. Put all
coordinates outside `T` equal to 1 and subtract their syndrome contribution.
Let `A=B_T`. Select an invertible column minor `M` and let `w` indicate its
columns. Set `nu=2` and

```text
q0 = (h^-1 * w - nu*h)/4;
(M M^t) z = 2(s_adjusted - A q0);
q = h * ((A^t z + w*h^-1)^2 - nu)/4.
```

Products and squares of vectors are coordinatewise. The quadratic syndrome
term vanishes because `h` annihilates all triple products of rows; the
linear term is `M M^t z/2`. The minor is square and invertible, so `M M^t`
is invertible even over this finite field. Nonvanishing follows because
`nu` is a nonsquare. This is the existing quadratic-ansatz algorithm, not a
new solver. [Ragavan-Shutty, Theorem 6.2 and Section 6.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00502v2#S6).

One can form all triple row products, take their nullspace, and select the
minor by Gaussian elimination. Even this unoptimized procedure is
polynomial in the explicit matrix dimensions. `p` is fixed, and the
nonsquare 2 is verified directly. There is no curve-recovery advice,
enumeration of all codewords, successful random-annihilator assumption, or
syndrome restriction. Nothing in this argument gives a uniform output law.

## 6. The 93-Section Mutation Obstruction

On the full Kummer curve, each radical `z_i=sqrt(x-i)` has simple poles
at the infinities and no finite poles. Pair products have poles at most
2. The functions `1,x,z_i,z_i*z_j (i<j)` therefore lie in `L(2I)`.

Over `F_p(x)`, the full field has degree `2^13` and its radical characters
are distinct. The 13 singleton and 78 pair characters are distinct from
each other and the trivial character. Character projection proves that no
linear relation can mix these terms; `1,x` are independent over `F_p`.
Thus there are 93 independent functions. Pullback on function fields is
injective. I also constructed a 93-by-93 finite evaluation matrix on two
split fibres and found rank 93, independently of a full 90,112-column code.

For any canonical divisor `K_X`, effective `E` of degree `r>=3`, and
`G=K_X+E`, put `D'=D_full-P`, where `0<=P<=D_full` and `deg P=u`.
Assume ordinary evaluation is defined, i.e. `supp G` avoids `supp D'`.
The linear equivalences `K_X~10I`, `D_full~22I` give

```text
K_X-3G+D' ~ 2I-3E-P.
```

Restriction to the finite subscheme of effective divisor `3E+P` imposes
at most `3r+u` linear conditions, including multiplicities and nonrational
places with their degrees. Therefore

```text
ell(K_X-3G+D') >= 93-3r-u.
```

Multiplication applies since `deg G=2g-2+r>=2g+1`. The residue kernel
`Omega(3G)` is zero, as above. Hence this is genuinely a lower bound on
the cube-annihilator dimension, not just on a function space with an
uncontrolled evaluation kernel. A universally nonnegative version is
`max(0,93-3r-u)`, as in the helper.

For positive lower bound, every nonzero annihilator has support at least
`3d-2g+2=20e+3r>d=10e+r`; the same solver applies whenever `B` is the
complete full-row-rank evaluation matrix. In particular `r=3,u<=83`
forces an annihilator; the bound at `u=84` is zero and proves neither
existence nor nonexistence. This proof does not assert that any degree-three
class is effective, or that arbitrary covers contain the full Kummer field.

## 7. Completeness of the Degree-Eight RR Construction

The degree-eight extension over `K` is degree 16 over `F_p(x)`. Its
characters are precisely the eight even subsets of `{0,1,2,3}` and their
complements in `{0,...,12}`. Choose `z_T^2=product_{i in T}(x-i)` with
consistent multiplicative signs. Complement terms are represented by
`y/z_T`, as in the implementation.

There is a useful integral-basis check beyond dimension matching. Project
any function regular at all finite places onto a character, possible because
16 is invertible. The component has the form `a_T(x) z_T`. At a branch
place its valuation is `2 ord(a_T)+epsilon`, `epsilon` either 0 or 1;
a negative coefficient valuation cannot remain regular. At all other
finite places there are no compensating zeros of `z_T`. Therefore every
`a_T` is polynomial, not a rational coefficient with hidden denominators.

The divisor `NI` is invariant under the full group, so projection preserves
its pole bound. Each component consequently has monomials `x^j z_T` with
`2j+|T|<=N`. This proves completeness of the ambient pole-filtered list.
Intersecting `L(13I)` with the jet conditions at the seven other infinities
therefore gives the full `L(10I+3R0)`, not just a convenient subcode.

The independent construction obtains 64 ambient terms, jet rank 21,
dimension 43, and evaluation rank 43. The input row space equals the row
space in the source receipt, not merely a space of matching dimensions.
The computed square/cube ranks are 126/173 and the nullity is 3.

As a separate equality check I built `L(3G)` directly using 39 as the
ambient infinity pole cap and 30 at the other infinities. Its function
dimension is 209, evaluation rank 173, and evaluation kernel dimension 36.
The evaluation row space equals the Schur cube. The residue image of
`L(2I-9R0)` has dimension 3 and equals the entire matrix cube annihilator.

The same independently assembled method gives 624 ambient terms and jet
rank 581 for `83R0`, leaving a full 43-dimensional space; its square/cube
ranks are again 126/173, and one matrix-only witness was verified. This
last result is explicitly finite-level evidence only.
