# A Paid Normalizer Route For Bounded-Index p-Group HSP

2026-10-23. Local mathematical proposal; INDEPENDENT REVIEW PENDING.
The scalable implementation checks public action algebra. Separate bounded
Fourier simulations now learn hidden intersections; they do NOT implement
black-box input acquisition, a scalable quantum receiver, or a speedup.

## Why This Pass

The repository's BOUNDED_CODIMENSION_HSP_REDUCTION.md needs explicit Lie
coordinates. Applying it to arbitrary growing-class black-box groups by
assuming an efficient Lazard conversion would be an unjustified input upgrade.
This note proposes avoiding that conversion. The original explicit backend
linearizes only the action on a public abelian normal subgroup, using its
full prime-power module. A subsequent primary-source audit found an older,
more general normalizer route: polynomial normalizer algorithms with paid
collection. Section2b explains why quantum collection appears sufficient
for ALL primes. This is reuse of existing machinery, not a new normalizer
discovery. See QUOTIENT_BRIDGE_AUDIT.md for the remaining interface review.

The resulting proposed corollary is polynomial-time quantum HSP for each
FIXED bound k on log_p([G:H]), in uniquely encoded finite p-groups of
arbitrary class and exponent, CONDITIONAL on the repository's fixed-class
Rees theorem. For k<=2 the terminal algorithm can instead be the published
nil-2 algorithm, so this specialization has no Rees dependency. Both the
new reduction and this corollary still need independent proof/prior-art
review. No claim of a Shor-level result follows.

This revision removes the previous p<=N sampling branch, but does NOT
assert an FPT bound uniform in k: the terminal algorithm's parameter
dependence still needs an all-prime audit. Nor does it solve the general
dihedral HSP, whose hidden index grows. It supplies no hard native source.

## 1. Exact Input And Paid Existing Algorithms

Input is a verified prime p, generators of a finite p-group G with unique
L-bit encodings and coherent multiplication/inversion, and an ordinary
coherent function f with f(x)=f(y) iff x^-1*y belongs to H. Promise
[G:H]<=p^k. Output generators for H in the ORIGINAL group. All black-box
operation and f costs, label widths, and classical output sizes are paid.
N=log_p(|G|)<=L is first computed; k may be replaced by min(k,N).

The following are existing quantum preprocessing, NOT new primitives:

- [Ivanyos--Magniez--Santha, quant-ph/0102014](https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0102014),
  Corollary5/Theorem6: constructive membership returning straight-line
  programs, presentations and nice composition-factor maps for uniquely
  encoded solvable groups. Theorem8 supplies normal HSP; Theorem10 treats
  a quotient by a supplied solvable normal subgroup. Section4.3 and Lemma9
  explicitly account for orthogonal coset-state encodings in quotients.
- [Watrous, quant-ph/0011023](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0011023): solvable
  group order/membership and approximate uniform subgroup states.
- Ordinary abelian HSP and abelian decomposition/coordinates; these are
  quantum work when the abelian group is only black-box encoded.
- [Ivanyos--Sanselme--Santha, 0707.1260](https://arxiv.org/abs/0707.1260):
  the nil-2 terminal solver, and the fixed-class outer reduction used by
  the repository's proposed Rees theorem.

In particular, a matrix-image element is NOT automatically a word in G.
Apply the constructive-membership algorithm in that explicit solvable
image, and evaluate the returned straight-line program on the original
generators. This pays for the lift without solving a fresh nonnormal HSP.

## 2a. A Large-Prime Public Normalizer Lemma

Let A be a known abelian normal subgroup, |A|=p^a, and suppose p>a.
Let K<=A be known completely. After abelian decomposition write

    A = direct_sum_i Z/(p^e_i),    sum_i e_i=a.

Conjugation by each public group generator gives an explicit automorphism
of this module. Let Gamma be the generated image. The kernel C_G(A) is
normal and is obtained with the known normal-HSP algorithm for this
public action map. Thus it remains to find Gamma_K and lift generators.

The semidirect p-group A rtimes Gamma has a Gamma-invariant composition
series 0=A_0<A_1<...<A_a=A with factors of order p. On each factor the
p-group Gamma acts trivially. Consequently every g-I lowers this flag.
The nonunital associative ring J generated by all g-I satisfies J^a=0.
No flag is needed for the algorithm: its existence proves termination.
The exact backend additionally checks all length-a products of the given
generator differences by additive-span propagation.

Since 1,...,a are units modulo exp(A), the finite polynomials

    log(g) = sum_(j=1)^(a-1) (-1)^(j+1)*(g-I)^j/j,
    exp(X) = I + sum_(j=1)^(a-1) X^j/j!

are inverse in J and 1+J. The usual nilpotent exp/log correspondence
(with all denominators prime to p) identifies log(Gamma) with the Lie
subring generated by the logs of its generators. Compute it by additive
subgroup closure and brackets. End(A) has p-length
sum_(i,j) min(e_i,e_j)<=a^2. Each strict enlargement raises its order by
at least p, so at most a^2 enlargements occur. Integer HNF/SNF, matrix
products and all coefficient bit lengths are polynomial in a and log p.

For L=log(Gamma), set

    M = { X in L : X*K subset K }.

This is an additive subgroup and a Lie subring. For g=exp(X),

    g(K)=K  iff  X(K) subset K.

Forward: g-I preserves K, hence its powers and their p-unit multiples do.
Backward: every exp term preserves K, and exp(-X) does too. Therefore
Gamma_K=exp(M). Additive generators of M exponentiate to GROUP generators:
their Lie closure is already M, so the same correspondence applies.

Solving X*K<=K is linear over finite abelian p-groups. In coordinates each
matrix entry from a p^e_j cyclic factor to a p^e_i factor is divisible by
p^max(e_i-e_j,0), with p^min(e_i,e_j) possible coefficients. Include these
divisibilities from the start. For every generator v of K impose
Xv=sum_j d_j*k_j modulo the coordinate moduli. A unimodular integer HNF
transformation gives the full relation lattice, including its saturation.
A rational nullspace with denominators cleared is not an adequate substitute.

Lift generators of Gamma_K to G using paid constructive membership.
Together with C_G(A) they generate exactly N_G(K). If A is self-centralizing,
the kernel is just A, so no further centralizer computation is needed here.

### Important Limits

- Reducing A or its action modulo p is invalid: on C_25 x C_5,
  [[1,5],[0,1]] reduces to the identity matrix modulo5 yet does not preserve
  the subgroup generated by (0,1).
- Taking only the additive span of the original generator logs misses
  commutators. Two adjacent UT3 generators have log span order p^2;
  their group and full log Lie ring have order p^3.
- Individual unipotence is insufficient. Opposite 2-by-2 elementary
  transvections each have nilpotent difference, but their common product
  ring is not nilpotent. The backend rejects the joint action.
- This is a SETWISE stabilizer, not the pointwise centralizer of K.
- The p>a condition is not dropped when denominators cease to be units.
- A proper subset of H intersect A cannot replace the complete intersection.
  The existing bounded-codimension counterexample still applies.

## 2b. A Known Normalizer Route For All Primes

Section3 of [Ivanyos--Sanselme--Santha](https://arxiv.org/html/0707.1260)
states that p-group normalizers are polynomial-time given refined
polycyclic presentations and efficient collection, via Eick's normalizer
method combined with Luks's subspace-stabilizer algorithm. We import that
statement, not an unstated polynomial guarantee for arbitrary presentations.
Their constant-class hypothesis supplies CLASSICAL collection in the
setting of that paper. Here we instead pay for QUANTUM collection.

Fix a valid cyclic composition series and its nice factor maps from IMS.
For an original encoded element, iteratively evaluate the next factor map
and remove the corresponding power of the fixed generator. This determines
one unique normal-word tuple. Compute a product by evaluating its two
normal words in the original black box, multiplying there, and computing
the tuple. All calls are polynomial-time quantum computations; no growing-
class classical collection bound is assumed.

The normalizer routine needs collection only on classical arguments and
returns a classical generating set. Substitute these bounded-error quantum
calls, amplify, and cap the Las Vegas preprocessing. Its output need not
be a unique quantum function. For genuinely coherent uses of coordinates,
use the retained-input compiler below, not this argument about classical
calls. Known solvable quotient preprocessing is always applied directly to
an original-group pair S/B, avoiding recursively nested wrapper costs.

Thus the proposed reduction can use the known normalizer for any p.
Section2a remains an independently testable transparent backend when p>a;
its restriction is NOT silently relaxed. The known-normalizer import and
its quantum-collection substitution still require independent review.
No implementation of Eick/Luks or the IMS preprocessing is in this bundle.

## 3. Find A Self-Centralizing Abelian Normal Subgroup

Start A=1. Compute C=C_G(A) via the public conjugation homomorphism and
normal HSP. Both C and A are normal in G. If C>A, form C_0=C and

    C_(j+1) = [G,C_j]*A.

Nilpotence ensures this reaches A within N steps. At the last term larger
than A choose a generator x outside A, using membership in A. Then
x centralizes A and [G,x]<=A. Adjoining x gives a strictly larger abelian
NORMAL subgroup. Repeat at most N times. Commutator normal closures and
generator pruning use the paid solvable-group operations above. They do
not enumerate conjugacy classes or elements of G.

At termination C_G(A)=A. The faithful action of G/A on A lowers a flag
of a order-p factors. The number of automorphisms trivial on those factors
is at most p^(a(a-1)/2): successively restrict to A_(a-1); the kernel has
at most p^(a-1) choices for the image of one top-factor lift, and induct.
Hence

    log_p |G| <= a(a+1)/2.

Also J^a=0 implies Gamma has class at most a-1, so gamma_a(G)<=A.
Another a commutators with G kill A through the flag. Therefore

    class(G) <= 2a-1.

These bounds do not require elementary exponent, a split extension, an
inclusion-maximal arbitrary abelian subgroup, or an ambient Lie presentation.
An arbitrary abelian subgroup is not enough: normality is essential.

## 4. Bounded Hidden Index Reduction

If k=0, return G from the promise. For ANY p, construct A as above.
If a<=k, the ambient group already has order at most
p^(k(k+1)/2) and class at most2k-1. Otherwise restrict the original oracle
to A and solve its abelian HSP to learn the COMPLETE K=H intersect A.
The index promise gives |K|>=p^(a-k)>1.

Compute S=N_G(K) by Section2b (or Section2a when p>a).
H<=S because A is normal and H normalizes
H intersect A. Then K is normal in S and contained in H. Replace the
instance by S/K with hidden subgroup H/K, and update

    k_new = k - log_p([G:S]).

This decreases log_p of the ambient order by at least one. There are at
most N reductions. Stop at an abelian remainder, k=0, or a<=k. Pull back
the terminal answer and all learned intersections to the original group.

### Quotients Are Paid, Not Implicitly Unique

Maintain original-group subgroups B<=H<=S with B normal in S. The current
group is Q=S/B. Every new quotient is kept as such a pair, with original
representative words. The known quotient algorithms provide public group
operations, coordinates in abelian factors, constructive membership, and
normal-HSP preprocessing. B is solvable and explicitly generated.

For a canonical quotient encoding one may use a nice cyclic composition
series: stripping its factor coordinates gives a UNIQUE normal-form tuple.
A representative is the ordered product of the fixed generators raised to
these coordinates. The representative need not be a homomorphic section.
The original f is constant on B-cosets, so evaluating f on representatives
hides H/B exactly. Each such evaluation uses ONE original f call plus
charged section arithmetic and cleanup; no label canonicalization is needed.

Coordinate computation or multiplication may itself use bounded-error
quantum algorithms. Do NOT silently treat these as perfect classical gates.
Each computes a unique function value with worst-input failure at most
delta. Retain the input, coherently compute/copy/uncompute the answer;
the initialized-scratch operator norm error is at most 2*sqrt(delta),
including an ARBITRARY quantum target of the XOR oracle. The sharper
sqrt(2*delta) identity holds for a fresh zero target only. For T compiled
calls, diamond error is at most 4*T*sqrt(delta); delta<=epsilon^2/(16*T^2)
therefore suffices for this part of the budget. Amplification cost is
logarithmic in that bound. Separately union-bound erroneous classical
preprocessing and all abelian-intersection failures. Use predetermined caps.

This is the place independent review must check carefully. A mere classical
membership decision, an unspecified presentation, or a convenient matrix
answer does not discharge these operations. The cited constructive/quotient
algorithms and the explicit unique-output cleanup are the claimed bridge.

## 5. Terminal Solver And Scientific Scope

For fixed k, the terminal class2k-1 is fixed, even if the original class
grew. Conditional on the repository's Rees whole proof and its known outer
reduction, solve this terminal HSP in polynomial time. The proposed
all-prime normalizer reduction itself does not depend on Rees.

For k<=2 the terminal group has order at most p^3. Every group of that
order has class at most two. The PUBLISHED nil-2 solver therefore suffices.
This is a proposed independent corollary from known quantum primitives
plus the normalizer reduction, not an independent certification of novelty.

The public-algebra tests include semidirect actions of classes4,8,12 whose
supplied codimension-two invariant intersections leave a two-dimensional
abelian module and a class-two group of order p^3. They use SUPPLIED
intersections and do not run hidden learning or black-box preprocessing.
The separate fixed class-three control DOES learn two intersections by
finite Fourier simulation from original oracle labels. Its address spaces
are enumerated, and the numeric oracle is classically easy. Its successful
pullback checks composition, not asymptotic advantage or scalable execution.

Classical collision/rejection algorithms still give p^(k/2) or p^k type
query upper bounds when public sampling and reconstruction are charged.
These are not lower bounds. Large p may make such bounds exponential in
log p, but explicit numeric labels or a special natural action can admit
much better classical algorithms. No hard native family has been found.

## 6. Attempts To Disprove The Proposal

1. Mixed exponents could invalidate a field-log argument. The construction
   uses End(A) with its actual torsion and p-unit denominators instead.
   Cyclic C_125 and noncommutative C_25 x C_5 actions pass exact controls.
2. Generator logs might fail to generate the full action. Commutator closure
   is retained and compared against every group element in bounded controls.
   The general assertion remains a nilpotent exp/log proof obligation.
3. Image generators might be unliftable cheaply. The verified primary source
   explicitly returns straight-line constructive membership, not just a bit.
4. Quotient encodings could hide a canonicalization cost. Section4 gives
   the representation and clean bounded-error compilation; these costs are
   part of the proposal, not implemented by the exact arithmetic backend.
5. Bounded index could be mistaken for small index cardinality. The input
   promise is exponent k; p^k may be exponential. The revised reduction
   does not pay p^k by sampling, but terminal parameter costs still matter.
6. This may already be a known corollary. Targeted primary-source searches
   did not certify novelty. No discovery admission is justified.
7. The reduction may have no useful hard application. This remains a real
   risk. Do not respond by adding more nearby parameter variants or synthetic
   oracle benchmarks; next scrutiny should target the whole quotient bridge,
   or an actual native source that avoids the prior classical falsifiers.
