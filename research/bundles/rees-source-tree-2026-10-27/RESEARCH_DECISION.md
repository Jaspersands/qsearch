## A Precise Separating Family For Specialist Comparison

Use the free nilpotent Lie algebra of class three on r>=3 generators over
F_p, p>3. Its homogeneous dimensions are

    w_1=r, w_2=r(r-1)/2, w_3=(r^3-r)/3,
    n=w_1+w_2+w_3.

These are standard free-Lie/Witt dimensions, not a new construction. In
weight three the Hall words [[x_j,x_i],x_k], j>i and k>=i, number
sum_(a=1..r-1) a(a+1)=(r^3-r)/3. See the standard
[Witt dimension treatment](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/bulletin-of-the-australian-mathematical-society/article/on-witts-dimension-formula-for-free-lie-algebras-and-a-theorem-of-klyachko/1E9387A845BAA2C3973E05F0D26539CB).
A
Hall-basis table has polynomial size in r and log p. The scope question is
arbitrary hidden Lie subalgebras, including nonhomogeneous ones; a supplied
grading of the AMBIENT does not give a hidden-subgroup-preserving dilation.

This family cannot simply be reidentified as an elementary-abelian-by-cyclic
exponent-p group. Here is the elementary obstruction. If I is any abelian
Lie subalgebra, its projection U into L/[L,L] has dimension at most one:
for two independent projected vectors, their bracket maps to their nonzero
exterior product in gamma_2/gamma_3 = exterior^2(F_p^r). Thus

    dim I <= dim[L,L]+1 = n-r+1.

For r>=3 there is no abelian subalgebra of codimension one, hence no such
ideal or index-p abelian subgroup under Lazard. This excludes that specific
direct identification. It does NOT exclude a more sophisticated reduction
to known algorithms, prove classical hardness, or establish novelty.

The other obvious overlaps are insufficient by their stated hypotheses:
class is three rather than two; primes and commutator rank can grow; the
abelian derived subgroup has exponent p and need not be smoothly solvable
in the bounded-exponent/polylog-index sense. The fixed-class known
semidirect theorem still matters for subfamilies and for the filiform
countercontrol in RESULT.md. Do not use that control as a novelty witness.

## What Must Be Established Next

The constructive mechanism is the public Rees lift preserving full degree-
one h, followed by all-record central descent and ordinary final Fourier
sampling. The claimed advantage is total time polynomial in n and log p
at FIXED class for an established oracle problem. This is not evidence for
a classical-input application or a practical speedup.

The strongest novelty falsifier is a valid prior reduction of arbitrary
hidden subgroups in the separating family (or all class-three inputs) to
the known HPGP/semidirect or smoothly-solvable algorithms with polynomial
TOTAL cost. Being able to write BCH as bounded-degree polynomials is not
enough: the resulting labels must satisfy the actual graph-state promise.
The existing `research/HPGP_COSET_INTERFACE_AUDIT.md` already treats fixed
public charts and naive quotient-register erasure, including quantitative
counterexamples. Repeating those restricted audits is not the next task;
an adaptive/collective reduction or another actual theorem match is needed.

The strongest correctness falsifiers remain failure of exact hidden fibers,
wrong nonnormal action orientation, an uncharged state-access operation,
record-dependent output, or a published diagonal routine whose exact
hypotheses do not match. The existing independent review addressed these
within its bounded scope. Tests cannot settle the universal theorem.

Next pass should concentrate on that single reduction-equivalence question,
or on a genuinely different constructive receiver with a paid application.
Do not add another registry/workbench, extend near-identical ring parameters,
or optimize scalar solvers to evade a source-consumption obstruction.
