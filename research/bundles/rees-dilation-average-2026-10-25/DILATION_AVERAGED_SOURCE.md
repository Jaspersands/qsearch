## Decision And Exact Task

The preceding state-source conversion charged the smallest displacement of
ANY non-symmetry. That can be exponentially small in log(p), even when the
state is a constant distance from acquiring any extra subgroup symmetry.
For class at most THREE and primes p>=131, the construction below replaces
that pointwise promise with a subgroup-averaged robustness promise. It uses
the SAME pure-state copies and controlled LINEAR group representation.

Input: explicit L/F_p of class <=3, G=exp(L), fresh IID copies of a pure
rho=|psi><psi| on a q-qubit register, delta>0, and a UNIFORM COHERENT action

    SELECT(U): |g>|v> -> |g> U(g)|v>.

U is an exact unitary linear representation in the ideal comparison. Let
C_U(eta) be the paid cost of implementing SELECT(U) to half-diamond error
at most eta, including its native width q and circuit description. Separate
classically indexed circuits U(g) do not by themselves supply this access.
State acquisition is a paid resource, not free preparation or inverse
preparation. The target remains

    H = {g : U(g) rho U(g)^* = rho}.

For K<=G write T_K(rho)=|K|^-1 sum_g U(g)rho U(g)^*. Promise

    TD(rho,T_K(rho)) >= delta whenever K is not contained in H.       (P)

Neither H nor a twirling oracle nor a density matrix is supplied. This is
a mathematical input promise, NOT something certified by the diagnostics.
The inverse of delta is charged. Only subgroups generated by the three
coefficients of a Rees evaluation curve actually enter the proof; (P) is a
simple stronger formulation. Mixed-state conjugation is NOT covered here.

The old pointwise trace-distance promise delta_0 implies (P) with
delta=delta_0/2: T_K(rho) is K-invariant, so every g in K moves rho by at
most 2 TD(rho,T_K(rho)). The converse has no prime-independent constant.

## 1. Universal Nonabelian Curve

Use the four-dimensional group F with coordinates (a,b,c,w) over F_p and

    (a,b,c,w)(a',b',c',w')
      = (a+a',b+b',c+c',w+w'+(ab'-ba')/2).
    gamma(t) = (t,t^2,t^3,0).

This is Heisenberg times an additive factor. In Lie coordinates its sole
nonzero bracket is [X1,X2]=W, with weights 1,2,3,3 respectively. Given
A_j in gamma_j(L), j=1,2,3, send Xj to A_j and W to [A1,A2]. All other
brackets vanish because their weight exceeds three. Thus this is a public
group homomorphism onto K=exp(Lie<A1,A2,A3>), and gamma(t) maps to
exp(t A1+t^2 A2+t^3 A3). This quotient fact is essential; the target Lie
algebra may have arbitrarily large dimension. No basis for an unknown H
is used. Vanishing or dependent coefficients cause no problem.

The curve generates F: three distinct nonzero t values span X1,X2,X3
in log coordinates by Vandermonde, and their Lie closure contains W.
This uses the exponent-p Lazard correspondence, with class two < p.

## 2. Uniform Walk Gap: Direct Proof And A Second Certificate

### 2.1 A Cubic Hankel Normal Form

This shorter argument was obtained after the four-step proof below. It
gives the stronger bound 2/sqrt(p) for the positive curve operator on ALL
nontrivial irreps, for every prime p>3. We retain p>=131 and gap1/8 in the
source contract and planner; enlarging the parameter range is not an
additional research result.

Write omega=exp(2*pi*i/p), X|y>=|y+1>, and Z|y>=omega^y|y>. The nonlinear
irreps are, for k!=0 and ell in F_p,

    pi_(k,ell)(a,b,c,w) = omega^(-kw+kab/2+ell*c) X^a Z^(kb).

Their product law is the law in Section1. Each is irreducible because
shift and clock generate the full matrix algebra; central characters
distinguish them. Together with p^3 linear characters their dimension
squares sum to p^4, so this list is complete.

Let M=E_t pi_(k,ell)(gamma(t)). Its (x,y) entry has t=x-y and is

    M[x,y] = omega^(c0*(x-y)^3+k*(x-y)^2*y)/p,
    c0=k/2+ell.

Put alpha=k-3c0 and beta=3c0-2k. The exponent expands to

    c0*x^3+(k-c0)*y^3+alpha*x^2*y+beta*x*y^2.

If alpha*beta!=0, relabel x=r*u with r=beta/alpha and put
lambda=beta^2/(3alpha). The exponent becomes EXACTLY

    (c0*r^3-lambda)*u^3+(k-c0-lambda)*y^3+lambda*(u+y)^3.

Thus unitary row permutation and diagonal phases turn M into the Hankel
matrix omega^(lambda*(u+y)^3)/p. Reversing the columns makes it circulant.
Its singular values are therefore

    |(1/p) sum_t omega^(lambda*t^3+z*t)|, z in F_p.

Because lambda!=0 and p>3, the additive
[Weil bound](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/bounded-gaps-between-primes/weil-exponential-sum-bound/67671DDEA0272CDF690C363F311ECCBC)
is at most 2/sqrt(p). This is an exact Fourier identity followed by a
uniform theorem, not a numerical singular-value extrapolation.

If alpha=0 or beta=0, they cannot BOTH vanish since alpha+beta=-k!=0.
After removing the cubic row/column phases, the kernel is respectively
omega^(beta*x*y^2)/p or omega^(alpha*x^2*y)/p. It is a Fourier matrix
composed with the squaring map (or the transpose). Nonzero square fibers
have two elements and the zero fiber one, so its norm is sqrt(2/p).
Ignoring these exceptional branches would invalidate the division above.

A nontrivial linear character gives a nonconstant degree-at-most-three
polynomial phase on gamma(t), with the same 2/sqrt(p) bound. Consequently
the positive-curve mean has norm <=2/sqrt(p) on the complement of invariants
in EVERY unitary representation of F, and hence in every quotient K.
For the retained p>=131 contract this is less than 7/8. The algebraic
coefficients can be computed without constructing a p-dimensional matrix;
the matrix implementations below are explicitly capped diagnostics only.

### 2.2 Alternating Word And Constructive Fibers

Let mu_+ be the law of gamma(t) for uniform t in F_p and mu_- its reflected
law. The alternating four-step map gamma(t0)^-1 gamma(t1)
gamma(t2)^-1 gamma(t3) has coordinate polynomials, for e=(-1,1,-1,1),

    F(t0,t1,t2,t3) = (sum e_i*t_i, sum e_i*t_i^2, sum e_i*t_i^3,
                     (1/2) sum_(i<j) e_i*e_j*(t_i*t_j^2-t_i^2*t_j)).

Their degrees are 1,2,3,3. Its Jacobian determinant is exactly

    6*(t0-t1)*(t1-t2)^2*(t2-t3)*(t0-t1-t2+t3).

It has degree five and value12 at(0,1,2,4), and is nonzero over EVERY
prime p>3. The symbolic certificate checks the whole identity, not a float.

Write d=t1-t0, e=t3-t2, g=t2-t1. Regularity means d,e,g,e-d are nonzero.
There are exactly p*(p-1)^2*(p-2) regular inputs. Singular mass is
4/p-5/p^2+2/p^3 <=4/p. An initial coarse Bezout bound was replaced by the
following ELEMENTARY constructive inverse; no generic fiber theorem is
needed for the final bound.

Given output(A,B,C,W), put R=C+6W-A^3, and let m=(t0+t1)/2,
n=(t2+t3)/2, D=n-m. Direct multiplication gives

    A=d+e, B=2(md+ne),
    C=3(m^2*d+n^2*e)+(d^3+e^3)/4,
    R=6de(D-A/2)=6deg.

For a regular input R!=0. If A!=0, define

    E=C-3B^2/(4A)-A^3/4,
    D=2AE/R-A/2, q=R/[6(D-A/2)].

Then d,e are the two DISTINCT roots of z^2-Az+q. Each ordering fixes
m=(B/2-eD)/A and n=m+D, hence all four original t values. Thus there are
at most TWO regular preimages. If A=0, regularity gives B!=0 and uniquely

    d=R/(3B), e=-d, D=-B/(2d), m+n=2C/(3B).

The midpoint sum and difference again fix the t values. A zero denominator
or failed original-word check rejects that branch. The implementation
returns all regular preimages, not all singular ones, using a quadratic
root routine and exact verification. It never enumerates F_p.

Split nu=mu_-*mu_+*mu_-*mu_+ into regular and singular input pushforwards.
Then nu_good(x)<=2/p^4, mass(nu_bad)<=4/p, and

    |F| sum_x nu_good(x)^2 <=2.

Every nonlinear irreducible representation of this Heisenberg product has
dimension p. Plancherel therefore implies, for each such pi,

    ||pi(nu_good)||_op <= ||pi(nu_good)||_HS <= sqrt(2/p),
    ||pi(nu)||_op <= sqrt(2/p)+4/p.

This is the ordinary finite-group Fourier dimension argument, not a new
mixing primitive; compare [Babai--Nikolov--Pyber](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A6152887a-76a4-4674-8e50-795b87300f87).
We use Plancherel directly, not a claim that F has no linear characters.

Put A=pi(mu_+). Crucially pi(nu)=(A^* A)^2, not A^4. For p>=131,
sqrt(2/p)+4/p<=1/2. Consequently

    ||A||^4 = ||(A^* A)^2|| <=1/2,
    ||A|| <=2^(-1/4) <7/8.

This controls singular values of the ACTUAL nonsymmetric positive-curve
operator. It does not infer an operator norm bound from spectral radius or
from A^4. Inverse group elements occur in this mathematical proof only;
the native source never prepares inverse or conjugate unknown states.

Linear characters were handled in Section2.1. This second route also proves
the retained gap1/8 and checks the nonabelian signs without depending on
the direct Hankel identity. It is a second argument by the SAME author,
not an independent review. No group or field enumeration is needed by the
source construction. The two-preimage inverse is an additional exact
certificate, not a physical source operation.

## 3. The Gap Seen By Native Pure Copies

Fix a Rees element a with log a=sum_j t^j A_j and K as in Section1.
Let rho be regarded as a unit vector in Hilbert-Schmidt space. Conjugation
is a unitary representation; its invariant projection is T_K. The walk
gap implies

    E_t |<psi|U(exp(sum_j t^j A_j))|psi>|^2
      <=1-(1/8)*||rho-T_K(rho)||_HS^2.

Indeed, decompose rho into invariant and orthogonal components. The walk
is identity on the first and has operator norm <=7/8 on the second.
Their cross terms vanish. The displayed expectation is real and nonnegative.
Conjugation/vectorization is used ONLY in the proof; conjugate copies are
not an input resource. No Hermiticity of the positive-curve mean is assumed.

For pure rho,

    ||rho-T_K(rho)||_HS^2 =1-Tr(rho*T_K(rho)),
    TD(rho,T_K(rho)) <=sqrt(1-Tr(rho*T_K(rho))).

If a is outside the lifted H, some A_j is outside h=log H, so K is not
contained in H. Promise(P) then lower-bounds the squared HS defect by
delta^2. This is where the new promise enters, and it cannot be omitted.

## 4. A Forward-Only Source Compiler

Reuse the EXISTING nonzero multiset S with sum_s s^j=0, j=1,2,3, and
at least three distinct values. Its construction costs and multiplicities
remain paid; the existing scalar design has M=|S|<=24. It is not a new
design theorem. Prepare M fresh psi copies and sample u uniformly in F_p.
On a public address a apply

    T_u(a) = tensor_(s in S) U(exp(E_(su)(log a))).

Each evaluation is a homomorphism. At u=0 all factors are identity, which
is a legal homomorphism for source preparation, NOT an invertible dilation
inside the receiver. Discard the scale record and targets after the
controlled-address operation. Independent packets use independent scales
and fresh native copies. Do not reuse or adapt to the discarded records.

The averaged overlap is

    Fbar(a)=E_u product_s <psi|U(exp(E_(su)(log a)))|psi>.

On the lifted subgroup it is EXACTLY one: the old zero-moment design
cancels every possible ray character, also after scaling by u. Off that
subgroup, retain any one factor s0 in the modulus bound. Since s0!=0,
s0*u is uniform, and Cauchy-Schwarz plus Section3 gives

    |Fbar(a)| <=sqrt(1-delta^2/8) <=1-delta^2/16.

The address kernel is Fbar(b^-1 a)/|A| with the old orientation convention.
k independent packets raise this kernel to the kth power. The existing
trace-norm amplification bound then gives an approximate mixed coset source
for the SAME lifted subgroup. One sufficient choice for B receiver leaves,
ell>=log2|A| and source error epsilon is

    gamma=delta^2/16,
    k=ceil((ell+ceil(log2(4B/epsilon)))/gamma),
    raw copies <= B*k*M.

The resulting amplification error is <=epsilon/4. Put Q=B*k*M. This is a
bound on BOTH native copies and coherent SELECT(U) calls, not a complete
elementary-gate bound. Allocate half-diamond error eta_U=epsilon/(8Q) per
SELECT call. The whole adaptive-channel hybrid then charges at most
Q*eta_U=epsilon/8 for these action approximations, including all references.

The controlled-action gate cost is at most Q*C_U(eta_U). Add public Rees
arithmetic, address preparation, moment-design preprocessing, uniform-scalar
sampling, native-copy acquisition and the full receiver. The native width
q and the action's uniform circuit description/cost cannot be omitted.
If SELECT(U) is supplied as a unit-cost oracle, the bound is explicitly
QUERY complexity. Conditional on a polynomial-cost uniform action and the
existing receiver, the full cost is polynomial in the complete input size
(including q and that description), log(p), inverse delta and inverse error.

The remaining 5epsilon/8 is available for the other implementation and
receiver errors; it is NOT certified by this source planner. Uniform field
scalars use capped public bit rejection, with their failure and synthesis
costs charged separately. The planner reports Q and eta_U but no total
gate estimate, native width, physical acquisition cost or valid receiver
cap. No native preparation inverse, twirling oracle, conjugate state, or
prime-sized bank is introduced. Mixed conjugation needs a separate argument.

## 5. Refutation Attempts And Scientific Limits

1. Adjacent phases: U(t)=diag(1,exp(2*pi*i*t/p)), psi=(|0>+|1>)/sqrt(2).
   H={0}; the old minimum trace displacement is sin(pi/p), while the only
   nontrivial subgroup twirl is distance 1/2. This proves the new promise
   is genuinely weaker with respect to p. It is an EASY ABELIAN control,
   already covered by known abelian methods, not a new hard application.
2. Almost invariant input: weights 1-epsilon and epsilon on those rays
   give twirl distance sqrt(epsilon*(1-epsilon)). The new gap also vanishes.
   The compiler does not extract arbitrarily tiny asymmetries for free.
3. Nonabelian signs: the actual positive-curve mean need not be Hermitian.
   Bounding A^4 is not a singular-value bound. The alternating four-step
   certificate bounds (A^* A)^2 and leaves the physical compiler unchanged.
4. Singular fibers: the two-preimage claim is for REGULAR inputs only.
   For example t0=t1 and t2=t3 give p^2 preimages of the identity. The
   proof explicitly removes the <=4/p singular input mass first.
5. This is not a generic fixed-class theorem. For class4, the universal
   curve includes additional independent brackets; the four-coordinate
   word map here no longer surjects onto the required subgroup. Nor does
   it apply to characteristic-two DCP, arbitrary mixed states, or noise
   that breaks the exact symmetry promise.
6. No classical separation follows. For an explicit density matrix one
   can compute symmetries directly with its representation-size costs;
   copy-only baselines must receive the same measurements/copies/actions.
   Known abelian learning and the repository's exposed diagonal-state
   attacks remain mandatory. There is still no established hard native
   nonabelian family or specialist novelty assessment.

The next useful question is a naturally motivated state family satisfying
(P), whose symmetry output matters and survives matched methods. Do not
extend to more nilpotency classes solely to create another parameter pass.

## 6. Application Gate

Existing algorithms already cover ordinary Pauli stabilizer learning and
abelian state symmetries; see [Hinsche--Eisert--Carrasco](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15770v2)
and [Holt--Subramanian](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38085v2).
The latter also studies nonabelian symmetry learning, so this note is not
a novelty claim for that broad task. A comparison must retain each paper's
group, source, gap, and computational-access assumptions.

Finding a symmetry of ONE state is not automatically finding a transporter
between TWO states. [Gheorghiu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12615v2)
reduce abelian pure-state isomorphism to a generalized-dihedral StateHSP.
Its swap/inversion element has order two. Our ambient groups have odd
prime exponent and cannot contain that nontrivial element. The standard
swap/wreath route therefore does not fit the theorem above. This rules
out an immediate application of THIS construction, not all possible
reductions or all state-isomorphism algorithms. No DCP speedup follows.

Before another receiver extension, require a concrete native family, a
paid preparation or physical copy source, proof of (P), and a useful output
not supplied by known stabilizer/abelian learning. Failing to find that
family is an application gap, not evidence of classical hardness.
