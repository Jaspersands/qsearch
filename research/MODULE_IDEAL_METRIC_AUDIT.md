# Literature Audit: Principal Ideals Are Not Module Geometry

EXACT COUNTEREXAMPLES / LOCAL DERIVATION REVIEW PENDING. No quantum speedup,
cryptographic attack, security guarantee, new hardness theorem or novelty
claim. This is a literature-to-falsifier pass, not an invented oracle problem.

## The Paper And The Exact Claim Scopes

[Luo, Part IV v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17412v1) claims a polynomial
quantum module-lattice attack. Section5.4's stated base case identifies
sqrt(2) as a unit and describes principal ideals through powers of a unit,
with exponents determined by ideal norm. Sections3.2/4.1 move from module
bases to ideal generators; Sections2.2/2.3/2.7 identify short-vector output
with affine secret recovery. The audit treats these as separate obligations,
not an established attack. The source record contains precise locations.

The FIRST claim has explicit algebraic counterexamples. The other two audits
show that specified shortcuts are insufficient; they are not a refutation
of a correctly formulated average-case metric theorem or decoding reduction
in a companion paper that has not been checked here. Do not promote this
audit into a general PIP or lattice-hardness theorem.

## Invalid Base Case Over Z[zeta8]

Use R=Z[x]/(x^4+1), x=zeta8. The real element a=x-x^3 satisfies a^2=2.
Its integer multiplication matrix has determinant4, so a is NOT a unit.
The element u=1+a has inverse a-1, since (1+a)*(a-1)=a^2-1=1.
Its norm is1, and all powers u^e generate the SAME unit ideal R. The ideal
norm therefore cannot determine e. The controls compute exact powers,
inverse products, multiplication determinants and integer HNF, without
floating embeddings or numerical eigenvalue classification.

The principal ideals (2) and (3) have integer indices16 and81 respectively.
Neither can be generated by any unit power. Thus the stated base case is
not a procedure for arbitrary principal ideals, even at this fixed field.
Replacing sqrt(2) with a genuine unit alone does not repair that assertion.
This says nothing against unrelated correct PIP algorithms.

## Same Principal Ideals, Unboundedly Different Module Metric

Let R=Z[x]/(x^d+1), d a power of two, and K>=1, N=K^2+1. Consider two
free rank-two R-modules in their ORIGINAL product coefficient metric:

    B_easy = [[N,0],[0,1]],
    B_shear = [[N,K],[0,1]].

Both have the same diagonal ideals (N),(1), determinant ideal (N) and full
integer Smith invariants: d copies of1 and d copies ofN. All those ideals
are already principal with known short rational generators. No class-number
or quantum-PIP hypothesis is needed.

The public unimodular R-basis change

    U=[[0,1],[1,-K]], det(U)=-1
    C=B_shear*U=[[K,1],[1,-K]]

has exact coefficient Gram C^T*C=N*I_(2d). Hence EVERY lattice vector has
squared coefficient length N*||z||_2^2 for an integer z, and

    lambda1(B_shear)^2=N,
    lambda1(B_easy)^2=1.

The ratio sqrt(N) grows without bound while rank stays TWO and the entire
diagonal ideal/Smith data remain equal. In the canonical metric that sums
all complex embeddings, power-two Parseval multiplies both squared lengths
by d; the ratio does not change. Full block matrix controls verify membership,
unimodularity, Gram and HNF identities. Larger cases use the same exact block
identities, not dense numerical determinants or experimental scaling fits.

This falsifies metric invariance, or a constant metric loss inferred ONLY
from these ideal data, for arbitrary free modules. It does NOT show that
the same bad geometry has nonnegligible probability under random MLWE.
Both engineered families have explicit polynomial classical short bases,
so they are not hard-instance generators or new algorithm candidates.
A valid module-to-ideal reduction must retain off-diagonal/coupled geometry,
provide a costed lift and prove its ORIGINAL-norm loss on the stated source.

## Homogeneous SVP Is Not Affine Key Recovery

For any odd q>=5, take public A=1 and t=2. The public homogeneous kernel is

    L={(x,y) in Z^2:x+y=0 modq}.

The vector v=(1,-1) is an EXACT shortest nonzero vector: squared norm2,
and none of the four squared-norm1 integer vectors belongs to L. Its SVP
factor is1<q/2. But its syndrome is0, not public target2. The actual bounded
secret (1,1) lies in the affine coset with syndrome2 and is unique under
coordinate bound1. Treating the exact SVP coordinates as the secret fails.

A valid attack may use short vectors as ingredients of a separate BDD,
embedding or decoding algorithm. That algorithm must be stated, costed and
proved under its uniqueness/error assumptions. This control refutes only
the direct identification shortcut, not every reduction from SVP to LWE,
nor random-source security or the possibility of a repaired attack.

## The Actual Public Kernel Has A Fixed Determinant Ideal

There is also a source-compatible statement, independent of the engineered
metric examples. For ANY public k-by-ell matrix A over R/qR, the public
kernel {(x,y):A*x+y=0 modq} has the explicit free R-basis

    [[q*I_k,-A],[0,I_ell]],

in coordinates (y,x). Its determinant ideal is ALWAYS (q^k), independent
of A. The coefficient-lattice determinant is q^(k*d). A change to any other
free R-basis multiplies its ring determinant by a unit and leaves this ideal
unchanged. This is valid for every public matrix, not an empirical assertion
about random inputs. The rank-two controls use the equivalent sign convention
g=A*f modq and verify the complete integer kernel basis.

Every generator of (q^k) is q^k*u for an integral unit u. Nonzero integral
coefficient vectors have norm>=1, and the signed ring monomials attain1.
Thus shortest coefficient generators have norm q^k and are signed monomials.
Power-two Parseval preserves this minimization in the all-embedding metric.
All their embedding moduli are q^k, so their projected log embedding is
EXACTLY zero. No floating logarithms, shortest-vector oracle or PIP is needed.

An iid centered small-entry ring matrix does NOT describe this public
kernel's determinant distribution. Its random-determinant variance law cannot
be inserted into that source ledger. An unknown short secret basis is not
available just because the secret coefficients are small. A useful different
ideal, weighted geometry or source-specific lift may still exist, but obtaining
it is a separate costed reduction, not information carried by (q^k).

## Research Decision

Do not import this paper's claimed attack into a candidate or baseline as
established. Preserve its individual claims and these independent falsifiers.
Principal-ideal, log-unit or norm-descent primitives may still be useful, but
only after the input ideal, metric transport, lift, access and decoding
obligations are actually satisfied. A short generator of a known determinant
ideal is not itself the missing collective receiver.

The independent checker must replay integer field arithmetic, all unit-power
identities, exact block relations, Smith controls and affine syndromes; scope
tampering must fail. Routine literature/registry/proof/CLI integration belongs
to Gemini/Antigravity. No claims about current deployed cryptographic security
follow from the controls.
