## Native Question And Selection

Input: fresh independent copies of an unknown mixed state rho, a uniquely
encoded finite solvable G with coherent public operations, and a costed
controlled unitary LINEAR representation R of G. Promise its one-sided
Bose stabilizer

    H={h : R(h) rho=rho}={h : Tr(R(h)rho)=1}

is permutable, and supply a usable lower bound 0<gamma<=1 such that
|Tr(R(g)rho)|<=1-gamma outside H. Output generators of H. Running time
and copy costs must be polynomial in the input/encoding size, 1/gamma,
and log(1/eta), where eta is the allowed failure probability. Physical
state-register size and controlled-representation gate costs are charged.
No preparation circuit, purification, inverse unknown source, or coherent
classical hiding labels are provided. Fresh IID copies are a source promise.

[Holt--Subramanian2609.38085v2, Problem6.41](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38085v2),
with the additional permutability promise and paid group representation.

Theorem7.13 extends full recovery with a Baer-index premise. Those are
strong quantum baselines, not automatic solutions of this input contract.
Abelian StateHSP already solves its abelian subfamilies. No claim about a
new classical separation follows from an unknown-quantum-state input.

## 1. Fresh Copies Produce Approximate Mixed Coset States

Let Q be any finite address set with paid coherent uniform preparation,
and sigma:Q->G a public representative map computed and cleaned while
retaining the address. Put N=|Q|. Prepare |Q> and t fresh IID copies of
rho. Apply controlled R(sigma(q)) to each copy and discard those copies.
The resulting address density is exactly

    S_t[q,r] = Tr(R(sigma(r)^-1 sigma(q)) rho)^t / N.

This is a controlled-unitary/partial-trace identity for mixed rho. It does
not treat a density matrix as an accessible state vector. If Q is a public
subgroup, take the inclusion. If Q=S/B with known B normal in S and B<=H,
take any paid forward section: sigma(r)^-1 sigma(q) belongs to H exactly
when r^-1 q belongs to (H intersect S)/B. No representation of Q on the
entire physical space or inverse section is needed.

Let C_Q be the ideal mixed coset density of that hidden intersection or
quotient. Its entries are 1/N on pairs in the same left coset and zero
otherwise. Same-coset entries of S_t are exactly 1/N; every other entry
differs by at most (1-gamma)^t/N. Thus

    ||S_t-C_Q||_F <= (1-gamma)^t,
    D_trace(S_t,C_Q) <= sqrt(N)/2 * (1-gamma)^t
                     <= sqrt(N)/2 * exp(-gamma*t).

Taking t>=ceil((ln(N)/2+ln(1/delta))/gamma), with t>=1, gives trace
distance at most delta. For a trivial address set return its unique basis
state directly. Original encoding width gives a safe upper bound for lnN.
Approximate uniform preparation, representatives and controlled gates add
their separately budgeted channel errors. There is no postselection or
index-dependent success probability.

For M adaptively chosen source requests, use delta<=eta/(4M) and fresh
copies on every request. The bound is uniform over correctly computed
public subgroups/sections, so a conditional-history hybrid costs M*delta.
Public preprocessing errors use a separate first-error budget. Correlated
banks with correct individual marginals are not covered.

## 2. Phase And Membership Boundaries

The source's modulus gap excludes nontrivial global phases. If instead
one assumes only 1-ReTr(R(g)rho)>=gamma outside the +1 stabilizer, use one
fresh |+> reference flag per copy and apply R(sigma(q)) only on flag1.
Discard flag and state together. The kernel becomes

    [(1+Tr(R(sigma(r)^-1 sigma(q))rho))/2]^t / N,

and |(1+z)/2|^2 <= (1+Re z)/2 <= 1-gamma/2 off the hidden cosets.
Then D_trace<=sqrt(N)/2 * exp(-gamma*t/4). This optional variant requires
the same consistently phased controlled representation; arbitrary unknown
unitaries cannot silently be controlled. Without the reference or a
modulus gap, tensoring a -1 overlap can create false +1 symmetries.

Candidate membership on a classical g is also paid: a real Hadamard test
has minus probability (1-ReTr(R(g)rho))/2. It is zero inside H and at least
gamma/2 outside. k fresh trials have false acceptance <=exp(-gamma*k/2).
This does not certify complete recovery from a list of verified generators.

Neither version concerns the conjugation stabilizer R(h)rhoR(h)^dag=rho.
It does not supply a coherent hiding-label function or a source purification.

## 4. The Two-Group Terminal Is Forward-Only In Bounded Error

[Imran--Ivanyos2304.08376, Lemma2 and Proposition3](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.08376)
supplies the needed conversion. Its exact Theorem1/Proposition2 uses a
source oracle AND its inverse. We do not use that exact interface.
Proposition3 omits the inverse but still phrases its input as a purification
oracle; the following copy-only statement is an operation-level CPTP
corollary, not a verbatim quotation of that theorem.

For a semi-elementary class-two 2-group Q, Q/Q' is elementary abelian
and Q' is central elementary abelian. The latter follows from
[x,y]^2=[x^2,y]=1. Put r=dim_F2 Q'. Lemma2 uses r+1 independent coset
sources, Fourier transforms over the public central subgroup, and a
nonempty zero-sum subset of their r-dimensional binary labels. Such a
subset is found deterministically by ordinary linear algebra. Append a
fresh uniform Q address and right-translate just the selected targets.
Retain the address, discard the targets/central labels/workspace, and push
to Q/Q'. The output is the subgroup state for HQ'/Q'. One erasure layer
suffices because the class is two.

The proof of Lemma2 acts only on accessible group registers and new public
registers. It never reads or transforms a source's purifying subsystem.
Therefore tracing that subsystem before the procedure gives the same
retained output. Fresh mixed copies suffice; this is neither cloning nor
an inverse preparation. Ordinary bounded-error abelian Fourier sampling
on the outputs recovers HQ'/Q', with no source inverse or postselection.

For arbitrary class-two 2-groups use the published semi-elementary descent
with the public repair in SEMI_ELEMENTARY_TORSION_REPAIR.md. Maintain
H0<=hiddenH, start K=N_Q(H0), and form the PUBLIC abelian quotient
A=K/(K'H0). Return immediately if H0=Q; at the start of every inner
iteration, return H0 if K=H0. The strict hidden-intersection invariant
below certifies this latter termination, including hiddenH=1. Otherwise
compute the COMPLETE A[2] using a paid abelian decomposition
or complete relation lattice; lift it together with ALL of K'H0. Do not
take top powers of an arbitrary generator list. The mixed generators
(1,0),(1,1) in C4 x C2 are the retained exact counterexample.

Restriction to this full preimage preserves a nontrivial hidden extension
of H0 because an element of order2 in (hiddenH intersect K)/H0 maps into
A[2]. Repeat until K/H0 is semi-elementary. The one-layer HSMC procedure
then computes (hiddenH intersect K)K'/H0. If this is K/H0, the p-group
Frattini argument gives K<=hiddenH, so STRICTLY increase H0 to K, using
the K>H0 guard. Otherwise restrict
K to its proper preimage and continue. Each outer growth and inner descent
has at most log2|Q| steps. All normalizers remain in the original Q; each
source section has a denominator KNOWN to lie in hiddenH.

K'H0 itself need not lie in hiddenH. Its quotient is used for public

## 6. The T-Conjugated-Stabilizer Application Fails This Promise

Primary [Lee et al. 2510.07872v3, DefinitionII.1 and Theorem1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07872v3)
uses G=D8^n and an order-two H={1,h}, with a reducible doubled physical
representation. Its published learner already handles the noncentral
involutions. Our permutability promise does not reach those cases.

In a p-group generated by elements of order p, every permutable H is
normal: for each generator x outside H, H has index p in the subgroup
H<x>, hence x normalizes H. D8^n is generated by involutions. A normal
order-two subgroup is central, so only h in Z(D8^n) survives. Restricting
the paid action to this public abelian center already solves those cases.
This is a scoped application rejection, independently reviewed; it is not
a classical simulation of unknown-state learning or a rejection of the
published nonabelian learner. Its arbitrary-representation extension has
additional partial-Fourier access that must not be silently supplied.

A separate representation guard: for any irreducible R and nonzero rho,
the core of its exact Bose stabilizer equals ker R. The core's nonzero
fixed space is G-invariant, hence is the whole representation. A faithful
irreducible representation with a nontrivial center cannot satisfy the
modulus gap: a nonidentity central element acts as a nontrivial scalar and
has overlap of modulus one. Neither observation rules out the flagged
real-gap variant. For example the faithful irreducible three-dimensional
M27 clock/shift representation, with a=diag(zeta9,zeta9^4,zeta9^7), has
nonnormal permutable stabilizer <b> on the uniform vector. Its overlaps
are zero off <a^3,b> and zeta3^j on a^(3j)<b>. It has real gap at least one
but violates the modulus gap. The paper's DOUBLED representation is
reducible, so the irreducible-kernel conclusion must not be applied to it.

Selection: park further permutable-StateHSP application work until a
motivated family passes these exact promise and prior-algorithm gates.
