Featured Exhibit 01 • Commutant Geometry

Anatomy of an invariant: resolving degeneracy for $S_8$ and $S_9$

A concrete walkthrough of how Q-Search generated a non-trivial algebraic invariant, mathematically proved it against known collisions, and mapped its physical falsification boundary.

Spectral Degeneracy & Natural-Access Obstruction

In non-abelian hidden subgroup problems on $S_n$, candidate quantum algorithms measure collective invariants of multiple coset states. The central challenge: Can an algebraic invariant separate hidden conjugate classes without exponentially decoding the full copy register?

§ Theorem 1 (Commutant Multiplicity Splitting)
For all $n \le 9$, let $T = T_{T1} + c T_{C1}$ be the synthesized commutant observable with coupling parameter $c \neq 0$. Then $T \in \operatorname{End}_{S_n \wr S_k}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 3})$ has simple spectrum on the commutant space, resolving 20 / 20 targets at $n=8$ and 27 / 27 targets at $n=9$ without pairwise eigenvalue collisions.
Proof Outline & Gram Determinant Check →
The proof computes fourth moments over the commutant basis and establishes that the discriminant $\Delta(c) = \det(\operatorname{Gram}(T))$ is non-vanishing for all $c \in \mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$. Verified deterministically in tests/test_commutant_splitting.py.
Δ Theorem 2 (Natural-Access Factorial Obstruction)
The natural weak-Fourier sampling probability $P_{\text{natural}}$ of obtaining the fixed-sector coupling tree basis states decays factorially: $$P_{\text{natural}} = \frac{d_W^3 d_\xi \operatorname{Tr}(F)}{(n!)^3} \le \frac{25}{3}\frac{n^9}{(n!)^3}$$ Consequently, fixed-sector postselection cannot yield a bounded-error polynomial quantum algorithm. Generic amplification requires uniform adaptation across high-dimensional partitions.
Representation Character Calculation →
Derived from the Schur-Weyl duality decomposition of the 3-copy state space $\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 3}$ and the Littlewood-Richardson coefficients of $S_n \wr S_3$. Verified in tests/test_fourier_decay.py.
Epistemic Verdict
The finite algebraic separation is mathematically certified, but fixed-sector postselection cannot yield an asymptotic speedup. A viable algorithm must consume typical high-dimensional irrep labels.

What Q-Search successfully killed

Negative results are first-class scientific assets. Here are three representative candidate shortcuts that Q-Search rigorously disproved with automated attack suites.

Dihedral HSP DHS-NEAREST-PLANE

Lattice Nearest-Plane Sieve Collapse

The Hypothesis

Nearest-plane lattice decoding on Gowers-sieve coset states recovers dihedral reflections in polynomial time.

The Falsifier

All-target censuses across $16.7\times 10^6$ assignments proved fixed-depth lists collapse exponentially at scale. Small-$n$ success was an artifact.

Code Equivalence CODE-GOPPA-SYZYGY

Goppa Code Syzygy Collisions

The Hypothesis

High-weight syzygies of alternant codes provide a quantum invariant that resists classical permutation attacks.

The Falsifier

An automated classical hull-projector reduction resolved the syzygy collisions in polynomial classical time, dequantizing the advantage.

Coset States COSET-NATURAL-ACCESS

Postselecting stable 3-copy coupling trees provides efficient binary coset state discrimination.

The Falsifier

Exact weak-Fourier trace proved natural sampling mass decays factorially as $O(n^9 / (n!)^3)$, ruling out generic amplification.
