How Q-Search works

Most quantum algorithm proposals fail because they collapse when scaled or when tested against classical dequantization. Q-Search operates an automated falsification loop designed to disprove its own candidates before any speedup is claimed.

Anatomy of an invariant & its boundary

A concrete walkthrough of how Q-Search generated a non-trivial algebraic invariant, proved it against known collisions, and immediately mapped its physical falsification boundary.

Resolving Coset Degeneracy for S8 and S9

In non-abelian hidden subgroup problems on the symmetric group Sn, candidate quantum algorithms measure collective invariants of multiple coset states. The central question: Can a low-degree invariant separate all hidden conjugacy classes without exponentially decoding the full copy register?

Step 01

The Hypothesis

Test whether central commutant invariants of Sn ≀ Sk can distinguish hidden involutions with an inverse-polynomial spectral gap.

Hypothesis
Step 02

The Collision Barrier

Standard two-generator invariants (TC2 / TT1) failed at n=8, collapsing into exact spectral degeneracies on 2 known graph targets.

Barrier Found
Step 03

The System's Discovery

Q-Search synthesized an independent third generator TT1 + c·TC1. Fourth moments proved simple spectrum for all c ≠ 0: separating 20/20 targets at n=8 and 27/27 targets at n=9.

Verified Invariant
Step 04

Automated Falsification

The reduction gate computed the exact natural-access sampling probability: bounded by (25/3)n9 / (n!)3. Postselecting this branch from raw cosets decays factorially.

Obstruction Proved
Step 05

Epistemic Verdict

The finite algebraic separation is mathematically certified, but fixed-sector postselection cannot yield a speedup. A viable algorithm must adapt to typical high-dimensional partitions.

Negative Results Spotlight

What Q-Search successfully killed

Negative results are first-class scientific assets. Here are three representative candidate shortcuts that Q-Search rigorously disproved with automated attacks.

Dihedral HSP DHS-NEAREST-PLANE

Lattice Nearest-Plane Sieve Collapse

The Hypothesis

Nearest-plane lattice decoding on Gowers-sieve coset states recovers dihedral reflections in polynomial time.

The Falsifier

All-target censuses across millions of targets proved fixed-depth lists collapse exponentially. Low-n success was a finite-dimension artifact.

Code Equivalence CODE-GOPPA-SYZYGY

Goppa Code Syzygy Collisions

The Hypothesis

High-weight syzygies of alternant codes provide a quantum invariant that resists classical permutation attacks.

The Falsifier

An automated classical hull-projector reduction resolved the syzygy collisions in polynomial classical time, dequantizing the advantage.

Coset States COSET-NATURAL-ACCESS

Fixed-Sector Frame Postselection

The Hypothesis

Postselecting stable 3-copy coupling trees provides efficient binary coset state discrimination.

The Falsifier

Exact weak-Fourier trace proved natural sampling mass decays factorially as O(n9 / (n!)3), ruling out generic amplification.
